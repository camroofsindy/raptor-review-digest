Who they are

Since their inception in 2012, Weathertight Roofing has grown from a local family-owned operation into what they position as central Indiana's roofing leader, providing quality service across Fishers, Monticello, and Muncie.

The company has achieved GAF Master Elite status and, critically, claims over 20 years of experience in handling insurance claims, with a specialization in post-disaster recovery.

They install premium roofing systems, gutters, and siding throughout central Indiana.

Their 307 Google reviews at a perfect 5.0 — with +9 in the last 30 days — make them a small but credibility-dense shop.

They operate with three locations and advertise 24/7 emergency availability.

The insurance-claim angle is their sharpest brand edge and the single most important thing to understand about how they compete.

What's driving them

The review velocity — +9 in 30 days for a sub-400 shop — is not organic luck; it is a triggered, post-job review request workflow, almost certainly using a tool like NiceJob or Podium.

A key trust accelerant on Nextdoor is their former-insurance-adjuster positioning, with customers explicitly noting that their insurance agent personally uses and recommends them

— word-of-mouth that is nearly impossible to buy and is compounding in HOA-dense Fishers and Carmel communities.

Their sales reps deploy a high-value storm tactic: showing up on-site when the insurance adjuster arrives, walking the claim in real time and identifying every line-item damage point alongside the homeowner

— a move that converts insurance approvals into signed contracts at a much higher rate than a quote-and-wait model.

They also use HOVER software to accurately measure roofs and provide estimates, giving customers an interactive 3D visualization of their home with different products and colors

— a tech-forward close tool that shrinks homeowner hesitation and differentiates them from price-sheet competitors.

On Nextdoor specifically, local homeowners in Fishers are actively and repeatedly surfacing their name for storm damage and hail claims

, which means their organic social proof is generating inbound they don't have to pay for.

Their weakness

According to the Indianapolis Department of Business and Neighborhood Services, the status of one of Weathertight's licenses was at one point listed as cancelled

— a public record flag that Raptor's sales reps should be aware of and ready to use if a prospect brings up contractor credentialing. More operationally exploitable: Weathertight's Facebook page shows only 12 reviews and minimal recent engagement despite 1,000+ page likes —

just 11 people talking about them actively

— signaling their social proof is concentrated entirely on Google and is thin everywhere else. In a sales conversation, lean hard on Raptor's multi-platform review depth (Google, Facebook, BBB) versus their single-platform dependency. If a prospect is comparing, the line is direct: "All of our reviews aren't in one basket — we're rated consistently across every platform where homeowners research contractors, not just one." A single-platform 5.0 is fragile; cross-platform dominance is a moat.
