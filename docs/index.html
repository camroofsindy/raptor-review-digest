Section 2

2. Tactic of the Week

Publish a Structured "Neighborhood Roof Cost Guide" for Five Specific Indianapolis Suburbs — This Week

The WHY

AI engines — including Google's AI Overviews, Perplexity, and Bing Copilot — answer hyperlocal cost questions constantly. Queries like "how much does a roof replacement cost in Carmel Indiana" or "average roof repair cost Fishers IN" are answered by pulling from pages that contain structured, location-specific pricing content with supporting context. Right now, Raptor's website contains strong brand narrative but limited structured, data-dense content that AI engines can extract and cite directly. The pages that get cited in AI Overviews for roofing cost queries are almost always formatted with: a clear H1 containing the city name and the query term, a data table or scannable cost range, an FAQ schema block, and a LocalBusiness schema tag with the service-area city named explicitly. None of Raptor's current city pages appear to be structured this way. Bone Dry and Indy Roof both have location-specific content that gets extracted; Raptor does not appear in these query results at all based on this week's scan.

There is a second mechanism at work here: cost-guide content earns links naturally. Home inspectors, real estate agents, mortgage brokers, and neighborhood Facebook group administrators regularly share "what does a roof cost in [my suburb]" content. A well-structured guide generates passive backlinks from exactly the local-domain sources Google trusts most for local pack ranking.

The HOW

This week, publish five separate pages — not one generic page — targeting these five specific markets: Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, and Zionsville. Each page should be 600–900 words. Here is the exact structure for each:

  1. H1: "Roof Replacement Cost in [City Name], Indiana (2026 Guide)"
  2. Opening paragraph: Two to three sentences on why [City Name] homeowners face the specific cost factors they do — home age, HOA requirements, storm frequency in that zip code.
  3. Cost table: A simple HTML table with three rows: minor repair, partial replacement, full replacement. Use real ranges your crews can stand behind (e.g., $350–$900 / $4,500–$9,000 / $9,000–$18,000). Label columns: Job Type, Typical Range, Factors That Move the Number.
  4. Three FAQ items formatted in FAQ schema (Question/Answer pairs in JSON-LD): "How long does a roof replacement take in [City]?", "Does [City]'s HOA affect my shingle choice?", "Will my insurance cover hail damage in [City]?"
  5. LocalBusiness schema block in JSON-LD with serviceArea set to the specific city, areaServed populated, and hasMap pointing to your GBP URL.
  6. Internal link to Raptor's main service page and to the contact/free-inspection page.
  7. One outbound link to a credible local source — the city's building permit page or the Indiana Department of Insurance storm-claims resource. This signals topical authority.

Deploy all five pages before Friday. Submit them to Google Search Console for indexing immediately after going live. Share each page URL in a Google Business Profile post tied to that city.
