2. Tactic of the Week

Publish a "Carmel / Fishers / Noblesville / Zionsville / Greenwood" service-area page cluster — one page today, one per week for five weeks — with embedded FAQ schema on each

The WHY (mechanism):

Homeowners are no longer scrolling through a list of ten blue links. Google's AI Overviews now lift an instant, chat-style answer above the fold, and only brands that feed Google strong entity signals and unmistakable local relevance earn a mention — let alone a click.

Right now, when someone types "roofing company Fishers Indiana" or "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," Google's AI Overview and Bing AI pull from pages that explicitly name the city in their URL, H1, body copy, and schema areaServed field. Raptor's site, based on its public profile, does not appear to have dedicated, indexed, keyword-rich city pages for Hamilton County suburbs — the highest-income, highest-roof-replacement-frequency ZIP codes in the metro. Competitors that do have these pages (Bone Dry, Cochran, Indy Roof) are the ones getting cited in AI answers for those suburb-level queries.

A proper LocalBusiness schema block includes the service area fields, making it easier for Google to confirm you are a local contractor serving specific towns

— and critically, for AI engines to extract that information programmatically when formulating an answer. Without it, the AI has to infer service area from unstructured body copy, which it does imperfectly.
