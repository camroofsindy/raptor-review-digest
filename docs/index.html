2. Tactic of the Week

Build a "Hail History" landing page for each of Raptor's top 5 suburb markets and submit it to three local real estate bloggers for citation this week.

Why this works — the mechanism:

Modern algorithms prioritize relevance and intent, making one link from a local home builders association or regional news outlet carry far more weight than dozens of links from spammy directories.

Hyperlocal, data-rich content is the fastest path to both organic links and AI engine citations for a roofing company at Raptor's current backlink scale.

Content that earns links in the modern search landscape must focus on original data or proprietary tools — for example, a "Hail Damage Case Study" provides unique value that journalists and bloggers will consider citing in their articles and posts.

Here is the specific asset to build: a page for each of five markets — Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, Zionsville — titled something like "Hail Damage History: [City] Indiana Roof Claims Guide." Each page should include: (1) NOAA hail event data for that zip code over the past five years, which is public and freely queryable at hailhistory.com or NOAA's storm events database; (2) Raptor's own documented job count from each market in the past 12 months; (3) average cost-per-square in that market based on Raptor's actual invoice data; (4) FAQ schema markup covering "how much does a roof replacement cost in Carmel Indiana," "does my homeowner's insurance cover hail damage," and "how long after a hail storm can I file a claim."

This format works for three reasons simultaneously. First, it targets high-buying-intent suburb queries ("roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "Fishers hail damage roofer") that Raptor's single homepage cannot rank for.

For service pros covering Carmel, Fishers, and the Hamilton County corridor directly, an aggressive suburb buildout makes the most sense given the higher ticket sizes and competitive pressure.

Second, it creates linkable assets.

A link from a regional news article about storm damage will carry more weight for an emergency roof repair page than a generic home-services directory link, because topical relevance and editorial context align.

Third, it feeds AI engine citation logic — AI Overviews pull from pages that answer a specific named-city query with structured, trustworthy data.

How — concrete steps this week:

Day 1: Pull NOAA storm events data for Hamilton County, Johnson County, Hendricks County from the NOAA Storm Events Database (publicly accessible, no paywall). Export the hail events table for 2020–2025. This takes 45 minutes.

Day 2: Draft the five pages. Each should be 600–900 words. Use the FAQ schema block for at least three questions per page. Add LocalBusiness schema with the specific city in the areaServed field. Title tags: "Hail Damage Roofing [City] IN | Raptor Roofing." Publish all five by end of day.

Day 3: Identify three to five Indianapolis-area real estate or home improvement bloggers, neighborhood Facebook group admins who run community websites (e.g., Noblesville Neighbors, Carmel Patch contributors), or local Nextdoor-adjacent content sites. Send a short, one-paragraph email: "We built a free hail history resource for [City] homeowners — thought it might be useful for your readers." No ask for a link. Just make the resource available. Organic citation follows when the content is genuinely useful.

Day 4: Submit each page URL to Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool and request indexing. This is often skipped and it costs nothing.

Success metric — what to measure in 30 days:
