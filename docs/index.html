Indy Rooftops

Who they are

Indy Rooftops was established in 2022 as a family venture, co-founded to create an opportunity for a father who had been a roofing salesman at another local company for many years.

It is family-owned and operated, headquartered in Brownsburg, Indiana,

with a physical office at

4254 N Raceway Rd, Indianapolis, IN 46234.

As a woman-owned business, they blend personalized attention with the support of national companies and products.

The company offers roof replacement, repair, and maintenance, along with inspections, leak repairs, shingle replacement, emergency services, siding replacement, gutter installation, window replacement, soffit and fascia repair, attic insulation, and specialty systems including EPDM, TPO, built-up, metal, and flat roofing.

Their geographic footprint spans Indianapolis, Carmel, Noblesville, and Westfield, IN.

They have been BBB Accredited since January 27, 2023.

What's driving them

The 11% review velocity (55 new Google reviews in 30 days on a base of 483) is being driven by at least three compounding engines working in parallel. First, there is a documented post-storm door-knocking operation:

one customer noted they were glad the team "knocked on our door after a hail storm" — the customer reported they never would have known work was needed otherwise.

This is classic storm-chasing canvassing executed at the neighborhood level, with reps dispatched to hail-affected zip codes immediately after weather events. Second, there is a clear and active realtor referral loop:

multiple Angi reviewers explicitly state "every realtor needs to know contractors — Indy Rooftops is one to trust,"

and

at least one reviewer who identifies as a realtor states they will refer the company to any client who could use their services.

Realtors generating pre-listing roof inspections are a high-velocity, repeatable lead channel that also naturally triggers review requests. Third,

at least one HomeAdvisor reviewer specifically calls out their referral program as "topnotch,"

signaling a structured incentive mechanism is in place — almost certainly a cash-or-credit referral reward communicated at job completion.

Customers frequently name specific reps by first name (Lexi, Carl, Dan, Kyle, Zack),

which is a tell that each rep is personally soliciting reviews by name, a tactic that significantly lifts conversion rates on review asks versus generic company-level requests.

Tactic to steal

The pattern across Indy Rooftops' Angi, HomeAdvisor, and Google profiles is unmistakable: customers name the specific field rep in their review, which means each rep is asking personally ("Would you mind leaving a review mentioning my name?"). This is not happening organically — it's coached. Execute this within 30 days by building a post-job SMS automation in Hatch or the review request module in JobNimbus that fires 24 hours after job-close, contains the rep's first name and photo in the message body, and links directly to the Raptor Google review page. Pair it with a brief rep training session: every closer and every install crew lead practices the verbal ask at project wrap. The psychological effect of a named, face-attached review request is materially higher conversion than a company-branded blast — and it builds rep-level reputations that compound into referral equity over time.

Their weakness

Indy Rooftops has only 42 Yelp reviews

against their 483 Google reviews — a gaping platform imbalance that signals their review engine is almost entirely Google-dependent and likely automated/SMS-driven without genuine cross-platform depth. More critically,

some third-party review aggregators note that the work may be subcontracted,

and

no warranties are discussed in the reviews.

In a sales conversation, lean hard on Raptor's warranty documentation, in-house crew credentials, and any manufacturer certifications. When a prospect mentions Indy Rooftops, the close is: "Their reviews are on Google — that's one platform. Ask them who actually shows up on your roof and what warranty is in writing. We use our own trained crews and back every job with [X manufacturer] certification. That's the difference between a great review and a roof that performs for 30 years."
