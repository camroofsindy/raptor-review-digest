🔍 Indy Rooftops — Competitive Intel Brief for Raptor Roofing

1. Who They Are

Indy Rooftops was established in 2022 by Mitchel Dotson and co-owner Cheryl Edens, originally founded to create a business for Mitchel's father Kevin Dotson, a veteran roofing salesman.

Kevin passed away suddenly in September 2022, and his legacy is now carried on by his son Mitchel — a compelling origin story that humanizes the brand.

Based out of Brownsburg, Indiana, they're a family-owned operation that positions themselves as "small enough to provide personalized attention" yet backed by national supplier relationships.

They market a woman-owned business designation — a differentiator that plays well for insurance claim work, government contracts, and consumer trust signals. Their service scope extends well beyond roofing: siding, gutters, windows, soffit/fascia, attic insulation, and specialty systems including EPDM, TPO, metal, and flat roofing.

2. What's Driving the Velocity

The 11% monthly review velocity is not coming from paid media — there's no evidence of Google LSA dominance or a Meta Ads funnel; their Facebook page sits at under 2,000 likes with modest engagement.

The real engine is post-storm door-knocking: one customer review explicitly states "so glad they knocked on our door after a hail storm, would never have known about the work needing done."

Layered on top of that is a deliberate realtor referral channel —

multiple Angi reviews from realtors explicitly call out Indy Rooftops as their go-to contractor, with one stating "Every realtor needs to know contractors — Indy Rooftops is one to trust!" and another saying "As a realtor, I'll be referring him to any of my clients."

Customers on HomeAdvisor also specifically call out their referral program as "topnotch," noting it's easy to tell others about having such a great experience.

The combination — storm canvassing that generates jobs, realtor relationships that generate steady pipeline, and a structured referral program — is what's compounding that review velocity fast.

3. One Tactic Raptor Should Steal

Steal the realtor referral channel — and systematize it in 30 days. Indy Rooftops has organically built real estate agent relationships, but it's informal; Raptor can out-execute them with structure. Here's how: pull a list of the top 50–100 active Indianapolis-area agents from Zillow's agent directory or MIBOR's member roster, then use a tool like NiceJob or Hatch to run a 3-touch drip sequence (email + text + handwritten card with a branded "Preferred Roofer for Your Clients" one-pager). Offer realtors a guaranteed 24-hour roof inspection turnaround for pre-listing clients — that's a hard, tangible commitment Indy Rooftops isn't making explicitly — and back it with a co-branded PDF report realtors can hand to buyers. This closes the loop that Indy Rooftops has left open and turns real estate agents into a repeatable, trackable referral engine within a single sales cycle.

4. One Weakness Raptor Can Exploit in Sales Conversations

Indy Rooftops has only 42 Yelp reviews — a thin presence on a platform that still drives high-intent homeowner searches, especially among 35–55-year-old homeowners who cross-reference platforms before calling.

Their own website encourages customers to check their Yelp rating, which signals they know it matters — but haven't invested in growing it.

In any sales conversation where a prospect mentions they've been "looking around," Raptor's team should proactively say: "Go check us on Google, Yelp, and HomeAdvisor — we're 5 stars across all three. Some competitors look great on one platform because that's the only one they manage." That plants doubt without naming Indy Rooftops directly, and leverages Raptor's review depth as a trust asset across every channel they control.

SECTION 2: Tactic of the Week

Build a "Roof Replacement Cost Indianapolis [Year]" Pillar Page — and Submit It to Patch.com This Week

THE WHY — What Mechanism Does This Leverage?

AEO takes SEO one step further. While SEO helps surface your content in search engine results, AEO ensures that AI-driven systems like Google's MUM, Siri, and ChatGPT can fully understand and trust your content enough to deliver it as a direct answer.

Right now, when a homeowner in Carmel types "how much does a roof replacement cost in Indianapolis," Google's AI Overview pulls from Indy Roof & Restoration's cost guide — not from Raptor — because Raptor has no authoritative long-form answer page on this topic. Raptor's entire site is currently a conversion-first architecture with no educational content that AI engines can cite.

The second mechanism:

Indianapolis businesses that earn links from local news outlets, industry associations, and Indy-based publications gain an edge.

Patch.com publishes hyperlocal editions for Carmel, Zionsville, Fishers, Noblesville, and Greenwood. Their domain authority is ~75. A Patch business listing with a link to your cost guide is a dofollow citation from a DA 75 publication targeting the exact suburban communities where Raptor's best customers live.

Google's AI Overviews now summarize information directly in search results, meaning a homeowner searching "how long does a roof replacement take" may see an AI-generated summary at the top of the page — pulling from multiple sources — before ever seeing a ranked link.

The only way into that summary is to be the source AI is trained to cite — which requires long-form, structured, factually specific content.

THE HOW — Concrete Steps This Week:

  1. Monday: Write (or have written) a 1,500–2,000 word "Roof Replacement Cost Indianapolis 2026" guide. Structure it with H2s that mirror natural language questions: "How much does a new roof cost in Indianapolis?", "What affects roof replacement cost in Carmel vs. Greenwood?", "Does homeowner's insurance cover roof replacement in Indiana?" Include a 5–7 item FAQ section at the bottom with direct, concise answers.

FAQPage schema is your highest-leverage schema type for conversational AI citations — despite Google restricting FAQ rich results for most sites in 2023, FAQPage schema remains a primary signal for AI answer extraction across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.

  1. Tuesday: Publish the page at raptorroofing.com/roof-replacement-cost-indianapolis/. Implement FAQPage schema in JSON-LD for every Q&A pair. Implement RoofingContractor + LocalBusiness schema in the site header if not already present.

Prefer RoofingContractor over the more general LocalBusiness type when applicable.

  1. Wednesday: Claim or update Patch.com business listings for Indianapolis, Carmel, Zionsville, Fishers, Noblesville, and Greenwood — six separate editions. Link each listing's website URL to your new cost guide page (not just the homepage). Each listing is a separate DA 75 backlink pointed at a keyword-rich page.
