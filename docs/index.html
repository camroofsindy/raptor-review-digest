2. Tactic of the Week

Submit a structured HARO/Connectively pitch to a home-improvement journalist this week. Target: one placement on a DA 40+ publication that covers Indiana home costs or storm season.

The WHY.

AI engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overview, Bing Copilot — do not cite roofing contractors directly from their own websites. They cite roofing contractors because a third-party publication cited them first. The mechanism is triangulation: an LLM's training data and live retrieval both weight entities that appear in multiple independent editorial sources above entities that only appear in self-published content. Raptor's raptorroofing.com content is solid, but it is self-referential. What Raptor is missing structurally is a dofollow editorial backlink from a publication a journalist or editor controls — a DA 40+ news or home-improvement site that mentions Raptor by name with a link. One such placement creates a new referring domain, improves Raptor's domain authority trajectory, and — critically — adds Raptor to the corpus that AI engines pull from when answering "who are the best roofers in Indianapolis."

The Home & Garden and Business categories routinely contain queries like "I'm writing about Indiana storm season roofing costs — need a local contractor expert." A 150-word pitch with two data points Raptor can provide — average cost of hail-related roof replacement in Central Indiana, and how many claims Raptor has processed year-to-date — is enough to get a quote.

The secondary play is Indianapolis Business Journal. IBJ accepts guest columns in its Real Estate/Construction section. A 600-word column on "What 2026's severe weather season means for Indianapolis homeowners' roofing budgets" with Raptor's name in the byline produces a dofollow link from a domain that multiple AI systems already cite for Indianapolis business queries.
