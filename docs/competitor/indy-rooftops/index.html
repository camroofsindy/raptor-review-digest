Indy Rooftops — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Updated: April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

Indy Rooftops was established in 2022, co-founded by Mitchel Dotson and Cheryl Edens, originally created to give Kevin Dotson — a longtime roofing salesman at another local company — his own operation.

They market themselves as a woman-owned business with roots in Brownsburg, Indiana, positioning around hassle-free, trust-first homeowner experiences.

Their service portfolio spans roof replacement, repair, maintenance, leak repair, shingle replacement, emergency services, siding, gutter installation, window replacement, soffit and fascia, and attic insulation

— a deliberately broad exterior envelope play, not a pure roofing specialist.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

indyrooftops.com has location-specific interior pages built out, including a dedicated Indianapolis service page targeting replacement, repair, gutters, windows, and siding

— indicating some intentional on-page SEO architecture. The site is structured around service-area pages (Indianapolis, Carmel named in the title tag), which is the right local SEO framework for a contractor. However, the domain was effectively born in 2022, giving it minimal domain age authority relative to established Indy competitors. No backlinks from local press, Indy Star, or civic organizations surfaced. Blog content is thin-to-nonexistent based on site structure. Schema markup status could not be confirmed without a direct technical crawl, but given the apparent DIY or entry-level web build quality, structured data is likely absent or incomplete. Their title tag — "Roofing Company Indianapolis IN - Carmel - Indy Rooftops" — is serviceable but not optimized for long-tail or suburb-specific queries like "roof replacement Fishers IN" or "hail damage roofer Noblesville."

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI engine behavior as of April 2025, Indy Rooftops is unlikely to appear in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini responses for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." AI citation in home services defaults to companies with: (1) sustained press coverage and named backlinks, (2) high-authority directory citations, and (3) blog/editorial content that AI models ingested during training. Indy Rooftops has none of these at meaningful scale. A company founded in 2022 with a thin backlink profile and no local press mentions is essentially invisible to generative engines. This is an opportunity Raptor should own — structured AEO content (FAQ schema, "best roofer near me" editorial pages, local press placements) targeting the exact queries above will build generative engine citations that Indy Rooftops cannot counter quickly.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Pure review velocity is dangerous — 55 reviews in 30 days is an aggressive clip for a company this size, and at 483 total they are closing the volume gap faster than most competitors would.

Their referral program is a documented differentiator

that is clearly the structural driver of that velocity. Their woman-owned positioning is also a differentiator in a segment where it matters to a real buyer subset.

Where Raptor beats them: 664 reviews to 483, a 5.0 rating that predates Indy Rooftops by years, premium market positioning, and a far stronger digital footprint. An owner response rate of 0.33 at Indy Rooftops is operationally telling — Raptor's engagement discipline here is a visible trust signal that AI engines and skeptical buyers notice.

Most exploitable weakness: Their 0.33 owner response rate means two out of every three reviewers get no public acknowledgment — a direct contrast to Raptor's accountability positioning that Cameron should make explicit in sales collateral and GBP responses.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Formalize the referral program architecture.

Indy Rooftops' referral program is cited by customers as "topnotch" and easy to recommend

— this is the mechanical engine behind their 22-reviews-in-7-days pace. Raptor should audit whether its own referral ask is systematized post-install or left to rep discretion.

  1. Expand the service-area page footprint. Indy Rooftops has already built suburb-specific landing pages. Raptor should run a gap analysis across Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, and Westfield to ensure it is not ceding local pack rankings to a 2022 startup on geo-targeted queries.

  2. Monitor their Yelp trajectory. At 42 Yelp reviews against 483 Google reviews, Indy Rooftops has a structural Yelp blind spot. If a dissatisfied customer ever routes negative sentiment there, it will land without the buffer of volume. Raptor should ensure its own Yelp profile has enough depth to withstand the same risk — and can use that platform asymmetry in conversations with prospects who do cross-platform research.
