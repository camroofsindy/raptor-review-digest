Competitive Intelligence Brief — Indy Rooftops

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: May 19, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2022 by Cheryl Edens and Mitchel Dotson, originally built around Kevin Dotson — a veteran roofing salesman who had spent years at another local company before going out on his own at 60.

Indy Rooftops brands itself as a woman-owned business rooted in Brownsburg, Indiana, serving the broader Indianapolis metro.

Their service menu spans roof replacement, repair, maintenance, siding, gutters, windows, skylights, and specialty systems including EPDM, TPO, metal, and flat roofing.

2. Estimated Scale

ZoomInfo lists Indy Rooftops' corporate office at 4254 N Raceway Rd, Indianapolis, with an employee band of 11–50.

Confidence: low — ZoomInfo bands at this size are notoriously sloppy and likely include 1099 sales reps.

BuildZoom confirms named personnel including Brian Weddle, Cheryl Edens, and Mitchell Dotson, and notes the company holds a General Contractor license and three additional licenses through the Indianapolis license board.

Revenue estimate: $2M–$5M ARR based on the 511-review volume at a plausible 15–20% review capture rate and typical residential ticket sizes. Confidence: low. No public financials or permit volume data found at this time.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No job postings for Indy Rooftops were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, ZipRecruiter, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days. The absence of active hiring ads suggests either a stable headcount or growth being handled through 1099/sales commission structures rather than W-2 adds. No marketing, sales director, or ops leadership roles detected. Flag as neutral, not clean.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

indyrooftops.com was registered/first indexed with a metadata age of July 2022, putting their domain at roughly 3 years old — young for competitive local SEO.

Their site copy leans on Indianapolis climate and code knowledge as an on-page differentiator,

which is table-stakes local SEO. No evidence of a blog, schema markup, press backlinks, or any content strategy beyond service pages surfaced.

They actively cross-reference Yelp, Angie's List, and BBB on their homepage,

suggesting they are citation-building but not executing a structured AEO or structured data play.

Their BuildZoom score of 93 ranks in the top 27% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors,

which adds a minor authority signal. The site is thin on content depth — no location-specific suburb pages (Fishers, Carmel, Zionsville, Greenwood) visible, which is a meaningful gap in a market where suburb-level landing pages drive LSA and organic share.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Indy Rooftops appears by name in Yelp's "Best Roofing in Indianapolis" list as of May 2026, alongside Bone Dry, Raptor Roofing, and others.

However, no evidence was found of Indy Rooftops being cited by name inside ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini-generated answers for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." Their thin content footprint — no blog, no suburb landing pages, no structured data — means generative engines have almost nothing to cite. This is a direct opportunity for Raptor: a structured AEO push with FAQ schema and suburb-level content targeting those exact query strings will capture AI citations that Indy Rooftops is currently invisible for.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Review velocity is their weapon. 62 reviews in 30 days is an aggressive collection rate, almost certainly supported by a systematic post-job text/email ask, possibly a referral incentive.

Their referral program is called out explicitly in reviews as "topnotch,"

which tells you it has a real structure behind it, not just an ask. At their current pace, they will cross 600 total reviews within 60–90 days, closing the gap on Raptor's 673 faster than it appears on a static snapshot.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor's 5.0 at 673 reviews is a harder number to fake or chase. The 44% owner response rate at Indy Rooftops is operationally mediocre — a signal that review management is volume-automated, not relationship-driven.

Their woman-owned positioning

is a differentiator, but "we don't outsource accountability" is a harder trust signal for premium ticket buyers.

Most exploitable weakness: Their website has zero suburb-level content depth, leaving every high-intent Carmel, Fishers, Zionsville, and Greenwood search query as an uncontested takeaway for Raptor.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Formalize the referral program.

Indy Rooftops' referral program is specifically named in customer reviews as a conversion factor.

If Raptor doesn't have a structured, named referral mechanism with a defined incentive and a post-job trigger sequence, this is money being left on the table every week.

  1. Monitor their Facebook ad posture. Their 313 "talking about this" on a 1,964-follower page is an outlier ratio that warrants a monthly Facebook Ad Library check (facebook.com/ads/library — search "Indy Rooftops"). If they activate paid social, Cameron will want to know before the next hail event.

  2. Weaponize suburb landing pages before they do.

Their site positions around Indianapolis broadly,

with no suburb-specific SEO infrastructure visible. Raptor should build and index dedicated pages for Carmel, Fishers, Greenwood, Zionsville, and Noblesville with local FAQ schema now — both for organic rank and to establish AEO citations in generative engines before Indy Rooftops closes that gap.
