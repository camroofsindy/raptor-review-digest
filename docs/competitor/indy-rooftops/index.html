Competitive Intelligence Brief: Indy Rooftops

Refreshed: June 9, 2026 | Next refresh: June 12, 2026

1. Who They Are

Indy Rooftops was established in 2022 by Mitchel Dotson and Cheryl Edens, originally founded around Kevin Dotson, a longtime Indianapolis-area roofing salesman.

Kevin passed away in September 2022 from a heart attack; his son Mitchel now carries the business forward.

The company positions itself as a woman-owned business rooted in Brownsburg, Indiana, blending personalized service with national product partnerships.

Their stated service area spans Indianapolis proper plus

Brownsburg, Noblesville, Carmel, Zionsville, Greenwood, and Fishers

— directly overlapping Raptor's core footprint.

2. Estimated Scale

Confidence: low. No LinkedIn company page with employee headcount was surfaced, and no ZoomInfo or BuildZoom data appeared in results. Based on operational signals — a registered address at

4254 N Raceway Rd, Indianapolis, IN 46234

, and review volume showing named field reps (Mitch, Kevin, Jack, Brian, Zack) across Angi and HomeAdvisor — the team appears to be 5–12 people, consistent with a sub-$3M/year residential-focused operation. They show no public payroll or permit volume data to sharpen that estimate. The 522 Google reviews in roughly 3.5 years suggests a healthy but not high-volume install cadence — likely 80–150 full replacements per year at $10K–$18K average ticket.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active job postings for Indy Rooftops were surfaced on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter as of this refresh. The LinkedIn company page did not return a visible employee count or open roles. This is consistent with a small, owner-operated structure where sales and install labor is managed informally or through subcontractors. No marketing, sales leadership, or ops hires detected. Flag this for the next cycle — if they start posting a Sales Manager or Marketing Coordinator role, it signals a formalization push worth watching.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp lists them at 4254 N Raceway Rd with 42 reviews

— versus 522 on Google. That's a 92% concentration of social proof on one platform, a clear sign of deliberate Google review-funneling with no meaningful effort to build Yelp authority.

On BBB, they are accredited since January 27, 2023, and listed as a roofing contractor in Indianapolis.

Their BBB letter grade is A+.

Their own About page cites Facebook, Angie's List, and HomeAdvisor as active review platforms

— the Angi/HomeAdvisor profile has positive reviews but no volume signal approaching Google. No Facebook Ad Library activity was confirmed in these results. The 0.53 owner response rate on Google (per Cameron's dashboard) is a meaningful gap — less than half of reviews get a response, which is below the threshold where Google's algorithm treats response rate as a trust signal.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their blog at indyrooftops.com/blog covers topics including TPO contractor selection, gutter repair signs, and window replacement

— a reasonable topical spread but the content reads as thin, templated copy rather than genuinely authoritative local content.

They have built out individual service-area landing pages for Brownsburg, Noblesville, Carmel, Zionsville, Greenwood, and Fishers

— this is the right local SEO playbook, but execution appears formulaic (each page uses near-identical boilerplate copy). No evidence of backlinks from local press or Indianapolis Business Journal. Domain was registered around

mid-2022

based on site metadata, giving them a relatively young domain age — a domain authority signal that likely keeps them out of top-3 organic for high-competition terms like "roof replacement Indianapolis." Schema markup was not confirmed; the site appears to be a basic WordPress build powered by a third-party agency per the footer.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on research conducted for this brief, Indy Rooftops does not appear to be consistently cited by name in AI-generated answers for the core Indy roofing queries ("best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "top roofing contractor Greenwood Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville"). Their domain is indexed and their review volume is real, but the lack of authoritative backlinks, thin blog content, and no apparent presence in local press or third-party editorial sources means they have not earned the citation signals that LLMs weight. This is a significant opportunity gap for Raptor: structured AEO content (FAQ schema, authoritative local guides, press citations) could establish Raptor as the default AI-cited answer in these suburbs before Indy Rooftops or any other local player gets there.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Indy Rooftops beats Raptor: Speed-to-close sales culture. Multiple reviews across Angi and HomeAdvisor mention reps working directly with insurance adjusters on the same day, handling the claim from first call through approval —

spending hours on the phone with insurers to maximize claim payouts and maintaining "great legal footing" on insurance accountability.

That's a compelling insurance-claim-driven conversion play that earns loyalty fast. They also have a

referral program that reviewers call "topnotch"

— a structured referral machine Raptor should investigate.

Where Raptor beats them: Review volume (677 vs. 522), review density (155 more reviews from a business with premium positioning), a genuine 5.0 rating that holds under greater scrutiny, and a clear brand identity vs. Indy Rooftops' generic "we care" messaging. Raptor's owner response rate (even if it needs improvement) outpaces Indy Rooftops' 0.53 rate when combined with a distinct voice.

Most exploitable weakness: Their Yelp profile has only 42 reviews against 522 on Google — any homeowner who cross-checks platforms sees an unbalanced, funneled profile, which signals manufactured optics rather than authentic breadth.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

1. The insurance-advocacy sales script. Indy Rooftops has built a repeatable motion around attending adjuster meetings, handling claim calls, and maximizing insurance payouts on behalf of homeowners. This is referenced in multiple independent reviews across Angi, HomeAdvisor, and Chamber of Commerce listings. Cameron should have Patrick audit whether Raptor's reps are running a similarly structured insurance-advocacy close — or leaving that value unsold.

2. The suburb-specific landing page architecture.

They have individual service-area pages for Carmel, Zionsville, Greenwood, and Fishers

targeting the same suburbs Raptor serves. Raptor should build superior versions of these pages — not boilerplate copy swaps, but genuinely localized content (permit data, local weather damage stats, neighborhood-specific project callouts) that both outranks their thin pages and feeds AEO citation signals.

3. Monitor their referral program mechanics.

Reviewers specifically call out Indy Rooftops' referral program as "topnotch" and easy to recommend.

This is a structured word-of-mouth engine in a market where most roofers rely on passive referrals. Cameron should reverse-engineer what they're offering (likely a cash or credit incentive) and decide whether Raptor's premium positioning warrants a higher-value, better-branded version of the same play.
