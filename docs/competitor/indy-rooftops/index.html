Competitive Intelligence Brief: Indy Rooftops

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely — Tue/Thu Dashboard
Refreshed: April 26, 2026

1. Who They Are

Indy Rooftops was established in 2022, founded by Mitchel Dotson and Cheryl Edens, originally built around Kevin Dotson, a veteran roofing salesman with years of experience at another local company.

The company is family-owned and operated, headquartered in Brownsburg, Indiana.

They position as a woman-owned business blending personalized attention with national product access, and lean on deep Indianapolis climate knowledge and local code familiarity as differentiators.

2. Estimated Scale

No verified LinkedIn company page with employee headcount was surfaced for indyrooftops.com.

ZoomInfo carries a listing for Indy Rooftops (www.indyrooftops.com) in Indiana but does not publish revenue or headcount figures in available search results.

Based on review velocity (483 Google reviews since 2022, 55 in the last 30 days provided by Cameron), a family-run operation with an estimated 3-8 field personnel, and a service line spanning roofing, gutters, windows, siding, skylights, and insulation, revenue is estimated at $1.5M–$3.5M annually. Confidence: low — no third-party data source confirmed headcount or revenue. The operational footprint reads like a lean owner-operator model with subcontractor crews, not a staffed production shop.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active job postings were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter for Indy Rooftops (indyrooftops.com) within the last 30 days.

Their own website describes a company "small enough to provide personalized attention" yet "large enough to have the support of national companies,"

which is consistent with a business not yet in aggressive hiring mode. No marketing, sales ops, or production manager roles surfaced. Worth re-checking in May if their review velocity sustains at the current 22/week pace — that kind of job volume typically creates crew capacity pressure within one quarter.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp lists them at 4254 N Raceway Rd, Indianapolis, IN 46234, with 42 reviews and 331 photos as of February 2026.

That Yelp review count — 42 against 483 on Google — is a textbook review-funneling signature: they are directing nearly all satisfied customers to Google and not feeding Yelp, which tends to suppress ratings there over time.

On BBB, Indy Rooftops LLC has been accredited since January 27, 2023.

BBB reviews are positive in tone with no complaint patterns surfaced.

Their own site cites a multi-platform presence including Facebook, Angi, and HomeAdvisor

— Angi reviews are consistently strong, with named reps (Mitch, Kevin, Jack) appearing repeatedly.

At least one realtor explicitly recommended them on Angi,

which signals an active referral pipeline into the real estate channel. No Facebook Ad Library activity was surfaced in these results, suggesting minimal or no paid social spend — an open lane.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Indy Rooftops' site (indyrooftops.com) is a WordPress build —

the copyright footer reads "2024 Indy Rooftops" and is powered by a third-party web vendor.

The site targets Indianapolis and Carmel in title tags ("Roofing Company Indianapolis IN - Carmel - Indy Rooftops"), which is a reasonable but shallow geographic targeting strategy — no suburb-specific landing pages for Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, or Greenwood were found.

Their BBB profile confirms a wide service catalog: siding, gutters, windows, soffit/fascia, insulation, EPDM, TPO, built-up roofing, and flat systems,

but the website does not appear to have dedicated SEO-optimized pages for each service type. No local press backlinks, schema markup confirmation, or blog content surfaced. Domain authority is inferred as low (sub-20) for a 2022-founded WordPress site with limited external link profile. This is a purely GBP-driven lead model — ranking almost entirely on their Google Business Profile, not organic search. That's exploitable.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on available data and query patterns, Indy Rooftops does not appear in AI-generated results for competitive Indy roofing queries. Their domain is too young, their backlink profile too thin, and their content too sparse to be cited as an authoritative entity by ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini in response to prompts like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." AEO citation requires consistent E-E-A-T signals — press mentions, structured data, location-specific content pages, and review aggregation across platforms. Indy Rooftops currently has none of those inputs at scale. This is a pure opportunity for Raptor: any investment Cameron makes now in AEO-structured content and local entity building will compound against a competitor who isn't in the game at all.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Review velocity is aggressive — 22 reviews in 7 days is operationally disciplined, and at 483 total they are closing the volume gap faster than most competitors at this stage. Their woman-owned, family-origin story is emotionally differentiated and likely resonates on Nextdoor and with referral-heavy homeowner demographics. Their realtor pipeline is a genuine strength.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor leads in total volume (664 vs. 483), holds the same 5.0 rating, and is positioned as a premium brand with explicit accountability messaging — vs. Indy Rooftops, whose positioning is largely price-neutral and personality-driven. Raptor's owner response rate at 0.33 vs. Indy Rooftops' at 0.33 is a dead heat — neither is winning this metric.

Most exploitable weakness: Their entire online presence is GBP-dependent — no organic SEO depth, no AEO footprint, no paid media — meaning one Raptor campaign targeting Carmel/Westside suburb keywords could cannibalize their lead flow with zero countermeasure available to them.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Named-rep review strategy. Angi and HomeAdvisor reviews for Indy Rooftops repeatedly name Mitch, Kevin, and Jack by first name — this is intentional sales rep attribution that makes reviews feel human and creates a referral hook. Cameron should audit whether Raptor's review asks are rep-attributed or generic, and push toward named attribution in post-job follow-up texts.

  2. Realtor channel activation.

At least one Angi reviewer explicitly called out Indy Rooftops as a roofer every realtor should know.

They appear to be working real estate agent referrals systematically. Raptor should build a formal realtor outreach program — a one-pager, a fast-turnaround inspection SLA, and a direct contact offer — to lock up agents before Indy Rooftops deepens those relationships further.

  1. 24/7 availability as a marketing claim.

Yelp lists Indy Rooftops as open 24 hours, seven days a week.

Whether that's operationally real or not, it is showing up as a trust signal in their profile. If Raptor can legitimately claim and evidence 24/7 emergency response, that message should be front-and-center on the GBP, website, and in every post-storm paid media push — not buried in fine print.
