Competitive Intelligence Brief — Indy Rooftops

Refreshed: Tuesday, July 22, 2026 | Prepared for: Cameron Blakely, Raptor Roofing

1. Who They Are

Indy Rooftops was established in 2022, founded by Mitchel Dotson and Cheryl Edens, originally built around Kevin Dotson, who had spent years as a roofing salesman at another local company.

The company markets itself as a woman-owned business, with roots in Brownsburg, Indiana, serving Indianapolis and surrounding suburbs.

Their stated service area spans Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, Zionsville, and surrounding suburbs

— a near-identical footprint to Raptor's. Their differentiator is a family-origin story layered over an insurance-claim-assist model, positioning as approachable rather than premium.

2. Estimated Scale

ZoomInfo places Indy Rooftops at 11–50 employees at their 4254 N Raceway Rd, Indianapolis, IN 46234 address.

Confidence: medium.

BuildZoom lists principals Brian Weddle, Cheryl Edens, and Mitchell Dotson, and notes the company holds a general contractor license plus 3 additional licenses per the Indianapolis license board.

Given their 2022 founding, review volume of 541 Google reviews, and geographic footprint, estimated annual revenue is in the $2M–$4M range — consistent with a lean owner-operated outfit with a canvassing sales team and subcontracted crews. Confidence: low (no public revenue filing found).

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active job postings for Indy Rooftops were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter within the last 30 days. However,

their Facebook page references a named canvassing team member preparing to leave for college at the University of Cincinnati

— a signal that their door-to-door canvassing operation relies on seasonal or transitional labor, not a hardened sales force. No marketing, ops leadership, or office management hires were detected. This is a company running lean on overhead, not scaling a professional sales infrastructure.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp shows Indy Rooftops at 4254 N Raceway Rd with 51 reviews and 380 photos.

With 541 Google reviews and only 51 on Yelp, there is a clear review-funneling pattern — nearly all solicitation is directed to Google.

They are BBB Accredited since January 27, 2023

, and the BBB profile lists both Mitchel Dotson (Owner) and Cheryl Edens (CEO).

They hold a Malarkey Premium Contractor credential and an IKO Craftsman Premier Contractor credential

, both factory-backed installer designations they actively tout.

Their Facebook page (facebook.com/IndyRooftops) shows 2,215 likes and 353 people actively talking about them

— above-average engagement for a sub-4-year-old shop. No Facebook Ad Library activity was confirmed in this pull; that warrants a direct manual check at facebook.com/ads/library. No significant Nextdoor footprint was surfaced.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their BuildZoom score of 93 ranks in the top 27% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors

— a useful proxy for backlink and citation health. Their website (indyrooftops.com) has geo-targeted location pages:

they have a dedicated "Our Work" page featuring completed projects across Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, and Zionsville

, and a city-specific page for Indianapolis (indyrooftops.com/indianapolis/) with locally tailored content about Indianapolis roof architecture and lifespan guidance.

That page includes specific content about Victorian and Craftsman homes in central Indianapolis, and mid-century ranches across Pike, Wayne, Decatur, and other townships

— this is deliberate AEO content, written to answer the kinds of neighborhood-specific questions that AI engines pull from. Their site homepage metadata claims "450+ five-star reviews" — notably stale vs. their actual 541, suggesting infrequent on-page updates.

BuildZoom also notes that at least one of their contractor licenses was at one point listed as cancelled by the Indianapolis Department of Business and Neighborhood Services

— worth verifying if Raptor wants to surface a credentialing contrast in sales conversations.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Indy Rooftops has taken deliberate steps to build AEO-ready content on their site (township-specific pages, lifespan education, insurance-claim walkthroughs).

Their Indianapolis page positions them as family-owned, highlights a 24–72 hour response window, and emphasizes that a real person answers the phone — not a call center

— language patterns that ChatGPT and Gemini tend to surface as trust signals in conversational queries. Their Yelp profile, Angi presence, BBB accreditation, and HomeAdvisor profile collectively create a multi-platform citation footprint that improves AI-engine discoverability. Based on available data, they are likely appearing in at least some generative engine results for broad "Indianapolis roofer" queries due to this citation density, though they lack the review volume that top AI-cited competitors like Indy Roof & Restoration carry. Raptor's 690 reviews at 5.0 is a stronger signal — but Raptor needs location-specific, FAQ-structured content pages to compete at the AEO layer.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Insurance claim advocacy is central to their pitch and explicitly embedded in sales rep behavior —

their technicians meet adjusters on site, document damage themselves, and stay with the claim through the final invoice

. That's a repeatable, customer-visible process that resonates in storm-driven markets. They also have more geo-targeted content pages than Raptor appears to currently maintain.

Where Raptor beats them: 690 reviews vs. 541, matching 5.0 rating with higher volume; premium brand positioning vs. Indy Rooftops' family-affordability framing; and Raptor's owner-accountability narrative is more durable long-term than a story built on helping dad find a job. Indy Rooftops' review velocity has fallen sharply — only 10 reviews in the last 90 days vs. Raptor actively collecting — and their 0.8 owner response rate signals weakening engagement at the top.

Most exploitable weakness: Their review engine has clearly stalled (10 reviews in 90 days), and their homepage review count ("450+") hasn't been updated to reflect actual volume — a credibility gap Raptor can exploit in direct sales comparisons.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Location-page content depth.

Their Indianapolis page contextualizes roofing by neighborhood era — Victorian homes, mid-century ranches, post-war split-levels

— the exact type of locally grounded, question-answering content that AEO favors. Cameron should commission equivalent pages for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, and Greenwood before Indy Rooftops closes the gap with fresh content.

  1. Canvassing-to-review pipeline. Their door-to-door team appears to be a meaningful review driver — customers on HomeAdvisor explicitly note that Indy Rooftops reps knocked on their door after storms.

One reviewer noted they would "never have known about the work needing done"

without the knock. Raptor should evaluate whether a structured storm-canvassing protocol with a review handoff could accelerate Google review velocity post-event.

  1. Multi-platform citation building. They actively cross-promote Google, Yelp, Angi, and BBB —

encouraging customers to check all four platforms

. Raptor's 690 reviews are heavily Google-concentrated. Diversifying onto Angi and Yelp with even a fraction of that volume builds the citation footprint that AI engines weight when constructing "best roofers in Indianapolis" answers.
