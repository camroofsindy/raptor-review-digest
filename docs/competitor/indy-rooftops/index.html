Competitor Brief: Indy Rooftops

Refreshed: May 19, 2025 | indyrooftops.com

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2022 by Mitchel Dotson and Cheryl Edens, Indy Rooftops was originally built around Kevin Dotson, a veteran roofing salesman from another local company.

The company markets itself as a woman-owned business and traces its roots to Brownsburg, Indiana, with a service area covering greater Indianapolis.

Their scope spans roof replacement, repair, maintenance, inspections, leak repairs, siding replacement, gutter installation, window replacement, soffit and fascia repair, and attic insulation — a broad exterior services bundle that keeps them relevant across the full homeowner renovation funnel.

2. Estimated Scale

ZoomInfo lists Indy Rooftops at 11–50 employees, headquartered at 4254 N Raceway Rd, Indianapolis, IN 46234.

Confidence: medium.

BuildZoom confirms named personnel including Brian Weddle, Cheryl Edens, and Mitchell Dotson, and notes the company holds a General Contractor license plus three additional licenses with the Indianapolis license board.

With a 3-year-old company, 11–50 headcount, and the volume of reviews accumulating (511 Google reviews, 62 in the last 30 days alone), estimated annual revenue is roughly $3–6M. Confidence: low — no public financials available. The review velocity implies strong job volume but does not distinguish ticket size.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active Indy Rooftops job postings were located on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days. The absence of open listings is itself a signal: at their current review velocity (13 reviews in 7 days), they are either staffed to capacity, using informal recruiting (referrals, door-to-door rep networks), or have not yet built a formal talent acquisition function.

Their HomeAdvisor profile highlights a referral program as a customer-acquisition tool, which suggests they may be leveraging the same informal network model for staffing.

Watch for sales rep postings in the next 60–90 days; that would signal a deliberate growth push rather than organic scaling.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Indy Rooftops holds a BuildZoom score of 93, ranking in the top 27% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors.

Their homepage title tag reads "Indianapolis Roofing Contractor - 5-Star Rated," which is basic but keyword-loaded. The site (indyrooftops.com) is a modest WordPress build — no evidence of structured schema markup, local FAQ schema, or breadcrumb schema surfaced.

They self-direct consumers to check their Google, Yelp, Angie's List, and BBB presence

BuildZoom also flags a historical note that at least one license was at one point listed as cancelled, though the current status may have changed

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current research, Indy Rooftops does not appear to generate structured citations in AI overviews for key Indy roofing queries. The lack of suburb-specific landing pages, blog content, and third-party editorial coverage (local press, HomeAdvisor editorial features, etc.) means their entity footprint is too thin for LLMs to cite them confidently. Their name does surface on HomeAdvisor and Angi, which helps entity recognition, but without keyword-rich authoritative content mapped to specific geo-queries ("roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing Zionsville"), they are not generating AI-driven lead flow. This is an opportunity Raptor should own ahead of them.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Review velocity is their most dangerous attribute — 62 reviews in 30 days at 5.0 stars is an aggressive cadence that, if sustained, closes the 162-review gap (511 vs. Raptor's 673) within 2–3 months and could eventually surpass total count. Their woman-owned positioning and veteran discount also give them two demographic hooks Raptor currently doesn't counter directly.

Where Raptor beats them: 673 reviews vs. 511, a longer operating track record, stronger off-Google presence, and a premium brand narrative that doesn't rely on "free estimates" or insurance claim hand-holding as its primary value prop. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" is a more defensible market position than Indy Rooftops' broadly service-bundled approach.

Most exploitable weakness: Their SEO and AEO infrastructure is essentially non-existent — no suburb pages, no schema, no editorial backlinks — making them entirely dependent on review volume and word-of-mouth for organic visibility, which Raptor can structurally outflank with content.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Review velocity discipline. 62 reviews in 30 days from a 3-year-old company is not accidental. Indy Rooftops has clearly built a post-job review ask into their field ops workflow — likely a text-based automation triggered at job close. Cameron should audit Raptor's current ask cadence and determine whether timing, channel (SMS vs. email), or rep accountability is the lever to push velocity past 70+/month.

  2. Veteran discount as a Nextdoor amplifier.

Indy Rooftops actively promotes a veteran discount on their contact page.

This is a low-cost, high-trust signal that performs disproportionately well in Nextdoor threads and neighborhood Facebook groups. Raptor should consider a structured veteran/first-responder program with a landing page, not just a footnote on a contact form.

  1. Realtor referral network.

Reviewer language explicitly calls out Indy Rooftops as a trusted contractor for realtors

, and multiple reviews reference pre-sale roof repairs facilitated quickly. This is a B2B2C channel — realtors feeding repeat residential jobs — that Raptor should formalize with a dedicated outreach program targeting Indy-area real estate brokerages, particularly in Carmel, Westfield, and Zionsville where Raptor's premium positioning aligns with higher-value home transactions.
