Competitive Intelligence Brief: Indy Rooftops

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: June 30, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2022, Indy Rooftops was started by Cheryl Edens and Mitchel Dotson, originally to create a business for Mitchel's father Kevin Dotson, who had been a roofing salesman at another local company for many years.

The company is woman-owned and rooted in Brownsburg, Indiana, operating across the greater Indianapolis market.

Their service scope extends well beyond roofing into siding replacement, gutter installation, window replacement, soffit and fascia repair, and attic insulation, with specialty systems including EPDM, TPO, built-up, metal, and flat roofing.

2. Estimated Scale

Their Facebook page lists 1,964 likes with 313 people actively talking about the business, and their described scope covers insurance claims, new builds, full replacements, repairs, siding, and gutters

— consistent with a multi-crew operation. Based on the volume of named field reps visible across review platforms (Carl, Kevin, Mitch, Zack, Jack, Brian W.), the company is running at least 6–10 active sales/field staff. No LinkedIn company page with headcount was surfaced.

Employee estimate: 10–20 (confidence: low) — derived from named rep frequency across Angi, HomeAdvisor, BBB, and Chamber of Commerce reviews, not a verified database.

Revenue estimate: $2M–$5M ARR (confidence: low) — a 533-review footprint for a 3-year-old residential/commercial roofing company in a mid-size metro points to meaningful volume, but no BuildZoom permit data or ZoomInfo record was surfaced to anchor this tighter.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active job postings for Indy Rooftops were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days. Their hiring, when it occurs, appears to happen through referral and informal channels rather than formal job boards — consistent with a founder-led company at their stage. No marketing, ops, or sales leadership hires detected. This is worth monitoring: if they post a Sales Manager or Marketing Coordinator role, it signals a deliberate scale push.

Their blog exists and covers commercial roofing problems in Indianapolis, flat roof contractor content, and EPDM guides

— mostly B-grade informational posts aimed at secondary commercial queries. The homepage title tag leads with "Indianapolis Roofing Contractor - 5-Star Rated," which is functional but not differentiated.

Their site copy leans on Indianapolis climate and roofing code requirements

as a trust signal, which is decent topical framing but generic in execution. No evidence of schema markup, structured FAQ schema, or review schema was surfaced from page source signals. Domain age is July 2022, giving them roughly 4 years of authority building, which is competitive but not dominant. No local press backlinks or media citations were found.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Indy Rooftops does appear in Yelp's Indianapolis roofing roundup —

Yelp's "Best Roofing in Indianapolis" list (last updated May 2026) names Indy Rooftops first, ahead of Bone Dry, Raptor Roofing, and others.

That Yelp list ranking is meaningful because generative AI engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) frequently pull from Yelp aggregation pages when constructing local contractor recommendations. Their presence at position one on that Yelp list gives them a structural advantage for AI citation over companies ranked lower. Their blog content, while thin, creates some topical surface area for AI indexing. However, without suburb-specific landing pages, named author bios with credentials, or structured data markup, their AEO posture is accidental rather than engineered. Raptor's review volume advantage (681 vs. 533) and perfect 5.0 rating should appear in AI-weighted trust signals when those engines parse Google data — but only if Raptor's pages are structured to be citable.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Indy Rooftops holds the #1 slot on Yelp's Indianapolis roofing aggregate page as of May 2026, giving them superior AI citation exposure from that source. Their woman-owned certification is a differentiator with specific buyer segments and B2B procurement channels.

Their storm canvassing model — knocking doors post-hail — generates unsolicited demand

that Raptor's inbound model does not replicate at scale.

Where Raptor beats them: 681 reviews at 5.0 vs. 533 at 5.0 — Raptor has a 28% larger review corpus with an identical rating, which matters for both Google Local Pack ranking signals and AI trust weighting. Raptor's premium positioning is cleaner; Indy Rooftops competes across a sprawling service menu (windows, insulation, skylights, commercial flat roofs) which dilutes their brand identity.

Most exploitable weakness: Their review velocity has nearly stalled — only 10 reviews in the last 90 days — meaning Raptor can widen the review gap aggressively in the next two quarters without Indy Rooftops being able to respond.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Realtor referral channel.

At least one Angi reviewer called Indy Rooftops a contractor "every realtor needs to know,"

which suggests an active real estate referral pipeline. Cameron should map out which Indy-area brokerages are feeding Indy Rooftops work and build a competing lunch-and-learn or co-marketing program with top-producing agents in Carmel, Fishers, and Zionsville before this channel hardens.

  1. Yelp position defense. Raptor currently sits behind Indy Rooftops on Yelp's Indianapolis roofing list — a platform that feeds AI recommendations. A systematic push to grow Raptor's Yelp review count with verified customers (legal, no incentivization) would directly compete for that #1 slot and improve generative engine citation.

  2. Suburb-level landing pages. Indy Rooftops has no visible geo-targeted pages for high-value northern suburbs. Raptor should build or reinforce dedicated, schema-marked service pages for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, and Greenwood — each with 1,000+ words of locally specific content — to capture both long-tail organic rankings and the suburb-specific queries that AI engines are increasingly answering directly.
