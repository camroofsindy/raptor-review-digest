Competitive Intelligence Brief — Bone Dry Roofing

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely | Refreshed: April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

Gene Judd is the owner and president of Bone Dry Roofing, which he opened in 1989 starting with a truck and a garage office in Speedway, Indiana.

Beginning in Indiana with headquarters in Indianapolis, they expanded to include offices in Ohio, Kentucky, Missouri, and Tennessee.

The Bone Dry Family of Companies now spans 20 businesses in eight states, including 11 Midwest locations, Bone Dry Commercial Roofing, Roof One, Quality Roofing Services (QRS), Ravlin's Roofing, RCV Roofing, Siding and Gutters, and Gary's Roofing.

Their positioning centers on a production supervisor on-site for every job and a no-payment-until-satisfaction policy — institutional-grade SOPs for a residential contractor.

2. Estimated Scale

Per Roofing Contractor Magazine's 2023 profile, the company reported 500 employees across 20 locations, with 81% residential and 19% commercial scope.

Confidence: high (named trade publication, 2023 data).

ZoomInfo notes that the hiring of a new CFO and COO followed 2023 revenue of over $183 million

— a step up from the $121.6M reported in 2022.

Of that 2022 total, approximately $36 million, or about 30%, came from the original Indianapolis office.

The Indianapolis branch alone is therefore roughly a $40–45M operation by current trajectory. Confidence: medium (extrapolated from 2022–2023 actuals; no 2024 filing available).

Bone Dry grew their employee count by 9% last year.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Indeed currently lists 79 open jobs company-wide for Bone Dry Roofing.

Indiana-specific active postings include:

a Commercial Repair Technician in Indianapolis, a Sales Representative in Fort Wayne, a Construction Assistant to the Masonry Production Manager in Indianapolis ($58K–$62K estimated), and a Field Project Foreman for the Commercial Division in Indianapolis.

The Assistant General Manager role posted on Indeed is the signal to watch —

that role is described as responsible for nurturing customer service culture, driving sales, and achieving revenue targets

— a direct ops-leadership push. No dedicated marketing, paid media, or SEO roles detected in the Indianapolis market in the last 30 days. Their CMO-level marketing is handled internally by Matt Tyner,

whose stated focus is strategic marketing to approach new markets, grow current markets, and generate lead volume and revenue.

4. Off-Google Presence

Bone Dry's Yelp profile for the 7735 Winton Dr location is active and updated as of April 2026, with 75 reviews.

The gap between 2,410 Google reviews and 75 Yelp reviews is a textbook review-funnel signal — they are aggressively directing satisfied customers to Google and largely ignoring Yelp as a channel.

They carry an A+ rating with the Better Business Bureau

and hold the BBB profile with a full executive leadership listing (Judd, Tyner/CMO, Smith/COO, Merlie/CFO).

Their Indianapolis/Greenwood Facebook page has 823 likes and positions as a family-owned, locally operated company.

Facebook URL structure includes explicit UTM tracking (utm_campaign=profile-link, campaign_code=BDR-DIG-ORGS-ROOF-IN-PHO-FB-202402), indicating a structured paid social operation.

Bone Dry also has an active community partnership with Indy Humane, supporting microchipping events in the Indianapolis area

— a deliberate local goodwill play that generates organic social content and brand mentions.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Bonedry.com is a multi-location domain carrying authority from 35+ years of backlink accumulation and national press coverage — Roofing Contractor Magazine, LeadIQ, GuildQuality, Tucker Home Services, and others.

Their tech stack includes Adobe Premiere Pro, WordPress, cdnjs, Module Federation, and Webpack

, pointing to a video-content production capability and a modern JavaScript framework — more than most regional roofers deploy. Location-specific pages (e.g., bonedry.com/indianapolis/) are structured for local SEO with unique content, reviews embeds, and team pages.

They deploy a production supervisor on-site for the duration of every project, who serves as the main point of contact for homeowners

— that differentiator is baked into their content at the page level.

They install in excess of 2,500 roof replacements and perform over 5,000 repairs annually

, giving them scale to generate review volume and repeat keyword coverage that smaller operators cannot match. Their blog is active; their domain breadth across 8+ states creates internal linking equity that flows back to the Indianapolis pages. This is the most SEO-advantaged competitor in the Indy market — do not underestimate it.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Bone Dry Roofing has strong AI citation exposure based on the data points driving most LLM training signals:

  • Volume: 2,410 Google reviews at 4.8 stars across the flagship Indianapolis location, with 5,000+ aggregate reviews across all locations per their own site copy.
  • Authority signals:

Named Roofing Contractor Magazine's 2023 Residential Roofing Contractor of the Year — a national award that represents one of the strongest third-party authority backlinks a residential roofer can earn.

  • Trade press coverage: The roofingcontractor.com profile is a high-DA editorial link that directly names Bone Dry in the context of Indianapolis residential roofing — exactly the kind of structured citation that feeds ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity answers for queries like "best roofing company Indianapolis" or "top roofer Carmel Indiana."
  • Verdict: Bone Dry is almost certainly cited by name in AI-generated answers for Indy roofing queries. Their national award, trade press volume, and multi-location web presence make them one of the default responses. Raptor's 5.0 star rating and premium positioning are not yet generating the structured third-party press citations needed to compete in the AEO layer. That is the gap to close.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Review volume (2,410 vs. 664), domain authority by a wide margin, national trade press citations (2023 Contractor of the Year), multi-service bundling (gutters, masonry, solar, insulation, siding), and an in-house training academy that becomes a recruiting and retention moat.

Bone Dry University, established in 2021, dedicates facilities and four full-time employees to production, sales, customer service, leadership, and communication training.

Where Raptor beats them: Star rating — 5.0 vs. 4.8 is a meaningful gap when consumers filter by rating. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is sharper and more emotionally resonant than Bone Dry's corporate "peace of mind" language. At $121M+ in revenue across 20+ businesses, Bone Dry is an enterprise — the personal-ownership story gets diluted.

Most exploitable weakness: The Yelp gap (2,410 Google reviews, 75 Yelp reviews) signals a brittle review strategy — any homeowner doing off-Google research finds a thin profile, and Bone Dry's owner response rate on secondary platforms is likely low. Raptor can own that alternative-channel real estate cleanly.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. On-site production supervisor as a content asset.

Bone Dry's policy of keeping a production supervisor on-site for the duration of every project, serving as the main homeowner point of contact

, is actively featured in their marketing copy and generates before/during/after photo and video content. Cameron should formalize this touchpoint into a documented content workflow — every job is a content shoot.

  1. Structured discount stack for high-affinity audiences.

Bone Dry runs a standing discount program for military members, veterans, senior citizens, first responders, teachers, and healthcare professionals, limited to single-family residential homes and not combinable with insurance claims.

This is a low-cost lead-generation and PR lever Raptor should evaluate — it generates Nextdoor and Facebook organic word-of-mouth in exactly the demographic most likely to refer neighbors.

  1. Trade press as AEO infrastructure. Bone Dry's 2023 Roofing Contractor Magazine Contractor of the Year placement is the single biggest reason they will be cited by AI engines for Indy roofing queries. Cameron should be actively pitching Patrick and the Raptor story to roofingcontractor.com, Remodeling Magazine's Top 550, and local Indy Business Journal for editorial coverage — not for ego, but because those citations are now direct inputs to generative engine answers.
