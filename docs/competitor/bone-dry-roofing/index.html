Competitive Intelligence Brief: Bone Dry Roofing

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: June 9, 2026

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1989, Bone Dry Roofing has grown into one of the largest residential roofing companies in the Midwest.

Owner Gene Judd grew up the son of a roofing contractor in Illinois, graduated from Indiana University, and stayed in central Indiana to build the business.

The Bone Dry family of companies now spans eight states and includes 15 Bone Dry Roofing locations plus acquired brands G&W Roofing, Ravlin's Roofing, RCV Roofing, Roof One, Bone Dry Commercial, QRS, and Nu-Tec.

Their differentiator is scale-with-soul: a national acquisition machine that still leads with a Midwest family-business narrative and leans heavily on insurance claim facilitation as a top-of-funnel wedge.

2. Estimated Scale

Per Roofing Contractor magazine, Bone Dry operates 20 locations, is 81% residential / 19% commercial, and reported 500 employees as of the 2023 feature.

ZoomInfo notes that 2023 revenue exceeded $183 million,

while

RocketReach pegs the 2026 annual revenue estimate at $191 million.

Confidence: medium — these are modeled figures, not audited financials. The Indy office specifically was a $36M contributor in 2022, per Roofing Contractor, so estimate Indianapolis-market revenue at roughly $40–45M today. Confidence: low on the market-level split.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Active postings are volume-heavy and skew heavily toward revenue-generating roles.

Their Lever ATS (jobs.lever.co/bonedry) shows open Indianapolis roles across commercial, customer service, roofing production, sales, and solar divisions simultaneously.

ZipRecruiter listings show active recruiting for sales reps specifically targeting the Southside Indianapolis and Greenwood area

— direct overlap with Raptor's core geography.

They are also recruiting a Claims Specialist and Insurance Specialist to work specifically on facilitating insurance claims for damaged roofs,

signaling a continued bet on storm/insurance work as a lead channel. No senior marketing or ops leadership hires flagged in the last 30 days, but

their CMO Matt Tyner was brought in specifically to pursue new markets, grow current markets, and generate lead volume and revenue

— his fingerprints are on whatever growth posture you're seeing from them now.

4. Off-Google Presence

On Yelp, the Winton Dr. Indianapolis location has only 75 reviews as of May 2026

against 2,431 on Google — a ratio of roughly 32:1. That is a textbook review-funneling signal; they are clearly directing happy customers to Google and not distributing social proof across platforms.

BBB carries an A+ rating with multiple consecutive Angie's List Super Service Awards on record.

Community partnership activity includes work with Indy Humane on microchipping events,

and

a donation of a full roof and labor to a Nashville first responder in partnership with Owens Corning and the Nashville Sounds.

Facebook Ad Library presence is active (they run paid campaigns) and their tech stack includes Facebook as a confirmed channel per LeadIQ data. No notable negative Reddit or Nextdoor threads surfaced in this pull.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

bonedry.com is a 35-year-old domain with dedicated location subfolders (bonedry.com/indianapolis/) and city-specific service pages — a structural SEO advantage that took decades to build.

Their tech stack includes WordPress, cdnjs, Webpack, and Adobe Premiere Pro,

suggesting active video content production alongside the CMS. They produce blog content under the Indianapolis subfolder (roof flashing tips, seasonal maintenance guides) and the site architecture is clearly built to capture both branded and non-branded long-tail queries. Given their domain age, link equity from national press coverage, and Roofing Contractor magazine features, their domain authority almost certainly sits well above any single-market independent competitor.

They hold the 2023 Roofing Contractor Residential Contractor of the Year award,

which generated high-authority editorial backlinks. AEO posture: their location pages are schema-friendly and their FAQ-style content is structured in a way that positions them for featured snippets and AI citation. They are almost certainly in the top 3 organic results for "Indianapolis roofing company."

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI engine behavior for home-services queries, Bone Dry is extremely likely to be cited in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity responses for Indianapolis roofing queries. The reasons are structural: 35-year domain age, extensive editorial coverage including a national trade award, a Wikipedia-adjacent profile footprint across ZoomInfo/LeadIQ/LinkedIn/BBB, and city-specific landing pages that AI crawlers use to verify local authority. For queries like "best roofers Indianapolis" or "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," they almost certainly appear by name as the volume-review anchor (2,431 Google reviews is a signal AI engines treat as a trust proxy). For "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," their active sales rep hiring in that specific corridor suggests they are also running paid traffic there, which builds behavioral signals AI models pick up over time. The gap Raptor should exploit: AI engines increasingly weight recency and specificity of review content, not just volume. Raptor's 5.0 average with 677 reviews is a quality signal that can be surfaced in AI answers if structured content explicitly frames it — Bone Dry's 4.8 at scale is a floor, not a ceiling.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Bone Dry beats Raptor: Raw review volume (2,431 vs. 677) dominates local pack rankings and AI citation confidence; domain authority and multi-location SEO architecture are essentially impossible to replicate quickly; the Owens Corning Platinum Preferred and CertainTeed ShingleMaster dual-credential play gives them manufacturer co-marketing budget and referral traffic Raptor likely does not have; their in-house "Bone Dry University" training program is a genuine retention and PR asset. Where Raptor beats Bone Dry: 5.0 vs. 4.8 is a meaningful quality-signal differential when framed correctly; Raptor's accountability positioning ("we don't outsource accountability") is a direct counter to the operational complexity risk of a 500-person, 20-location company; review velocity at Bone Dry Indianapolis has slowed (35 reviews in 30 days on 2,431 total is a 1.4% monthly growth rate, which is decelerating). Most exploitable weakness: At their scale, Bone Dry cannot credibly claim the owner is personally accountable on your job — Raptor can, and should drive that contrast hard in every sales conversation and review request.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Insurance claims specialist as a role, not just a service line.

Bone Dry is actively recruiting dedicated Claims Specialists and Insurance Specialists

— meaning they have built a structured insurance-facilitation function that generates leads and closes jobs faster. If Raptor is handling insurance work ad hoc, formalizing that process (even with one designated person and a documented workflow) would close a conversion gap.

  1. The Owens Corning / manufacturer co-marketing play.

Bone Dry holds both Owens Corning Platinum Preferred Contractor and CertainTeed ShingleMaster Select Contractor status simultaneously.

These credentials unlock co-op ad dollars, manufacturer referral traffic, and consumer-facing trust badges that show up in AI training data. Cameron should pursue or display any manufacturer certifications Raptor holds as structured schema on the website, not just as footer logos.

  1. Monitor their Southside/Greenwood sales push.

They are actively recruiting sales reps for the Southside Indianapolis and Greenwood corridor right now.

This is a market incursion signal. Watch for new Google Business Profile coverage in that area and prepare a hyper-local review and content campaign in Greenwood specifically before Bone Dry's new reps get traction.
