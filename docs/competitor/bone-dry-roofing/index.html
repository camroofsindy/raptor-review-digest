Bone Dry Roofing - Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard
Refreshed: Tuesday, May 20, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1989, Bone Dry Roofing has grown into one of the largest residential roofing companies in the Midwest.

Owner Gene Judd grew up the son of a roofing contractor in Illinois, graduated from Indiana University, and stayed in central Indiana to build the company.

The Bone Dry Family of Companies now spans 20 businesses in eight states, including 11 Midwest locations plus Bone Dry Roofing West, Florida, Commercial, Roof One, QRS, Ravlin's, RCV, and Gary's Roofing

— making them a regional roll-up operator, not a pure local competitor. Their core identity is volume-driven "peace of mind" service backed by deep manufacturer relationships;

they hold Owens Corning Platinum Preferred Contractor and CertainTeed Select ShingleMaster Applicator status.

2. Estimated Scale

As of their 2023 Residential Contractor of the Year profile, Bone Dry reported 500 employees company-wide, with a scope of 81% residential and 19% commercial.

LeadIQ pegged company-wide headcount at approximately 240 employees as of March 2026

— indicating meaningful variance across data sources. Confidence: medium (estimates range from 240 to 500 depending on whether subsidiary headcount is consolidated). On revenue:

ZoomInfo notes that the hiring of a new CFO and COO came "on the heels of Bone Dry Roofing's financial performance in 2023 that included revenue of over $183 million."

Of the $121M reported in 2022, approximately $36 million (30%) came from the Indianapolis office alone.

Revenue confidence: medium — the Indianapolis location is likely a $40-50M+ operation on its own by 2025 based on trajectory.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Active and broad.

There are currently 78 open jobs at Bone Dry Roofing Inc., with 21 of those based in Indianapolis.

Roles posted on ZipRecruiter and Indeed include:

Assistant Production Manager

;

Systems Analyst for business operations, noted as being in "growth mode"

;

Division Manager (Roofing), responsible for overall management of sales and production

; and

Home Solution Specialists (in-home sales reps) and Commercial Service Technicians.

The Systems Analyst and Division Manager postings are the ones to watch — ops-level builds like that precede market expansion pushes. No standalone marketing coordinator or CMO-level hire is currently visible in Indiana, which tracks with their in-house CMO (Matt Tyner) being tenured.

4. Off-Google Presence

On Yelp, the Indianapolis location at 7735 Winton Dr has 75 reviews

— versus 2,422 on Google. That is a 97% review-funneling gap, the clearest sign in this market of a team that has mastered Google review capture but has done nothing to cultivate Yelp. Their Facebook presence (

825 likes, 264 people talking about it

) is active but thin for a company of their scale. The UTM parameters in their Facebook profile links (utm_campaign=profile-link&campaign_code=BDR-DIG-ORGS-ROOF-IN-PHO-FB-202402) show they are tracking social-to-web traffic with some discipline.

They carry an A+ BBB rating and have won multiple Angie's List Super Service Awards.

They have a partnership with Indy Humane

that generates community goodwill mentions. BBB complaints page is live but complaint volume is not alarming based on available data. No notable Reddit presence identified for the Indy location.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Bone Dry operates bonedry.com with a dedicated subdirectory at bonedry.com/indianapolis/ and location-specific service pages (e.g., bonedry.com/indianapolis/roofing/).

Their tech stack includes WordPress, Adobe Premiere Pro, Webpack, and Module Federation

— a competent but not cutting-edge setup. The Indianapolis page targets head terms like "roofing contractor Indianapolis" and "roof replacement Indianapolis" with repetitive on-page copy rather than topical authority content. No evidence of a robust blog targeting long-tail question queries ("how much does a roof cost in Carmel IN," etc.) — their content investment appears shallow relative to their domain age and backlink footprint. Their multi-location architecture gives them a domain authority advantage that a single-location operator cannot easily replicate; bonedry.com almost certainly carries a DA of 45+ based on press coverage, Owens Corning partner links, and the 2023 Roofing Contractor Magazine profile. AEO posture is not differentiated — no visible FAQ schema, no structured "ask" content targeting voice or AI-surfaced queries.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Bone Dry Roofing is almost certainly named in AI answers for broad Indianapolis roofing queries. The combination of their volume (2,422 Google reviews), national press coverage (Roofing Contractor Magazine's 2023 Contractor of the Year), multi-state footprint, and aged domain makes them a high-probability citation for prompts like "best roofers Indianapolis" or "roof replacement Carmel Indiana" in ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. Their BBB A+ status and F.C. Tucker partnership (

Bone Dry is a member of F.C. Tucker's Home Services network

) generate structured third-party mentions that LLMs index heavily. However, their lack of specific suburb-level content (Zionsville, Fishers, Noblesville pages are thin or absent) creates a gap — AI answers for hyper-local prompts like "roofing company Fishers Noblesville" are less certain. Raptor should be targeting exactly those prompts with purpose-built content.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Bone Dry beats Raptor: Review volume is not close — 2,422 vs. 673 — and that gap alone dominates Local Pack rankings and AI citation frequency. They have a named CMO (Matt Tyner), an in-house training academy (Bone Dry University), a multi-channel sales system, company-provided vehicles for reps, and a national award that functions as evergreen PR.

Their policy of keeping a production supervisor on-site for every project's duration

is a process differentiator that is easy to market and hard to argue against. Where Raptor beats Bone Dry: A 5.0 star rating versus 4.8 is a genuine differentiator at the premium tier — no sophisticated buyer reads 2,422 reviews; they filter by rating first. Raptor's accountability positioning ("we don't outsource accountability") hits directly at the most common grievance in Bone Dry's employee reviews. Most exploitable weakness:

Indeed reviews score Bone Dry's management accountability at 3.3 out of 5, with employees noting that "higher up managers are not held accountable for anything" and feel "disconnected."

That internal dysfunction surfaces in customer experience — Raptor's owner-present, single-market focus is the direct counter-positioning.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

1. On-site production supervisor as a standard deliverable.

Bone Dry's policy of a production supervisor present for the entire project, with no payment taken until the customer has "expressed total agreement and satisfaction," is a process they market explicitly.

Raptor should formalize and name an equivalent standard in all sales materials and on the website — make it a named commitment, not just a verbal promise.

2. The F.C. Tucker referral partnership.

Bone Dry is embedded in F.C. Tucker's Home Services network

— an inbound referral channel from one of Indy's dominant real estate brands. Raptor should identify equivalent real estate brokerage, insurance agent, and property management partnership channels that Bone Dry is not already locked into. These are pre-qualified, high-intent homeowner referrals that bypass paid search entirely.

3. Formalized veteran and first responder discount as a brand signal.

Bone Dry has partnered with Military.com and pledged support for the veteran community

as a recruiting and PR tool.

Their discount program covers military members, veterans, senior citizens, first responders, teachers, and healthcare professionals.

Raptor should either match this with a cleaner, more premium-positioned version (fewer categories, stronger guarantee) or own a distinct community vertical Bone Dry has not claimed.
