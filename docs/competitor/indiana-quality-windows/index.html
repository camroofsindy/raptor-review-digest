1. Who They Are

Indiana Quality Home Exteriors is a family-owned business based in Indianapolis, specializing in home exterior solutions such as windows, doors, roofing, siding, gutters, and painting.

Established in 1987 as a full-service home exterior company

, the BBB lists the business entity start date as April 1997, suggesting a possible restructuring or reincorporation at some point.

Key contacts on file at BBB include Nita Kelso (Office Manager) and Christopher McCarty (Member).

Their stated differentiator mirrors Raptor's own language almost verbatim:

"as a family-owned company, we take pride in handling every project ourselves, never outsourcing to subcontractors."

3. Recent Hiring Signals

A January 13 Facebook post announced they are hiring an "In-Store Product Promoter / Brand Ambassador" — an outgoing, reliable individual to promote product inside a retail store and generate customer leads.

That is a big tell: retail kiosk or big-box store canvassing (think Menards/Home Depot lobby) is a classic low-cost lead gen play for small exterior remodelers. No postings found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter for any marketing, sales, or ops leadership roles in the last 30 days. The brand ambassador hire is the only active recruiting signal and it points toward volume lead generation, not operational scaling.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Longevity narrative (37 years), multi-trade bundling (windows + doors + siding + roofing in one crew), and the Nextdoor Neighborhood Favorite badge from prior years give them credibility with older, price-sensitive West-side homeowners who distrust newer brands. Their no-subcontractor positioning is near-identical to Raptor's, which means on a side-by-side they can neutralize that differentiator in conversation.

Where Raptor beats them: 701 reviews at 5.0 vs. 332 at 4.8 — Raptor has more than double the volume at a higher rating. Raptor's review velocity (active) vs. Indiana Quality's (3 in 90 days) signals Raptor is winning the job pipeline right now. Raptor is AI-visible; Indiana Quality is not. Raptor's digital infrastructure is clearly superior.

Most exploitable weakness: Their review engine is clinically dead — 3 reviews in 90 days from a company claiming 37 years of work — which means their Google Business Profile will begin losing local pack position to momentum-driven competitors like Raptor within the next 6–12 months.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The no-subcontractor messaging. Indiana Quality has been using this framing for years on Nextdoor and their website. It clearly resonates with West-side Indy homeowners. Raptor should ensure this point is front-loaded in every paid search ad and GBP description targeting the 46241/46222/46224 zip codes where Indiana Quality has home-field awareness.

  2. BBB Accreditation as a trust badge.

Indiana Quality has held BBB Accreditation since October 20, 2002.

For older homeowners who distrust Google reviews as fake, BBB still indexes as a credibility anchor. If Raptor is not BBB-accredited or is not actively displaying that badge in print collateral and door hangers in overlapping zip codes, that is a quick trust gap to close.

  1. Retail/kiosk canvassing. Their January brand ambassador hire points toward an in-store lead generation model — likely a Menards or Home Depot table. Cameron should confirm which store(s) they are working and evaluate whether a competing presence (or a targeted proximity campaign via Meta geo-fencing around those stores) would intercept the same homeowner consideration moment before the lead reaches Indiana Quality's sales rep.
