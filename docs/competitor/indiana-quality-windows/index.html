Indiana Quality Windows (Indiana Quality Home Exteriors) — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing Dashboard | Refreshed: June 11, 2025

1. Who They Are

Indiana Quality Home Exteriors is a family-owned business based in Indianapolis, operating since 1987. They specialize in home exterior solutions — windows, doors, roofing, siding, gutters, and painting — serving both residential and commercial properties.

The company's core differentiator is that they are family-owned and handle every project themselves, never outsourcing to subcontractors

— a direct messaging mirror of Raptor's own positioning that Cameron should take note of.

2. Estimated Scale

Per owner Christopher McCarty's LinkedIn profile, the company self-describes as "a small business with ~21 full time W2 employees."

Confidence: high (owner-stated). No verified revenue figure is publicly available from ZoomInfo or BuildZoom; based on ~21 W2 employees in a multi-trade exterior contracting context, estimated annual revenue is in the $3M–$6M range. Confidence: low.

BuildZoom lists 95 permitted projects and assigns them a score of 106, ranking in the top 7% of 22,324 licensed Indiana contractors.

Permit volume is light relative to their tenure, suggesting either strong repeat/referral business or underperformance in documented project activity.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

As of January 13, 2025, Indiana Quality posted on Facebook for an "In-Store Product Promoter / Brand Ambassador" — an outgoing individual to promote product inside a retail store and generate customer leads.

This is a Lowe's or big-box retail lead-gen play, not a digital acquisition signal. No active postings were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days. The retail ambassador role is the only visible hiring signal — it points to a reliance on in-store foot traffic rather than paid digital or inbound SEO.

The Google/HomeAdvisor gap is classic review-funneling — happy customers get pushed to Google; friction cases land on third-party platforms. Raptor's 100% owner response rate versus their 1.0 is a stark operational difference worth amplifying in sales conversations.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Indiana Quality's domain (indianaquality.com) is a low-investment Wix-based site.

Their technology stack includes reCAPTCHA, PHP, Google Font API, and Microsoft 365

— no advanced CMS or SEO tooling visible. No active blog was found. The site's title tag leads with "Window Replacement Near Me," signaling that windows — not roofing — is their primary SEO priority. There is no evidence of LocalBusiness or Service schema markup, no FAQ schema, and no identifiable backlinks from local Indy press.

Per a Nextdoor comment from the owner's family, they deliberately don't advertise or promote to keep costs down

— a strategy that also suppresses organic visibility. They are not appearing in map-pack results for roofing-specific Indy queries. AEO posture is effectively zero.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Indiana Quality Windows does not appear in AI-generated responses for any of the target queries: "best roofers Indianapolis," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." Their lack of blog content, absence of structured schema, and minimal press backlinks make them invisible to LLM training data and real-time retrieval layers. This is entirely an opportunity gap for Raptor — any AEO investment Cameron makes (FAQ schema, "best of" citation building, third-party feature placements) will widen the gap further against a competitor not even trying to play in this space.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Breadth of services (windows, doors, siding, painting, drywall on top of roofing and gutters) creates more touchpoints per household and enables cross-sell over a customer's lifetime. A homeowner who buys windows can be naturally converted to a roof years later.

BBB accredited since October 2002

also gives them credibility with an older demographic that still checks BBB.

Where Raptor beats them: Review velocity is dead — 1 review in 30 days versus Raptor's active cadence. Owner response rate of 1.0 is near-zero engagement. Raptor's 677 reviews at 5.0 versus their 332 at 4.8 is a wider gap than the numbers suggest when you layer in the 2.8 on HomeAdvisor. No digital ad presence, no AEO footprint, no blog.

Most exploitable weakness: Their HomeAdvisor/Angi rating of 2.8 is publicly searchable and directly contradicts their Google score — any homeowner doing due diligence beyond Google will find a materially different picture, and Raptor can own that contrast in paid search ad copy targeting their brand keywords.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Lowe's/Big-Box Partnership Model. Their in-store brand ambassador play is a real lead-gen channel that bypasses digital CPCs entirely. Cameron should evaluate whether a partnership with a local Lowe's, Menards, or roofing supply showroom — where Raptor captures warm leads from customers already in buy mode — is worth piloting. It's low-cost acquisition compared to Google LSA rates.

  2. Multi-Trade Lifetime Value Framing. Indiana Quality's messaging around being a one-stop exterior contractor naturally drives repeat and referral business across product categories. Raptor doesn't need to add services, but positioning roofing as the entry point into a longer homeowner relationship (gutters, maintenance plans, referrals to trusted trade partners) is a retention play worth formalizing.

  3. BBB Accreditation as a Trust Signal for Older Demos. Their 2002 accreditation date is a credibility anchor with 55+ homeowners — a segment that writes large checks for full replacements. Raptor should audit whether its BBB profile is fully optimized with current photos, service descriptions, and a response cadence, since that audience is unlikely to be swayed by TikTok presence but will absolutely check BBB before signing a contract.
