Indiana Quality Windows (Indiana Quality Home Exteriors) — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Updated: April 28, 2026 | Raptor Dashboard

1. Who They Are

Indiana Quality Home Exteriors is a local small business serving Indianapolis and surrounding areas since 1987.

They brand themselves as a family-owned business that handles all installations without subcontracting

— a direct mirror of Raptor's own accountability positioning.

Key principals on record: Christopher McCarty (Member/Owner) and Nita Kelso (Office Manager), per BBB.

Their service area appears to be Marion County and immediate suburbs, with no evidence of consistent reach into Carmel, Fishers, or Noblesville the way Raptor does.

2. Estimated Scale

Indiana Quality Windows & Doors holds a Renovation license and 2 other licenses according to the EPA license board.

BuildZoom records show 95 permitted projects on file

, though

permit records indicate they have worked on at least 1 project over the past 3 years

— a significant drop-off signal. No public employee count is available; LinkedIn profile (linkedin.com/company/indiana-quality-windows) returns no headcount data. Based on BBB filing, ZoomInfo profile, and permit volume, this reads like a 5-12 person operation doing roughly $1.5M–$3.5M annually across all trades. Confidence: low. Notably,

according to the Indianapolis Department of Business and Neighborhood Services, the status of one license was at one point cancelled

— worth monitoring.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

As of January 13, Indiana Quality posted a "Now Hiring: In-Store Product Promoter / Brand Ambassador" role on Facebook, looking for someone to promote their product inside a retail store and help generate customer leads.

This is their only visible recent hiring activity and it's a lead-gen play, not an ops or management hire. No postings found on Indeed, LinkedIn Jobs, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days. The retail-store canvassing model (likely a big-box partnership) is the only growth signal visible right now — flag this, as it suggests they are hungry for top-of-funnel volume without investing in digital.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Indiana Quality's domain (indianaquality.com) is a Wix-class site with thin content. No blog infrastructure visible, no schema markup evident from search snippets, and no local press backlinks found. Their page title tags do attempt keyword targeting ("Roofers Contractors Near Me, Windows, Doors, Siding, Gutters, Painting") but the execution is generic and unfocused across too many service lines to rank competitively for any single roofing query.

Per their own Nextdoor presence, they self-describe as a company that does not advertise or promote in order to keep costs down

— which is a growth ceiling, not a virtue.

Their tech stack includes reCAPTCHA, PHP, Google Font API, and Microsoft 365

— nothing that signals investment in SEO tooling or content infrastructure. No Google LSA badges, no Angi Top Pro badge visible. AEO posture is effectively zero.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Indiana Quality does not appear in AI-generated recommendation lists for Indianapolis roofing. Tested query patterns — "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "top roofing contractor Fishers Noblesville" — return regionally dominant brands with strong review velocity, citation-rich websites, and press mentions. Indiana Quality has none of those signals. Their 37-year history is their only potential AEO asset, but it is not surfaced in structured data or authored content anywhere. This is a clean gap for Raptor: Cameron should be publishing structured FAQ content and location-specific service pages to own the AI answer layer that Indiana Quality will never compete in.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Longevity narrative (1987 vs. Raptor's founding) and multi-trade bundling (windows, doors, siding, gutters, roofing, painting under one roof) gives them an easier cross-sell conversation on full exterior remodels. BBB A+ accreditation since 2002 carries weight with the 55+ homeowner who distrusts newer companies. Their Lowe's installation partnership (birdeye.com) funnels manufacturer-referred leads Raptor currently does not capture.

Where Raptor beats them: Review velocity is not even close — 3 reviews in 90 days vs. Raptor's pace is near-stagnant. Raptor's 5.0 at 664 reviews vs. their 4.8 at 332 wins on both dimensions. Digital infrastructure, AEO presence, and paid media are entirely absent for Indiana Quality.

Most exploitable weakness: Their HomeAdvisor rating of 2.8 is publicly visible to price-comparison shoppers — Cameron should run comparison-style paid search or GBP posts that surface Raptor's cross-platform consistency as a trust signal.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Big-box retail partnership model.

Indiana Quality serves as an installation provider for Lowe's window customers in their area.

This is recurring, low-CAC lead flow. Cameron should investigate whether a similar preferred-installer relationship with Lowe's, Home Depot, or a local lumber yard is available on the roofing side — particularly for storm-damage referrals.

  1. "No subcontracting" as a positioning anchor. Indiana Quality uses this message on every platform — Nextdoor, their website, BBB. Raptor already owns this message but should be more aggressive making it a direct-response hook in paid ads, especially targeting homeowners who have had bad contractor experiences. Indiana Quality's HomeAdvisor complaints about workmanship give Raptor a live opening to own this claim credibly.

  2. Monitor their Nextdoor Neighborhood Favorite status. They held that badge in 2021 and 2022 and appear to have lost it. Cameron should ensure Raptor's team is actively generating Nextdoor recommendations in every ZIP they serve — this is an AEO-adjacent signal as Nextdoor data increasingly feeds into local AI recommendations.
