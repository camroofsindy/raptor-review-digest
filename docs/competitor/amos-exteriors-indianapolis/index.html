Competitive Intelligence Brief — Amos Exteriors

7301 E 46th St, Indianapolis, IN 46226 | amosexteriors.com
Refreshed: April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1971, Amos Exteriors is a leading exterior remodeling company serving homeowners and businesses throughout Central Indiana.

Robert Amos is listed as owner on the BBB profile

, and

the company operates locations in both Indianapolis and Lafayette.

Their differentiator is breadth — roofing, siding, windows, doors, gutters, masonry, and commercial work all under one roof — paired with a 54-year local legacy and a heavy insurance-claims sales motion targeting storm-damaged homeowners.

2. Estimated Scale

ZoomInfo lists Amos Exteriors' corporate office at 7301 E 46th St and pegs the employee count at 40.

This is corroborated by SBA PPP data:

Amos Exteriors received an SBA PPP loan of $584,200 from Stock Yards Bank & Trust, with the stated purpose of retaining 40 jobs.

Confidence: medium — 40 is the PPP-era headcount; the Lafayette expansion likely pushes current FTE count to 45–55. At a blended ticket of ~$12K–$15K across roofing and exteriors, a 40-person operation in this trade running canvassers and multi-service upsells reasonably generates $8M–$14M in annual revenue. Confidence: low — no public revenue filing available.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current knowledge of how LLMs weight local contractor citations: Amos Exteriors' review volume (3,046 on Google) and their long-running domain give them real probability of appearing in AI-generated answers to broad queries like "best roofers Indianapolis." However, their near-zero owner response rate — 0 responses logged against thousands of reviews — and their thin blog content mean they are not generating the kind of structured, FAQ-style, entity-rich content that earns citations in ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity answers. Their GAF Master Elite status and BBB A+ are cited credentials that LLMs tend to surface, but without recent content or structured schema reinforcing those signals, their AEO posture is passive. Raptor has a clear path to own AI-cited answers for suburb-specific queries (Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville) where Amos has location pages but no differentiated narrative content.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume is not close — 3,046 vs. 664. In GBP ranking algorithms and in consumer trust shortcuts ("more reviews = safer bet"), that gap matters, especially for homeowners who don't read past the number. Their multi-service model (roofing + siding + windows + gutters) also means higher average ticket and more cross-sell per lead. Their GAF Master Elite and multi-manufacturer certifications give them a credential wall that functions as a conversion tool.

Where Raptor beats them: 5.0 vs. 4.9 is a meaningful signal when both are visible side-by-side in the Local Pack. Zero owner responses at Amos over the last 90 days — and apparently no response cadence at all — versus Raptor's accountability positioning is a real trust gap. The Yelp 2.5 rating is an exploitable liability if Raptor earns positive Yelp reviews. Raptor's premium, single-trade focus also runs circles around Amos on the "we specialize, they generalize" narrative.

Most exploitable weakness: Amos has completely abandoned review recency — zero new Google reviews in 90 days on a 3,046-review profile signals either a broken post-job follow-up process or deliberate disengagement, and a competitor who surfaces this contrast in ad copy wins the recency credibility battle outright.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GAF Master Elite as a conversion anchor.

Amos leans on the GAF Master Elite designation and the GAF System Plus Warranty

throughout their funnel. If Raptor holds or can pursue this designation, it should be front-and-center in every paid ad headline and GBP description — not buried in an about page.

  1. Weather history lead magnet.

Amos deploys an address lookup tool that generates a free report of weather events impacting a home based on historical extreme weather data.

This is a strong top-of-funnel lead capture play that pre-qualifies storm-damage prospects before a sales call — Cameron should evaluate a similar tool or white-label equivalent to capture the same intent traffic.

  1. Insurance claims as a sales lane.

Amos markets dedicated insurance claims experts who work directly with homeowners and their insurance companies.

Structuring a named "Claims Concierge" or equivalent process — and promoting it in paid and organic content — is a high-converting differentiator in the post-storm window that Raptor can operationalize without Amos's scale.
