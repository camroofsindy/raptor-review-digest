Trojan Roofing — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Updated April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

Trojan Roofing, Inc. was founded in 2020, incorporated November 5, 2020, and is owned and operated by Tyler Morgan.

Since 2019, they claim to have been serving residential and commercial clients throughout the Indianapolis area.

Their services extend beyond roofing to include siding and gutters, and they present themselves as authorized installers of IKO Dynasty and Owens Corning products.

Their primary GTM motion is storm-damage canvassing with a heavy insurance-claim conversion focus — a volume-over-relationship play that sits at the opposite end of the market from Raptor's premium positioning.

2. Estimated Scale

Salary data estimated from approximately 135 employees, users, and past and present job advertisements on Indeed.

Confidence: low — this figure conflates W-2 staff, commission-only reps, and subcontractors. The real W-2 headcount is likely 20-40.

BuildZoom lists Tyler Morgan as the named employee on their general contractor license, with a BuildZoom score of 98 ranking them in the top 16% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors.

No verified revenue figure is publicly available; based on review volume (643 Google reviews since 2020), storm-restoration average job size (~$12K-$15K), and their canvassing model, a rough estimate is $5M-$12M ARR. Confidence: low.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

ZipRecruiter shows Trojan actively hiring Outside Sales Representatives and a Territory Sales Manager.

Job ads emphasize corporate admin support for lead generation, supplements, and estimating, and describe an "ambitious sales approach" built on canvassing, referrals, and social media prospecting.

Indeed lists 15 open Trojan Roofing positions in Indianapolis, IN.

No marketing, ops leadership, or project management hires detected — every active posting is field sales. That is a tell: they are scaling canvass reps, not building infrastructure. Zero evidence of a marketing hire, digital strategist, or sales ops director in the last 30 days.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform picture here is damaging and exploitable.

Trojan Roofing is NOT BBB Accredited and carries an F rating, citing failure to respond to 4 complaints and advertising issues flagged by the BBB.

BBB complaint filings include one customer alleging a $12K+ payment with no work performed, and another alleging a roof was installed without obtaining a required building permit.

Their Facebook page has only 233 likes

with no visible paid ad activity detected. On Yelp, their listing exists at 333 Alabama St but carries no meaningful review volume — a classic review-funneling pattern where all traffic is directed to Google while third-party platforms sit dormant.

Their Indeed employer profile shows a 1.0 out of 5 stars rating from employees in Indianapolis.

This is a four-front liability: F on BBB, near-zero Yelp presence, hostile employer brand on Indeed, and Facebook with no ad investment.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

The website at indytrojanroofing.com runs a templated, heavily keyword-stuffed structure with dedicated service pages for roof repair, commercial roofing, and emergency roofing — all targeting exact-match terms like "roof repair Indianapolis IN" and "commercial roofing Indianapolis IN." It is a volume-content play, not an authority-content play.

Their BuildZoom score of 98 places them in the top 16% of Indiana contractors

, suggesting some backlink/citation traction from directory profiles. However, there is no evidence of local press backlinks, editorial content, or schema markup beyond what a basic WordPress/Elementor build auto-generates. They also operate a separate subdomain at jobs.indytrojanroofing.com, splitting domain authority unnecessarily. No blog content with genuine topical depth was found. Their AEO posture is effectively nonexistent — no FAQ schema, no structured data, no apparent E-E-A-T signals.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current research, Trojan Roofing does not appear to be a named citation in AI-generated answers for high-intent Indianapolis roofing queries such as "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." Their content is keyword-dense but thin on the trust signals — named owner expertise, third-party editorial mentions, association memberships, warranty specifics — that cause LLMs to surface a contractor by name. Their BBB F rating and documented complaints would actively work against them in any AI system that indexes consumer review aggregators. This is a clean lane for Raptor: structured content, press mentions, and owner-attributed expertise content will drive AI citation share that Trojan cannot realistically compete for given their reputation profile.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Trojan beats Raptor: Raw review count is close (643 vs. 664), and their canvassing model generates high first-touch volume in storm-affected neighborhoods — they will always be in front of homeowners faster post-event. Their insurance-assist pitch is a frictionless entry point that Raptor's premium model does not replicate.

Where Raptor beats Trojan: Every meaningful trust signal. Raptor holds a 5.0 vs. Trojan's 4.9, a clean BBB profile vs. an F rating, a 6% owner response rate for Trojan is a hard indictment of post-sale engagement, and Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is a direct counter to Trojan's documented complaint history of ghosting customers post-payment.

Most exploitable weakness:

The BBB F rating for failure to respond to complaints

is publicly indexed and permanent — Raptor should be referencing this contrast explicitly in sales collateral and local content without naming the competitor directly (e.g., "some Indy roofers have F ratings with the BBB — here's how to check before you sign").

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Dedicated jobs subdomain as a recruiting signal. Trojan runs jobs.indytrojanroofing.com as a standalone recruiting page. It fragments their domain authority, but the intent is right: a standalone page that markets the company to potential hires. Raptor should build a /careers page that aggressively sells the culture and serves as an AEO target for "roofing jobs Indianapolis" queries — a lead-gen asset that doubles as employer brand.

  2. Commission structure transparency in sales job ads.

Trojan publicly advertises that their team structure lets reps focus on selling while back-office handles logistics, and they pay commissions on total collected amount — not net profit.

This is a compelling recruiter pitch that attracts high-output canvassers. Cameron should monitor whether this produces a surge in Trojan's sales headcount heading into storm season and be ready to counter with a differentiated comp structure targeting quality-over-volume reps.

  1. TikTok as a lead-gen channel.

At least one Trojan customer review cites finding them on TikTok after storm damage.

In a market where competitors are not investing in short-form video, even a minimal presence can drive inbound. Raptor's premium brand story — crew ownership, material quality, the velociraptor brand itself — is far more filmable than a canvassing operation. A consistent TikTok presence tied to Patrick's project walk-throughs or before/after installs is a low-cost channel Trojan is already proving out.
