Competitive Intelligence Brief: Trojan Roofing

Updated: April 26, 2025 | Prepared for Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Trojan Roofing is a family-owned and operated business based in Indianapolis, serving the greater Indiana community.

The business was started January 1, 2020, incorporated November 5, 2020, and is owned and operated by Tyler Morgan.

The company covers roofing along with adjacent services including siding and gutters

, and runs an aggressive door-to-door and neighborhood canvassing sales model as a core customer acquisition strategy — a storm-chasing playbook dressed up as a residential roofer.

2. Estimated Scale

Glassdoor shows 12 Trojan Roofing employee reviews in Indianapolis

, suggesting a lean operation — likely 10–25 W-2 and 1099 personnel combined, with heavy reliance on sales reps and subcontracted install crews. Confidence: low — no LinkedIn employee count or ZoomInfo profile for this entity was surfaced.

Indeed salary data shows a compensation range from approximately $40,891/year for sales roles to $103,945/year for manager-level positions.

Given volume (643 Google reviews since 2020, storm-driven pipeline, and dual residential/commercial scope), estimated annual revenue is $3M–$6M. Confidence: low — no verified financial data available.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active job postings on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter were surfaced for Trojan Roofing Indianapolis in the last 30 days.

Glassdoor reviews reference employee terminations and non-payment of wages

, which suggests ongoing turnover rather than a structured growth hire cycle.

Indeed reviewers flag management struggles to delegate, leading to micro-management that has cost the company sales.

No marketing, ops leadership, or estimator postings detected. The absence of hiring signals combined with near-zero recent review velocity (2 reviews in 30 days) points to a business in contraction or plateau — not expansion.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform picture here is damaging and exploitable.

Trojan Roofing is NOT BBB Accredited and carries an F rating, citing failure to respond to 4 complaints and advertising issues flagged by BBB.

One BBB complaint alleges a customer paid over $12K and the owner blocked their number without completing the work.

Another complaint states Trojan put a roof on without obtaining a building permit.

Their Facebook page

has only 233 likes

— thin for a company claiming the "#1" position in Indiana. No meaningful Yelp review presence was found beyond a listing stub. The core discrepancy: 643 Google reviews at 4.9 stars versus an F at BBB with unresolved consumer fraud allegations. This is textbook review-funneling — Google pipeline is tightly managed, complaints are siloed to channels they don't monitor.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Trojan Roofing ranks in the top 16% of licensed contractors in Indiana per BuildZoom, with a BuildZoom score of 98.

Their primary domain is indytrojanroofing.com with a secondary at trojanroofs.com. The site has location-specific service pages (Indianapolis, Greenwood) and product name-drops (

IKO Dynasty and Owens Corning systems are referenced explicitly

), which is a serviceable mid-tier local SEO tactic. However, the site copy reads as templated — no original blog content, no local press backlinks, and no schema markup was evident. The dual-domain setup creates crawl dilution; they're splitting authority between two properties with no clear canonical strategy.

One review mentions finding Trojan Roofing on TikTok

, which indicates some short-form video investment — though no sustained TikTok content strategy was verifiable. No evidence of AEO-specific content (FAQ schema, structured Q&A pages, entity disambiguation) was found.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No current search results confirmed Trojan Roofing being cited by name in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini responses to queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," or "roof replacement Carmel Indiana." Their dual-domain structure, absence of authoritative local press backlinks, and templated site content all suppress entity recognition in LLM training data and retrieval-augmented generation pipelines. This is a clean opportunity for Raptor: structured content (FAQ schema, named-entity pages for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville) paired with third-party citation building (local press, HomeAdvisor editorial, Houzz profiles) is the direct path to owning AI-generated answer results for Indy roofing queries that Trojan is not competing for.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Trojan beats Raptor: Raw review volume is competitive at 643 vs. Raptor's 664 — close enough that a casual Google searcher sees rough parity. Their storm-chasing, door-knocking sales model generates leads at a cost-per-acquisition Raptor's premium positioning doesn't chase, which means they are moving volume in commodity insurance-claim jobs Raptor may not want anyway.

Where Raptor beats Trojan: Raptor holds a 5.0 vs. Trojan's 4.9 — and more importantly, Raptor's review velocity is the metric to watch. Trojan's 0 reviews in the last 7 days and only 6 in 90 days signals the pipeline has dried up, the sales team has shrunk, or review generation was never systematized. An F at the BBB, multiple unresolved fraud allegations, and a 0.06 owner response rate on Google collectively represent a trust deficit that Raptor's premium positioning can directly exploit in ad copy and sales conversations.

Most exploitable weakness: Trojan's BBB F-rating and documented permit violations are public record — a targeted "why your roofer's reputation matters beyond Google" content campaign would land directly on the audience cross-shopping both companies.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. TikTok/short-form video for lead gen.

At least one verified customer found Trojan specifically through TikTok before reaching out

— Trojan is generating awareness from a channel most Indy roofers ignore. Cameron should assess whether a systematic short-form video content cadence (job site walkthroughs, before/afters, material education) belongs in Raptor's mix before a better-capitalized competitor locks in that audience.

  1. Named staff in review responses and site content.

Customer reviews consistently call out individual reps by name — Tyler, Brice, Grant — as a trust signal.

Trojan is (accidentally or not) building individual brand equity within their team. Raptor should formalize this: named sales reps with headshots and brief bios on estimate confirmation emails and the website create accountability cues that premium buyers respond to.

  1. Monitor their BBB complaint feed monthly. The BBB complaints page is a live intelligence feed. New complaints signal either a sales surge (more volume = more disputes) or operational collapse. Either event is a targeting trigger — paid search budget can be shifted toward Trojan's core zip codes the moment their reputation surface degrades further.
