Competitive Intelligence Brief: Trojan Roofing

Raptor Roofing — Cameron Blakely Dashboard
Refreshed: June 11, 2025

1. Who They Are

Trojan Roofing, Inc. was incorporated November 5, 2020, with business operations starting January 1, 2020, and is owned by Tyler Morgan.

Morgan is a former accountant who transitioned into construction and roofing.

Operating since 2019 out of Indianapolis, the company handles both residential and commercial work and self-describes as coordinating subcontractors across the full project lifecycle.

Their primary hook is insurance-claim facilitation —

Morgan personally climbs roofs with insurance adjusters and positions himself as an advocate for maximizing homeowner insurance settlements.

2. Estimated Scale

Cortera lists Trojan Roofing as a single-location private company founded in 2019, with an estimated 1 to 5 employees and a sales range of under $500,000.

Confidence: low — this data is almost certainly stale relative to their current operational footprint.

Their own team page lists one Founder/Owner and six Project Manager roles

, suggesting actual headcount is materially higher than the Cortera filing. A more realistic revenue estimate based on that project-manager bench and 641 Google reviews accumulated since 2020 is $2M–$5M annually. Confidence: low.

BuildZoom ranks them in the top 99% of licensed contractors in Indiana.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

ZipRecruiter shows active listings for Field Tech Inspectors and Outside Sales Representatives

, and

a separate entry-level Recruiter role specifically tasked with growing the sales and field team is also posted.

This three-pronged hiring push — inspector, outside sales, internal recruiter — is a textbook storm-chasing scale-up pattern. They are clearly trying to add canvassing headcount, not operational depth. No marketing, ops leadership, or technology hires detected.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform picture here is damaging and worth documenting.

The BBB has assigned Trojan Roofing an F rating, citing failure to respond to 4 complaints and advertising issues — and the company is not BBB accredited.

One BBB complaint specifically notes the company avoided providing a written agreement, telling the customer they would charge whatever the insurance company determined.

On Nextdoor,

they have just 1 "Fave" from Indianapolis neighbors

— a near-zero signal.

Facebook shows only 233 page likes

and no visible Facebook Ad Library activity found.

Glassdoor has 12 Indianapolis employee reviews

, and publicly visible sentiment includes negative commentary about internal practices. The gap between 641 Google reviews at 4.9 stars and an F on BBB with no Nextdoor presence is one of the starkest review-funneling discrepancies in the Indy market right now.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Trojan Roofing operates two domains — indytrojanroofing.com and trojanroofs.com — which immediately creates authority dilution and NAP inconsistency problems for local SEO. Their website copy is heavily templated and generic; the FAQ and blog content visible on the site reads as boilerplate rather than original authority content.

They do carry IKO Dynasty and Owens Corning certified installer credentials

, which represent legitimate schema/trust signals if properly marked up — but there's no evidence of structured data implementation.

A customer review notes they discovered Trojan Roofing on TikTok

, suggesting some short-form video presence, but it does not appear to be a consistent or strategic channel. No press backlinks, no local editorial coverage found. Domain authority is likely sub-20.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Trojan Roofing does not appear to be cited by name in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini responses for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." The third-party editorial roundups that AI engines pull from — Yahoo Local, Expertise.com, Angi — do not include Trojan in their Indianapolis rankings.

One local roundup from introindianapolis.com places Raptor Roofing at the top of the 2025 Indianapolis list

, which is a positive AEO signal for Raptor. Trojan's two-domain split, lack of structured content, and absence from editorial lists means they are effectively invisible to generative engines.

AI tools like ChatGPT and Gemini cite only 3–5 sources per answer; if a business is not in that group, it is invisible regardless of Google ranking.

That's where Trojan sits today.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Trojan beats Raptor: Review volume is close (641 vs. 677) and their 4.9 rating gives them surface-level credibility with homeowners doing a quick Google scan. Their insurance-adjuster advocacy positioning is a compelling sales narrative that resonates in a hail-prone market, and their canvassing/door-knocking model generates high touchpoints in storm-affected neighborhoods.

Where Raptor beats Trojan: 5.0 star rating is cleaner and more defensible. Raptor has zero BBB complaint exposure versus Trojan's F rating and 4 unresolved complaints. Owner response rate of 0.06 at Trojan is essentially non-existent — a signal of operational disengagement that Raptor can exploit in messaging. Raptor also has a single-domain, coherent brand identity versus Trojan's split-domain SEO liability.

Most exploitable weakness: Trojan's BBB F rating and complete silence on owner responses (0.06 rate) create a credibility vacuum that Raptor should be filling with targeted "why reviews alone aren't enough" content aimed at in-market homeowners doing due diligence.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Insurance-advocacy sales script.

Trojan's owner personally joins insurance adjusters on roof inspections and positions the company as a fighter for maximum settlement value.

Whether or not Raptor adopts that exact tactic, codifying a clear insurance-claim support process into sales collateral — and promoting it explicitly — is a conversion lever Trojan has already validated in this market.

  1. Outside sales canvassing at scale. Their active hiring of multiple Outside Sales Representatives and an internal recruiter signals a high-volume door-knocking operation in storm corridors. Cameron should track which subdivisions Trojan is working post-storm (Nextdoor, neighborhood Facebook groups) and ensure Raptor has a counter-presence — digital or physical — in those same ZIP codes within 48 hours of a weather event.

  2. TikTok job documentation.

At least one customer found Trojan through TikTok before hiring them.

Short-form before/after job video is an underpenetrated channel in the Indy roofing space and a direct AEO feed —

actual project photos and video build AI credibility in ways stock imagery cannot, and every job documented from day one compounds over time.

Raptor should be owning this lane before Trojan figures out it's an AEO asset, not just a vanity metric.
