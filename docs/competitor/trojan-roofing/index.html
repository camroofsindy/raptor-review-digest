Competitive Intelligence Brief: Trojan Roofing

Refreshed: July 2, 2025 | Analyst: Raptor CMO Dashboard

1. Who They Are

Trojan Roofing opened in 2019, founded by Tyler Morgan, an accountant-turned-contractor who pivoted into the roofing industry.

The business was formally incorporated November 5, 2020, and operates as a corporation registered in Indiana.

They serve residential and commercial clients throughout the Indianapolis area

, with their primary identity built around insurance claim-driven storm restoration and a canvassing-heavy, commission-first sales model.

2. Estimated Scale

Indeed salary data is estimated from approximately 135 employees, users, and past and present job advertisements.

That figure blends current and former data, so a working headcount estimate of 20–45 active W-2/1099 staff is more realistic given the canvass-and-close model. Confidence: low. Revenue is harder to pin without permit volume data. With 641 Google reviews accumulated since 2019 at an average ticket of ~$12,000–$15,000 for residential storm work, a rough trailing-twelve-months estimate of $4M–$8M is plausible. Confidence: low.

BuildZoom ranks them in the top 16% of licensed contractors in Indiana

, which adds modest credibility to a mid-market scale assumption.

However, BuildZoom also notes that the status of at least one of their licenses was at one point cancelled

— worth monitoring.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Heavy, sustained sales-rep recruitment is the dominant signal.

ZipRecruiter currently shows Trojan posting for a Field Tech Inspector and Outside Sales Representatives, describing itself as "rapidly growing."

A separate active posting also seeks an Entry-Level Recruiter to help grow their sales and field team.

Indeed currently lists 31 open jobs under their profile.

No marketing, ops leadership, or technology roles were found. The pattern is pure sales-funnel scaling — canvassers and inspectors — with zero investment in marketing or ops infrastructure hires visible. That is a structural tell.

4. Off-Google Presence

The review picture outside Google is damaging.

Trojan Roofing holds an F rating on BBB, with failure to respond to four complaints filed against the business, plus advertising issues flagged by BBB staff.

A BBB complaint details a customer who reported Trojan was "canvasing the neighborhood" and then made "every attempt to avoid providing a written agreement."

On Glassdoor, employees rate Trojan 2.1 out of 5 stars based on 15 anonymous reviews.

Only 24% of employees would recommend working there to a friend.

Their Facebook page has a thin 233 likes

and no evidence of a paid Facebook Ad Library presence emerged from search. Yelp presence exists at the Alabama St. address but shows no substantive review volume. The pattern is classic review-funneling: Google reviews are aggressively solicited at close, while the off-Google profile is ignored and quietly decaying.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their primary domain is indytrojanroofing.com with a secondary at trojanroofs.com. Running two separate domains for one brand is an SEO split that dilutes link equity — a self-inflicted wound. The site content is generic, climate-filler copy templated for local SEO ("hot summers, cold winters") with no evidence of earned local press backlinks or schema-rich service pages.

Their "Meet Our Team" page lists a Founder/Owner and six Project Manager titles

with no names shown — a thin trust signal that undermines E-E-A-T. Blog posts were last dated April 25, 2025.

They do hold IKO Dynasty and Owens Corning preferred installer credentials

, which are linkable brand assets they are not visibly leveraging for AEO entity authority. No structured FAQ, no review schema, no "Ask an Expert" content pattern found.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on available research signals, Trojan Roofing is unlikely to surface as a named recommendation in AI engine responses for core Indy roofing queries. Their dual-domain structure, thin blog content, zero BBB accreditation, 2.1 Glassdoor rating, and F BBB grade create a negative entity signal that LLMs — which weigh authoritative third-party corroboration — would actively deprioritize.

Industry observers have noted that BBB listings and reviews on that platform "seem to be gaining traction as an authoritative source" in AI searches

— a channel where Trojan is currently radioactive. For Raptor, the path to owning "best roofer Indianapolis" in ChatGPT and Gemini answers runs directly through a clean, multi-platform reputation Trojan cannot match.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Trojan beats Raptor: Raw Google review volume is close (641 vs. 682), and they have more name recognition in the insurance-claim/storm-damage segment due to a larger canvassing sales force. Their trojanroofs.com site does show manufacturer awards prominently, which is a trust lever Raptor should be equally loud about.

Where Raptor beats Trojan: Perfect 5.0 vs. 4.9 rating, genuine 0% owner response rate on Trojan's side vs. Raptor's active engagement, and a completely clean off-platform profile versus Trojan's F BBB grade, 2.1 Glassdoor score, and unresolved wage complaints. Raptor's premium positioning is differentiated; Trojan's is cosmetic.

Most exploitable weakness:

Trojan's F BBB rating and documented failure to respond to complaints

is publicly verifiable and directly contradicts their "#1 roofing company" homepage claim — Cameron can make Raptor's owner-accountability story the explicit counter-narrative in local content and sales collateral.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Manufacturer credential amplification.

Trojan promotes itself as an authorized installer of IKO Dynasty and Owens Corning

and leads with those badges. If Raptor holds comparable credentials, they should be front-and-center on the homepage, in Google Business Profile posts, and embedded as structured data — not buried.

  1. TikTok as a lead source.

At least one Trojan customer found them on TikTok and hired them after watching their videos.

For a premium brand like Raptor, short-form video showing real installs, before/after storm damage, and owner-on-site moments is an untapped AEO trust-builder that also feeds social proof back into Google's entity graph.

  1. Counter the canvass model with a review-velocity moat. Trojan's Google review count is close to Raptor's, but their acquisition has stalled — zero reviews in the last 30 days. Raptor should be systematically widening the gap while simultaneously building reviews on BBB and Yelp, platforms where Trojan is exposed, to guarantee a clean sweep in AI engine citations as LLMs increasingly weight multi-platform corroboration.
