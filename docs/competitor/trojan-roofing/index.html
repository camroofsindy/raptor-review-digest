Trojan Roofing -- Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely | Refreshed July 23, 2025

1. Who They Are

Trojan Roofing has been operating since 2019

, incorporated as Trojan Roofing, Inc.

with the business incorporated on 11/5/2020 and the BBB file opened 2/9/2022.

Owner is Tyler Morgan.

The company describes itself as a family-owned and operated business based in Indianapolis, specializing in comprehensive roofing services for both residential and commercial properties

, with

services extending to siding and gutters, and authorized installations of IKO Dynasty and Owens Corning.

Their registered office address (333 Alabama St Ste#350) is a shared commercial suite -- a virtual office flag worth noting.

2. Estimated Scale

Glassdoor shows 12 Trojan Roofing reviews in Indianapolis

, suggesting a headcount in the 10-25 range for a storm-focused shop of this age.

BuildZoom lists Tyler Morgan as the named employee, holding a General Contractor license and 3 other licenses, with a BuildZoom score of 98 ranking in the top 16% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors.

A dedicated Recruiting Manager (Maxwell Bunch, per LinkedIn) signals active headcount growth. Estimated annual revenue: $2M-$5M. Confidence: low -- no ZoomInfo pull available; estimate is based on review velocity, license count, and Glassdoor headcount signal.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Trojan Roofing jobs are listed on Indeed in Indianapolis, with the most visible recent role being a Billing Admin.

Their own careers page is actively recruiting sales professionals

, with the pitch centered on

uncapped earning potential, comprehensive training, and leadership growth opportunities with flexible schedules.

No marketing, ops director, or digital lead roles were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days. The Recruiting Manager role at LinkedIn is the most interesting signal -- it suggests they are trying to industrialize their sales rep pipeline, not upgrade strategic leadership. No CMO-equivalent hire visible.

4. Off-Google Presence

The platform discrepancy here is significant. On Google: 639 reviews at 4.9 stars -- strong by volume.

On BBB, the business is NOT accredited and carries an F rating, citing failure to respond to 4 complaints and advertising issues flagged by the BBB.

One BBB complaint details an attempt to avoid providing a written agreement, with a second describing a deeply unprofessional employment experience including alleged non-payment of wages.

On Indeed, Trojan Roofing carries a 2.0 out of 5 stars employer rating.

Facebook shows only 233 page likes

with no evidence of active paid ad campaigns in Meta Ad Library.

Yelp shows no reviews posted

-- a textbook review-funneling pattern (Google only, Yelp scrubbed). The BBB F-rating and 2.0 Indeed score vs. 4.9 Google is the single biggest credibility gap on their profile.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

The domain indytrojanroofing.com has built out a standard service-page architecture: dedicated pages for residential roofing, commercial roofing, roof repair, roof installation, and emergency roofing.

They promote IKO Dynasty and Owens Corning installations backed by a limited lifetime warranty

-- warranty language on service pages is an AEO-friendly trust signal. However, the on-page copy is heavily keyword-stuffed ("#1 Roofing Indianapolis IN Company" pattern repeated across every page title) with no evidence of editorial blog content, local press backlinks, or structured FAQ schema that would build genuine AEO authority. The site appears optimized for basic local SEO but is not doing anything sophisticated -- no city-cluster pages for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, or Zionsville were found. Domain authority signal is thin relative to their Google Maps strength, meaning their Maps ranking is driven by review volume, not domain trust.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI answer patterns for "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," and "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville": Trojan Roofing does not consistently appear in generative engine citations. Their lack of editorial content, absence of local press mentions, and thin backlink profile mean LLMs have little sourced material to cite. Their Google Maps strength does not translate to AEO visibility because AI engines pull from indexed written content and authority sources -- neither of which Trojan has invested in. This is a Raptor opportunity: a well-sourced blog post, a local press feature, or a structured FAQ page targeting those suburb queries would outrank Trojan in AI-generated answers even while Trojan holds a Maps position.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Review volume is close (639 vs. 692) and their 4.9 is defensible in casual consumer scanning. Their storm-canvassing sales model generates reviews at scale by pushing reps to solicit feedback immediately post-appointment -- a velocity tactic Raptor's premium model doesn't replicate.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor holds a 5.0 vs. 4.9, more total reviews, zero BBB complaints, and a credible premium positioning narrative. Trojan's BBB F-rating, 2.0 Indeed employer score, and alleged wage non-payment create a real accountability story Raptor can own without ever naming them.

Most exploitable weakness:

Trojan's BBB F-rating for failure to respond to consumer complaints

combined with a 0.0% Google owner response rate means any homeowner doing due diligence beyond Google Maps will find a company that goes silent when things go wrong -- the exact inverse of Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Storm canvassing + immediate review solicitation. Trojan's review velocity (639 reviews in roughly 5 years) suggests disciplined in-the-field review asks by sales reps right after the appointment, not a drip email sequence. Cameron should audit whether Raptor's review ask is happening at the peak emotional moment -- job completion handoff -- rather than days later via text.

  2. Service-page architecture for suburb keywords.

Trojan has dedicated landing pages for roof installation targeting Indianapolis

but has not built suburb-level pages. Raptor should move first on geo-specific pages for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, and Greenwood with genuine local content (permit data, neighborhood case studies) to capture both organic and AI citation traffic before Trojan closes that gap.

  1. Monitor the BBB complaint trajectory.

Four unresolved BBB complaints with an F rating

is the kind of thing that surfaces in AI-generated "who should I trust" queries. Raptor should publish a page or content asset explicitly about what accountability looks like post-job -- response policy, warranty claims process, owner contact -- to contrast with Trojan's visible silence and own the "trustworthy roofer Indianapolis" answer in generative engines.
