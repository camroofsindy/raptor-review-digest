🦅 Raptor Roofing — Competitor Intelligence Brief

Competitor: Bone Dry Roofing (Indianapolis HQ)
Refreshed: Thursday, April 25, 2026
Prepared for Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Gene Judd is the owner and President of Bone Dry Roofing, which he opened in 1989 with a truck and a garage office, not long after graduating from Indiana University.

Beginning in Indiana with HQ in Indianapolis, they expanded to Ohio, Kentucky, Missouri, and Tennessee.

The Bone Dry Family of Companies now spans 20 businesses in eight states, including 11 Midwest locations, Bone Dry Commercial Roofing, Roof One, Quality Roofing Services (QRS), Ravlin's Roofing, RCV Roofing, and Gary's Roofing.

Their differentiator is brand depth — they aren't just a roofer, they're a multi-trade home solutions platform with solar, masonry, insulation, gutters, and interior work, backed by a dedicated internal training academy ("Bone Dry University").

2. Estimated Scale

Per their own job postings: "a family-owned and operated business with 19 locations and 500+ employees."

Confidence: High (self-disclosed across multiple active job listings on ZipRecruiter and Lever as of April 2026).

They have dedicated location pages (e.g., /indianapolis/service-areas/indianapolis-in/) optimized for city + service combinations.

across all locations — that review corpus is enormous for citation-building.

Raptor opportunity: Target hyper-local suburb queries (Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, Westfield, Greenwood) with tighter, more specific content and faster-loading pages — Bone Dry's multi-location architecture makes nimble suburb targeting harder for them.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Tested across ChatGPT (GPT-4o), Claude, and Gemini for "best roofers Indianapolis," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," and "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville."

Bone Dry is consistently cited by name in AI answers — particularly in ChatGPT and Gemini responses — primarily because of three factors: (1) their 2023 Roofing Contractor Magazine national award (roofingcontractor.com article), (2) their heavy presence on third-party review aggregators (Angi, GuildQuality, BBB), and (3) volume of indexed location pages.

Bone Dry was named Roofing Contractor Magazine's 2023 Residential Roofing Contractor of the Year — a national award that honors them as one of the best in the country.

That press coverage is exactly the type of entity-authority signal that LLMs train on and retrieve. Raptor is not yet appearing in these AI-generated lists. This is the single biggest AEO gap to close — Raptor needs earned media placements (local press, trade publications, neighborhood outlet features) that LLMs can anchor citations to.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Brand recognition and institutional gravity — 35+ years of Indianapolis-market presence, a national award, 500 employees, and a training academy create a defensible enterprise moat. Their multi-service upsell machine (roofing → gutters → insulation → solar → masonry) means higher average job value per customer.

Where Raptor beats them: The numbers on Cameron's dashboard don't lie. Raptor holds 664 reviews at a 5.0 vs. Bone Dry's Indianapolis GBP showing 643 reviews at 4.9 — Raptor leads on both volume and rating in the head-to-head. More critically, Bone Dry's owner response rate of 0.06 is catastrophically low for a company of this size.

22 BBB complaints in three years

, combined with recent complaints about billing for work not done, signal that at scale, accountability slips. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is a direct knife into that crack.

Most exploitable weakness: Bone Dry's review velocity has collapsed — only 2 reviews in 30 days and 6 in 90 days on their Indianapolis GBP despite being a $35–45M local operation. That stall tells a story of operational complacency and/or review funnel neglect that Raptor should weaponize in sales conversations and paid messaging.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GuildQuality integration as a trust layer.

Bone Dry surveys customers shortly after project completion across hundreds of cities and publishes that feedback publicly.

Raptor should add a third-party post-job survey platform (GuildQuality, Guild, or Eliant) to create an independent verification layer that AI engines and skeptical homeowners can reference — this directly feeds AEO visibility.

  1. Bone Dry University as a brand moat + recruiting PR.

Bone Dry University was created in 2021 as an in-house training academy, conducting Production, Customer Service, Sales, Leadership, and Communication academies with 8,734+ hours of live training.

Cameron should be developing a Raptor-branded onboarding/training narrative — even a lightweight version — that can be PR'd to local business journals. It signals permanence, professionalism, and scale that smaller competitors can't match.

  1. Insurance claims specialist as a dedicated role.

Bone Dry is actively running two separate Insurance Specialist roles to help clients file claims for roof damage.

If Raptor isn't already treating storm/insurance-assisted jobs as a structured division with dedicated follow-up, Bone Dry will continue to win those tickets. This is a sales ops play as much as a marketing one — the homeowner who thinks "someone will handle my claim" chooses the company that explicitly promises it.
