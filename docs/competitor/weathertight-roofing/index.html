Weathertight Roofing — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Updated August 13, 2026

1. Who They Are

Weathertight Roofing LLC has served central Indiana since 2012 and operates as a family-owned business with a stated emphasis on exceptional customer service and premium product delivery.

The company claims three locations, covering Fishers, Monticello, and Muncie.

Their public-facing identity is built around GAF Master Elite status and the slogan "Roof With The Best Or Leak Like The Rest,"

leaning into certification-as-differentiator rather than any distinct operational or warranty story.

2. Estimated Scale

BuildZoom lists three named individuals associated with the business — Alex Grim, Ashley Leitherer, and Corey Cates

— suggesting a lean core team. Confidence: low on headcount; no LinkedIn company page with employee count was surfaced.

BuildZoom records show 28 building permits totaling $535,800 in permitted project value,

which is a floor, not a ceiling — most residential reroofs don't trigger permits. Estimated annual revenue is likely $2M–$4M based on permit cadence and a three-market footprint. Confidence: low.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

A Facebook post from Weathertight Roofing LLC advertised an open Production Supervisor role based in the Fishers/Indianapolis area,

which is the single most meaningful signal in the last 30 days. This is an ops-layer hire — Production Supervisor typically means they are trying to systematize crew management and reduce owner dependency on day-of job oversight. No marketing, sales leadership, or estimator postings were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter under the Weathertight brand in the last 30 days. The Production Supervisor hire is worth watching: it's the move a company makes just before it tries to scale volume.

4. Off-Google Presence

Weathertight Roofing LLC earned BBB Accreditation on July 12, 2024, holds an A+ rating, and lists Corey Cates as owner.

Clean record, no complaints surfaced.

Their Facebook page carries only 12 reviews at 100% recommend

— a paper-thin social proof footprint relative to their 309 Google reviews, indicating they funnel nearly all review requests to Google and ignore Facebook as a conversion asset. Yelp presence exists across two separate listings (listing 1, listing 2) but neither shows meaningful review volume — a classic sign of deliberate Google-only funneling. No Facebook Ad Library ads were surfaced in search results, suggesting limited or no paid social activity.

Manta lists them with zero reviews.

The split Yelp listing is a minor but exploitable GBP hygiene gap — duplicate profiles dilute any organic Yelp equity they'd otherwise build.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Weathertight uses HOVER software for measurement and estimate visualization, including an interactive 3D model feature,

which they feature on their homepage — a legitimate conversion tool that doubles as a content differentiator. Their site (weathertightroofingllc.com) has city-specific subpages (Noblesville, Muncie, Lawrence) suggesting some local SEO intent, but the page titles are generic ("Trusted Roofing Companies in Noblesville, IN") with no evidence of schema markup, blog content, or backlinks from local press in any results returned.

Their BuildZoom score of 106 ranks in the top 7% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors,

which provides some third-party citation authority but is not a substitute for owned content. No blog, no FAQ schema, no visible AEO-optimized content structure. Their domain authority signal is weak for a 12-year-old company — Cameron's team should be able to outrank them on any non-branded Fishers/Carmel/Noblesville query with a focused content push.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No search results returned Weathertight Roofing as a cited or named result in any AI-generated answer for "best roofers Indianapolis," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." Their absence from generative engine outputs is consistent with their thin backlink profile, lack of structured content, and no visible coverage from local press or home-services aggregators beyond basic directory listings. This is a wide-open lane: any competitor that invests in AEO-formatted content (FAQ pages, structured comparison content, local schema) will take the AI citation share Weathertight has left on the table.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: GAF Master Elite certification is featured prominently and is a legitimate third-party credential that resonates with insurance-claim customers. HOVER's 3D visualization tool is a sales-floor differentiator Raptor does not visibly advertise. Three-market footprint (Fishers, Monticello, Muncie) gives them geographic surface area.

Where Raptor beats them: 699 reviews vs. 309 — more than double the social proof volume, both at 5.0. Raptor's review velocity (and owner response discipline) is institutionally stronger; Weathertight's 1.0 owner response rate and 3 reviews in 90 days signals a review program that has stalled, not scaled. Raptor's premium positioning is intentional; Weathertight's "$$ pricing" label on Facebook and "affordable pricing" language on their contact page signals a middle-market value play that cannot command the same ticket size.

Most exploitable weakness: Their review engine has essentially stopped — 3 reviews in 90 days on a 309-review profile is stagnation, and Cameron should be closing that gap publicly at every customer touchpoint.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. HOVER 3D visualization in the sales process.

HOVER allows accurate roof measurement and lets customers visualize renovations on an interactive 3D model.

If Raptor is not already using a comparable tool in proposals, this is a closing-rate gap worth closing. Customers who can see the finished product say yes faster and at higher ticket values.

  1. GAF Master Elite as a content pillar.

Weathertight's GAF Master Elite certification is prominently surfaced across third-party directories.

If Raptor holds or can pursue this certification, it should be on every landing page, in every LSA profile, and in every AI-optimized FAQ answer — not buried. It is one of the few contractor credentials that generative engines are trained to recognize as a quality signal.

  1. Production Supervisor hire as a growth tell. The Facebook job post for a Production Supervisor suggests Weathertight is attempting to decentralize job-site oversight so Corey Cates is no longer the single point of quality control. If they successfully systematize that layer, volume and speed-to-install improve. Cameron should monitor whether their review velocity picks back up in Q4 2026 — that's the leading indicator that the ops hire is working and they're scaling into Raptor's zip codes.
