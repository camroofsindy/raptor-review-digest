Competitive Intelligence Brief: Weathertight Roofing LLC

Fishers, IN | 8945 South St | 317-983-7663 | weathertightroofingllc.com
Refreshed: Tuesday, May 20, 2025

1. Who They Are

Owner Corey Cates has been in roofing since 2006, cutting his teeth on post-Katrina disaster recovery in Louisiana before graduating from Indiana University in 2012 and founding Weathertight Roofing that same May.

The company has grown from a local family-owned operation and now claims a multi-location footprint spanning Fishers, Monticello, and Muncie.

Co-principal Alex Grim brings nearly a decade of background as an insurance adjuster, positioning the company heavily around the storm restoration and insurance claims workflow.

2. Estimated Scale

BuildZoom lists three named employees on record — Alex Grim, Ashley Leitherer, and Corey Cates

— suggesting a lean core office team with field crews likely sub-contracted or unlisted. Confidence: low (no LinkedIn headcount available; Corey Cates' LinkedIn profile shows only 55 connections, indicating minimal platform investment).

BuildZoom records 28 building permits totaling $535,800 in declared value.

Typical permit size runs approximately $24,950 per job.

At that average across a reasonable job-to-permit ratio, annual revenue likely sits in the $2M–$4M range — consistent with a high-touch, owner-involved operation rather than a volume shop. Confidence: low.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

LinkedIn surfaces one Weathertight Roofing posting in the last 30 days: an Accounts Payable Specialist role based in Fishers, listed approximately one month ago.

No sales, marketing, or ops leadership roles were found on LinkedIn, Indeed, or ZipRecruiter in the current search window. The AP hire is a back-office signal — either they are formalizing finance controls as revenue grows or cleaning up books in anticipation of something (a credit line, acquisition interest, or franchise play). Worth watching for a sales manager or project manager post next.

No evidence of an active blog, press mentions, or local backlinks surfaced.

Their BuildZoom score of 106 places them in the top 7% of Indiana contractors

Domain authority is almost certainly sub-30 based on the thin content footprint. The multi-location URL structure is uncoordinated — it will not consolidate authority effectively without internal linking discipline.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Insurance claims expertise is baked into the identity —

Weathertight claims 20+ years of experience navigating insurance claims and positions the adjuster background as a consumer-facing differentiator.

For storm-hit homeowners who want a single point of contact through the claims process, this is a credible hook. Where Raptor beats them: Review velocity is the most visible gap — 673 reviews vs. 291, and Weathertight has generated zero new Google reviews in the last 90 days, signaling a complete breakdown in their post-job review-request system. Raptor also has a coherent premium brand identity that Weathertight lacks. Most exploitable weakness: Their 90-day review blackout and zero owner response rate on Google mean their social proof is stale and their GBP is algorithmically deprioritized — this is a direct ranking vulnerability Cameron can widen with consistent review cadence and owner responses on Raptor's profile.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Insurance adjuster co-branding: Weathertight's Alex Grim carries a decade of adjuster experience and they make it front-facing in their pitch.

Clients receive advocacy through the claims process specifically because of that background.

Raptor should evaluate whether there is a similar credential or partnership worth making explicit in sales collateral — or use the contrast ("we don't just file your claim, we own the outcome") as a counter-positioning point in paid ads.

  1. HOVER for sales conversion, not just quoting:

HOVER's 3D interactive model lets customers visualize renovations and see different products and colors before committing.

Weathertight deploys this as a close tool. If Raptor isn't using a visual estimating layer in the sales process, this is a documented conversion driver worth adopting or matching with a comparable tool (Hover, RoofSnap, or EagleView Assess).

  1. Multi-city page architecture with better execution: Weathertight is attempting geo-targeted service pages for Fishers, Muncie, Monticello, Lawrence, and Noblesville. The strategy is correct; the execution is thin. Raptor should build out superior suburb-specific landing pages — Carmel, Zionsville, Noblesville, Greenwood — with actual local content (permit data, neighborhood references, real project photos), structured FAQ schema, and internal linking. Done right, this captures both the organic long-tail and the AEO citation pool that Weathertight is leaving completely uncontested.
