Weathertight Roofing — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Updated: July 23, 2025 | Cameron Blakely, CEO

1. Who They Are

Owner Corey Cates has been in roofing since 2006, originally logging time on post-Katrina repair work in Louisiana. He graduated from Indiana University with a Biology degree in 2012 and founded Weathertight Roofing that same year.

The company has grown from a local family-owned operation into a central Indiana operation serving Fishers, Monticello, and Muncie.

Their differentiator claim is GAF Master Elite certification, anchored by the slogan "Roof With The Best Or Leak Like The Rest."

2. Estimated Scale

BuildZoom lists named employees Alex Grim, Ashley Leitherer, and Corey Cates on file with the Indianapolis license board

— suggesting a lean operation. ZoomInfo data points to a similarly small headcount, though that record appears to conflate a Tennessee-address entity. Confidence: low on employee count; the named-roster data from BuildZoom suggests 5–12 total including field crews.

BuildZoom records show 28 building permits totaling $535,800 in declared permit value.

Typical permit size runs approximately $24,950 per project.

Applying a standard residential roofing revenue-to-permit-value multiplier, rough annual revenue likely falls in the $1M–$2.5M range. Confidence: low — permit data notoriously undercounts storm work billed outside permit thresholds.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Weathertight posted a Production Supervisor role for the Fishers/Indianapolis market on Facebook

— the most concrete hiring signal surfaced. No active postings were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter specifically attributed to Weathertight Roofing LLC within the last 30 days. The Production Supervisor post is an ops-side signal worth monitoring: if they're adding install management capacity, volume is likely growing or crew retention is a problem. Either is worth tracking.

4. Off-Google Presence

Facebook page shows only 12 reviews with a "100% recommend" badge

— a trivially small sample that carries no real social proof weight. No active Facebook Ad Library spend was detectable from public search results.

BBB profile carries an A+ rating; they became BBB Accredited as recently as July 12, 2024

— late to that credential for a 13-year-old company, suggesting they recently shored up trust signals.

Yelp shows a live listing at yelp.com/biz/weathertight-roofing-fishers-2 but no visible star rating or substantive review count surfaced in search results — consistent with a near-zero Yelp presence. No Nextdoor business page activity was detected. The pattern: 300 Google reviews, 12 Facebook reviews, negligible Yelp — textbook review-funneling entirely to Google GBP. That works until it doesn't, and it means their off-Google trust footprint is paper thin.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Expertise.com lists Weathertight as a top Fishers roofer, noting they use a dump trailer for debris protection and deploy the HOVER mobile app to generate 3D roof models from photos

— that's a sales tool with a content angle they are not visibly exploiting in blog form. Their website at weathertightroofingllc.com has service pages structured around city-name URLs (Lawrence, Noblesville, Muncie, Fishers), a reasonable local SEO approach, but the page title strategy is inconsistent — the "About Us" page targets "Muncie, IN" in its title tag while the contact page targets "Lawrence, IN," signaling either a thin-site content farm build or poor ongoing optimization oversight. No press backlinks or local media coverage found. Schema markup and blog content presence not confirmed. Domain authority signal is weak relative to larger regional competitors.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No evidence surfaced of Weathertight Roofing being named in AI-generated answers for queries including "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." Their city-page SEO structure is not generating the authoritative, cited content (local guides, storm damage explainers, material comparisons) that large language models pull from when constructing recommendations. Their GBP review volume (300) is below the threshold that currently gets contractors surfaced in Gemini and ChatGPT's local answer stacks. This is a gap — and a Raptor opportunity. At 692 reviews, Raptor already has the volume signal; pairing that with AEO-optimized FAQ content would widen the lead materially.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: GAF Master Elite certification is a credentialed differentiator they surface in all brand touchpoints; if Raptor does not hold that designation, it's a procurement objection in insurance-adjuster-driven jobs. Their insurance claims specialization messaging —

"over 20 years of experience dealing with insurance claims" and explicit positioning around honesty and communication through the process

— is tightly packaged and easy for storm-damaged homeowners to latch onto.

Where Raptor beats them: 692 reviews vs. 300 is a 2.3x volume advantage, and Raptor's zero-response-rate opponent (Corey does not appear to respond to reviews publicly) versus any active owner response program Raptor runs is a visible trust gap. Raptor's premium accountability positioning is more defensible in a high-ticket sales conversation.

Most exploitable weakness: Their review velocity has gone completely flat — zero new Google reviews in 90 days on a 300-review profile signals either a broken solicitation process or an owner-capacity bottleneck, and any homeowner doing due diligence in Q2–Q3 2025 is looking at a stale social proof record.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Insurance-first intake framing. Weathertight leads with insurance claim expertise across their homepage, service pages, and verbal brand. Cameron should audit whether Raptor's current landing pages and paid search ad copy explicitly intercept the "storm damage + insurance claim" search intent — that's the highest-urgency, highest-ticket inbound segment and Weathertight has staked ground there.

  2. HOVER 3D visualization in the sales process.

Weathertight uses the HOVER mobile app to measure roofs and transform building photos into 3D models

as part of their estimate experience. If Raptor isn't already using a visual estimation tool in the sales appointment, this is a closing-rate lever worth piloting — it justifies premium pricing by making the scope tangible before a shingle is touched.

  1. Multi-city URL architecture. Their city-specific service pages (even if inconsistently executed) are indexed and generating third-party directory placements. Raptor should ensure every primary suburb it serves — Zionsville, Noblesville, Carmel, Greenwood, Westfield — has a dedicated, substantive service page built for both traditional SEO and AI answer retrieval, not just a GBP pin.
