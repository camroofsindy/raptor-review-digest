RAPTOR ROOFING — COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE BRIEF
Competitor: Weathertight Roofing LLC
Updated: April 26, 2026 | Next refresh: April 29, 2026

1. Who They Are

Owner Corey Cates has been in roofing since 2006, originally picking up the trade on a Hurricane Katrina mission trip in Louisiana. He graduated with a Biology degree from Indiana University and founded Weathertight Roofing in May 2012.

Since inception, the company has grown from a local family-owned operation into what it claims is central Indiana's roofing leader, providing service across Fishers, Monticello, and Muncie.

Their core differentiator is GAF Master Elite certification, marketed under the slogan "Roof With The Best Or Leak Like The Rest."

2. Estimated Scale

BuildZoom lists three named individuals at the company — Alex Grim, Ashley Leitherer, and Corey Cates — and notes they hold a General Contractor license plus 6 other licenses through the Indianapolis license board.

Confidence: low on headcount (no LinkedIn company page surfaced for the Fishers LLC; the three names likely represent the core office/sales layer with crews subcontracted).

BuildZoom records 28 permitted projects totaling $535,800 in declared permit value.

The typical permitted job value runs approximately $24,950.

Rough revenue estimate: with sub-permit-threshold residential jobs factored in and a three-location footprint, $2M–$4M ARR is a reasonable range. Confidence: low. They are almost certainly running leaner than their "three locations" marketing implies.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: GAF Master Elite certification is a credentialed differentiator they actively market, and their HOVER-based 3D visualization is a sales tool Raptor should evaluate. Their multi-city location page structure gives them modest geographic spread in organic search.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor carries 664 reviews vs. Weathertight's 290 — more than double the social proof volume at the same 5.0 rating. Raptor's review velocity (data-tracked weekly) is a compounding moat. Weathertight's 30-day review pace of 8 reviews is anemic and suggests either a narrowing project pipeline or a broken review-ask process. Their Facebook page at under 1,000 likes after 13 years signals no real social investment.

Most exploitable weakness: Their entire reputation infrastructure sits on a single platform (Google), with zero review depth anywhere else — one crisis-level Google review could crater their LSA ranking overnight.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. HOVER 3D Visualization in the Sales Process.

Weathertight promotes HOVER as a tool to accurately measure roofs and let customers visualize their home on an interactive 3D model with different products and colors, which they claim removes homeowner hesitation around the project.

If Raptor is not using HOVER or a comparable tool (e.g., EagleView, RoofSnap), this is a close-rate lever worth evaluating — especially on premium replacement jobs.

  1. Insurance Claim Positioning as a Marketing Pillar.

Weathertight claims over 20 years of experience handling insurance claims and specifically markets their ability to guide clients through the process with honesty and communication.

This is a high-intent audience segment (storm damage homeowners) that Raptor could own more explicitly with dedicated landing pages and targeted paid search.

  1. Multi-Location Page Architecture. Their practice of building out geo-targeted service pages for Noblesville, Lawrence, Muncie, and Fishers is worth auditing against Raptor's own site structure. Even thin location pages create organic footprint; Raptor should ensure every suburb in Hamilton, Boone, and Hendricks County has a properly built, content-rich page — not just a duplicated template.
