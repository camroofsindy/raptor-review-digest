Competitive Intelligence Brief: Richmond Exteriors

Refreshed: Tuesday, May 19, 2026 | Compiled for Cameron Blakely, Raptor Roofing

1. Who They Are

Richmond Exteriors is a second-generation, family-owned and -operated exterior remodeling company serving the Greater Indianapolis area since 1987.

The BBB lists Jerrad Richmond as Sales & Marketing Manager, indicating the family name is still actively embedded in day-to-day operations — BBB-accredited since September 19, 1994.

Their differentiator is breadth, not depth:

services span roofing (flat and sloped), siding, windows, doors, awnings, guttering, fencing, decking, and railing

— making them a general exteriors shop rather than a roofing-first brand.

2. Estimated Scale

Richmond Guttering/Exteriors received an SBA PPP loan of $686,499 from Centier Bank, resulting in the retention of 65 jobs

— which is the most reliable headcount anchor available. Confidence: medium (PPP data is circa 2020-21; current headcount may have shifted).

Their LinkedIn company page has 176 followers

, indicating minimal investment in employer brand and a relatively small professional footprint online. At 65 retained employees and a multi-trade model, estimated annual revenue is likely in the $8M–$14M range. Confidence: low — no current ZoomInfo revenue figure is publicly surfaced without a paid pull.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Indeed lists Richmond Exteriors' Indianapolis office with a 3.3 out of 5 employee rating, with workers fielding questions about hiring process, promotions, and working environment

— but no active open roles are publicly visible on Indeed or LinkedIn as of this refresh.

LinkedIn shows a General Manager role within their Commercial Division (Steve Walker, Install Manager)

, but no new postings in the last 30 days are surfaced. No marketing, sales leadership, or ops leadership hiring signals detected. This is a quiet hiring posture — either they're fully staffed, running lean, or hiring off-market.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform review picture reveals a meaningful gap. Their Google profile carries 610 reviews at 4.5 stars.

On Yelp, they have only 23 reviews

— classic review-funneling behavior where happy customers get directed to Google while Yelp collects the organic, often dissatisfied, overflow.

Facebook shows 47 reviews at a 92% recommendation rate

, which is a softer signal but consistent with an older customer base that uses Facebook.

BBB rates them A+ and they have been accredited since 1994

— a genuine longevity asset. However, the BBB complaint file is not clean:

at least one customer stated they took legal action against Richmond and received a $65,000 settlement for damages caused.

Angi rates them 4.2 out of 5

, and

they do not offer emergency services according to Angi

— a notable gap for storm-season lead capture. The Yelp-to-Google ratio (23 vs. 610 reviews) is the clearest funneling signal in the market.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Richmond operates under the domain richmondguttering.com — a legacy domain name rooted in gutters, not roofing, which is a structural SEO liability for anyone targeting "roof replacement Indianapolis" queries at the top of the funnel. Their site runs on WordPress (

confirmed via ZoomInfo technology stack detection, which also lists Facebook Pixel and Facebook Web Custom Audiences as active tools

). Their roofing service page (richmondguttering.com/roofing/) exists but is thin — it reads as a feature list with minimal long-form content or FAQ schema. No evidence of a structured blog publishing cadence targeting Indy suburb keywords (Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville). The domain's age (1987-era brand) likely gives it some authority, but the mismatch between domain name and primary service intent is an exploitable SEO gap for Raptor.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on analysis of current AI answer patterns for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," and "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville": Richmond Exteriors is not consistently surfaced as a named citation in generative engine responses (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude). Their domain-name mismatch (richmondguttering.com for a roofing query) likely suppresses LLM confidence in categorizing them as a roofing specialist. They have volume — 610 Google reviews — but the 4.5 star average, the BBB complaint history, and the generalist brand identity dilute the trust signals that LLMs weight when constructing recommendation lists. This is a live opportunity: Raptor's tighter roofing-specific positioning and 5.0 rating are exactly the signals that AEO-optimized content can leverage to own named citations in AI answers.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Brand longevity (38 years) and service breadth give them cross-sell leverage — a customer who gets a roof can become a fence and gutter customer, which Raptor doesn't capture. Their BBB accreditation since 1994 carries weight with older homeowner segments.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor holds 673 reviews at a perfect 5.0 versus Richmond's 610 at 4.5 — a meaningful quality gap, not just a rounding difference. Raptor's review velocity (14 in 30 days) is competitive with Richmond's identical 14-in-30 pace, but Raptor's zero-defect rating compounds over time. Richmond's 85% owner response rate is decent but not exceptional, and their BBB complaint log including a $65K legal settlement is permanently indexed, searchable, and citable.

Most exploitable weakness: Richmond's legacy gutter-branded domain actively undermines their roofing SEO, giving Raptor a clean lane to own suburb-level roofing queries they cannot easily compete for without a domain restructure.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Financing as a front-door offer.

Richmond promotes $89/month payments or 12 months no payments, no interest as a lead-gen hook.

This is on their homepage and roofing pages. If Raptor has financing available, it should be positioned just as prominently — not buried in the proposal stage. Loss of deals to financing competitors happens before the estimate call.

  1. BBB accreditation as a trust badge in paid media. Richmond's A+ BBB status since 1994 is actively referenced across their profiles. Raptor should audit whether its own trust badges (manufacturer certifications, any accreditations) are being deployed in Google Ads copy and landing pages — especially for queries where Richmond is a direct competitor in the ad auction.

  2. Commercial division as a second revenue floor.

Richmond has a dedicated General Manager for their Commercial Division

— a revenue stream Raptor should evaluate. Storm-season commercial work (apartment complexes, HOAs, light commercial) often runs at better margins than one-off residential and creates annuity-style relationships that residential-only competitors can't match.
