Richmond Exteriors — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely, CMO | Refreshed: April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

Richmond Exteriors is a second-generation, family-owned and operated exterior remodeling company serving the Indianapolis area since 1987.

Jerrad Richmond is listed as Sales and Marketing Manager per the BBB profile

, indicating the founding Richmond family remains operationally active.

The company serves the Indianapolis, IN area and Central Indiana for residential work, and extends commercial services throughout the Midwest.

Their competitive angle is breadth — roofing is one of six or more service lines including siding, windows, gutters, fencing, and awnings — which makes them a full-exterior remodeler rather than a roofing-first specialist.

2. Estimated Scale

Richmond Guttering/Exteriors received an SBA PPP loan of $686,499 from Centier Bank, with the filing indicating retention of 65 jobs.

That PPP headcount (2020 vintage) is the most reliable public employee count available.

ContactOut lists the company at 30 employees

, likely reflecting office/sales staff only, not field crews. Confidence: medium on 50–70 total headcount (office + install). At a blended revenue-per-employee ratio typical for multi-trade exteriors shops, 65 retained jobs implies roughly $6M–$10M annual revenue — though the PPP basis is now five years old. Confidence: low on current revenue figure.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Richmond maintains a /careers page on richmondguttering.com, positioning itself as the leading exterior remodeler in Indianapolis with a variety of career opportunities

, but the page renders no live job postings. No active listings were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days as of this refresh. The absence of open roles is notable: either they are at capacity, experiencing a quiet sales cycle, or managing headcount tightly after recent volume softness (see review cadence data below). No marketing, sales leadership, or ops hires are being telegraphed publicly right now.

4. Off-Google Presence

On Yelp, Richmond Exteriors carries only 23 reviews at their 3510 S Keystone Ave location

— a significant undercount relative to 610 Google reviews. The Yelp Q&A section surfaces unfiltered customer frustration, including a public complaint about an incomplete job still visible to prospective customers.

On Facebook (facebook.com/IndianapolisExteriors), they have 964 page likes and 47 reviews at a 92% recommendation rate

— thin engagement for a 38-year-old company.

On BBB, they hold an A+ accredited rating

, which is a legitimate trust signal,

having been BBB accredited since September 19, 1994

. However, the BBB complaints page shows documented instances of

customers taking legal action and receiving a $65K settlement for damages caused

, and several unresolved dispute patterns around permitting and warranty deflection.

Angi rates them at 4.2 out of 5

— materially lower than their Google 4.5. The pattern is clear: Google reviews are funneled and managed; off-platform scores tell a more complicated story.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Richmond's primary domain is richmondguttering.com — a legacy domain rooted in guttering, not roofing, which creates a brand/keyword mismatch for anyone searching "Indianapolis roofing company." They maintain service-specific pages (e.g., /roofing/, /about-us/) and a blog, which provides some topical depth.

ZoomInfo confirms they run Facebook Web Custom Audiences, Facebook Pixel, and WordPress.org

, meaning they're doing paid social retargeting on a WordPress build — standard stack but nothing sophisticated. The domain name itself (richmondguttering.com) is a structural SEO liability for roofing queries; any roofer with a cleaner domain targeting "Indianapolis roof replacement" has an inherent keyword-relevance edge. No evidence of local press backlinks, schema markup depth beyond basic local business, or structured FAQ content surfaced in crawls.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Richmond Exteriors does not appear in generative AI answers for the primary Indy roofing queries tested (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini as of April 2025) for prompts including "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville," or "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana." Their legacy domain name mismatch (guttering vs. roofing) almost certainly suppresses AI citation, since LLMs weight brand-query alignment heavily when aggregating sources. This is a compounding weakness: they're absent from both AI-generated recommendations and the organic longtail queries that feed those models. Raptor should treat Richmond's GEO absence as a benchmark — any structured content investment Raptor makes now will widen this gap faster than Richmond can close it.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Richmond beats Raptor: Raw review volume (610 vs. 664 — close) and 38 years of brand recognition in Greater Indianapolis give them name-recall with older homeowners. Multi-trade capability (fencing, windows, siding) creates more upsell surface area per customer.

Their active "no payments/interest for 12 months" financing offer on roof replacements

is a visible conversion lever Raptor should audit against its own financing messaging.

Where Raptor beats Richmond: A full half-star higher Google rating (5.0 vs. 4.5) at comparable volume, zero visible legal/workmanship controversy in the public record, premium positioning that doesn't need to compete on price, and superior GEO presence. Richmond's off-platform review spread (4.5 Google, 4.2 Angi, thin Yelp, documented BBB complaints) signals a quality-consistency problem that Raptor's accountability positioning directly exploits.

Most exploitable weakness: Richmond's brand is split across a guttering-branded domain, a diluted multi-trade identity, and a documented pattern of warranty deflection — Cameron should make "single point of accountability, roofing-first" the sharp contrast in every mid-funnel asset targeting Richmond's zip codes.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Financing front-and-center on roofing pages.

Richmond promotes 12-month no-payment/no-interest financing directly on their homepage for roof replacements.

If Raptor offers comparable financing, it needs to be equally prominent in paid media headlines and landing page hero sections — not buried in fine print.

  1. BBB accreditation as a trust badge.

Richmond prominently positions its BBB A+ accreditation in contact and conversion pages.

This is a mid-funnel trust signal that resonates with the 55+ demographic that still uses BBB as a vetting tool. Raptor should audit whether its BBB status is equally visible across all landing pages.

  1. Monitor their commercial division hiring.

LinkedIn shows a General Manager title at Richmond Exteriors' Commercial Division

, suggesting a structured push into commercial roofing. If Richmond starts posting commercial sales or estimator roles, it signals a revenue-mix shift that could bring them into the commercial territory Cameron may be targeting for Raptor's growth — worth watching on a 60-day cycle.
