Richmond Exteriors — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Updated: August 11, 2026

1. Who They Are

Richmond Exteriors has maintained a reputation as the Greater Indianapolis area's leading exterior remodeling company since 1987.

It is a second-generation, family-owned and operated company

with

Jerrad Richmond listed as Sales & Marketing Manager on the BBB profile

, indicating the family name is tied directly to leadership.

The company has expanded to serve both residential Metro Indianapolis/Central Indiana clients and commercial clientele throughout the Midwest

, with roofing as one of several verticals alongside gutters, siding, windows, fencing, and decking.

2. Estimated Scale

RocketReach estimates Richmond Exteriors at approximately 39 employees and $25.2 million in annual revenue.

Confidence: medium. This aligns roughly with

a disclosed SBA PPP loan of $686,499 from Centier Bank that retained 65 jobs

— suggesting the workforce was larger during peak COVID periods and may have contracted since.

LinkedIn shows only 176 followers

, which signals low investment in organic brand-building on that platform. The $25M figure from RocketReach is a third-party estimate; treat it as a ceiling, not a floor given the flattening review velocity discussed below.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active job postings for Richmond Exteriors were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter within the last 30 days.

LinkedIn does show a General Manager of the Commercial Division role filled by Steve Walker

, and

a Construction Manager role held by Mark Brumback

— both appear to be existing headcount, not new hires. The absence of current postings suggests either a hiring freeze, heavy reliance on subcontractors, or a company that recruits through word-of-mouth rather than public boards. No marketing, sales leadership, or ops hires are visible in the last 30 days. Flag this space for re-check on the next cycle.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform review picture is damaging relative to their 4.5 Google score.

On Yelp, the roofing listing shows only 23 reviews

— a classic sign of Google review funneling where satisfied customers are steered to one platform while complaints surface elsewhere.

Angi currently rates them at 2.9 out of 5

, with at least one documented customer stating

they hired Richmond for James Hardie siding and had to take legal action, resulting in Richmond paying $65K in damages.

The BBB carries an A+ accredited rating

, which they lean on publicly, but

the BBB complaint log includes instances where the business either failed to make a good-faith resolution effort or failed to respond to a dispute at all.

Facebook shows 968 page likes with only 6 people currently talking about the business

— a dead page by any engagement standard. No evidence of active Facebook Ad Library campaigns was found.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Richmond operates two web properties — richmondguttering.com and the older richmondexteriors.com — which splits domain authority and dilutes link equity. The primary domain (richmondguttering.com) has localized service-area pages and dedicated roofing subpages with basic keyword targeting.

Their tech stack runs on WordPress with Cloudflare Rocket Loader and ClickCease

, indicating they are running paid search and paying to filter click fraud — they are active in PPC. Blog content exists but is thin and not updated at any aggressive cadence. No evidence of structured schema markup for FAQs, reviews, or local business beyond standard GBP signals. The dual-domain problem is the biggest exploitable SEO gap — their authority is fragmented.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on available data and known AI citation patterns, Richmond Exteriors is unlikely to surface prominently in generative engine results for high-intent queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." AI engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) predominantly surface companies with: (1) consistent high-volume reviews across multiple platforms, (2) authoritative content tied to a single strong domain, and (3) press/directory mentions from credible local sources. Richmond's split domain structure, 2.9 Angi rating, and near-silent blog disqualify them from the citation tier that generative engines draw from. Their GBP review count of 627 at 4.5 gives them modest Google prominence but not the cross-platform trust signals AI models weight. This is an area where Raptor's 697 reviews at 5.0, if paired with an AEO content strategy on a single authoritative domain, wins the AI answer box cleanly.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Richmond beats Raptor: 39-year operating history gives them inherited name recognition in south Indianapolis and Beech Grove zip codes, and their multi-vertical model (fencing, windows, siding) means they cross-sell into jobs Raptor doesn't compete for, keeping their pipeline diversified. Their BBB accreditation since 1994 functions as trust shorthand with older demographics.

Where Raptor beats Richmond: 697 reviews at 5.0 vs. 627 at 4.5 is a visible, meaningful gap — Raptor has more reviews AND a higher rating, which is rare. Raptor's owner response rate, while not published here, is almost certainly higher than Richmond's disclosed 0.4 (meaning Richmond responds to fewer than 1 in 2.5 reviews). Raptor's single-brand, roofing-focused identity makes it far more citable by AI engines. The accountability-first positioning directly counters Richmond's documented complaint pattern around unresponsive management.

Most exploitable weakness: Richmond's Angi score of 2.9 and documented $65K legal settlement are publicly indexed — a targeted campaign around "accountability" and verified cross-platform ratings converts their reputation gap into Raptor's pipeline.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Indianapolis Home Show presence.

Richmond actively exhibits at the Indianapolis Home Show at the Indiana State Fairgrounds

— this is a high-intent, in-person lead channel. Cameron should evaluate whether Raptor has a presence there and, if not, price out booth placement for the January show as a brand authority move that Richmond currently owns.

  1. Financing promotion as a top-of-funnel hook.

Richmond prominently advertises no payments/no interest for 12 months on roof replacement

as a headline offer. If Raptor has financing available, it should be front-loaded in ad creative and landing pages the same way — not buried in the fine print.

  1. Monitor their GBP review velocity. Richmond pulled only 9 reviews in 90 days against a 627-review base — that is a stalled acquisition engine. If that pace holds through Q4, it is a signal either of volume decline or a review platform algorithm issue. Raptor should track this monthly; a competitor whose social proof is stagnating while yours grows is a compounding advantage worth accelerating into.
