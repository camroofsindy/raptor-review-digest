AmeriPro Roofing — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely, CMO
Refreshed: July 14, 2025

1. Who They Are

AmeriPro is a family-owned business founded in 2000, specializing in storm and natural disaster restoration of roofs, siding, and gutters, having expanded to over 20 locations and earning the GAF President Club Award.

Formerly operating as American Dream Home Improvement, the company's core focus is exterior home repair and storm restoration.

They operate as a national roofing contractor across 40 markets in 19 states, with the majority of their revenue driven by thunderstorm insurance claim events — not retail sales.

2. Estimated Scale

Growjo estimates AmeriPro at approximately 375 employees and $135.9M in annual revenue nationally.

Confidence: medium — third-party estimate with no audited financials;

Prospeo pegs revenue lower at $87.5M with an estimated valuation of $280M.

The variance between sources suggests the national figure is likely somewhere in the $90–135M range. For the Carmel/Indianapolis branch specifically, no standalone revenue is published; treat the branch as one of ~21 offices, suggesting a rough per-market contribution of $4–6M annually. Confidence: low.

Growjo also notes the company grew their employee count 38% last year

— a meaningful flag for a competitor actively scaling.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Heavy sales rep recruitment is the dominant signal.

ZipRecruiter shows 8 open Outside Sales Representative positions specifically in Indiana.

Careerjet confirms AmeriPro is immediately hiring Outside Sales Representatives in the Indianapolis and Bloomington areas.

Indeed lists 141 total open AmeriPro jobs nationally

, with no marketing, ops leadership, or digital roles visible for the Indianapolis market specifically.

Job descriptions indicate reps are expected to prospect door-to-door, perform inspections, and operate out of ServiceTitan CRM for pricing and scheduling.

No CMO, marketing manager, or digital roles surfaced for this branch — their growth play is field sales headcount, not marketing infrastructure.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform review picture is ugly and exploitable. Their Google profile shows 258 reviews at 4.5 stars with zero reviews in the last 90 days and no owner response rate — a dead profile by any active management standard.

Angi rates AmeriPro at 3.0 out of 5

, with at least one reviewer calling it "one of the worst dealings I have ever had with any contractor."

Yelp lists only 15 reviews

for the Carmel location, suggesting minimal organic Yelp activity.

Trustindex surfaces a reported pattern of aggressive unsolicited phone calls — a reviewer described repeated calls where the rep "would not let my wife get a word in" and called back after being hung up on.

A Yahoo Local review accuses the company of misappropriating insurance claim funds and damaging a roof.

The BBB profile notes an

A- rating with a government action on file against the business.

No Facebook Ad Library activity was confirmed in this search cycle.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

AmeriPro has dedicated location pages (e.g., ameriproroofing.com/locations/carmel-in/) with keyword-dense copy covering storm damage, hail, ice dams, and HOA compliance — standard franchise SEO playbook.

Their tech stack includes Facebook Pixel, Facebook Web Custom Audiences, and WooCommerce

, indicating at least some paid social retargeting infrastructure. However, the location page content reads as templated and interchangeable with other franchise markets — thin on genuine local signals like named neighborhoods, permit references, or Indiana-specific weather events.

Their Carmel page claims to be "the only Indianapolis roofers who offer customers 100 percent peace of mind"

— bold language that is unsubstantiated and increasingly distrusted by consumers doing pre-purchase research. No evidence of a blog, earned backlinks from local Indianapolis press, or active schema markup was surfaced.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

AmeriPro carries national brand weight on paper —

their own job postings claim they are the "#1 residential roofing contractor in the country by volume."

However, their local Indianapolis-market AI visibility is structurally weak. Their location page is templated with no original content that an LLM can extract as a differentiating local authority signal. No earned local press coverage, no blog, and a dead Google review profile (zero reviews in 90 days) all suppress their likelihood of being cited in generative results for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis" or "roof replacement Carmel Indiana." In testing equivalent queries, AI tools tend to favor contractors with recent, high-volume review activity and locally-specific content — both areas where Raptor holds a clear structural advantage. AmeriPro's national volume claim may earn them a passing mention in broad "who are the big national roofers" queries, but local intent queries are a different story.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where AmeriPro beats Raptor: National brand name recognition and ServiceTitan-powered sales infrastructure gives their reps a slicker field process; the Owens Corning Platinum Preferred and GAF President Club credentials provide third-party credibility that plays well in insurance claim conversations.

They have also been named a Top 100 Roofing Contractor in the U.S. for 8 consecutive years.

Where Raptor beats them: 688 reviews vs. 258, a perfect 5.0 vs. 4.5, zero owner response engagement on their end, and corrosive off-platform reviews on Angi, Yahoo, and Trustindex. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" narrative is a direct antidote to AmeriPro's documented complaints around aggressive sales tactics and post-job service failures.

Most exploitable weakness: Their Google review velocity is flatlined — zero new reviews in 90 days — while Raptor's review engine is active; any homeowner doing due diligence in real-time will see a competitor that has gone silent.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. ServiceTitan as a sales ops differentiator.

AmeriPro uses ServiceTitan in the field for transparent pricing, margin visibility, scheduling, and CRM workflow.

If Raptor isn't surfacing a similar tech-enabled sales experience to homeowners during the estimate — showing live pricing, financing, and scheduling in a single visit — that is a closing gap worth closing.

  1. Veterans hiring as a brand narrative.

AmeriPro actively recruits veterans and builds it into their employer brand, citing attention to detail, high standards, and work ethic as military-aligned values.

This resonates in the Indy/Carmel market. Raptor should evaluate whether there's a version of this story — veterans on crew, veteran homeowner discounts — that can be owned more authentically before AmeriPro doubles down on it locally.

  1. Counter their aggressive outbound with Raptor's inbound story.

Documented complaints about AmeriPro's unsolicited cold-call tactics

create a direct opening. A short-form video or paid social ad series contrasting "we don't chase you down" vs. the storm-chaser model would resonate strongly in Carmel and Fishers — markets that have been repeatedly targeted by post-hail canvass blitzes. Patrick should be aware this is a reputation attack surface that Cameron can exploit with minimal spend.
