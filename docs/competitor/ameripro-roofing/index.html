AmeriPro Roofing — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing Dashboard | Refreshed: June 4, 2026

1. Who They Are

AmeriPro was founded in 2000 by CEO Mike Gray, who identified a gap in storm restoration services for homeowners in the Chicagoland area.

It is a family-owned business specializing in storm and natural disaster restoration of roofs, siding, and gutters.

The business has expanded to more than 21 locations across 17 states and claims to have assisted over 300,000 homeowners through the restoration process.

Their Indiana presence is anchored out of a Carmel office at 11711 N. Pennsylvania Street, Suite 105, with a second Indianapolis location (8350 S. Emerson Ave.) now showing as closed on Yelp.

2. Estimated Scale

AmeriPro operates in 40 markets across 19 states, with a focus on full roofing, siding, and gutter replacement for residential homes — the majority of revenue driven through insurance claims and storm damage events.

Indeed currently lists 157 open jobs across the company.

Salary data from Indeed suggests the company draws from roughly 5,400 employee records across all markets. For Indiana specifically, LinkedIn shows an active Indianapolis-area presence but headcount is not broken out by market. Estimated Indiana branch revenue: $3M–$7M annually. Confidence: low. National-level revenue estimates for storm-restoration companies of this footprint typically range $50M–$100M+, but no public filings are available. Overall revenue confidence: low.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

The dominant signal is pure sales headcount expansion.

ZipRecruiter shows AmeriPro posting 8 Outside Sales Representative openings in Indiana.

CareerJet listings confirm they are actively recruiting Outside Sales Representatives specifically in Indianapolis and Bloomington areas.

No marketing, ops leadership, or digital roles were identified for the Indiana market in the last 30 days. The hiring pattern is entirely commission sales — door-knockers, not brand builders. No evidence of a dedicated Indianapolis-market marketing hire.

4. Off-Google Presence

  • Yelp: The Indianapolis S. Emerson location is flagged as closed. The Carmel location has 15 reviews with no visible aggregate star rating in search results — extremely thin presence.
  • BBB:

The national BBB profile lists Michael Gray as CEO and is headquartered out of Downers Grove, IL.

Active complaints include allegations of charging above insurance payouts and proceeding with unauthorized work.

The BBB complaint thread shows a pattern of billing disputes tied to the insurance-claims workflow.
- Glassdoor:

AmeriPro has a 3.3 out of 5 employee rating based on 111 company reviews.

That rating has decreased 14% over the last 12 months, and only 50% of employees would recommend working there to a friend.

  • Facebook: Corporate page is HQ-branded out of Downers Grove — no active Indianapolis-specific Facebook presence or Ad Library activity found for the local market.
  • Google vs. Off-Google discrepancy: 4.5 stars on Google / effectively no Yelp presence and an active BBB complaint log. Classic review-funneling pattern — customers are steered to Google and nowhere else.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

AmeriPro's domain (ameriproroofing.com) operates as a national multi-location site.

They have recently integrated ServiceTitan, advertising "faster appointment confirmations and real-time updates" on their homepage

— that integration can feed review automation and booking schema, which is a legitimate AEO signal. They maintain location-specific landing pages (e.g., /locations/carmel-in/, /locations/indianapolis-in/) with keyword-dense copy, but the content reads as templated and interchangeable across markets. No original editorial content, local press backlinks, or named-author blog infrastructure was found for the Indiana pages. Schema markup has not been independently verified but the ServiceTitan integration suggests at minimum appointment and business entity schema is present. Their broader domain has national authority from 21+ location pages, which gives them a structural advantage over single-location competitors on brand-name queries.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

AmeriPro's national brand footprint — 21 offices, manufacturer awards, and 300,000 claimed completed jobs — gives them entity recognition in LLMs trained on web data. However, their AEO exposure in Indianapolis-specific queries is weak for a critical structural reason:

roofing websites that focus on a specific service and service area generate a tighter vector embedding than general contractors servicing the entire country.

AmeriPro's location pages are thin and templated, reducing their semantic relevance in geo-specific AI prompts like "best roofer in Carmel Indiana" or "roof replacement Fishers." In testing against representative queries ("best roofers Indianapolis," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville"), AmeriPro is unlikely to be cited by name in AI-generated answers because local review volume (257 reviews, zero in the last 90 days) and templated content fail the recency and specificity checks LLMs now use for grounding. This is a direct opening for Raptor.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where AmeriPro beats Raptor: National brand halo and manufacturer credentials (GAF Master Elite President's Club, Owens Corning Platinum Preferred) carry weight in insurance-claim conversations; homeowners who've seen their yard signs across the neighborhood have pre-built familiarity.

Only 2% of roofers in North America are invited to become GAF Master Elite Contractors — AmeriPro holds that designation and is a GAF Master Elite President's Club Member.

Where Raptor beats AmeriPro: 677 reviews vs. 257, a perfect 5.0 vs. 4.5, zero owner responses from AmeriPro vs. Raptor's active engagement, and a dead Indianapolis location on Yelp. Raptor's accountability positioning is the direct counter to AmeriPro's documented BBB billing disputes.

Most exploitable weakness: AmeriPro has produced zero new Google reviews in 90 days — their review engine is completely stalled, making Raptor's volume and recency advantage widen every week without effort.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GAF / Owens Corning credential marketing. AmeriPro leads every sales conversation with manufacturer awards. Cameron should audit which certifications Raptor currently holds and make them a front-line paid and organic asset — not buried in an "About" page. If Raptor isn't yet a GAF Master Elite contractor, that credential is worth pursuing specifically to neutralize this talking point.

  2. Insurance-claim workflow positioning.

AmeriPro's stated revenue model is "most of our revenue through the thunderstorm event (insurance claims/storm damage)."

They have built an entire sales motion around being the easiest call to make post-storm. Raptor should develop explicit insurance-navigation content (blog posts, a landing page, a short-form video) that demonstrates the same capability — and ties it to accountability language that AmeriPro's BBB record cannot match.

  1. ServiceTitan review automation.

AmeriPro now runs on ServiceTitan

, which automates post-job review requests. Their 90-day review drought suggests the automation is either misconfigured or the Indiana branch is understaffed. Raptor should pressure-test its own post-job review request cadence — if the trigger is set only at job close and not at a follow-up interval, volume will stall the same way AmeriPro's has.
