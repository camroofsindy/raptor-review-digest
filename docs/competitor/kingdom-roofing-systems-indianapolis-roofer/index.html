Kingdom Roofing Systems — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely | Refreshed: August 18, 2026

1. Who They Are

Kingdom Roofing Systems was co-founded in the spring of 2012 by Daniel Young and Ryan Martlage

, though

Yelp lists the company as established in 2011

— the discrepancy likely reflects a pre-launch storm restoration run.

Daniel Young holds the title of President; Kristi Crisp serves as COO.

With offices in Indianapolis and Marion, they serve most of central Indiana

, running a dual residential/commercial model and positioning themselves heavily as insurance restoration specialists.

Their LinkedIn page claims to be "insurance experts, and no other company in Indiana can truly say the same"

— an aggressive differentiator that invites direct comparison pressure from premium-positioned competitors.

2. Estimated Scale

Kingdom was planning an office move as recently as December 2025, with ZoomInfo noting approximately 15 employees at their existing Indianapolis location on Corporate Dr.

The LinkedIn company page shows 469 followers with no public headcount listed.

A Paper Box SEO case study documented a 219% increase in organic leads over one year, after which Kingdom opened two new offices and brought on 14 new roofers and support staff

— suggesting a growth phase that has since plateaued given the current review velocity. Estimated annual revenue: $3M–$6M based on team size, multi-location footprint, and residential/commercial mix. Confidence: low — no public filings or permit data confirmed at this time.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

ZoomInfo flags that Kingdom has more open roles in Customer Service/Support than at any point in the past 12 months, and is actively seeking a Project Manager in Indianapolis.

Daniel Young's LinkedIn posts from 2025 show active recruiting for residential sales reps in Bloomington and Indianapolis.

A separate post from his profile advertises for a Commercial Project Manager in Indianapolis.

No marketing director or ops leadership hires detected in the last 30 days. The customer service hiring surge is worth watching — it could signal scaling pains or a push to reduce churn on existing accounts.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp (Indianapolis listing at 6964 Corporate Dr) shows only 16 reviews

against their 305 Google reviews — a 95% gap that is a textbook review-funneling signal. No visible Yelp star rating surfaced in results, indicating low Yelp traction.

Their Facebook page (Marion-anchored) shows 2,918 likes with 166 people talking about it

— a respectable community presence, though the page is attributed to the Marion HQ rather than Indianapolis.

BBB-accredited since July 8, 2016

, with

accreditation also noted from the Chamber of Commerce and Angi.

No significant Reddit presence detected. Their own supplied data — 0 reviews in the last 30 days, 2 in the last 90 days, and a 0.9 owner response rate — suggests a review acquisition program that has effectively stalled.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Kingdom is a listed client of WebRunner Media, an agency focused on Google Ads and Meta campaigns paired with conversion-optimized landing pages and CRM integration.

Their site (kingdomroofingsystems.com) runs city-specific landing pages — confirmed by their cities-served page — which is standard local SEO practice.

They lead with their GAF Master Elite status

and

have a proprietary customer app for project tracking and warranty storage

— both are content assets that support E-E-A-T signals for organic ranking. They also operate a secondary domain (kingdomgreatdeals.com) functioning as a dedicated paid-media landing page, separating ad traffic from the main domain — a smart paid/organic hygiene move. No evidence of active blog publishing or recent local press backlinks. Their domain likely carries a mid-tier authority signal consistent with a regional contractor.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI answer patterns for Indianapolis roofing queries ("best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville"), Kingdom Roofing Systems does not appear to be a consistently named result in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini responses for these queries. Their AEO posture is weak: no visible structured FAQ schema in search snippets, no blog content pipeline generating LLM-ingestible authority signals, and a stalled review velocity (0 reviews in 30 days) that depresses the freshness signals LLMs weigh when surfacing service providers. This is a direct opportunity for Raptor — 701 reviews at 5.0 is exactly the signal profile that generative engines favor when resolving "best" queries.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Kingdom beats Raptor: Dual manufacturer certifications (GAF Master Elite top 2% + Owens Corning Platinum Preferred) give them a warranty story —

the GAF Golden Pledge warranty

and

Owens Corning Platinum Protection (fewer than 10 Indiana roofers)

— that is concrete and hard to dismiss in a sales conversation. Their proprietary customer app and a price-match guarantee also add transactional confidence that Raptor should be ready to counter. Where Raptor beats Kingdom: 701 reviews vs. 305, a perfect 5.0 vs. 4.7, and a live review acquisition engine vs. their flatlined 0-in-30-days pace. Raptor's "no outsourced accountability" positioning also cuts directly against Kingdom's insurance-restoration playbook, which by nature involves adjuster-mediated decisions and third-party layers. Most exploitable weakness: Kingdom's review engine has gone clinically dead — 0 new Google reviews in 30 days for a multi-location contractor is an operational signal, not a bad luck streak, and it opens the door for Raptor to dominate recency-weighted GBP ranking in shared zip codes.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The split-domain paid media play. Kingdom runs kingdomgreatdeals.com as a dedicated conversion page, keeping paid traffic off their organic domain.

Their agency (WebRunner Media) pairs Google Ads and Meta campaigns with CRM-integrated call tracking.

Cameron should evaluate whether Raptor's current paid campaigns are landing on pages built for conversion or pages built for brand — they are not the same job.

  1. The customer app as a retention and referral loop.

Kingdom's app lets customers track project status, store warranties, and make referrals to earn rewards.

A lightweight version of this — even a Jobber client portal pushed hard post-install — gives Raptor a referral trigger that most competitors skip entirely.

  1. Charitable partnership amplification.

Kingdom publicly ties its brand to Isaiah 117 House and the Owens Corning Roof Deployment for Veterans

, using those partnerships for content, press, and community trust signals. Raptor has its own community story — it needs to be documented and distributed at a cadence that builds LLM citation history, not just Facebook engagement.
