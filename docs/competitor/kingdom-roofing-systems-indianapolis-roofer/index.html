Kingdom Roofing Systems — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: June 16, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2010 by Daniel Young in Marion, Indiana, with a subsequent expansion to the Park 100 area in northwest Indianapolis.

Leadership includes Daniel Young as President and Kristi Crisp as COO.

In 2023, Young sold 80% of the business to Morgan Stanley Capital Partners and now operates as Brand President covering Indiana

— meaning this is no longer an independent contractor; it is a PE-backed platform play operating under the

Allstar Home Services family, a fast-growing national network of exterior remodeling brands.

That structural shift is the single most important fact in this brief.

2. Estimated Scale

Growjo estimates annual revenue at $15.7M with 60 employees

;

Daniel Young's own Intch profile cites top-line revenue of $18M.

Confidence: medium — the PE acquisition complicates standalone revenue isolation.

Growjo also flags a -30% employee count decline last year

, which aligns with

ZoomInfo reporting a planned office move out of 6964 Corporate Dr, Indianapolis in December 2025, affecting approximately 15 employees.

Headcount is contracting, not growing — a meaningful signal.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Active postings skew heavily toward production and sales, not marketing leadership.

ZipRecruiter shows an open Roofing and Siding Production Manager role based in Marion, IN.

ZoomInfo notes Kingdom has more open Customer Service/Support roles than at any point in the past 12 months, alongside an active Project Manager — Roofing search in Indianapolis.

No CMO, digital marketing, or demand-generation hires visible.

Indeed employee reviews flag high turnover, high-pressure sales culture, and management instability

— consistent with a PE integration shakeout, not a growth posture.

4. Off-Google Presence

The review profile tells a disjointed story. Google sits at 305 reviews / 4.7 stars (per Cameron's data).

Yelp shows only 16 reviews on the Indianapolis location listing at 6964 Corporate Dr

— a significant underperformance given their claimed decade-plus tenure.

Facebook shows 100 reviews with an 88% recommendation rate

and

2,893 page likes

— modest engagement for a $15M+ operation.

BBB shows 4 total complaints in the last 3 years across 3 locations, with 1 complaint closed in the last 12 months.

BBB accreditation is current with an A+ rating. The Yelp gap (16 reviews vs. 305 on Google) is a textbook review-funneling pattern — they are steering customers exclusively to Google, leaving Yelp and Facebook essentially unguarded.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

A Paper Box SEO case study documents that Kingdom specifically targeted Indianapolis given the concentration of Indiana roofs there, engaged an SEO agency to optimize their Google Business Profile and GMB landing page for keywords, and

achieved a 219% increase in organic leads over one year, resulting in two new offices and 14 new hires.

That case study is now dated, but it confirms they have agency-driven SEO history. Their site runs on

WordPress with Cloudflare Rocket Loader

— a lightweight but capable stack. They have dedicated city/service landing pages (e.g., kingdomroofingsystems.com/indianapolis-roof-replacement/) and a full cities-served index page.

They lead their homepage with GAF Master Elite status

and

Owens Corning top 1% contractor designation

— both credentialing signals that carry keyword weight. No visible blog cadence or recent press backlinks found. AEO schema markup not confirmed; their WordPress-based site may have basic local business schema but nothing advanced enough to dominate AI-generated answers.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on known AI citation patterns and the data surfaced here: Kingdom Roofing Systems has the raw ingredients for AI citation — manufacturer certifications, multi-location presence, named-city landing pages, and third-party coverage from roofingcontractor.com and Zionsville Monthly Magazine. However, their review velocity is nearly dead (3 reviews in 90 days per Cameron's data), their blog is inactive, and their Yelp profile is thin. AI engines — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity — weight recency and cross-platform signal density. Kingdom's stagnant review count and absence of fresh content mean they are likely losing AEO ground in real time, even if they appear occasionally in legacy-indexed AI responses. This is an open window for Raptor.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Kingdom beats Raptor: Multi-location footprint, GAF Master Elite + Owens Corning Platinum dual-certification stack, a proprietary customer app for project tracking and warranty storage, and PE-backed resources that could fund a sudden marketing surge. Their 0.98 owner response rate matches Raptor's discipline on reputation management.

Where Raptor beats Kingdom: 679 reviews at a perfect 5.0 vs. Kingdom's 305 at 4.7 — that is a statistically dominant trust signal at volume. Raptor's review velocity is the differentiator; Kingdom's is functionally dead. Kingdom is mid-PE-integration with documented employee turnover and an office relocation — internally distracted. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is a direct counter to what a PE rollup structurally cannot credibly promise.

Most exploitable weakness: Kingdom's review acquisition engine has stalled (3 reviews in 90 days) during a period of organizational disruption — Cameron should be flooding local AI results and Google with fresh Raptor reviews right now while the gap is widest.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The dual-certification credential stack.

Kingdom's GAF Master Elite designation — held by only 2% of roofing contractors nationwide

— is prominently headline-positioned on their homepage.

Their Owens Corning Platinum Protection claim of fewer than 10 roofers in Indiana qualifying

is the kind of scarcity framing that converts. Cameron should audit Raptor's current certifications and determine whether any can be positioned with the same exclusivity language in hero copy and GBP attributes.

  1. The proprietary customer app.

Kingdom offers a branded app for project notifications, warranty storage, and referral rewards.

This is a referral and retention flywheel that most Indy roofers do not have. Raptor does not need to build an app — a post-job SMS/email sequence with a referral incentive accomplishes the same loop at a fraction of the cost and is deployable in weeks.

  1. Monitor the PE integration fallout.

Indeed reviews from Kingdom employees cite high turnover, bad management, and failure to pay commissions.

Disgruntled sales reps and project managers from a PE-backed competitor are warm recruiting targets. Raptor should have a standing "Join Us" page that speaks directly to tradespeople tired of corporate roofing — this is a talent acquisition and brand differentiation move simultaneously.
