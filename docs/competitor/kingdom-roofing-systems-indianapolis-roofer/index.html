Competitive Intelligence Brief — Kingdom Roofing Systems

Refreshed: July 7, 2026 | Prepared for: Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Kingdom Roofing Systems was founded in the spring of 2012 by co-founders Daniel Young and Ryan Martlage.

The company operates as an exterior remodeling and restoration contractor with a presence in Marion, Kokomo, and Indianapolis, specializing in roofing, windows, and siding.

Their primary market differentiator is GAF Master Elite status — an honor held by only 2% of roofing contractors nationwide — which enables them to offer the GAF Golden Pledge Warranty.

A second differentiator worth flagging:

Kingdom is now part of the Allstar Home Services family, described as a fast-growing national network of exterior remodeling brands backed by Morgan Stanley Capital Partners.

This is a material strategic shift from the founder-owned story they still market.

2. Estimated Scale

ZoomInfo flagged that Kingdom Roofing Systems was planning an office move in December 2025, noting the move would impact approximately 15 employees at the Indianapolis Corporate Drive location.

Indeed estimates salary data drawn from approximately 94 employees, past and present.

That 94-person figure likely spans all Indiana locations and includes field crews. Confidence: medium (ZoomInfo + Indeed, not a direct headcount).

The company previously reported six locations throughout Indiana.

Based on multi-location volume, revenue is estimated in the $8M–$15M range. Confidence: low (no public financials; private company).

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Kingdom runs kingdomroofingsystems.com on WordPress with Cloudflare optimization.

Their known tech stack includes Cloudflare Rocket Loader, WordPress.org, PHP, and Cloudflare Website Optimization.

They maintain dedicated city-service pages (their /cities-served/ page confirms this), which is solid local SEO hygiene. They have a documented history of working with an SEO agency:

Paper Box SEO improved their Google Business Profile, helped them earn more reviews, and optimized GMB landing pages — achieving a 219% increase in organic leads over one year.

Whether that agency relationship is ongoing is unclear. They have a second promotional domain (kingdomgreatdeals.com) that appears to run discount-oriented landing pages — a thin-content, CRO-focused play that likely segments their price-sensitive traffic. No evidence of active blog content or local press backlinks surfaced. AEO schema markup status is unconfirmed but the WordPress stack makes it implementable if they've prioritized it.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No external data source confirms Kingdom Roofing Systems appearing in AI-generated responses for target Indy queries ("best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville"). Their Roofing Contractor Magazine feature is the strongest earned-media asset they have that could train LLM citations — it is an industry trade piece, not a consumer-facing local publication. For generative engines pulling from local consumer signals, their thin Yelp presence, sparse blog output, and absence from local press (Indy Star, IBJ, Patch) all reduce their AEO footprint. This is an exploitable gap: Raptor should be building citation-worthy assets (local press placements, structured FAQ content, review schema) specifically designed to be quoted in AI answers for Indy roofing queries where Kingdom is currently invisible.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Kingdom beats Raptor: Dual manufacturer certifications (GAF Master Elite + Owens Corning Platinum Preferred) give them a warranty story that resonates in insurance-restoration sales conversations; their branded customer app (kingdomroofingsystems.com/about/) creates post-sale retention and referral infrastructure Raptor does not currently publicize. Where Raptor beats Kingdom: 685 reviews at 5.0 vs. Kingdom's 306 at 4.7 is not a marginal difference — it's a closing-table win in every LSA, Maps, and AI comparison; Raptor's owner-accountability positioning is cleaner and more credible than Kingdom's, which now has PE ownership quietly contradicting the "local family" brand. Most exploitable weakness: Kingdom's Allstar/Morgan Stanley parent entity is publicly visible on their Facebook page but hidden everywhere else — Raptor can own the "genuinely local, genuinely accountable" lane before Kingdom's PE transition erodes their community trust.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The branded customer app.

Kingdom's Customer Connection App lets homeowners track project status, store warranty documents, and make referrals to earn rewards — available in the App Store and Google Play.

This is a retention and referral engine that Raptor lacks. Even a lightweight version (project tracking + warranty storage via a third-party platform like JobNimbus's client portal) would close that gap and reinforce Raptor's accountability brand.

  1. Price-assurance guarantee as a conversion tool.

Kingdom offers a price-match refund if a customer can find any shingle roofing system at a lower price before the first install date.

This is a deliberate move to defuse mid-funnel price objections without discounting. Raptor should have a counter-narrative ready — not a price match, but a value-articulation tool (e.g., a written scope comparison showing what Kingdom's quote actually includes vs. Raptor's) to neutralize it at the sales table.

  1. Monitor Allstar/Morgan Stanley integration closely.

Kingdom is part of a PE-backed national network under Morgan Stanley Capital Partners.

Regional rollups under PE typically bring pricing pressure, crew turnover, and brand dilution within 18–24 months. Cameron should watch for review velocity decline, sales staff churn on LinkedIn, and any rebrand signals — those are the trigger points to aggressively push Raptor's local ownership story in paid and organic channels.
