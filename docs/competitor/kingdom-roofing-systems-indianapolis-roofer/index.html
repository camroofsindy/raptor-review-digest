Competitive Intelligence Brief: Kingdom Roofing Systems

Updated: Thursday, May 22, 2026 | Prepared for: Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Co-founders Daniel Young and Ryan Martlage launched Kingdom Roofing Systems in the spring of 2012

, rooted in Marion, IN before expanding into Indianapolis.

Daniel Young holds the President title; Kristi Crisp serves as COO.

With offices in Indianapolis and Marion, they serve most of central Indiana

, operating across residential and commercial segments with roofing, siding, and gutters. Their differentiator is dual premium manufacturer status:

GAF Master Elite contractor — an honor held by only 2% of roofing contractors nationwide — allowing them to offer the GAF Golden Pledge Warranty

, and

fewer than 10 roofers in Indiana can offer Platinum Protection from Owens Corning, but Kingdom can.

Critically,

Kingdom is part of the Allstar Home Services family — a fast-growing national network of leading exterior remodeling brands backed by Morgan Stanley Capital Partners.

That PE backing is the single most important strategic context for reading Kingdom's moves right now.

2. Estimated Scale

PitchBook lists Kingdom Roofing Systems at 40 total employees.

Confidence: medium (PitchBook data is often 6-12 months stale and doesn't always capture field crews). An earlier profile in Zionsville Monthly Magazine and Roofing Contractor pegged them at roughly 30 employees.

According to Young, Kingdom installs 500 roofs and 700 total jobs every year

— at blended Indy pricing of ~$12K–$15K per job, that implies a gross revenue run rate of $8.4M–$10.5M annually. Revenue confidence: medium-low — those figures are from a prior publication cycle; PE ownership typically accelerates job volume. Post-Allstar acquisition, that number is likely higher.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Active open roles visible on ZipRecruiter and LinkedIn as of this refresh:

Roofing & Siding Production Manager based in Marion, IN or nearby service areas (full-time, competitive salary + performance bonuses).

High-income Sales Representative role in Indianapolis — uncapped commission, paid training, pre-qualified leads.

Residential Sales Reps in Bloomington and Indianapolis, posted directly from Daniel Young's LinkedIn.

A Commercial Project Manager role in Indianapolis was also posted on Daniel Young's LinkedIn.

The Production Manager and Commercial PM posts together signal a push into higher-ticket commercial volume — consistent with what PE platforms typically mandate for EBITDA expansion. No marketing-specific hires visible, which means their paid and SEO program is likely agency-run with limited in-house oversight.

4. Off-Google Presence

  • Yelp:

16 reviews at their Indianapolis location (6964 Corporate Dr).

No star rating surfaced in results, but 16 reviews against 305 on Google is a stark imbalance — a classic review-funneling pattern where sales reps direct happy customers straight to Google and unhappy ones get lost. Monitor for Yelp star rating specifically.
- BBB:

BBB Accredited since July 8, 2016.

Complaint themes include aggressive follow-up sales tactics and contract cancellation disputes — exploitable brand vulnerability.

Page managed by Allstar Home Services LLC; 100 Facebook reviews at 88% recommendation rate.

88% recommend on Facebook is notably below their Google average and signals some consumer friction not captured in the Google funnel.
- Nextdoor:

Listed as an established exterior remodeling and restoration contractor serving Marion, Kokomo, and Indianapolis.

Appears claimed but not actively managed for neighborhood-level engagement.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Kingdom operates kingdomroofingsystems.com plus a secondary deal-capture domain kingdomgreatdeals.com, likely used for paid landing pages. They have a dedicated Indianapolis roof replacement page and a cities served page, showing intentional geo-page architecture. A Paper Box SEO case study documents their prior SEO engagement:

they improved their Google My Business listing, gained reviews, and optimized GMB landing pages — resulting in a 219% increase in organic leads, two new offices, and 14 new hires.

They clearly invested heavily in local SEO at a prior stage. However, their current review velocity (2 reviews in 90 days) suggests either a deliberate throttle or a broken post-sale review solicitation process — which creates a structural DA and GBP authority gap that is widening in Raptor's favor. No evidence of a current blog content program or link-building from local press.

They do operate a branded Customer Connection App available on the App Store and Google Play

, which is a differentiator for post-sale engagement and warranty management but does not directly improve organic search posture.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on available intelligence from this refresh cycle (no live AI query tool available), Kingdom's AI citation probability is assessed as moderate-to-high for general Indy roofing queries, primarily due to their GAF Master Elite and Owens Corning Platinum Preferred credentials — both of which are frequently cited in AI training data as proxies for "top contractor" signals. Their dual-manufacturer certification, BBB accreditation history, and volume of third-party directory listings (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Directorii, BirdEye, Yelp) create a broad citation footprint that LLMs draw from. However, their review stagnation (2 reviews in 90 days vs. Raptor's sustained velocity) is increasingly relevant: LLMs that weight recency signals will begin deprioritizing Kingdom in favor of operators generating fresh review content. This is an active, time-sensitive window for Raptor to capture AI-answer real estate.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Kingdom beats Raptor: Scale, service diversity (commercial + residential + siding + gutters), PE-backed war chest for paid media, dual elite manufacturer certification, and a proprietary customer app that creates a defensible post-sale moat.

Where Raptor beats Kingdom: 675 reviews vs. 305; 5.0 stars vs. 4.7; zero BBB complaints vs. Kingdom's documented aggressive sales follow-up complaints; and a consistent review velocity that signals an operational review engine rather than a one-time campaign. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" premium positioning is cleanly differentiated from Kingdom's insurance-restoration / volume-play identity.

Most exploitable weakness: Kingdom's BBB complaint record of aggressive post-signing sales pressure and their 88% Facebook recommendation rate (below Google average) tells a story about a high-pressure sales culture that Raptor can directly counter in sales conversations and review responses without ever naming Kingdom.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The dual-domain paid capture play. Kingdom runs kingdomgreatdeals.com as a separate landing page domain for promotions and paid traffic, isolating conversion pages from the main site. Cameron should evaluate whether a dedicated storm-season or financing-offer subdomain/domain could segment paid traffic without diluting raptorroofing.com's brand positioning.

  2. The branded mobile app as a review and referral engine.

The Kingdom app lets customers track project status, reference warranty work for years to come, and make referrals to earn rewards.

The referral-rewards mechanic embedded in an app is a systematic way to generate reviews and word-of-mouth — a structure Raptor could replicate through a lighter-weight tool like a Jobber client portal or a simple post-job SMS automation, without the overhead of building a native app.

  1. Monitor the Allstar/Morgan Stanley expansion cadence.

As part of the Allstar Home Services family backed by Morgan Stanley Capital Partners

, Kingdom is likely on a roll-up acquisition and market-share mandate. Watch for new Kingdom locations in Carmel, Fishers, or Noblesville — markets where Raptor currently has review and brand authority — and get ahead of it with hyperlocal GBP posts and geo-targeted content before Kingdom's paid spend floods those ZIP codes.
