Competitive Intelligence Brief — Big Sky Roofing

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely's Dashboard | Refreshed: May 6, 2025

1. Who They Are

Big Sky Roofing, LLC was founded on May 20, 2021, and is owned and operated by Seth Hobbs.

The company was co-developed by Seth Hobbs and Mitch Widau — Seth holds a construction management degree with a background in real estate, while Mitch brings a finance degree and experience building a digital media tech company.

Based in Brownsburg, Indiana, they position as the go-to provider of residential and commercial roof replacement, storm damage restoration, siding, and gutters across the Indianapolis metro area.

2. Estimated Scale

Per ZoomInfo, Big Sky Roofing operates in the commercial and residential construction industry, employs 5–9 people, and carries reported revenue under $500K.

Confidence: low — ZoomInfo data for sub-$1M firms is notoriously stale.

BuildZoom records show 13 permitted projects totaling $146,600 in permit value,

which, combined with their review velocity (32 reviews in 30 days is aggressive for a sub-10-person shop), suggests actual revenue is likely $1M–$2.5M once uninspected residential work is factored in. Revenue confidence: low. Real headcount is probably 8–15 when subcontractors are included.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active job postings found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter as of this refresh. LinkedIn's company page (linkedin.com/company/big-sky-roofing-llc) returned no public employee count or open roles. The absence of hiring activity at their current review velocity is notable — either they are running lean on subs, or they are not yet investing in a formal sales or marketing hire. Watch for a sales rep or canvassing coordinator post, which would signal an intentional volume push into Raptor's territory.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

bigskyroof.com is a tight, service-page-heavy site targeting Brownsburg and Hendricks County terms.

Primary targeting is "Brownsburg roofing contractor" and they cover roof repair, replacement, storm damage, siding, and gutters.

No visible blog or editorial content layer — meaning zero long-tail content production and no AEO-targeted FAQ schema that would feed generative engine results.

Their BuildZoom score of 102 ranks them in the top 11% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors,

which improves third-party citation trust signals passively. They hold Owens Corning Preferred Contractor status (owenscorning.com) — a legitimate backlink from a high-DA manufacturer domain. No evidence of local press backlinks, structured FAQ schema, or topic-cluster SEO strategy. Their domain is not built to rank outside Brownsburg/Hendricks County.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on research into their SEO posture — no blog content, no FAQ schema, limited geographic content beyond Brownsburg — Big Sky Roofing almost certainly does not appear in ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude responses to queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." Their content footprint is anchored entirely to Brownsburg and Hendricks County. Generative engines pull named contractors from review aggregators, editorial mentions, and structured content; Big Sky has none of those signals outside their immediate market. This is a direct opportunity for Raptor — owning AEO-optimized content for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, and Zionsville keeps Big Sky structurally invisible in those AI results while Raptor earns the citation.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Big Sky beats Raptor: Hyper-local Hendricks County brand identity. Brownsburg homeowners see them as the neighborhood contractor in a way that Raptor, coming from the east side of the metro, may not immediately project. Their review velocity (32 in 30 days) is strong for a company their size and closing that gap requires sustained operational discipline.

Where Raptor beats Big Sky: 664 reviews at a perfect 5.0 vs. 323 at 4.9 — twice the social proof mass at a higher rating. Raptor's premium positioning and explicit accountability message holds more weight in higher-ticket Carmel/Zionsville/Fishers markets where Big Sky has no content or brand presence. Big Sky's "outstanding prices" language actively places them in the value tier, which is a ceiling on ticket size.

Most exploitable weakness: Big Sky has no BBB accreditation, no Nextdoor presence, and one "Fave" in their own backyard — for a contractor whose entire moat is hyper-local trust, that is an embarrassingly thin off-Google footprint that Raptor can outrun in any adjacent market.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Communication as a marketing message.

Big Sky's recent Facebook content explicitly calls out contractor communication failures as a differentiator

— this resonates with homeowners and is worth mirroring in Raptor's paid social creative with more specificity (e.g., "Your call returned within 60 minutes, guaranteed").

  1. Owens Corning preferred contractor citation as a backlink strategy. Big Sky is leveraging their OC preferred status as an authoritative manufacturer backlink. Raptor should audit every manufacturer and supplier relationship (GAF, Owens Corning, Velux, etc.) for unclaimed contractor directory profile pages that carry legitimate DA and feed AI citation engines.

  2. Monitor Big Sky's review velocity. Their 90-day count (41) is lower than their 30-day count (32) implies for a consistent pace — suggesting a recent ramp-up, possibly from a new CRM trigger or post-job text sequence. If they sustain 30+ reviews/month through summer, they will close Raptor's volume gap faster than the star-rating gap matters. Cameron should watch the 60-day mark on this.
