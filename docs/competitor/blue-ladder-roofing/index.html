Competitive Intelligence Brief — Blue Ladder Roofing

Refreshed: Tuesday, May 20, 2025 | Prepared for Cameron Blakely, Raptor Roofing

1. Who They Are

Blue Ladder Roofing, LLC was founded on December 31, 2019 and is owned and operated by Austin Chastain, operating as a sole proprietorship out of Fishers, IN.

They serve homeowners and businesses across Indiana, Central Missouri, and the New Ozarks

, making them a multi-market storm restoration play rather than a pure Indy-focused operator.

Their core identity centers on insurance claim navigation

and storm restoration — they are fundamentally an insurance-claim sales machine wearing a roofing company's logo.

2. Estimated Scale

A job posting self-discloses company revenue projected to double from 2025 to 2026, growing from $8 million worth of restoration projects, with an expectation to hit $20 million-plus for 2026.

That's a self-reported figure from a recruitment ad, so take it with some skepticism, but it's directionally meaningful. Confidence: medium.

Their own about page describes a team of roofing professionals, project managers, and customer support specialists

— no headcount stated. LinkedIn and ZoomInfo do not surface a verified employee count for the Indiana entity. Based on the multi-market structure, sales pod model, and revenue claim, estimate 15–30 total staff (W-2 plus 1099 reps). Employee count confidence: low.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Active and notable.

Blue Ladder is currently recruiting 1–2 individuals to join their storm restoration team, with representatives focused on handling the influx of insurance claims due to hail and wind damage affecting the metropolitan area.

All sales reps start as 1099 independent contractors for immediate earning flexibility, with a contract-to-career path converting consistent performers to W-2 employment.

A January 2025 Glassdoor listing (now expired) shows they also recruited a Customer Success Manager to support outside sales, handle lead intake, scheduling, follow-ups, and CRM management.

The CSM hire signals they are building inside sales infrastructure to back a growing canvassing force — this is a scalable volume-sales model, not a referral-and-repeat model.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Blue Ladder operates across at least three active domains: blueladderroofs.com, blueladderroofingindy.com, and blueladderroofingin.com — a fragmented domain strategy that dilutes link equity and creates brand confusion.

The blueladderroofs.com/fishers-in page deploys localized, weather-specific content tying Fishers climate challenges directly to service offerings

, which is solid on-page SEO hygiene. However, the multi-domain setup means no single root domain is accumulating the full authority it should.

Their IKO Craftsman Premier certification is featured prominently

, giving them a manufacturer-directory backlink. No local press coverage or .edu/.gov backlinks surfaced. The split-domain structure is the single biggest exploitable SEO weakness — Raptor's consolidated domain will compound authority faster.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI engine behavior and the data surfaced here: Blue Ladder Roofing is unlikely to appear prominently in AI-generated answers for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." Their fragmented domain footprint, absence of authoritative press coverage, and thin structured data signals mean generative engines lack strong citation hooks. The Roofing Insights affiliation and IKO directory listing are their best AI-visibility assets, but neither drives consistent AI citation. This is a clear opportunity for Raptor — building AEO-optimized FAQ schema, earning structured local press mentions, and consolidating authority on a single domain will produce AI citation placement that Blue Ladder is not positioned to compete for.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Blue Ladder beats Raptor: Volume velocity — 390 reviews acquired since 2019 shows strong close volume and a systematic ask cadence baked into their sales process. Their multi-market expansion into Missouri also signals a capital-light franchising instinct that adds brand credibility to buyers who research online. Their financing offer

starting at $97/month provides a specific, low-friction objection handler for homeowners whose claims fall short

— Raptor needs a comparably specific counter.

Where Raptor beats Blue Ladder: 5.0 stars at 673 reviews vs. 4.8 at 390 is not a minor gap — at the premium tier, that 0.2-star difference is a closing tool. A 0.0% owner response rate is a credibility liability Blue Ladder has left completely unaddressed. Raptor's single-domain, single-brand identity gives it compounding SEO and AEO advantages Blue Ladder cannot easily fix.

Most exploitable weakness: A 0.0% owner response rate on 390 reviews signals zero post-job relationship management — Cameron should be screenshotting this for every sales conversation in Fishers.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The inside sales pod model.

Blue Ladder is building sales pods of 2–4 outside reps backed by an inside sales function handling CRM, follow-ups, and lead management

— this is a leverage model worth studying. When Raptor scales, a dedicated inside sales person feeding Patrick's production calendar could meaningfully increase close rate without adding field headcount.

  1. Financing messaging specificity. Blue Ladder leads with

"plans starting as low as $97/month"

as a concrete objection-handler front-and-center on their homepage. Raptor's premium positioning does not require discounting, but a comparably specific financing call-out (not just "financing available") addresses the fence-sitter before the sales conversation even starts.

  1. Multi-market domain fragmentation as a cautionary tale. Blue Ladder's three-domain structure is a strategic mistake Cameron should avoid as Raptor considers any suburban sub-market expansion. One brand, one root domain, all location pages underneath — the SEO authority compounds, the AI citation footprint grows, and the brand story stays clean.
