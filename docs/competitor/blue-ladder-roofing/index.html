Competitor: Bone Dry Roofing (Indianapolis/Fishers Division)

Refreshed: Thursday, April 25, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1989, Bone Dry started humbly in a small garage in Speedway, Indiana and has since grown into one of the largest residential, family-owned roofing companies in the area.

Leadership includes Gene Judd (President), Matt Tyner (Chief Marketing Officer), Billy Smith (COO), and Tony Merlie (CFO).

The Bone Dry Family of Companies now spans 20 businesses in eight states, including Bone Dry Roofing, Inc.'s 11 Midwest locations, plus Bone Dry Roofing West, G&W Roofing, Roof One, QRS, and others

— making the Fishers address a regional spoke in a sophisticated multi-state machine, not a scrappy local operator.

2. Estimated Scale

The hiring of a new CFO and COO came on the heels of the company's 2023 revenue of over $183 million, with total team count reaching 550 — the highest in company history.

As of March 2026, LeadIQ estimates approximately 240 employees

, suggesting some contraction from the 550 peak — possibly reflecting geographic restructuring or subcontractor reclassification. Revenue confidence: HIGH (trade press-confirmed). Employee count confidence: MEDIUM (LeadIQ vs. Roofing Contractor Magazine discrepancy likely reflects corporate vs. field labor counting methodology).

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Indeed currently lists 79 open Bone Dry Roofing positions.

Active Indianapolis-area postings include

Sales Representatives for the Repairs Division, Roofing Sales Representatives, and Service Technicians.

A commercial Sales Representative role for the Commercial Roofing Division is also live in Indianapolis.

Flag: No marketing, digital, or ops leadership roles are visible for the Indy/Fishers market specifically — suggesting the CMO (Tyner) and digital strategy are being managed centrally from HQ, not at the branch level. That's a structural vulnerability: a centralized marketing team rarely executes with local agility.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

A third-party analysis notes that while Bone Dry maintains consistent traffic due to backlink growth and branding, it is losing organic visibility — with most keywords ranking in positions 50–100 rather than 1–3, a decline attributed to authority-splitting across a high number of site errors.

The site runs on WordPress (confirmed via LeadIQ tech stack), and

maintains an active blog covering maintenance, materials, and storm damage topics.

They have deep location-page architecture (separate URLs for every city × service combination, e.g., bonedry.com/indianapolis/service-areas/fishers-in/roofing/installation/), which earns crawl coverage but dilutes page authority.

Their lead capture is weak — primarily free quote CTAs and inbound calls — and they are missing chat widgets and online booking, which modern buyers expect.

Opportunity for Raptor: Own the top-3 organic positions on Fishers/Noblesville/Zionsville queries where Bone Dry's authority dilution is worst.

Bottom line: Bone Dry holds AI name recognition from industry press, but is not winning the recommended-for-your-home framing that Raptor can own. 8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Bone Dry University / Internal Training as Marketing Content.

Bone Dry University, created in 2021, has conducted over 8,734 hours of live training and completed 18,103+ e-learning modules.

They publish this internally but don't weaponize it publicly enough. Raptor should create visible, customer-facing content about how its crews are trained — short video clips, certification badges on the website, "here's what happens before your crew shows up" content. This speaks directly to Cameron's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning.

  1. Discount/Offer Architecture for First Responders, Military, Teachers.

Bone Dry actively runs discount programs for military members, veterans, senior citizens, first responders, teachers, and healthcare professionals.

This isn't charity — it's a Nextdoor and Facebook group conversion machine. These identity groups share heavily within community feeds. Raptor should formalize this offer with a dedicated landing page and run it as an always-on Facebook/Nextdoor campaign through storm season.

  1. Monitor Their Review Velocity as a Storm-Response Proxy. Bone Dry went from 0 reviews in 7 days to 10 in 30 days — that's a slow trickle, not a storm spike. When that number jumps (e.g., 40+ in 30 days), a major hail event is in their pipeline. Cameron and Patrick should track Bone Dry's GBP review velocity weekly as a real-time storm-job indicator, then surge Raptor's paid media and canvassing in the same zip codes within 48 hours.
