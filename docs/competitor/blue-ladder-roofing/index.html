Competitive Intelligence Brief: Blue Ladder Roofing

Raptor Roofing — Cameron Blakely Dashboard
Refreshed: April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

Blue Ladder Roofing, LLC — owner Austin Chastain — was formally started December 31, 2019, with the BBB file opened February 6, 2020, and has been BBB Accredited since March 4, 2022.

However,

HomeAdvisor copy self-reports that they "started in this industry in 2016,"

suggesting pre-LLC storm-chasing activity before formal incorporation.

They serve homeowners and businesses across Indiana, Central Missouri, and the New Ozarks

— a multi-state expansion posture that distinguishes them from most single-market Indy competitors. Their brand positioning is firmly insurance-claim-first:

they market themselves as "insurance claim specialists, licensed professionals with years of experience delivering top-quality results."

2. Estimated Scale

Employee count: confidence: low.

Indeed estimates salary data from 211 employees, users, and past and present job advertisements

— a figure that inflates actual headcount due to aggregated user data; real W-2 employee count likely sits in the 10–25 range.

The BBB lists the entity as a Sole Proprietorship

, which is a structural indicator of a smaller owner-operated core.

BuildZoom shows a score of 0 with no active verified license on file

, limiting public permit-trail data. Rough annual revenue estimate: $3M–$6M (confidence: low), based on review velocity and multi-state footprint suggesting above-average job volume but limited operational infrastructure.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Blue Ladder Roofing posted a Customer Success Manager (CSM) role on Glassdoor to "support our outside sales team and ensure a seamless customer experience."

This is a meaningful signal: a dedicated inside CSM role indicates they are trying to systematize the handoff between aggressive outside door-knocking sales reps and post-sale customer retention — a pain point surfaced in BBB complaints. No marketing, ops leadership, or field management roles were found on Indeed or LinkedIn in the last 30 days. The CSM hire is the only notable personnel signal and it reads as reactive, not strategic.

4. Off-Google Presence

On Yelp, Blue Ladder Roofing lists under the Indianapolis/Fishers market providing "residential roofing services and storm restoration"

— the Yelp page has only 15 photos and minimal review volume, a significant under-investment relative to their 385 Google reviews. This is classic review-funneling: Google reviews are harvested aggressively while Yelp is effectively ignored, which makes their 4.8 Google rating an artificially clean signal.

On Facebook, the page shows 2,847 likes with 6 people talking

— low engagement for the follower count, suggesting the page is not actively managed for organic reach.

The BBB awards an A+ rating

, but the complaints file tells a different story:

one BBB complaint describes a $24,866 job where "shingles are falling down and the gutters are installed incorrectly"

and

a second complaint describes Blue Ladder missing multiple tarp-removal appointments, and as of the filing date the tarp remained on the roof with no communication from the company.

Zero owner response rate on Google (provided data) aligns with this pattern of post-sale disengagement.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Blue Ladder operates at least three active web properties — blueladderroofs.com, blueladderroofingindy.com, and blueladderroofingin.com — which is a domain authority dilution trap. Splitting link equity and crawl budget across three separate domains suppresses all three from ranking competitively.

The Fishers location page demonstrates geo-specific content strategy, citing local weather challenges like humidity causing mold and wood rot, and positioning Blue Ladder's materials as the solution

— this is above-average local SEO effort for a contractor.

The service area page lists dozens of Indiana suburb pages

(Carmel, Greenwood, Fishers, Noblesville, etc.), indicating a programmatic local-landing-page strategy. However, with a BuildZoom score of 0 and no verified permit history, their backlink profile from authoritative local sources appears weak. No evidence of schema markup, structured FAQ content, or blog posts optimized for informational queries was found.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on query patterns for "best roofers Indianapolis," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," and "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville": Blue Ladder does not appear to be a named citation in current AI-generated answer results from ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini for these queries. Their three-domain split prevents any single property from accumulating the citation authority that GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) requires. Their Roofing Insights "Recognition of Excellence" badge —

awarded by Roofing Insights, backed by a $20,000 guarantee

— is the one third-party credentialing signal that could theoretically appear in AI training data, but it has not translated to AI citation visibility. This is a clear window for Raptor: any competitor not showing up in AI answers is effectively invisible to a growing segment of high-intent shoppers.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Blue Ladder beats Raptor: Insurance-claim workflow is deeply baked into their sales motion — door-knocking reps identify storm leads, walk homeowners through claims, and close on the adjuster's check.

They offer financing if claims fall short, with plans starting as low as $97/month

, reducing price objection friction Raptor may not be matching. Their programmatic suburb landing pages give them broader geographic coverage in organic search.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor carries 664 reviews at a 5.0 — a 279-review volume advantage with a higher star rating and a 0.2-point gap that is meaningful to algorithmic ranking. Blue Ladder's 0.0% owner response rate and active BBB complaints around post-job communication are brand liabilities Raptor can exploit publicly. Raptor's premium positioning ("we don't outsource accountability") is the direct counter-narrative to the BBB complaint pattern here.

Most exploitable weakness: Blue Ladder's post-sale communication collapses — zero owner responses to 385 reviews and documented BBB ghosting — makes them highly vulnerable to a Raptor campaign built around transparent, public owner engagement.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Suburb landing page depth.

Blue Ladder has created individual location pages for dozens of Indianapolis suburbs

from Carmel to Zionsville to Greenwood. Cameron should audit whether Raptor has equivalent geo-targeted pages for each suburb. If not, this is a near-term SEO gap to close before the spring storm season indexes.

  1. Roofing Insights "Recognition of Excellence" badge.

Blue Ladder carries a Roofing Insights Recognition of Excellence award backed by a $20,000 guarantee.

This is a third-party trust signal that appears across their web properties and sales collateral. Cameron should evaluate whether earning this credentialing — or a comparable third-party guarantee badge — adds conversion value on Raptor's estimate pages.

  1. Inside sales / CSM function. Blue Ladder is building a formal bridge between outside sales reps and the customer experience post-contract. Raptor should watch whether this reduces their review velocity decline (currently 0 reviews in 7 days) and close rate. If it does, it's a structural operations change worth mirroring — a dedicated post-sale touchpoint owner who also drives Google review requests is a lever Raptor could activate without a full hire.
