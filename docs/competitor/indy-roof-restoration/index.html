Competitive Intelligence Brief — Indy Roof & Restoration

Refreshed: Tuesday, July 22, 2025 | Prepared for Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Aaron Christy founded Indy Roof & Restoration in 2018.

The company is led by COO Adam Kaminski alongside founder Aaron Christy, and covers central Indiana with both exterior and interior home restoration services.

Their public identity is built squarely around insurance claim navigation and storm restoration —

they position themselves as trusted by real estate agents and residential and commercial customers across a wide Indiana service area

, with volume-of-reviews as their primary credibility signal.

2. Estimated Scale

Employee count:

Clodura.AI lists 11–50 employees, with named roles including a Branch Manager, Marketing Director, Sales Manager, and Project Manager on their org chart.

RocketReach pegs it at 17 employees (confidence: medium).

Growjo reported their employee count grew 38% year-over-year

, which tracks with the active hiring posture detailed below.

Revenue: A LinkedIn post headline references growth from $3M to $10M in a single year (confidence: low-medium — unverified, sourced from a third-party podcast promo). Prospeo's model puts them at $2.4M ARR, but that almost certainly lags reality given review volume. The Clodura range of $11M–$100M is too wide to act on. Best working estimate: $8–12M, consistent with a 17–25 person storm-restoration operation running multiple insurance-claim crews. Confidence: low.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Active recruitment on multiple fronts.

ZipRecruiter shows an active posting for a Lead Water Restoration Technician at $20–$25/hour, full-time, framed as expanding their water restoration division.

Their own careers page is running an open commission-based Field Inspector role, where the primary function is conducting roof and exterior inspections and helping homeowners navigate the insurance claims process.

No marketing, ops leadership, or digital roles were found active in the last 30 days on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter — the hiring push is exclusively field sales and production. That signals they are staffing for job volume, not brand-building, right now.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp has placed a Consumer Alert on their business page warning users about abnormal activity or attempts to mislead them

— this is a material discrepancy against their 4.9 Google rating and 2,308 reviews.

Facebook shows 4,778 page likes with only 11 people actively talking about the page

, suggesting a low organic engagement rate relative to their follower count. No active Facebook Ad Library spend was detected in a current pull — they are not running visible paid social at this time.

BBB accredited since July 5, 2019

, with an A+ rating publicly maintained. The Yelp Consumer Alert is the key off-platform vulnerability: it signals aggressive review-solicitation tactics to Yelp's trust system, which is exactly the kind of ammunition Raptor can use in sales conversations without ever naming a competitor directly.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their tech stack includes Facebook Web Custom Audiences, Facebook Pixel, and WordPress

, a standard but not sophisticated setup.

Their careers page explicitly lists Carmel, Geist, Fishers, Greenwood, Brownsburg, Westfield, and Zionsville as active service areas

, suggesting geo-landing pages exist or are in development for those suburbs. They operate two domains — indyroofandrestoration.com and indyroofcompany.com — which is a split-authority liability; backlink equity is divided rather than consolidated. No evidence of schema markup beyond basic WordPress defaults, no press backlinks from Indianapolis Business Journal or IndyStar surfaced. Their content strategy appears to rely on review volume for Map Pack dominance rather than topical authority or long-form SEO content. That is a copyable gap for Raptor: a structured content cluster around suburb-specific storm damage pages with proper FAQ schema would directly compete with their GBP dominance.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

ServiceAgent.ai — an AI-aggregated directory increasingly cited by generative engines — lists Indy Roof & Restoration prominently, noting their high-volume operation and suggesting their review velocity implies a fleet-style operation with multiple crews.

The platform flags their 4.9-star average across 2,000+ reviews as in the top 5% of Indianapolis providers and calls them "a solid top-3 pick."

This means when ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity pull from aggregator sources to answer "best roofers Indianapolis," Indy Roof & Restoration is likely cited by implication through these third-party summaries. Raptor's 5.0 rating at 690 reviews does not yet appear in these AI-aggregated rankings at comparable frequency — primarily because volume triggers the algorithmic confidence threshold these tools use. Getting Raptor cited in local press (IBJ, IndyStar, Patch) and structured into directories like ServiceAgent is the fastest AEO lever available.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume (2,308 vs. 690) dominates Map Pack positioning and AI aggregator citations. Their insurance claim workflow is systematized and clearly scripted across their field sales team, giving them a repeatable storm-chasing machine that Raptor likely cannot match at current headcount.

Where Raptor beats them: A perfect 5.0 vs. their 4.9 is a meaningful signal at comparable volume — but Raptor's 690 reviews at 5.0 is verifiably cleaner than their 2,308 with a Yelp Consumer Alert on file. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" premium positioning has no direct counter in Indy Roof's marketing. Their 1.0 owner response rate on Google reviews is a visible indifference signal that Raptor can exploit on any platform where homeowners compare.

Most exploitable weakness: The Yelp Consumer Alert combined with a 1.0 owner response rate tells a story of a company optimized for volume acquisition but not relationship retention — Raptor should press that contrast in every sales conversation and review response.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Charity-as-content strategy.

Indy Roof is publicly tied to Brownsburg Little League Softball, Beech Grove High School Marching Band, Habitat for Humanity, and the Shelbyville Animal Shelter.

Each of these is a named, geo-specific backlink and brand mention opportunity. Cameron should identify two or three Indy-north hyperlocal charitable or athletic partnerships — Carmel Dad's Club, Fishers Youth Sports — and make them a content and backlink engine, not just a goodwill gesture.

  1. Dual-domain consolidation as counter-move. They are splitting SEO authority between indyroofandrestoration.com and indyroofcompany.com. Raptor should ensure all brand mentions, directory listings, and backlinks point to a single canonical domain and actively build topical suburb pages that prey on their divided authority.

  2. Water restoration as a revenue extension signal.

Their active hiring of a Lead Water Restoration Technician signals a deliberate expansion into water mitigation as an adjacent revenue line.

This is a classic storm-restoration upsell play. Cameron should assess whether Raptor has a referral or subcontractor relationship with a water mitigation firm to capture that same job-expansion revenue without the overhead of an in-house tech — and market it as a coordinated homeowner solution before Indy Roof can fully build that capability out.
