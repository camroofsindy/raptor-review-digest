Competitor Brief: Indy Roof & Restoration

Raptor Roofing Intelligence Dashboard | Refreshed: June 9, 2025

1. Who They Are

Aaron Christy founded Indy Roof & Restoration in 2018.

The company is led by COO Adam Kaminski alongside founder Aaron Christy

, with

Greg Russell serving as Operations Manager.

They serve residential and commercial customers across a wide Indiana service area, leaning heavily into insurance claim work and real estate agent referral pipelines.

Their primary differentiator is volume — 2,229 Google reviews positions them as the highest-review-count roofing contractor in the Indy market by a wide margin.

2. Estimated Scale

Clodura.AI estimates 11–50 employees

;

SignalHire pegs the number at approximately 30

;

Prospeo estimates 13 employees across 5 departments

— a wide spread that signals heavy use of subcontracted labor versus a fully W-2 crew.

Prospeo estimates annual revenue at approximately $2.4 million

, though this almost certainly understates a company with their review velocity. Confidence: low on both figures — third-party data tools consistently undercount field-heavy contractors. Real revenue is plausibly $5M–$10M+ given their production pace.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Their own careers page at indyroofandrestoration.com/indy-careers-recruitment-page is actively recruiting.

The primary open role is Field Inspector — responsible for roof and exterior inspections — with a commission-based comp structure tied directly to insurance claim navigation.

They advertise cash bonuses paid every Tuesday as a recruiting hook.

The sales department is led by Daniel Ralston (Sales Manager) with 4 reported sales employees

— suggesting they are perpetually building out their canvassing/D2D sales bench rather than investing in inbound marketing talent. No marketing, ops leadership, or digital roles detected on Indeed or LinkedIn in the last 30 days. That is a tell.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp has placed a Consumer Alert on their business page warning users about abnormal activity or attempts to mislead them

— a serious flag. Their Yelp presence shows only 28 reviews against 2,229 on Google, a ratio that is nearly impossible without aggressive review funneling to Google.

Facebook shows 4,783 page likes and they self-describe as having "2000+ 5-star reviews"

— pure social proof amplification.

BBB-accredited since July 5, 2019

, with an A+ rating displayed prominently, but the BBB page contains at least two detailed written complaints including one from April 2025 alleging forged signatures and unresolved payment disputes. The review-funnel discrepancy (2,229 Google vs. 28 Yelp + active Yelp Consumer Alert) is the single most exploitable reputation gap in this market.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their domain indyroofandrestoration.com runs on WordPress with

Facebook Pixel and Facebook Web Custom Audiences confirmed

— meaning they are retargeting site visitors via Meta. They operate suburb-specific landing pages covering

Carmel, Geist, Fishers, Greenwood, Brownsburg, Westfield, and Zionsville

, which is solid local SEO coverage. They have earned local press backlinks through community coverage —

WISH-TV (wishtv.com) ran a feature on their charitable initiatives

, which generates a high-DA backlink most competitors lack. No evidence of structured FAQ schema, HowTo schema, or blog content targeting insurance claim keywords — a gap worth noting. Their AEO posture appears to rely on review volume and brand mentions rather than structured data or authoritative long-form content.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No confirmed, source-cited appearances of Indy Roof & Restoration in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini responses for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville" were surfaced in current search results. Their review volume and WISH-TV press mention give them raw citation signals, but without structured schema, FAQ content, or third-party authoritative mentions (Angi, HomeAdvisor editorial, local news beyond one segment), they are unlikely to be cited by name in AI-generated answers with any reliability. This is a wide-open lane for Raptor — a company with a 5.0 rating, a clear differentiated narrative ("we don't outsource accountability"), and Cameron actively building content authority can outpace them in GEO before Indy Roof invests in it.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume (2,229 vs. 677) gives them dominant Google Map Pack presence and brand recall with price-shopper homeowners. Their suburb landing page network is more built out, and the WISH-TV earned media link is a real SEO asset.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor's 5.0 rating is a perfect score on higher volume than anyone else in the top 10. The Yelp Consumer Alert and BBB complaints are a quality-signal vulnerability that Raptor's "accountability" positioning exploits directly. Owner response rate of 0.9% at Indy Roof is essentially zero — Raptor should be responding to every review.

Most exploitable weakness: The Yelp Consumer Alert for abnormal review activity is a third-party credibility breach that Raptor can reference implicitly by leaning hard into platform-agnostic review transparency and a perfect verified rating.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. WISH-TV and local TV earned media.

Indy Roof earned a WISH-TV segment by tying charitable activity directly to a company spokesperson appearance.

Cameron and Patrick should pursue the same playbook — Toys for Tots, Habitat builds, or a named scholarship — and pitch it directly to WISH, FOX59, and WTHR producers. One local TV segment produces a DA-50+ backlink and AEO citation fodder that money cannot buy.

  1. Commission-Tuesday culture as a recruiting and sales-volume driver.

Indy Roof's weekly cash bonus cadence is a visible recruiting tactic.

The underlying mechanic — frequent, visible performance payouts — accelerates D2D and canvassing output. If Raptor is not already running a structured weekly payout tied to measurable KPIs, this is worth modeling regardless of whether canvassing is in the playbook.

  1. Real estate agent referral infrastructure.

Indy Roof explicitly calls out being "trusted by real estate agents" as a positioning pillar

across their website. Raptor should audit whether it has a formal agent referral program with co-branded inspection reports, referral tracking, and an agent-specific landing page. At Raptor's rating level, a structured realtor referral engine would convert at a higher rate than Indy Roof's version simply because of the star-rating delta.
