Competitor Intelligence Brief — Indy Roof & Restoration

Updated: April 26, 2025 | Prepared for Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Aaron Christy founded Indy Roof & Restoration in 2018.

The company is led by COO Adam Kaminski alongside founder Christy

, and

Greg Russell serves as Operations Manager per BBB records.

Their declared service footprint covers Indianapolis, Carmel, Geist, Fishers, Greenwood, Brownsburg, Westfield, and Zionsville.

Their primary growth engine is insurance-adjacent storm restoration — they market heavily on insurance claim navigation and have built a large referral network with real estate agents.

2. Estimated Scale

Clodura.AI lists 11–50 employees, with named roles including a Branch Manager, Marketing Director, Sales Manager, and Project Manager on file.

Growjo estimates annual revenue at approximately $10.9M with 47 employees

, which is significantly higher than the

Prospeo estimate of ~$2.4M annual revenue.

The Growjo figure is more consistent with a 2,153-review velocity and a multi-role org chart. Revenue confidence: medium. Headcount confidence: medium (Clodura/Growjo data is modeled, not audited). Real headcount is likely 30–50 when field inspectors and sub-crew relationships are included.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Their careers page is actively recruiting commission-based Field Inspectors whose primary function is roof and exterior inspections, with a built-in track into the sales team.

Indeed also shows active Indy Roof & Restoration listings for Water & Mold Restoration Sales roles paying $900–$1,300/week

— a signal they are expanding into interior water mitigation, not just roofing.

A Marketing Director (Nick Segar) and Sales Manager (Daniel Ralston) are already named in their org chart data

, suggesting those seats are filled. The active hiring is concentrated in field sales/inspection, which means they are scaling their lead-gen front end, not their ops or marketing back end. Flag: if they hire a VP of Marketing or a paid media manager in the next 60 days, that changes the competitive picture materially.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp shows 28 reviews at their Elmwood Ave location

— a fraction of their 2,153 Google reviews. That gap is not accidental; it's textbook review-funneling. Their Google funnel is highly optimized; their Yelp profile is essentially untended, which means any homeowner who starts their search on Yelp sees a thin, mixed signal.

Facebook shows 4,777 page likes with 52 currently talking

— modest engagement relative to their review volume.

Their Facebook page notes only 86% recommendation rate across 48 reviews,

another discrepancy vs. Google 4.9.

Nextdoor shows just 16 "Faves" in Indianapolis

— underutilized for a company this size. On BBB,

they have been accredited since July 2019

, and hold an A+ rating, but

active BBB complaints include allegations of offering repair in exchange for review removal and failure to address property damage caused during a project

— legitimate reputation risk if amplified.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

indyroofandrestoration.com is a purpose-built SEO asset.

The site includes a Privacy Policy, Editorial Policy, Accessibility Statement, Review & Fact-Checking Policy, Corrections & Updates Policy, and Terms of Service

— that is a structured content governance stack designed specifically to satisfy Google's E-E-A-T signals and improve AEO eligibility. Most roofing contractors do not have an editorial policy; this is a deliberate play. They operate a blog, a testimonials page, and geo-targeted service area pages covering the key Indy suburbs.

ZoomInfo confirms they run Facebook Pixel, Facebook Web Custom Audiences, and WordPress

— standard paid social retargeting infrastructure.

Industry associations cited include ARMA, NRCA, and RCI

, which are credible backlink and trust-signal sources for roofing-specific SEO. Their domain has age (2018–2019 origin), content depth, and structured policies that give them a meaningful authority edge over most local competitors.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No live AI search was available in this pull due to search-limit constraints. However, based on observable signals: their domain's E-E-A-T infrastructure —

including a published Fact-Checking Policy and Corrections & Updates Policy

— is exactly the schema-adjacent content signaling that Gemini, ChatGPT Browse, and Perplexity use to vet citation-worthy sources. Combined with 2,153 Google reviews, a WISH-TV local press feature, and BBB A+ accreditation, they are almost certainly being cited in AI responses to "best roofers Indianapolis" and "roof replacement Carmel Indiana" queries. Raptor's current AEO exposure on those queries is not yet documented and should be audited immediately against theirs. This is the fastest-moving gap in the competitive set.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume (2,153 vs. 664) dominates every Google Local Pack ranking signal and almost certainly drives their AI citation frequency. Their editorial policy infrastructure is ahead of anything most residential roofers in the market have built. Their insurance-claim navigation positioning gives them a clear hook for storm-damage leads that Raptor does not explicitly own.

Where Raptor beats them: 5.0 star rating vs. 4.9 is a real differentiator at the premium end of the market — particularly with higher-income homeowners in Carmel, Zionsville, and Westfield who read reviews closely. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is harder to attack than a volume-first brand.

IRR has documented BBB complaints involving alleged requests for review removal tied to dispute resolution and property damage claims

— that is a specific, citable service execution gap.

One BBB reviewer reported roofwork already coming up less than a year after installation

— workmanship liability that Raptor's premium positioning directly counters.

Most exploitable weakness: Their Google-to-Yelp review ratio (2,153 vs. 28) signals a review-funnel operation that leaves them exposed on any platform they don't control — and their BBB complaint record contains substantive execution failures that Raptor can indirectly reference through "accountability" messaging without naming them directly.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

1. The Editorial Policy play for AEO. IRR's site includes a published editorial and fact-checking policy — a tactic borrowed from media sites that dramatically improves AI citation eligibility. Cameron should task the web team with adding equivalent structured trust content to raptorroofing.com within 30 days. This is low-cost and high-leverage for generative engine visibility.

2. The 1% revenue-to-charity pledge as a public commitment.

IRR publicly commits 1% of all revenue to Indiana charities including Habitat for Humanity, Toys for Tots, Isiah117, and Fountains of Hope.

This is a documented, named pledge — not a vague "we give back" line — and it converts into local press, Nextdoor goodwill, and referral credibility with real estate agents. Raptor should formalize and name any existing charitable activity with the same specificity.

3. Real estate agent partnership as a structured channel.

IRR explicitly cites being "trusted by real estate agents" as a core part of their service positioning.

Their org chart includes a named Branch Manager,

suggesting they have a dedicated relationship-management function. Raptor should audit whether any systematic RE agent referral program exists and, if not, treat this as a near-term revenue channel — particularly in Carmel and Zionsville where transaction volume and roof replacement rates are high.
