Indy Roof & Restoration — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Refreshed: August 11, 2025 | Raptor Roofing Dashboard

1. Who They Are

Indy Roof & Restoration was founded in 2018 by Aaron Christy

, with

Adam Kaminski serving as COO.

They operate across a wide Indiana service area, working with insurance companies and real estate agents on both residential and commercial accounts.

Their identity is built almost entirely around storm damage restoration and insurance claim navigation — they are an insurance-first shop, not a premium-craft shop, which defines both their volume advantage and their trust ceiling.

2. Estimated Scale

Clodura.AI lists their headcount at 11–50 employees, with named roles including a Branch Manager, Marketing Director, and Sales Manager.

RocketReach separately pegs them at 32 employees.

Best estimate: 25–40 field-plus-office staff. Confidence: medium. On revenue, a LinkedIn post title (visible via search) references growth "from $3M to $10M in a year," and

Clodura places their revenue in the $11M–$100M band

— almost certainly the low end of that range given headcount. Call it $8M–$12M ARR. Confidence: low-medium.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Their careers page actively recruits Field Inspectors — a sales-forward role where the primary job is conducting roof and exterior inspections and helping homeowners navigate the insurance process.

Clodura shows a named Marketing Director (Nick Segar) and Sales Manager (Daniel Ralston) already in seat

, so the current push appears to be expanding the Field Inspector sales force, not adding marketing infrastructure. No new leadership postings on Indeed or LinkedIn in the last 30 days specific to Indy Roof & Restoration were surfaced. Their open-ended careers page (indyroofandrestoration.com/indy-careers-recruitment-page) functions as a perpetual funnel for commission sales reps rather than a response to a specific ops gap.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp has placed a Consumer Alert on their business page to warn users about abnormal activity or attempts to mislead them.

Despite that flag,

their Yelp listing shows only 28 reviews against 84 photos

— a tiny fraction of their 2,329 Google reviews. This is a textbook review-funneling gap: homeowners are clearly being steered to Google and away from platforms where Yelp's filters apply.

Their Facebook page has 4,778 likes with only 11 people currently talking about it

— low engagement relative to page size, suggesting the account is maintained but not actively monetized through content.

They maintain an A+ BBB rating since 2018

, but

recent BBB complaints include a detailed account of forged paperwork and a sales rep who allegedly signed documents without homeowner knowledge

— the kind of complaint that surfaces in insurance-churn sales models. No current Facebook Ad Library activity was returned in search; paid social posture is unclear.

Their tech stack includes Simpli.fi

, which is a programmatic display/geofencing platform — indicating they are running targeted digital advertising beyond Google.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their website runs on

WordPress with Elementor, WP Rocket caching, Cloudflare CDN, and Google Analytics

— a competent but not differentiated stack. They operate two domains: indyroofandrestoration.com and indyroofcompany.com, which splits link equity and creates brand confusion.

ZoomInfo shows they use Facebook Pixel and Facebook Web Custom Audiences

, so retargeting is in place.

Their careers page explicitly names Carmel, Geist, Fishers, Greenwood, Brownsburg, Westfield, and Zionsville as target markets

, suggesting they have geo-targeted landing pages or at minimum geo-aware content for these suburbs. Their review velocity — 2,329 reviews — means Google's algorithm treats them as an established authority in Indianapolis roofing queries. That said, the Yelp Consumer Alert and split domain structure are exploitable credibility and technical SEO weaknesses.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on research into how AI engines synthesize Indianapolis roofing results: Indy Roof & Restoration's review volume (2,329 at 4.9) makes them a likely citation in AI-generated "best roofers Indianapolis" answers, particularly from Gemini and ChatGPT, which heavily weight Google Business Profile data and aggregate review counts when composing local contractor lists. Their brand name is semantically clean and unambiguous for LLMs. However, the Yelp Consumer Alert and the BBB complaint narrative are now indexed content — LLMs trained on or RAG-ing from web sources may surface these as risk signals. The dual-domain issue (indyroofandrestoration.com vs. indyroofcompany.com) creates inconsistent entity signals that can suppress clean AEO citation. This is an area Raptor can exploit by building a tighter, single-entity knowledge graph with consistent NAP, structured FAQ schema, and authoritative local press mentions targeting Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, and Zionsville specifically.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume (2,329 vs. 697) gives them dominant AI citation probability and a perception of scale that closes leads before a conversation starts. Their insurance-first positioning also captures a large storm-damage segment that Raptor may not be optimized to own.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor's 5.0 rating is a clean, uncontested signal — no Yelp Consumer Alert, no BBB complaint narratives, no split-domain confusion. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is a direct counter to the sales-rep churn model that generates Indy Roof's BBB complaints.

Most exploitable weakness: The Yelp Consumer Alert and BBB forged-signature complaint are indexed, crawlable, and increasingly visible to AI engines — Raptor should be proactively building content and third-party citations that contrast its accountability model against exactly these failure patterns.

  1. Community PR as review velocity engine.
