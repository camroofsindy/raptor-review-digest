Competitive Intelligence Brief — White Oak Construction

Updated: Tuesday, May 20, 2025 | Prepared for: Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

White Oak Construction Corporation has been BBB-accredited since August 1992, putting their founding at 1982 — over 43 years in business.

Leadership is James (Sam) Luppino, President, and David Wilson, Vice President.

They serve Indianapolis, Lafayette, and Evansville, Indiana, as well as Sellersburg, Kentucky

, and position themselves as a full home-improvement company (roofing, siding, windows, gutters, doors, and interior remodeling) rather than a pure-play roofing contractor — a meaningfully different competitive posture than Raptor.

2. Estimated Scale

LinkedIn lists their company size as 51–200 employees, and their own LinkedIn profile claims a team of 150+ trained employees.

Confidence: medium — LinkedIn self-reporting at this scale is often aspirational, and Indeed employee review counts suggest a smaller active headcount.

With four physical locations (Indianapolis, Lafayette, Sellersburg, and Warsaw)

, they are operating as a regional multi-branch contractor. No public revenue figure exists; at ~150 employees across four branches in a multi-trade residential model, a $15M–$25M annual revenue estimate is reasonable. Confidence: low — no verified third-party figure available.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Indeed currently shows 5 open jobs at White Oak Construction.

Active postings include Event Services Associate ($16–17/hr) posted within the last 2 days, Survey Collection Specialist ($16–17/hr), and a Sales Representative role (from $100,000/year).

Additional listings visible on Indeed include Sales Representative roles in Sellersburg ($70K–$160K) and Lafayette (from $100K), plus Advertising Canvasser in Indianapolis ($16–17/hr).

No marketing, ops leadership, or digital-specific roles are visible. The heavy canvasser and door-to-door sales hiring signals that their primary lead-gen engine remains offline, high-touch, and field-dependent — a structurally expensive model in a post-COVID market.

4. Off-Google Presence

Google: 1,043 reviews at 4.2 stars (provided). Review velocity has dropped to roughly 1/week as of May 2025 — a meaningful deceleration for a company this size.

Yelp shows 75 reviews with 141 photos as of April 2026

— no star rating surfaced in the snippet, but the presence is thin relative to their Google volume, a classic review-funneling pattern.

BBB shows an average of 3.43 out of 5 stars across 28 customer reviews

— that is a 0.77-star gap below their Google rating, which is a significant credibility discrepancy.

One January 2026 review describes a sales rep staying over two hours and quoting $92K for a roof, then offering to bring it to $65K on the spot

— a high-pressure in-home sales tactic complaint that is damaging on a trust platform like BBB.

BBB complaints: 9 total in the last 3 years, with 1 complaint closed in the last 12 months.

Angi rating is 4.7. No Facebook Ad Library data was retrievable in this refresh cycle — flag for the next pull.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

White Oak has invested meaningfully in on-site content.

Their blog targets high-intent local queries with pages like "How to Compare Roofing Contractors in Indianapolis," covering service areas from Marion County to Brownsburg, Plainfield, Carmel, Greenwood, and Westfield.

They hold a GAF certified contractor listing

, which generates a credible backlink from a manufacturer domain.

Their product differentiation language — "CAT4 wind-rated" systems with a "50-year warranty covering materials AND labor"

— is schema-friendly and appears consistently across multiple service pages, indicating structured content deployment. They are ranking for or targeting queries like "roofing contractors Indianapolis," "roof replacement Indianapolis," and suburb-specific variants. Domain authority is not publicly confirmed this cycle, but their content strategy and multi-location citation footprint suggest a meaningful organic presence. Their key vulnerability: the content reads generic and could apply to any city with a find-replace — no hyper-local depth.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No direct AI citation data was returned in this research cycle. Based on their content architecture — consistent service-area pages, FAQ schema patterns, GAF backlink, and longstanding domain — they have the structural ingredients to surface in AI-generated answers for broad Indianapolis roofing queries. However, their 4.2 Google rating and 3.43 BBB rating create a trust signal gap that generative engines increasingly weigh. Raptor's 5.0 rating at 673 verified reviews is a stronger trust-signal package for AEO, particularly for queries like "best roofer in Indianapolis" or suburb-specific prompts where AI models tend to anchor on rating quality over raw volume.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where White Oak beats Raptor: Raw review volume (1,043 vs. 673), four-location geographic footprint, GAF certification creating manufacturer backlinks, and a 43-year tenure that weighs well in local trust signals. Their multi-trade model also generates repeat-customer touchpoints Raptor does not have.

Where Raptor beats White Oak: 5.0 vs. 4.2 on Google — a full 0.8-star gap that matters enormously in LSA and map-pack rankings. Raptor's 0.92 owner response rate is competitive; White Oak's BBB trail of high-pressure sales complaints (the $92K-to-$65K in-appointment flip) creates an exploitable brand vulnerability in a market where homeowners are comparing contractors on trust, not just price.

Most exploitable weakness: Their documented in-home sales pressure tactics and 3.43 BBB rating create a direct contrast play — Raptor's transparent, premium positioning and perfect rating is the cleaner story in any head-to-head moment.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GAF or manufacturer certification backlinks.

White Oak's GAF listing at gaf.com generates a high-authority manufacturer backlink.

Cameron should audit whether Raptor has equivalent manufacturer directory listings (GAF, Owens Corning, CertainTeed) and claim every eligible one — these are free, high-DA backlinks with zero content lift required.

  1. Service-area content depth at the suburb level. White Oak runs dedicated location pages for Indianapolis, Carmel, Brownsburg, and others. Their execution is thin and templated, but the architecture is right. Raptor should build tighter, genuinely local suburb pages — referencing actual neighborhoods, HOA permit processes, and local weather patterns — that AI models and Google will prefer over find-and-replace copy.

  2. Monitor their canvassing-to-review pipeline.

Indeed employee reviews describe White Oak's canvasser-heavy model as generating "constant churn and turnover."

If their field sales volume drops, their review velocity — already at ~1/week — will crater further. Raptor should track their 30/90-day review cadence monthly; if White Oak stalls below 4.0, an aggressive local PR push (press release, Nextdoor campaign, neighborhood sponsorship) during that window could permanently widen the star-rating gap in the local map pack.
