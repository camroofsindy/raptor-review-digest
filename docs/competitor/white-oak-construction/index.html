Competitive Intelligence Brief: White Oak Construction

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed June 9, 2025

1. Who They Are

White Oak Construction Corporation has been BBB Accredited since 8/19/1992 — making it a 43-year-old operation.

Leadership is President James (Sam) Luppino, VP David Wilson, and Office Manager April Roberts.

They operate across Indianapolis, Lafayette, and Evansville, Indiana, and Sellersburg, Kentucky

, positioning as a broad-scope home improvement contractor rather than a roofing specialist —

roofing, siding, windows, and gutters all carry equal weight in their pitch.

2. Estimated Scale

Growjo estimates White Oak Construction at approximately $48.2M in annual revenue with 151 employees.

Their own LinkedIn profile claims "over 150+ trained employees."

Confidence: medium — the revenue figure is a modeled estimate from Growjo; no audited financials are public.

Four confirmed office locations: Indianapolis, Lafayette, Sellersburg, and Warsaw, IN

— suggesting genuine multi-market infrastructure, not just a landing-page presence.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Indeed currently lists 5 open jobs at White Oak Construction.

Active postings include Event Services Associate ($16–$17/hr) and a Survey Collection Specialist ($16–$17/hr) in Indianapolis — both canvassing/event-marketing roles.

Additional open roles include Sales Representative (from $100K) in Indianapolis and Lafayette, and Installers in Sellersburg.

No marketing director, ops leadership, or digital hire visible. The dominant hiring signal is front-line door-to-door sales and event canvassing — their lead-gen model is clearly labor-intensive and offline-first.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform review picture is fractured in a way that should raise flags. Their Google profile shows 1,047 reviews at 4.2 stars — high volume but meaningfully below a 4.5 threshold.

Yelp shows 75 reviews

— a fraction of their Google count, suggesting reviews are selectively funneled to Google and not organically distributed.

At least one BBB complaint describes a sales rep who stayed at a homeowner's house for over two hours and quoted $92K for a roof, offering to reduce it to $65K if they committed that night

— a textbook high-pressure close that draws consistent negative chatter.

Another BBB complaint describes a "price good today only" same-appointment pressure tactic.

BBB rates them A+

, but complaint patterns describe fulfillment and communication issues. No Facebook Ad Library activity was confirmed in this search cycle — worth a manual check given their canvassing-heavy acquisition model.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

White Oak is producing blog content targeting buyer-funnel queries directly.

Their post "How to Compare Roofing Contractors in Indianapolis" explicitly positions them as the answer at the bottom of a comparison search

— that's a deliberate AEO play.

Their product pages promote CAT4 wind-rated systems with a 50-year labor-and-materials warranty

— language designed to rank for high-intent warranty and storm-damage queries.

They hold a GAF contractor listing

, which provides a credible backlink from a high-DA manufacturer domain. Domain authority signal is moderate for a company this size; their blog is active but thin on depth. Schema markup status was not confirmed — worth a Screaming Frog pull. Their geographic page structure (indianapolis/, carmel/, etc.) mirrors a standard local SEO silo build.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

White Oak Construction does not appear as a named citation in current AI responses for "best roofers Indianapolis," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." Their 4.2 Google rating and the high-pressure sales complaints documented publicly are likely suppressing AI citation. Generative engines tend to surface companies with cleaner review profiles and editorial coverage — neither of which White Oak has in abundance. This is a gap Raptor can actively own with consistent AEO content targeting those exact query strings.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where White Oak beats Raptor: Raw review volume (1,047 vs. 677) gives them more GBP surface area, and their 43-year tenure plus four-location footprint makes them credible to price-comparison shoppers. Their 50-year labor-and-materials warranty is a strong product-level differentiator they market aggressively.

Where Raptor beats White Oak: 5.0 vs. 4.2 is not a rounding error — that's a category-defining gap. Raptor's 0.0% owner response rate from White Oak combined with zero reviews in the last 30 days signals a review engine that has stalled or been deprioritized. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is a direct counter to White Oak's documented high-pressure, rep-driven sales complaints.

Most exploitable weakness: White Oak's same-appointment pressure tactics generate recurring, searchable BBB and Yelp complaints that Raptor can indirectly neutralize by making "no-pressure, transparent process" a front-and-center message in paid and organic content targeting the exact queries where White Oak ranks.

Raptor does not need to replicate that labor-intensive model, but Cameron should ensure Raptor has visibility (sponsored presence, review-generation push) at the same home shows where White Oak is fielding canvassers. Own the digital aftermath of their offline spend.
