Competitive Intelligence Brief: Cox Roofing

Updated: April 26, 2026 | Researched by: Raptor Roofing CMO Dashboard

1. Who They Are

Cox Roofing is a family-owned business based out of Zionsville, Indiana, serving Indiana and Colorado since 2013.

Mishell and Justin Cox are the co-founders and CEOs — Justin brings hands-on construction expertise and also serves as a full-time firefighter, a credential the company leans on publicly for credibility.

The company has been aggressively expanding its national footprint, building offices in Florida, Colorado, Kentucky, and Wyoming, with Illinois next on the target list — operating with a stated "copy and paste" model for new markets.

2. Estimated Scale

Chris Sanson serves as COO, overseeing organizational development and connecting internal and external teams

— suggesting a leadership bench that goes beyond the owners, which is typical of a $5M–$15M operator. LinkedIn shows the company page for COX RESIDENTIAL LLC with a headcount signal in the 11–50 range across all markets, though no precise number is publicly confirmed. Confidence: low. 3. Recent Hiring Signals

No Cox Roofing-specific job postings on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter surfaced in the last 30 days for the Indianapolis market during this research cycle. However, the company's LinkedIn page has historically posted for management-track roles with language like

"COX ROOFING is hiring — if you know anyone that is passionate about roofing and has management experience, send them our way."

The COO's stated mission is notable:

positioning Cox Roofing as "the top exterior restoration contractor in the nation by 2030"

— a signal that leadership and ops hires will accelerate. Watch for sales manager and regional ops postings tied to the Illinois expansion push.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Cox Roofing operates a dedicated Indianapolis service area page at coxroofingsystems.com/indiana/service-areas/indianapolis-in/, positioned as a local family-owned contractor serving Indiana since 2013 and holding Owens Corning Platinum Preferred status.

The site is built on WordPress (per ZoomInfo tech stack) with Cloudflare Rocket Loader for performance — a functional but not elite technical setup. They have built out location-specific service area landing pages across their footprint (Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, Zionsville, Noblesville, Westfield, etc.), which is correct local SEO architecture.

Starting in 2024, Cox began pushing regular educational articles on LinkedIn covering roofing and exterior topics

— content they are also likely distributing to their site blog, building thin topical authority. One notable press hit:

a feature in Towne Post's Torch Indy publication profiling Cox as a "valuable staple of the greater Indianapolis community."

Local press backlinks like Towne Post carry real local authority signal. Their AEO posture — schema markup, FAQ pages, structured answers for AI citation — is not clearly documented in public-facing results and should be treated as a gap to exploit.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No search results from this research cycle confirm Cox Roofing being named as a top-cited result in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini outputs for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." Their multi-location SEO page structure gives them crawlable content that could earn AI citations over time, but the absence of HAAG certification callouts, rich FAQ schema, and authoritative third-party mentions in the results suggests their AEO footprint is underdeveloped relative to their Google review volume. This is a direct opportunity: Raptor producing structured, city-specific FAQ content with LocalBusiness schema has a clear lane to own AI-generated "best roofer in [suburb]" answers that Cox is not currently locking down.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Cox beats Raptor: Multi-vertical scope (commercial, multi-family, painting, siding, drywall) gives them a broader average ticket and cross-sell story; Owens Corning Platinum Preferred certification is a manufactured goods credential Raptor should audit whether it holds or can pursue; and the firefighter founder narrative is a trust shortcut that lands with homeowners emotionally. Their 503 Google reviews with a 4.8 rating shows real volume, though their 7 reviews in the last 30 days is anemic — roughly one-quarter of what a healthy review velocity should look like at this scale.

Where Raptor beats Cox: 664 reviews at a clean 5.0 stars versus Cox's 4.8 is a statistically significant perception gap at the top of the funnel. Raptor's review velocity (7 reviews/30 days for Cox vs. Raptor's current pace) and the 5.0 rating are defensible premium positioning proof points Cox cannot match without a fundamental service delivery change.

Most exploitable weakness: Cox's Yelp presence is nearly empty despite 503 Google reviews — a textbook review-funneling pattern that leaves them exposed on any comparison platform or AI query that weights multi-platform consistency.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The AI content character "IVO." Cox is experimenting with a branded AI persona for roofing education on Facebook. This is ahead of most local competitors. Cameron should evaluate whether a Raptor-branded educational content series — delivered in short video or structured FAQ format — can out-execute this concept and earn more AEO citations by being more structurally optimized (proper schema, authoritative tone, city-specific targeting).

  2. Local press placement strategy.

Cox earned a Towne Post Torch Indy feature explicitly positioning them as a community staple

— a backlink and brand mention that carries real local authority weight. Raptor should be pitching Carmel Monthly, Westfield Washington Township publications, and IBJ's home services coverage for similar earned media that builds both domain authority and AI citation probability.

  1. The national expansion signal as a local positioning counter.

Cox's stated goal is to have more offices than any other roofing company in the nation while keeping individual offices "small enough to maintain culture."

As they scale into Illinois and beyond, their local attention will fracture. Raptor should sharpen its "Indianapolis-only, we don't spread thin" messaging now — before Cox's growth story becomes a local liability they haven't started defending against yet.
