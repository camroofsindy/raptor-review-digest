Cox Roofing — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: June 30, 2025

1. Who They Are

Cox Roofing is a family-owned business serving Indiana and Colorado, founded in 2013.

Owners Justin and Mishell Cox are the driving forces behind the business — Justin brings hands-on construction expertise while Mishell leads business development and customer service.

Their brand identity leans into "Firefighter Founded | Family Owned | Certified Women & Minority Owned," a differentiation play that gives them a story most roofing contractors lack.

They operate out of two Indianapolis-area offices (Zionsville and Brownsburg) plus a Denver, CO location, and are pushing commercial, multi-family, and residential simultaneously — a wide net that creates both reach and operational risk.

Their website (coxroofingsystems.com) has dedicated service-area landing pages built out for Indianapolis

and surrounding suburbs including Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, and Zionsville — a standard local SEO play.

They announced a 2024 initiative to publish regular blog/article content on roofing, siding, and guttering topics

They highlight NRCA membership and OSHA certification

likely generates some manufacturer backlink authority. No evidence of schema markup, local press backlinks, or structured FAQ content targeting AI-readable queries — an exploitable gap. Domain authority is likely in the 20–30 range given their content volume and backlink profile; no third-party DA data surfaced directly.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on searches run across ChatGPT and Gemini for "best roofers Indianapolis," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," and "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville" — Cox Roofing does not appear to be consistently cited by name in AI-generated answers. Their SEO infrastructure (service area pages, blog content) is present but lacks the structured entity signals — FAQ schema, authoritative local press mentions, citation consistency across directories — that drive AI answer inclusion. This is a material gap for them and an opportunity for Raptor to build the AEO content layer (structured Q&A pages, schema-marked service pages, third-party citation volume) that will increasingly determine who gets named in LLM responses first.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Cox beats Raptor: Multi-state footprint (Indiana + Colorado) gives them a larger total addressable market and storm-chasing scale; their "Firefighter Founded / Women & Minority Owned" identity story is genuinely differentiated and earns media-friendly PR angles that a standard premium-residential brand cannot easily replicate. Their Inc. 5000 appearances (2019–2022) signal real growth credibility.

Where Raptor beats Cox: 681 reviews vs. 521, a full 5.0 vs. 4.8 rating, and — critically — owner response rate. Cox's 1.0 response rate on Google reviews is indistinguishable from zero engagement; Raptor's active owner response posture signals accountability in a way Cox's brand promise ("Trust, Integrity, Roofing") simply does not execute on in public. BBB complaints documenting follow-through failures confirm the gap is real, not just perception.

Most exploitable weakness: Cox talks accountability loudly in marketing copy but their near-zero owner review response rate and documented post-job follow-through complaints are a visible, provable contradiction — Cameron should make Raptor's response discipline a named, marketed differentiator.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Identity-anchored storytelling. Cox's "Firefighter Founded" origin story drives trust signals in a commoditized market. Raptor should formalize and amplify Cameron's personal founder story and the premium-accountability positioning into a repeatable narrative across video, GBP posts, and social — giving homeowners a "why" beyond price and stars.

  2. Service-area landing page architecture.

Cox builds dedicated location pages covering individual suburbs

like Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, and Zionsville. Raptor should audit whether its own suburb-level pages are thinner in keyword depth or structured data — and close the gap, especially targeting Greenwood, Westfield, and Avon where Cox has lighter footprints.

  1. Momnt financing integration.

Cox's website prominently features a partnership with Momnt for financing plans.

Embedded financing at the proposal stage is a conversion lever that neutralizes price objections before they kill a close — worth evaluating whether Raptor's current sales process has a comparable embedded option or leaves that conversation to the homeowner to figure out on their own.
