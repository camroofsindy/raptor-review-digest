Cox Roofing — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Refreshed: May 19, 2025 | Next refresh: May 22, 2025

1. Who They Are

Cox Roofing, also known as Cox Residential, is a family-owned roofing contractor based in Zionsville, Indiana, founded in 2012 and established as a trusted provider of roofing solutions in the Indianapolis area.

The company is family-owned, minority-owned, and women-owned, with locations across three states — Indiana, Kentucky, and Colorado.

They hold Owens Corning Platinum Preferred contractor status and an A+ BBB rating

, and they have diversified well beyond shingles into siding, gutters, painting, drywall, commercial, and multi-family — making them one of the broader-scope exterior contractors in the Indy market.

2. Estimated Scale

With a workforce of 11 to 50 employees, the company emphasizes quality workmanship and exceptional customer service.

Confidence: medium (sourced from Inven.ai company profile; LinkedIn does not surface a precise headcount).

Indeed salary data is estimated from 27 employees, users, and past and present job advertisements

, which loosely corroborates the lower end of the 11–50 band.

BBB lists Cox Roofing at 3 locations.

Confidence: medium. Revenue estimate: at 508 Google reviews over roughly 12 years of operation, with multi-state coverage and a diversified service mix, a rough estimate of $5M–$10M annual revenue is reasonable for an operation this size — but no public filing or ZoomInfo record was found to pin it tighter. Confidence: low.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Cox Roofing currently has 22 open jobs listed on Indeed.

That is an abnormally high open-role count for a sub-50-person company — a signal of either aggressive expansion or high turnover.

A self-described Chief Operating Officer at Cox Roofing posted on LinkedIn about building out a dedicated supplement department

, which indicates they are investing in insurance claim maximization infrastructure — a direct revenue play. No discrete marketing director or CMO-level post was found in the last 30 days, but the volume of open roles suggests active ops and field-level hiring. The COO's stated mission —

"to be the top Exterior Restoration contractor in the nation by 2030"

— is worth flagging as an ambition signal.

4. Off-Google Presence

Cox Roofing's Yelp listing is registered at 10837 Andrade Dr, Zionsville, IN 46077, with 24 photos

— a thin photo presence for a company with 508 Google reviews, and no visible star rating surfaced in search snippets, which typically means very few Yelp reviews. That gap (500+ Google reviews, almost no Yelp traction) is a textbook review-funneling pattern — they almost certainly direct customers straight to Google and do not work the Yelp funnel.

Their Facebook page (Cox Roofing, Zionsville) has 2,864 likes and 17 people currently talking about it

— decent follower count but minimal active engagement. Notably,

they introduced a persona called "IVO" on Facebook — described as "your roofing expert at Cox Roofing" — positioned to deliver tips, insights, and educational content.

This is an AI-character or mascot content play worth watching.

Cox Roofing has been BBB Accredited since April 14, 2017

, with the A+ rating intact. No Facebook Ad Library data surfaced in results, suggesting either no active paid social or minimal spend there.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Cox Roofing operates coxroofingsystems.com and a legacy domain coxres.com, which creates a split-authority problem — backlinks and indexing are likely fragmented across two root domains.

Their Indianapolis service-area page claims service since 2013 and Owens Corning Platinum Preferred status

, which is solid schema fodder, but the page itself is thin on content depth. They have built out geo-targeted service-area pages (Indianapolis, Zionsville, etc.) and category pages for commercial, multi-family, and residential, which is a correct structural approach.

Third-party sites like Cedur.com are citing Cox Roofing in "best roofers in Indiana" roundup articles

, which generates referral backlinks — a meaningful AEO and DR signal. The dual-domain architecture and lack of deep blog content are the most exploitable weaknesses from Raptor's perspective.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on available search results, Cox Roofing is being cited by name in third-party editorial roundups that generative engines pull from. Specifically,

Inven.ai's "Top 20 Roofing Companies in Indiana" names Cox Roofing explicitly, describing their full service mix

— and that type of structured list is exactly what ChatGPT and Gemini cite when answering "best roofers Indianapolis" queries.

Cedur.com also names Cox Roofing in their Indiana best-roofers editorial

, another AI-readable citation source. Cox does not appear to have published any AEO-specific content (FAQ schema, Q&A pages, structured "how to choose a roofer" guides) on their own domain, meaning their AI visibility is dependent on third-party lists rather than owned content — a fragile position that Raptor can undercut by building stronger first-party AEO content.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Cox beats Raptor: Sheer review volume — 508 reviews vs. Raptor's 673, but the gap is closing and Cox has broader brand recognition from 12+ years in market. Their three-state footprint, Owens Corning Platinum Preferred status, and multi-family/commercial verticals give them a wider revenue surface and more referral entry points than a residential-only operator. The "IVO" mascot/AI persona on social is a differentiated content play Raptor has not matched. Their 0.95 owner response rate is strong but Raptor's 5.0 star rating at comparable volume is a harder number to argue with.

Where Raptor beats Cox: Rating is unambiguous — 5.0 vs. 4.8 at scale. Raptor's review velocity (5 in 7 days vs. Cox's 9 in 30 days) is meaningfully faster right now, meaning Raptor is pulling away in recency signals that Google's local algorithm weights heavily. The most exploitable weakness: Cox's dual-domain SEO architecture and near-zero Yelp presence indicate an underdeveloped digital infrastructure that a focused AEO and off-Google reputation push from Raptor could outflank quickly.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The "IVO" persona / educational content character.

Cox introduced a named roofing expert persona on Facebook to deliver tips and insights.

Whether this is AI-generated or a real person-branded approach, it creates a content engine that builds algorithmic trust. Cameron should consider a parallel play — a named, credentialed voice (human or brand persona) producing short-form educational content that feeds both social and AEO citation pools.

  1. Owens Corning Platinum Preferred as conversion copy. Cox leans on this credential explicitly on every service-area page. If Raptor holds or can pursue comparable manufacturer certification, deploying it above the fold on geo-targeted pages is a proven trust trigger that also feeds AI-answer citations.

  2. Multi-family and commercial as a review-volume accelerator.

Cox provides comprehensive multi-family roofing services across Indiana and Colorado.

Commercial and multi-family jobs generate reviews from property managers who are often repeat clients and prolific reviewers. If Raptor is not actively soliciting reviews from any commercial work, that segment is an untapped review-volume lever worth pursuing.
