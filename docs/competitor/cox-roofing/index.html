Competitive Intelligence Brief: Cox Roofing (Cox Residential LLC)

Refreshed: August 11, 2026 | Prepared for Cameron Blakely, Raptor Roofing

1. Who They Are

Cox Roofing was established in 2013 as Cox Residential Roofing, based in the Carmel/Zionsville area.

The company is owned by Justin Cox, with Mishell Cox serving as Manager of Business Development.

They serve Indiana and Colorado with residential, commercial, and multi-family roofing, making them a multi-state operator — unusual for an Indy-market competitor.

Their public identity leans into the "firefighter-owned, trust-first" narrative and an Owens Corning Platinum Preferred designation, which they treat as a primary trust signal in their marketing.

2. Estimated Scale

Cox Roofing Systems describes itself as "one of the fastest-growing roofing companies in the country, recognized on the Inc. 500 list multiple years in a row."

Their LinkedIn company page shows 263 followers

, and visible employee profiles on LinkedIn number in the 10–20 range for Indiana operations.

BBB lists Cox Roofing at 3 locations.

Given multi-state operations, Inc. 500 recognition, and a multi-location footprint, estimated annual revenue is in the $5M–$12M range. Confidence: medium — no ZoomInfo or BuildZoom permit data surfaced to pin this tighter.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Cox Residential Roofing has an active Indeed posting for a Sales Manager — described as a leadership role responsible for "directly impacting results, growing the business while embracing Cox's core values."

This is a significant flag: a dedicated Sales Manager hire signals they are trying to systematize and scale their sales org, not just add another rep.

A prior LinkedIn post also advertised an accounting/payroll opening requiring a bachelor's degree in business or finance, an in-office role.

No dedicated marketing, SEO, or digital ads hires are visible in the last 30 days — their marketing infrastructure appears to remain thin at the leadership level.

4. Off-Google Presence

On Yelp, Cox Roofing is listed at 10837 Andrade Dr, Zionsville, IN 46077

with minimal review volume — only 24 photos and no visible Yelp review count surfaced, a stark contrast to their 523 Google reviews. This is a classic review-funneling pattern: heavy Google investment, near-zero Yelp cultivation.

On BBB, they have been accredited since April 14, 2017,

and carry an A+ BBB rating.

No notable negative complaint threads surfaced on BBB. No Facebook Ad Library activity was confirmed in this pull.

LinkedIn activity shows participation in local events like the Suburban Indy Show at Grand Park Sports Campus,

suggesting some community/partnership play is in motion.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Cox operates coxroofingsystems.com, a purpose-built, multi-page site with

dedicated service-area pages for Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, Westfield, and other Indy suburbs, plus service-line landing pages for residential, commercial, and multi-family.

This is a structured, SEO-intentional architecture.

They announced in 2024 that Cox Roofing would begin publishing regular blog/article content

covering roofing materials, gutters, and seasonal topics — a content cadence that builds topical authority over time, even if the posts are generic.

The Owens Corning Platinum Preferred designation

likely earns them a backlink from owenscorning.com, which is a legitimate domain-authority signal. No confirmed schema markup or AEO-optimized FAQ structure was identified, but their service-area page depth is their primary SEO weapon.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No paid AI-engine audit tool was available in this pull, but based on site architecture and known GEO ranking factors: Cox has meaningful generative engine risk for Raptor. Their multi-location, multi-state footprint and Inc. 500 citation create the kind of third-party reference signals (named entities, press mentions) that LLMs like ChatGPT and Gemini index when answering "best roofers Indianapolis" or "roofing company Carmel Indiana."

Their Inc. 500 recognition as "one of the fastest-growing roofing companies in the country"

is exactly the type of credential that surfaces in AI-generated recommendation summaries. Raptor's 5.0 star rating and volume advantage (697 reviews) are stronger trust signals for AI retrieval — but Raptor needs third-party editorial mentions (press, awards, local news) to compete on generative engine recall.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Cox beats Raptor: Multi-state brand scale, Inc. 500 credibility, Owens Corning Platinum designation as a sales tool, a deeper service-area page architecture for SEO, and a more diversified service menu (commercial, multi-family, painting, drywall) that opens them to larger average ticket sizes. Their Sales Manager hiring signals a maturing sales process.

Where Raptor beats Cox: 697 reviews vs. 523 at a perfect 5.0 vs. Cox's 4.8 — that 0.2-star gap is meaningful at the top of the rating scale and increasingly matters in AI retrieval ranking. Raptor's owner response rate and review velocity tell a tighter accountability story. Cox's Yelp presence is essentially abandoned, suggesting their review strategy is entirely Google-dependent.

Most exploitable weakness: Cox's review velocity has flatlined — only 10 reviews in 90 days signals either slowing job volume, a broken post-job review capture system, or both. Raptor should press hard on review volume now while the gap is widening.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Service-area page depth. Cox's subdirectory structure (/indiana/service-areas/[city]/) is the right move for local SEO. Cameron should audit whether Raptor has equivalent depth for Greenwood, Westfield, Zionsville, and Noblesville — these are pages Cox is winning on, and they're defensible with better content.

  2. Owens Corning Platinum as a conversion tool.

Cox leads with their Owens Corning Platinum Preferred status

across every surface. If Raptor carries a manufacturer designation, it needs to be front-loaded on the homepage, GMB profile, and every sales deck — not buried in the footer.

  1. Multi-family and commercial as a secondary revenue channel.

Cox explicitly markets comprehensive multi-family roofing services across Indiana and Colorado

as a growth lane. Cameron should evaluate whether Raptor is leaving B2B and property management revenue on the table by staying purely residential-focused in its messaging.
