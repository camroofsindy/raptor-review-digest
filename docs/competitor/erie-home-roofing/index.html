Erie Home Roofing — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: June 25, 2026

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1982, Erie Home grew from Midwest roots into a national direct-to-consumer re-roofing and home improvement platform, handling everything from lead generation to final installation.

Headquartered in Toledo, Ohio, the company is now led by CEO Jenilee Common — formerly VP/GM of Roofing Components at Owens Corning — while founder Pat Trompeter serves as Chairman of the Board.

The Indianapolis office serves Noblesville, Avon, Zionsville, Westfield, and surrounding Central Indiana communities

, and the product mix includes both metal and asphalt shingle systems with a lifetime warranty as the core sales anchor.

2. Estimated Scale

Erie Home operates more than 90 locations across 37 states nationally.

In 2022, the company generated $448 million in revenue from 58 offices

— implying the national run rate is likely well north of $500M today given documented expansion. The Indianapolis location is one branch of that corporate machine; estimating the local office at $8–15M annually is reasonable given typical Erie branch economics. Confidence: medium (branch-level revenue not publicly disclosed).

Erie Home has 6,697 LinkedIn followers nationally

; Indianapolis-specific headcount is not broken out but a branch office typically runs 15–35 field and sales staff. Confidence: low for local headcount.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Erie Home currently lists 166 open roles nationally on Indeed, with Field Marketer and Sales Representative being the most frequently advertised Indianapolis-area positions.

Active ZipRecruiter postings for Indianapolis specifically are targeting Field Marketing canvassers

— door-to-door lead generation paid on a base-plus-bonus structure.

Indiana postings also include subcontract roofing crew leads (up to $2,000 sign-on bonus) and Sales Representatives ($3,000 quick-start incentive).

No marketing leadership, ops director, or digital roles flagged for the Indianapolis market in the last 30 days — the hiring pattern is pure top-of-funnel volume, not capability building.

4. Off-Google Presence

Erie Home holds a BBB A+ accreditation under the legal entity Erie Construction Mid-West, LLC

, which is the cleanest part of their off-Google profile. The Birdeye aggregate shows

384 reviews across sources

, but Google (278 reviews, 4.6 stars per Cameron's data) tells the real story: zero reviews in the last 90 days. That is a dead review funnel for a national brand with an active sales floor — almost certainly a sign of a corporate review-management shutdown or GBP suppression issue.

Third-party review sites surface complaints about aggressive high-pressure sales tactics and quotes exceeding $102K for a 47-square roof.

At least one Birdeye reviewer flagged Erie's lending partners as "predatory."

Facebook Ad Library activity is consistent with a company running national campaign templates locally —

at least one customer review confirms conversion via Facebook campaign

, confirming paid social is part of their lead mix. No meaningful Nextdoor or Reddit presence specific to Indianapolis found.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Erie Home's SEO posture is corporate-template heavy. The Indianapolis location page at eriehome.com/locations/roofing/indianapolis-in/ is a thin, boilerplate location page with keyword repetition and no locally differentiated content — the kind of page Google's helpful content updates have been devaluing since 2023.

The brand carries third-party credibility from a Qualified Remodeler "#10 Remodeler in USA" designation

, which is cited on-page as social proof, but that's a national vanity ranking, not a local authority signal. There is no evidence of Indianapolis-specific blog content, local backlinks from Indy press, or schema markup tailored to the local branch. The domain authority of eriehome.com is high by virtue of national scale, but the Indianapolis subpage does not leverage it meaningfully. AEO posture is weak at the local level — their content architecture is too generic to generate tight vector embeddings for Indianapolis-specific queries.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Erie Home does surface in AI-generated responses for broad national queries about re-roofing and metal roofing, primarily because of their domain age, national footprint, and brand mentions in trade publications like Roofing Contractor and Qualified Remodeler. However, for hyper-local Indianapolis prompts — "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville" — Erie Home is not a dominant AI citation. AI engines building local roofing answers tend to weight Google review volume, recency, and locally authoritative content;

roofing websites focused on a specific service and service area generate tighter vector embeddings than general contractors servicing the entire country.

Erie's national-template pages work against them here. The 90-day review cliff (zero new reviews) further suppresses their recency signal in retrieval-augmented generation. This is a material opening for Raptor.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Erie beats Raptor: Brand scale, financing infrastructure (third-party $0-down offers), a metal roofing product line that Raptor doesn't carry, and national corporate backing that lets them absorb marketing spend at a level a local operator cannot match. Their BBB A+ and Birdeye aggregate volume give them credibility signals at the top of the funnel.

Where Raptor beats Erie: 681 reviews vs. 278 — more than double the volume — at a 5.0 vs. 4.6 star rating, with a live and accelerating review cadence vs. Erie's 90-day flatline. Owner responsiveness is a core Raptor differentiator; Erie's owner response rate is listed as none. Raptor's accountability positioning directly counter-programs Erie's known weakness:

Erie customers have flagged extreme reluctance from sales reps to allow a 24-hour decision window

and post-sale follow-through failures.

Most exploitable weakness: Erie's high-pressure, one-night-close sales model generates a steady drip of negative word-of-mouth that Raptor can weaponize in sales conversations and review responses without ever naming the competitor.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The $0-down financing offer as a lead magnet.

Erie actively promotes "$0 down on a new roof" as a campaign hook.

Whether or not Raptor wants to compete on financing terms, Cameron should understand what financing partners Erie is using and be able to articulate Raptor's own financing story clearly — because price-anchoring to Erie's high quotes is an opportunity in the close.

  1. Field marketer canvassing as a lead channel.

Erie runs a structured Field Marketing team doing neighborhood canvassing on a base-hourly-plus-bonus model

— not commission-only. This is a scalable, trainable door-knocking operation that generates owned leads outside of Google. If Raptor isn't running a structured canvass program post-storm, Erie is capturing those conversations first.

  1. LeafFilter partnership cross-sell.

Erie is actively upselling LeafFilter gutter protection alongside roofing projects

, bundling a referral/partnership product into the ticket. This increases average job value and creates a co-marketing relationship. Cameron should explore whether a comparable local gutter, HVAC, or window partner could serve the same function for Raptor without the corporate mandate overhead.
