Rees Restoration — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: June 30, 2026

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2014 by Johnny Rees, Rees Restoration is headquartered in Greenwood, IN and serves the greater Central Indiana market.

Per BBB filings, ownership is listed as Johnny Rees (Owner) and Amanda Rees (Manager), making this a family-run operation.

They position as a "one-stop shop" for exterior renovations, leaning heavily on their OC Platinum Preferred and James Hardie Preferred contractor designations

— and they claim a differentiator most Indy roofers don't:

they market themselves as the only roofer in Central Indiana using HOVER technology, creating precise 3D models from photos for remote measurement and estimating.

2. Estimated Scale

Indeed salary data is estimated from only 5 past and present job postings

, which suggests a smaller core office staff. LinkedIn's company page returns no verifiable headcount.

BuildZoom permit records show 32 permitted projects on file

— likely understated for their volume given Indiana's lax permitting requirements. Based on 580 Google reviews accumulated over 11 years, multi-line sales rep activity visible across reviews (Pete Oaks, Eric Voss, Sam Gorall, Chris, Micah Coyle, Ryan Clark, Randy Wilson), and full exterior services scope, estimated employee count is 15–30, with annual revenue likely in the $5M–$10M range. Confidence: low — no ZoomInfo or Dun & Bradstreet data surfaced; estimate is inference from review volume and permit activity.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Their careers page is live and actively soliciting applicants, framing the company as a growing team that "values growth, innovation, and collaboration."

No specific named roles with posting dates were found on Indeed or LinkedIn in the last 30 days for Rees Restoration specifically — broader Indianapolis restoration job boards are active but not Rees-branded listings. The volume of named sales reps appearing across recent reviews (at least 6–7 distinct reps) suggests an active outside sales team that is already at scale, not currently expanding in marketing or ops leadership. No marketing, digital, or ops leadership postings detected in the last 30 days.

4. Off-Google Presence

Nextdoor is a genuine strength: Rees holds "Neighborhood Favorite" status in 18 neighborhoods spanning Bargersville, Greenwood, Franklin, Indianapolis, and Avon.

That is a real earned-media moat that takes years to build.

On BBB they hold an A+ rating and an Angi "Super Service Award."

However, BBB complaints tell a different story:

at least one complaint shows the business "responded to the dispute but failed to make a good faith effort to resolve it,"

and

a separate complaint documents a warranty issue where a customer tried contacting their sales rep repeatedly over 6 months with no reply.

On Facebook, the page exists at facebook.com/Reesroofingrestoration but no paid ad creative was surfaced via Ad Library — no evidence of active Facebook/Meta paid campaigns. Yelp shows a profile exists but ratings data was not publicly returned in search results. The 0.0% owner response rate on Google (580 reviews, zero responses) is a critical red flag that aligns with the BBB complaint pattern of post-sale disengagement.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their domain (reesrestorationindy.net) has built a wide geographic footprint with dedicated city landing pages — the Indianapolis roof replacement page, the general roofing page, and a repair page all exist as distinct URLs, which is solid local SEO architecture.

They claim 20+ years of industry experience and hold OC "Top of the House," Platinum Preferred, GAF Low-Slope Certified, and Mule Hide Certified designations

, all of which are credentialing content that feeds E-E-A-T signals. Blog content exists (attic insulation, ice dams, ventilation topics were indexed). No evidence of structured schema markup (LocalBusiness, Review, FAQPage) was confirmed in returned page source.

Their BuildZoom score of 105 ranks them in the top 8% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors

— that's a credible third-party authority signal Google can read. No backlinks from local press (Indianapolis Star, IBJ, Patch) were detected. Their HOVER technology page is a potential featured-snippet target for "how do roofers measure a roof" type queries — Raptor should check if they're owning that SERP.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current search result patterns and the data surfaced, Rees Restoration has the asset profile — volume of reviews, city landing pages, OC Platinum Preferred status, HOVER differentiator — that AI engines tend to cite when surfacing mid-tier regional roofers. Their HOVER claim ("only roofer in Central Indiana using HOVER technology") is exactly the kind of distinctive, specific, quotable claim that gets pulled into generative answers. However, their 4.7 star rating, 0% owner response rate on Google, and BBB complaint record create trust signal gaps that better-optimized competitors can exploit in AI visibility. Cameron should run live queries in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity for "best roofing contractors Indianapolis," "roof replacement Greenwood Indiana," and "roofing company Carmel Indiana" to see if Rees is being cited by name. If they are appearing, the HOVER claim is likely the hook — Raptor needs a comparably quotable, specific differentiator to counter it in AEO content.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Rees beats Raptor: Nextdoor neighborhood penetration (18 neighborhoods vs. likely far fewer for Raptor) and full exterior services scope (siding, windows, doors, gutters, commercial) give them a broader referral surface. Their HOVER technology claim is a single, memorable differentiator they actively market across all pages.

Where Raptor beats Rees: 681 reviews at 5.0 vs. 580 at 4.7 is a meaningful gap — Raptor's review profile is objectively stronger by both volume and rating. Raptor's 0% owner response rate vs. whatever Raptor's actual response cadence is represents a reputation management vulnerability Rees has completely abandoned. The BBB complaint pattern and post-sale unresponsiveness documented across multiple channels signal an ops/accountability problem that Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning directly exploits.

Most exploitable weakness: Rees has 580 Google reviews and responds to zero of them — a sitting target for any prospect who Googles "Rees Restoration reviews" and lands on the BBB complaint page or a warranty horror story.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Steal the Nextdoor playbook, systematically.

Rees holds Neighborhood Favorite status in 18 neighborhoods

— that didn't happen by accident. They have reps actively asking customers to recommend them on Nextdoor post-job. Cameron should build a Nextdoor ask into Raptor's post-install follow-up sequence, targeting Carmel, Fishers, Zionsville, and Noblesville neighborhoods specifically.

  1. Counter the HOVER claim with a Raptor-specific tech differentiator.

Rees markets HOVER as producing "precise 3D models from simple photos" for estimates.

Whether or not Raptor uses HOVER or a comparable tool, the lesson is that a single named technology claim drives AI citations and prospect recall. Raptor needs one ownable, named process claim on every page.

  1. Weaponize the response rate gap in content and ads. Zero owner responses across 580 reviews is a publicly visible accountability failure. A targeted Google or Facebook ad campaign — or even an organic post — positioning Raptor's owner-response commitment directly answers the question Rees's prospects are silently asking after reading a BBB complaint. Cameron and Patrick should make this concrete: "Every review gets a personal response within 24 hours" is a claim Rees literally cannot match right now.
