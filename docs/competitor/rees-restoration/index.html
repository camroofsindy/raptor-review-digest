Competitive Intelligence Brief: Rees Restoration

Raptor Roofing | Updated August 11, 2026

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2014 by Johnny Rees, Rees Restoration was built around the goal of becoming the premier restoration company in Central Indiana.

The BBB lists Johnny Rees as Owner, Amanda Rees as Manager, and Sean Guilfoy as Business Manager, with operations based out of Greenwood, IN and serving the broader Indianapolis metro.

Their key differentiation pitch is holding Owens Corning "Top of the House" Platinum Preferred status plus GAF Low-Slope and Mule-Hide certifications

— positioning themselves as a multi-manufacturer certified full-exterior shop rather than a pure roofing play.

2. Estimated Scale

Salary.com puts headcount at 11–20 employees.

LinkedIn shows only 81 followers

, which is anemic for a company this age and suggests minimal investment in professional brand-building.

Salary.com estimates annual revenue at $1M–$5M.

Given their review velocity — 582 Google reviews over roughly 10 years — the lower end of that band ($2–3M) is the more credible estimate for a company this size in the Indy market. Confidence: low (no ZoomInfo or BuildZoom financial data available; third-party revenue estimates for small private contractors are notoriously wide).

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Indeed reflects only 5 past and present job postings total for Rees Restoration in Indiana

— no active listings surfaced in the last 30 days at the time of this pull.

Their careers page exists but lists no current open roles

, and their LinkedIn company page shows no job postings. No marketing, sales leadership, or ops hires are visible in the last 30 days. This is a flat-hiring posture, which aligns with a company coasting on storm-chase revenue rather than building infrastructure for growth.

4. Off-Google Presence

On BBB they hold an A+ rating and claim an Angi "Super Service Award."

However, the BBB complaint section tells a different story.

At least one complainant fired their contractor, then described encountering an "arrogant and accusatory supervisor" — and alleged the company does not honor contracts.

A separate BBB complaint flags a contract with

no dollar amounts listed — the contract price field reportedly showed "$PRICE HERE"

— which a customer alleged violated Indiana's Home Improvement Contract Act.

GuildQuality has at least one verified reviewer from Carmel (Jul 2022) calling the company "extremely unprofessional."

No active Facebook Ad Library spend was surfaced. LinkedIn is essentially dormant at 81 followers. The gap between 582 Google reviews at 4.7 stars and the texture of off-platform complaints is a classic review-funneling signal.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Rees has deployed HOVER technology and uses it as a visible website feature — "precise 3D models from simple photos" for remote estimates

— which is a smart content and conversion hook. Their site (reesrestorationindy.net) has dedicated city/service landing pages (Indianapolis, Carmel, etc.) and service-specific pages for roofing, siding, windows, gutters, and commercial. Blog content is thin and appears SEO-templated rather than authoritative.

Their BuildZoom score of 105 ranks in the top 8% of Indiana licensed contractors

, which helps with trust signals on third-party directories. No evidence of local press backlinks or earned media. Domain authority is likely mid-range for a 10-year-old local contractor site — competitive for map-pack terms but unlikely to challenge on high-intent organic head terms without a stronger content investment.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No specific citation of Rees Restoration was found in generative AI results for key queries including "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." This is an exploitable gap. AI engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) currently favor contractors with strong review signals on multiple authoritative platforms, cited local press coverage, structured FAQ schema, and consistent NAP data across the web. Rees's thin off-Google footprint and dormant LinkedIn make them a non-factor in AEO right now. Raptor has a clear runway to own this channel first.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Sheer review volume (582 vs. 697 is closer than it looks given their age), multi-manufacturer certifications that let them cross-sell siding and windows into every roofing job, and storm insurance claim handling as an embedded sales tool. Where Raptor beats them: 5.0 vs. 4.7 is a material gap at the comparison stage; Raptor's 0.1 owner response rate for Rees is a catastrophic trust signal against Raptor's engagement posture; and Raptor's premium positioning holds cleaner under scrutiny than Rees's mixed off-platform reviews. Most exploitable weakness: A 0.1 owner response rate on 582 reviews signals either operational overwhelm or indifference — Cameron should make Raptor's review responsiveness a direct call-out in sales conversations and ads.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. HOVER technology as a front-end differentiator.

Rees actively markets their HOVER integration as a trust and transparency tool — "precise 3D models from simple photos" for detailed estimates.

If Raptor isn't already using HOVER or a comparable remote measurement tool as a conversion asset on the website and in sales decks, Rees is winning that comparison.

  1. Multi-certification stacking for manufacturer authority.

Rees stacks Owens Corning Platinum Preferred, GAF Low-Slope, and Mule-Hide certifications

and leads with them prominently. Cameron should audit whether Raptor's certification badges are as visible and search-indexable (schema-tagged) as Rees's.

  1. BBB complaint vulnerabilities as paid media targeting fuel. The contract transparency issues and unresolved BBB complaints are a gift. Raptor should build a tight paid search ad sequence (and a dedicated landing page) around terms like "honest roofing contractor Indianapolis" and "roofing company no hidden costs Indy" — directly absorbing the dissatisfied Rees customer base without naming them by name.
