Rees Restoration — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Refreshed: June 9, 2026 | Prepared for Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2014 by Johnny Rees, Rees Restoration was built around the vision of becoming the top restoration company in Central Indiana.

Leadership at the BBB-registered entity includes Johnny Rees (Owner), Amanda Rees (Manager), and Sean Guilfoy (Business Manager), and the company is based in Greenwood, IN.

They have positioned themselves as a leading exterior remodeling company serving Central Indiana since 2014, covering roofing, siding, windows, gutters, and storm restoration for both residential and commercial clients.

2. Estimated Scale

Salary.com records 11–20 employees at Rees Restoration Inc.

Confidence: medium (Salary.com data lags; actual field crew headcount is likely higher given multi-crew job references in reviews).

RocketReach pegged annual revenue at approximately $5 million for 2025.

Confidence: low (third-party estimate; no public filing). The $1M–$5M range from Salary.com and the $5M figure from RocketReach together suggest a company operating in the $3M–$6M revenue band — roughly half to two-thirds of a typical mid-market Indy roofing operator at their review volume.

BuildZoom shows 32 permitted projects on record

, which is thin for a 10-year-old company and suggests a significant portion of their volume is either residential reroof-only work (often unpermitted in Indiana) or insurance-claim replacements that fly below the permit threshold.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

ZoomInfo notes that Rees Restoration is expanding and recently hired new employees

, but the public-facing detail is paywalled.

Indeed shows salary data estimated from only 5 past and present job postings

, indicating a historically light recruiting footprint on that platform. No active job postings for marketing, sales leadership, or ops roles were surfaced on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days as of this refresh. The named sales reps appearing repeatedly in reviews (Pete, Sam, Ryan, Chris, Riley, Micah, Eric, Darryl) suggest a sales team of 6–9 reps, but there are no posted roles to indicate imminent expansion or a marketing hire. Flag: no marketing-side hiring signal means no dedicated digital or content staff in place — a structural weakness Raptor should exploit.

4. Off-Google Presence

Rees self-reports an A+ BBB rating and an Angi "Super Service Award"

— both used prominently in their credibility stack. However,

the BBB profile carries at least one negative customer review calling out a sales-driven process gone wrong on a roof and gutters job

, and

the BBB complaints section includes a customer threatening legal action, alleging installation quality so poor that two independent roofers recommended full replacement.

That is a material discrepancy between a polished Google profile (4.7 / 580 reviews) and the BBB complaints ledger.

GuildQuality surfaces at least one verified review from Carmel calling the company "extremely unprofessional" with "extremely poor service."

Facebook Custom Audiences pixel is confirmed active on their site per technology stack data, meaning they are running retargeting — but no specific ad creative or spend data is publicly available in Meta Ad Library at the time of this refresh.

BuildZoom flags that at least one Indianapolis Department of Business and Neighborhood Services license was at one point cancelled

, though current active status is noted. This is worth monitoring.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Rees operates reesrestorationindy.net with a full city/service-area page architecture (Indianapolis, Greenwood, and sub-pages by service type: /indianapolis-in/roofing/replacement/, /storm-damage/, /commercial-roofing/, etc.). This silo structure is competent mid-market SEO execution — not sophisticated, but not negligible.

Their most notable differentiator on the site is a HOVER technology pitch — they claim to be "the only roofer in Central Indiana" using it to generate 3D models from photos for estimates without physically accessing the roof.

This is being used as both an SEO content hook and a conversion angle.

Certifications include Owens Corning "Top of the House" and Platinum Preferred, GAF Low-Slope Certified, and Mule-Hide Certified

— all of which generate manufacturer directory backlinks that contribute domain authority. No evidence of a consistent blog or FAQ content program was found, which limits their AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) surface area. Schema markup presence is unconfirmed without a direct crawl but the site structure implies basic LocalBusiness schema at minimum.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Rees Restoration does not appear as a named citation in AI-generated responses to queries such as "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville" when tested across ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini as of this refresh cycle. Their absence from AI answers is consistent with a company that has no active blog, no local press backlinks, and no structured FAQ content for AI engines to pull from.

AI assistants answering "who are the best roofers in [city]?" queries draw from consistent online mentions, forums, and authoritative local content

— none of which Rees is actively producing. This is a clean opportunity for Raptor: being cited first and consistently in AI answers for Greenwood, Carmel, and Fishers queries would directly intercept the segment Rees dominates in Google Maps.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Rees beats Raptor: Broader service line (siding, windows, doors, commercial flat roofing, fire/water damage) creates more touch points per household and referral paths through insurance adjusters. Their HOVER technology pitch is a tangible, explainable differentiator in the estimate process that Raptor currently has no public counter-narrative to. At 580 reviews, they have critical mass for map pack visibility, and the Angi and BBB A+ badges function as third-party social proof with older homeowner demographics.

Where Raptor beats them: 5.0 stars at 677 reviews vs. 4.7 is not a rounding error — it is a statistically significant signal of operational consistency. An owner response rate of 0.28 at Rees is indefensibly low for a company this size; Raptor's active response posture compounds trust at every touchpoint. The BBB complaint trail and GuildQuality negatives create real gaps between their Google reputation and their actual service delivery record.

Most exploitable weakness: Their 0.28 owner response rate and publicly visible BBB complaints mean that any homeowner who goes one click past Google reviews will find friction — Raptor should make sure those shoppers land on Raptor content instead.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. HOVER technology as a sales and marketing asset.

Rees is marketing HOVER-generated 3D models as a transparency and accuracy tool in the estimate process.

Whether or not Raptor adopts the same tech, Cameron should build a visible "how we measure and estimate" content page that counters the implication that Raptor's process is less precise or less transparent. If Raptor is already using a comparable tool, it is not being marketed — fix that.

  1. Insurance claims assistance as a top-of-funnel hook.

Rees leans hard into claims navigation — positioning reps as adjuster advocates who handle every phase of the filing process.

This is the highest-conversion content category in storm-season markets. Raptor should have at least one dedicated landing page and a short-form video (30–60 seconds) on the claims process that ranks for "how to file a roof insurance claim Indianapolis" — a query Rees is targeting but not owning with strong content.

  1. GuildQuality / third-party survey platform as a review diversification channel.

Rees uses GuildQuality for verified third-party surveys and touts that alongside BBB and Angi ratings.

For Raptor, getting verified reviews onto GuildQuality, Angi, and Houzz would mean Cameron's 5.0 / 677 score gets amplified across platforms that AI engines and "best of" aggregators actually scrape for generative answers — accelerating AEO visibility in markets like Carmel, Fishers, and Noblesville where Rees currently has no strong AI footprint either.
