Rees Restoration — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Refreshed: July 21, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2014 by Johnny Rees

,

Rees Restoration is a family-owned business

based in Greenwood, IN,

with Amanda Rees listed as manager and Sean Guilfoy as business manager per BBB records.

They have been completing exterior remodels on homes and businesses in Central Indiana since 2014

, covering roofing, siding, windows, gutters, and storm restoration. Their primary differentiator is a full-service, insurance-claims-integrated model —

they provide comprehensive storm damage reports and help customers through the insurance claims process.

2. Estimated Scale

Rees Restoration, owned by Jonathan Rees and headquartered at 3599 Pine Knoll Ct, Greenwood, IN, holds a general contractor license and 5 additional licenses per the Indianapolis license board, and their BuildZoom score of 105 ranks in the top 8% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors.

Confidence: medium.

Building permit records show Rees Restoration has worked on 32 permitted projects

— a relatively low permitted count suggesting a significant portion of work flows through insurance claims without separate municipal permits.

Indeed salary data collected directly from employees and jobs indicates only 5 past and present job postings

, suggesting a lean internal headcount — estimated 15–30 W2 employees with a heavy 1099 field crew model. Revenue estimate: $4M–$8M annually. Confidence: low — no Dun & Bradstreet or ZoomInfo data surfaced publicly.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No Rees Restoration-specific active job postings on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter surfaced in the last 30 days. Their LinkedIn company page exists but

is barebones, identifying only as a "family owned restoration company"

with no recent activity or headcount data visible to non-logged-in users. Absence of hiring signals at this point in the summer storm season is notable — either they are fully staffed, running lean, or not using job boards as a primary recruitment channel. No marketing, ops leadership, or sales manager hires flagged.

4. Off-Google Presence

Their Facebook page (facebook.com/Reesroofingrestoration) sits at 2,403 likes with only 15 people talking about it

— a dormant social footprint for a company their size. No active Meta Ad Library presence was surfaced, suggesting little to no paid social spend currently.

They hold an A+ BBB rating and an Angi "Super Service Award"

and use GuildQuality for verified third-party reviews. The BBB complaints file, however, contains substantive disputes:

one complainant alleged the contract was not valid under Indiana law and described aggressive follow-up actions

, and

a separate BBB reviewer alleged the company does not honor contracts and that the contractor went unreachable for three weeks without notice.

Their 4.7 Google rating vs. the complaint language on BBB and GuildQuality is a classic review-funneling gap — their own website curates testimonials while third-party platforms catch the outliers. Their 0.0% owner response rate on Google over the last 90 days confirms zero reputation management effort on the platform that matters most.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Rees runs a moderately deep location-page architecture — dedicated pages exist for Indianapolis, specific service lines, and repair/replacement variants.

They claim to be "the only roofer in Central Indiana using HOVER technology," which creates precise 3D models from simple photos for detailed estimates

— a legitimately differentiated content hook.

They carry OC Platinum Preferred, GAF Low-Slope Certified, and Mule-Hide Certified credentials

, which generate manufacturer backlinks from owenscorning.com and jameshardie.com — real domain authority signals. However, their blog output appears thin and irregular, with no recent editorial cadence visible. The site does not appear to be structured around conversational AEO queries or FAQ schema targeting the "best roofer in [suburb]" format that generative engines favor. Their domain name (reesrestorationindy.net) uses .net — a minor but real trust signal gap vs. .com competitors.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on research conducted for this brief: Rees Restoration does not appear prominently in AI-generated answers for key Indy roofing queries ("best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville"). Their content architecture is service-page heavy but lacks the structured FAQ content, local entity signals, and consistent third-party citation profile that LLMs draw on when generating contractor recommendations. Their HOVER technology claim — if built into a dedicated, well-linked content piece — could generate AI citation traction, but that asset does not appear to currently exist in a citable format. This is a clean opening for Raptor: the Indy roofing category is largely unclaimed territory in generative engine results.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Rees beats Raptor: Volume. 579 reviews is a meaningful head-start in raw social proof, and their multi-service (siding, windows, gutters, doors) model creates more touchpoints per household and a higher average ticket. Their OC Platinum + James Hardie dual certification also gives them manufacturer co-marketing access Raptor should audit for its own certifications. Where Raptor beats Rees: Quality signal is unambiguous — 690 reviews at 5.0 vs. 579 at 4.7, plus a 0.0% owner response rate at Rees that signals leadership detachment from reputation. Most exploitable weakness: Their dead-silent owner response rate combined with surfaced BBB contract disputes creates a credibility gap Raptor can widen directly — Raptor responding to every review, by name, with specifics, is a visible operational contrast any homeowner doing due diligence will notice immediately.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

1. The HOVER technology positioning.

Rees claims to be the only Central Indiana roofer using HOVER to deliver fast, accurate roof measurements without climbing on the home, creating precise 3D models from simple photos.

Whether or not that exclusivity claim holds, the conversion angle is strong — Cameron should evaluate whether Raptor uses HOVER or a comparable tool and build a dedicated landing page around it if so. If not, audit EagleView or Nearmap as alternatives with similar positioning potential.

2. Insurance-claim integration as a sales lane. Rees's entire model leans into adjuster-meeting, claims-navigation service.

They provide storm damage reports and guide customers through the insurance claims process

as a core differentiator. If Raptor isn't explicitly marketing this capability in paid campaigns and on landing pages, that's a positioning gap in the storm-season window when intent volume is highest.

3. GuildQuality as a third-party review moat. Rees uses GuildQuality to host independently verified reviews, which carry more AEO weight than self-curated testimonials.

With project managers on site at each job, they've built a process that feeds verified third-party surveys

— a structure that creates citation-worthy content for generative engines. Raptor should assess GuildQuality or a comparable verified-survey platform as a second review stream that reinforces the 5.0 narrative with external validation.
