Rees Restoration — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2014 by Johnny Rees, who set out to build the best restoration company in Central Indiana.

The BBB profile lists Johnny Rees as Owner, Amanda Rees as Manager, and Sean Guilfoy as Business Manager.

Their stated service footprint spans Greenwood, Indianapolis, Carmel, Zionsville, Fishers, Geist, Westfield, Noblesville, Lawrence, Franklin, Brownsburg, Avon, Mooresville, Martinsville, Whiteland, Columbus, New Palestine, and Greenfield

— covering nearly the entire metro.

They position as an OC Platinum Preferred Contractor and a James Hardie Preferred Contractor

, with a strong insurance-claim-assist and storm restoration angle as their primary lead driver.

2. Estimated Scale

LinkedIn shows 81 followers on their company page

, with minimal organic employee activity — suggesting a lean corporate staff supplemented by a larger field and sales force.

BuildZoom records Rees Restoration at 3599 Pine Knoll Ct, Greenwood, IN, owned by Jonathan Rees, holding a general contractor license and 5 additional licenses, with a BuildZoom score of 105 that ranks in the top 8% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors.

Permit records show 32 permitted projects on BuildZoom

, which likely understates actual volume given how infrequently residential roofing pulls permits. Based on review velocity (579 Google reviews over ~11 years), named reps visible across review platforms (Pete Oakes, Sam Gorall, Chris Clark, Phil Blazek, Eric Voss, Jonny Cook, Ryan Clark, Darryl, Brandon, Micah Coyle, Riley — 10+ distinct field sales reps), and multi-market service area, estimated headcount is 25–45 total (field + admin). Confidence: low — no third-party firmographic data confirms this. Estimated annual revenue: $3M–$6M. Confidence: low.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Rees Restoration maintains a careers page actively recruiting and framing open positions around growth, innovation, and collaboration.

ZoomInfo flagged that Rees Restoration is expanding and recently hired new employees

, though specific role titles are paywalled.

Indeed reflects only 5 past and present job postings total

, and no active Rees-specific job postings surfaced on LinkedIn, Indeed, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days. No marketing, ops leadership, or sales management hires are currently detectable. This is either a company hiring quietly through referral networks (common at this size) or one that is not actively scaling headcount at the moment.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

The site was originally developed through Harness Design Indy in partnership with Jennifer Forrest Design, and was later converted to a WordPress CMS platform to allow for more flexible self-updates.

The domain is reesrestorationindy.net — note the .net TLD, which is a known SEO disadvantage vs. .com.

Their primary tech differentiator is HOVER, which they claim makes them "the only roofer in Central Indiana" using it, generating 3D models from photos for precise estimates.

The site has location-specific landing pages (Indianapolis, Carmel, Greenwood sub-pages) and a sitemap — basic SEO hygiene is in place. However, the site is WordPress-based with a local design shop behind it, unlikely to have advanced schema markup, structured data for reviews, or AEO-optimized FAQ content. Blog activity and local press backlinks were not surfaced in searches.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on the research gathered: Rees Restoration has sufficient Google review volume (579) and a multi-market presence to potentially appear in AI-generated "best roofer" lists, but several factors work against them in generative engine results. Their .net domain signals lower authority. No local press backlinks or third-party editorial citations were detected. Their HOVER technology claim is the most distinctive, citable hook an AI engine would latch onto — but it is an equipment-level differentiator, not a brand-level trust signal. No confirmed citations in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini results for Indianapolis-area roofing queries were obtainable in this research cycle. This is an open field: Raptor's 5.0 rating and premium positioning are exactly the signals AI engines weight heavily in home-services queries, and Raptor should be building AEO-optimized FAQ content around "best Indianapolis roofer" and suburb-specific queries now.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Rees beats Raptor: Raw review volume (579 vs. 664 is close, but Rees gets there with a broader multi-service scope); Nextdoor "Neighborhood Favorite" status across 18 neighborhoods is a meaningful organic distribution Raptor should close; and their HOVER pitch is a concrete, demo-able sales tool that resonates with homeowners who distrust ladder-on-the-roof estimates. Their one-stop-shop positioning (roofing + siding + windows + gutters) also increases average job value.

Where Raptor beats Rees: 5.0 vs. 4.7 is a meaningful gap at this review volume — it is not statistical noise. Raptor's owner response rate, if substantially above 0.52, is a visible trust signal on every Google listing. Rees has documented warranty and follow-through complaints on BBB and GuildQuality. Raptor's premium "accountability" positioning is a direct counter to Rees's pattern of post-job service gaps.

Most exploitable weakness: The 0.52 owner response rate combined with multiple unresolved BBB complaints is a latent trust gap that one bad hail season or viral negative review could turn into a public credibility problem — Raptor should ensure Cameron is responding to every review and make that response discipline a visible part of the brand story.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Nextdoor "Neighborhood Favorite" campaign.

Rees has Neighborhood Favorite status in 18 neighborhoods

— this does not happen by accident. It requires consistent Nextdoor business page activity and likely systematic asks from reps post-job. Raptor should audit its own Nextdoor presence neighborhood by neighborhood and build a post-job ask sequence specifically targeting Nextdoor recommendations, especially in Carmel, Fishers, and Zionsville.

  1. HOVER as a sales-stage differentiator, not just an ops tool.

Rees markets HOVER as "no climbing on your home" and uses it to generate 3D models and transparent estimates.

Whether Raptor uses HOVER or a comparable tool, the consumer-facing framing of "we measure differently so you get a more accurate estimate" is a conversion-stage argument that reduces friction and skepticism. If Raptor is not already leading with a tech-enabled estimate story in its sales deck, this is a gap.

  1. Monitor their BBB complaint resolution pattern. The combination of a 4.7 star Google rating, an A+ BBB badge they prominently tout, and a BBB complaints log that includes warranty non-responses and wrong-product deliveries is a ready-made contrast Raptor can own — not by name-dropping Rees, but by making Raptor's warranty, post-job follow-through, and documentation process an explicit part of proposal and close language. When a prospect says "I've heard mixed things about storm restoration companies," that is the opening.
