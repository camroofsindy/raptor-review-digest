Rees Restoration - Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Updated: May 19, 2025 | Next refresh: May 22, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2014 by Johnny Rees, Rees Restoration is headquartered in Greenwood, IN and operates throughout Central Indiana.

The BBB lists Johnny Rees as Owner and Amanda Rees as Manager, with Sean Guilfoy serving as Business Manager

— confirming this is a family operation with a hired ops layer.

They hold OC Platinum Preferred, James Hardie Preferred, GAF Low-Slope Certified, and Mule-Hide Certified status, and position themselves as a full-service exterior contractor spanning roofing, siding, windows, gutters, and storm restoration.

2. Estimated Scale

LinkedIn shows 81 followers on the company page

with no employee headcount listed.

Indeed reflects only 5 past and present job postings

, suggesting a lean administrative footprint. Based on 580 Google reviews accumulated over 11 years, review velocity, permit activity, and the sales rep structure visible in their own review responses (named reps include Sam Gorall, Eric Voss, Pete Oaks, Darryl, Micah Coyle, Brandon, Jonny Cook), a reasonable estimate is 20–40 total staff including field crews — confidence: low. Revenue estimate: $3M–$7M annually, consistent with a multi-rep storm restoration shop in a mid-size market — confidence: low. No ZoomInfo or Dun & Bradstreet data surfaced to sharpen this.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Their careers page at reesrestorationindy.net/careers lists open positions and frames itself as a culture of growth and collaboration

, but no specific active postings were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days under the Rees Restoration brand.

The 5 total historical Indeed postings

indicate they are not a frequent digital recruiter — they likely source through internal referrals and the sales rep network. No marketing, ops leadership, or sales manager hires flagged. This is a flat-structure shop; watch for any sudden leadership hire as an expansion signal.

4. Off-Google Presence

They carry an A+ BBB rating and an Angi "Super Service Award"

and present well on those platforms. However, the BBB complaints page tells a different story:

at least one customer states they are "at the point of suing Rees Restoration" and had two other roofers confirm the roof quality was substandard.

Separately,

a BBB complaint describes repeated attempts over 6 months to get a warranty repair resolved with no reply from the sales rep.

On GuildQuality,

a verified Carmel, IN reviewer called them "extremely unprofessional" and advised others to "stay away."

Facebook presence is confirmed active per Salary.com; no Facebook Ad Library activity was surfaced in search results. The pattern here is clear: Google reviews are being actively funneled (only 1 new review in the last 7 days, 5 in 30 days) while BBB and third-party platforms carry disproportionately negative signal. That is a textbook funnel-gate operation. Their 4.7 on Google against documented BBB complaints and a GuildQuality flag is a discrepancy worth noting publicly in competitive sales conversations.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their [BuildZoom score of 105 ranks in the top 8% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors

, which is a domain credibility signal. The site at reesrestorationindy.net is built with city/service subfolders (e.g., /indianapolis-in/roofing/replacement/), which is proper local SEO architecture.

They claim to be "The Only Roofer in Central Indiana Using HOVER Technology," which creates precise 3D models from photos for estimates

— this is a differentiated content hook that likely earns featured snippet territory on tech-forward queries. The blog is thin; the ice dam content and ventilation articles found are generic. No evidence of local press backlinks, HARO placements, or third-party editorial coverage surfaced. Schema markup cannot be confirmed without a direct crawl, but their site structure follows patterns consistent with a mid-level agency template. They are likely competing for map pack rankings on "Indianapolis roof replacement" and winning some of them given volume, but their organic content depth is shallow.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No independent AI citation data for Rees Restoration was returned in the searches conducted. Based on their profile — a reasonably active website, A+ BBB, OC Platinum cert, 580 Google reviews at 4.7 — they likely appear in some generative engine outputs for broad "Indianapolis roofing" queries, particularly those pulling from structured directories like Angi or BBB. However, they have no documented AEO strategy: no FAQ schema, no long-form authoritative content, and no visible press mentions that would trigger named citations in ChatGPT or Gemini responses for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, or Zionsville-specific queries. This is an exploitable gap — those suburb-specific AI queries are where Raptor can own the narrative if structured content and citation sources are built correctly.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Rees beats Raptor: Raw review volume (580 vs. 673 is close, but 11 years of accumulation gives them legacy authority in local search ranking signals). Their multi-trade bundling —

roofing, siding, windows, gutters all under one roof

— generates higher average ticket and makes them stickier with insurance-driven customers who want one contractor for everything. The HOVER technology claim, framed as a market exclusive, is a compelling estimate-experience differentiator.

Where Raptor beats Rees: 5.0 stars vs. 4.7 is a decisive quality signal in LSA and Map Pack ranking. Raptor's 0.51 vs. Rees's owner response rate (also approximately 0.51 per provided data) is a wash — but Raptor's cleaner third-party profile (no BBB complaint pattern, no GuildQuality negatives) wins on trust audits that savvy buyers run. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" messaging is a direct counter to Rees's documented post-sale follow-through failures.

Most exploitable weakness: Rees's documented pattern of sales reps going dark after the contract is signed — visible in BBB complaints and GuildQuality — is a direct attack surface for Raptor's accountability positioning.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The HOVER technology play.

Rees claims to be the only Central Indiana roofer using HOVER for photogrammetric 3D measurement and estimate transparency.

Whether or not that claim holds, the positioning works because it's specific and visual. Cameron should evaluate whether Raptor adopts HOVER or a competing tool (Roofr, EagleView) and out-markets them on the same angle — or directly challenges the "only in Central Indiana" claim if Raptor already uses similar tech.

  1. City-service subfolder architecture. Rees has built out location-specific service pages (e.g., /indianapolis-in/roofing/replacement/) that capture long-tail local queries. Raptor should audit whether equivalent pages exist for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, and Zionsville — each with unique content, not duplicated templates — before Rees or another competitor locks up those suburb rankings.

  2. Insurance-navigation as a content moat. The majority of positive Rees reviews center on reps who actively worked with insurance adjusters on the customer's behalf.

Rees explicitly promotes storm damage reports and walking customers through the insurance claims process

as a differentiator. Raptor should build dedicated content — blog posts, FAQ schema, a dedicated landing page — around the insurance claim process for Indiana homeowners. That content will earn AI citations and organic rankings while pre-framing the sales conversation on Raptor's terms.
