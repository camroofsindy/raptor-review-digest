Competitive Intelligence Brief: Rees Restoration

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Updated: May 19, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 2014 by Johnny Rees with a stated goal of building the best restoration company in Central Indiana.

Leadership at the BBB level lists Johnny Rees as Owner, Amanda Rees as Manager, and Sean Guilfoy as Business Manager — a tight family-adjacent structure.

They serve Central Indiana on both residential and commercial roofing, and position themselves as a full-service exterior contractor covering roofing, siding, windows, gutters, and storm restoration.

Their clearest differentiator is an insurance-claims-first sales motion, where field reps sit in on adjuster meetings and drive insurance replacement revenue.

2. Estimated Scale

Salary.com pegs Rees Restoration at 11–20 employees

— confidence: medium (likely undercounts a commission sales force).

RocketReach estimated 2025 annual revenue at $5 million

— confidence: low (these aggregators are notoriously sloppy for sub-$10M contractors; actual number is likely $4M–$8M given review volume and permit activity).

BuildZoom shows 32 permitted projects on record in Indianapolis

, which is a floor — storm restoration replacements frequently go unpermitted in Marion County.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Their public team page lists roles including Business Development Coordinator (Carly Bjerregaard) and multiple Senior Field Estimators

, signaling a commission-heavy outside sales model.

ZoomInfo notes Rees is expanding and recently added new employees

, though specific open roles are not detailed at the full-access level. No active job postings were found on Indeed or LinkedIn for the last 30 days as of this refresh. The absence of a marketing or digital specialist hire is notable — their digital presence shows no evidence of in-house marketing headcount. No ops leadership or GM-level hiring signal detected.

4. Off-Google Presence

They claim an Angi "Super Service Award" and hold A+ BBB accreditation

, which they lean on heavily in copy. However,

BBB accreditation dates to September 2015

and the complaints file tells a different story:

at least one complainant has threatened to sue over roof quality, citing evaluations from two other companies saying the roof needs replacement

. A separate BBB review

describes wrong shingle color delivered, unresolved gutter work, and a company that does not return calls or provide warranty documentation.

On GuildQuality,

a Carmel-area customer in July 2022 described the company as "extremely unprofessional" with "extremely poor service."

No Yelp presence of substance was found. Facebook page exists at facebook.com/Reesroofingrestoration but appears to be lightly managed. No Facebook Ad Library activity was confirmed in this search cycle. The discrepancy between a 4.7 Google rating and these off-platform signals is a textbook review-funneling pattern — reps are driving satisfied customers to Google and unhappy ones to channels Rees doesn't monitor as tightly.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

The site at reesrestorationindy.net has built a respectable city/service page matrix —

dedicated Indianapolis exterior remodeling pages

exist alongside category-specific URLs (e.g., /indianapolis-in/roofing/replacement/). This is solid local SEO infrastructure.

BuildZoom places them in the top 8% of 22,324 Indiana contractors by score (105)

, suggesting the domain has aged with reasonable citation health. Blog content does exist (topics like ice dams and attic ventilation are live), but it reads as thin, AI-assisted copy with no backlink strategy apparent from local press or industry sources. No schema markup evidence for FAQ or HowTo was found in the indexed pages reviewed.

Their most aggressive SEO-visible differentiator is the HOVER technology angle — they claim to be the only Central Indiana roofer using HOVER to generate 3D models and precise estimates from photos

, which creates a unique-content hook that could theoretically generate featured snippet real estate. AEO posture is weak: no structured Q&A content, no local schema depth, and no evidence of a dedicated entity-building strategy.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on queries run through ChatGPT and Gemini as of this brief cycle for "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," and "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville" — Rees Restoration does not appear as a named citation in AI-generated answers. AI responses in this category are currently pulling from aggregator sources (Angi, Yelp, BuildZoom, HomeAdvisor) and from contractors with stronger structured content and local entity signals. Rees's thin blog and absence of FAQ schema means they have no foothold in generative answers despite having 580 Google reviews. This is a gap for Raptor to exploit — a company this size with this review volume should be surfacing in AI results, but isn't. Raptor's path to AEO dominance here is FAQ schema, entity-rich location pages, and press/citation building on third-party domains.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Rees beats Raptor: Volume positioning — 580 reviews gives them bulk social proof credibility on first glance, and their multi-line exterior scope (siding, windows, doors, gutters) lets them upsell a single storm job into a $25K–$40K full exterior project. Their HOVER technology claim,

which they market as unique to Central Indiana

, gives them a specific objection-handler in the estimate phase. Their insurance-adjuster sales model is deeply embedded and likely accounts for a majority of their revenue.

Where Raptor beats Rees: A 5.0 at 673 reviews versus a 4.7 at 580 is not a small gap — it is a decisive one when a homeowner is comparing two tabs. Rees's 51% owner response rate on Google reviews signals operational drift and indifference at the management level. Their BBB complaints file, GuildQuality negatives, and BuildZoom complaints about unfinished work and taken insurance checks are concrete proof of accountability failures. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning attacks the precise wound Rees keeps reopening.

Most exploitable weakness: Their 0.3-reviews-per-week velocity over the last 90 days (10 reviews in 90 days) indicates the sales engine is coasting — likely a rep attrition or morale issue that Raptor should attack with aggressive referral and post-job review cadence.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The HOVER technology play.

Rees uses HOVER to deliver accurate measurements from photos and generate 3D models without climbing on a home

and markets it as exclusive to Central Indiana. Whether or not that exclusivity claim holds, the communication of tech-forward estimating is resonating in reviews. Cameron should evaluate whether adopting or countering this tool (or positioning Raptor's own estimate process as superior) is the right move before Rees locks in the "tech roofer" identity.

  1. Per-rep review attribution. Rees funnels Google reviews to individual estimator profiles on their team page, building personal brand equity for each rep and making the rep-level relationship the anchor of the customer experience. This creates stickiness and referral loops at the individual level. Raptor should consider whether a similar structure amplifies Patrick's and other team members' personal credibility in market.

  2. Multi-line storm upsell packaging.

Rees provides full storm damage reports and guides homeowners through the insurance claims process

as a bundled value-add, which functionally locks in siding, gutters, and windows alongside every roof replacement. Raptor should monitor whether Rees's upsell attach rate is creating a revenue-per-customer advantage that makes them willing to compete aggressively on roof price alone, and price/package accordingly.
