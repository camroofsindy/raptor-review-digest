Competitor Brief: Indy Roof & Restoration

indyroofandrestoration.com | Refreshed: April 26, 2026 (Thu)

1. Who They Are

Aaron Christy founded Indy Roof & Restoration in 2018.

The company is led day-to-day by COO Adam Kaminski alongside Christy, serving residential and commercial customers across central Indiana.

They hold BBB A+ status, Owens Corning Preferred Contractor certification, and Malarkey Certified Contractor status

— with insurance-claim advocacy and storm restoration as the clear core of their identity.

Named service markets include Carmel, Geist, Fishers, Greenwood, Brownsburg, Westfield, and Zionsville.

2. Estimated Scale

Employee count signals are inconsistent across sources.

Clodura.AI lists 11–50 employees with named roles including a Branch Manager, Project Manager, Marketing Director, and Sales Manager.

SignalHire estimates approximately 30 employees

, while

Growjo pegs Indy Roof Company at $10.9M revenue and 47 employees

— the most operationally plausible figure given their review velocity.

Growjo also reports 38% employee count growth in the last year.

Confidence: medium. Revenue estimate of $8M–$12M is reasonable for this review volume and geographic spread;

Prospeo's figure of ~$2.4M annual revenue

appears significantly understated and should be disregarded. Revenue confidence: medium.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Indeed currently shows Indy Roof & Restoration actively recruiting for a Water & Mold Restoration Sales / Restoration Technician role in Indianapolis

, signaling a deliberate push into interior remediation as a revenue line beyond roofing. This is a meaningful strategic tell — they are building a full-service restoration division that competes on insurance jobs end-to-end. No marketing, ops leadership, or executive-level postings were surfaced in the last 30 days on LinkedIn or Indeed beyond the above. Notably,

their former Marketing Director, Nick Segar, departed after nearly two years at the company

, leaving a marketing leadership gap that has not yet surfaced as a posted opening — watch for that role to hit the boards within 60 days.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp has placed a Consumer Alert on their business page warning users about abnormal activity or attempts to mislead them

— a significant discrepancy from their 4.9 Google rating with 2,153 reviews. This is a classic review-funneling flag: direct customers to Google via post-job SMS/email, Yelp gets organic traffic that skews negative or thin.

Their Facebook page has 4,777 likes with 52 currently talking about it

— engagement is modest relative to their review scale.

HomeAdvisor lists them with charitable giving (1% of revenue) to Habitat for Humanity, Toys for Tots, Isaiah 117, and Fountains of Hope

, and they lean hard into that narrative on all platforms. BBB shows an A+ rating but includes substantive complaint content, including

at least one complaint alleging a forged signature on inspection paperwork.

No Facebook Ad Library activity was confirmable in this cycle, but their Facebook Pixel and Custom Audiences presence (via ZoomInfo tech stack data) confirms active paid social targeting.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their SEO operation is more sophisticated than most Indy competitors.

They self-publish a "10 Best Roofing Companies in Indianapolis for 2026" list on their own domain, positioning themselves as the #1 choice

— a textbook barnacle SEO and AEO play designed to capture "best roofer Indianapolis" queries and appear as a cited source in AI-generated answers.

Their confirmed tech stack includes Facebook Pixel, Facebook Web Custom Audiences, WordPress, and PHP

— a lean but functional setup.

Third-party roundups describe them as "well recognized for insurance advocacy, 24/7 emergency response, and a high review volume in central Indiana."

They have suburb-specific landing pages (Carmel, Fishers, Greenwood, etc.) which is standard. The self-published "best of" list is the tactic to watch most closely — it is engineered for AEO citation, not just organic ranking.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Indy Roof & Restoration has meaningful generative engine exposure, largely driven by their self-published content strategy. Their "10 Best Roofing Companies in Indianapolis for 2026" page on their own domain is explicitly designed to surface when ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity pulls from indexed web content for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis" or "top roofing contractor Carmel Indiana."

A competing roofer's third-party roundup lists them as a top contender and honorable mention, specifically recommending them to "homeowners who want intense insurance-claim support and mitigation services"

— meaning their brand appears in competitor-published content that AI engines also ingest. Their high review volume (2,153) makes them a default citation candidate when AI models weigh social proof signals. Raptor's gap here is not presence — it's differentiation. Raptor is not yet being described in the indexed web with the kind of specific, AEO-optimized language ("premium residential roofing," "non-storm, retail replacement specialist") that would cause AI engines to cite it for non-insurance-claim queries.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume (2,153 vs. 664) gives them de facto AI citation priority and map pack authority; their storm/insurance vertical is a well-oiled lead machine with Realtor and adjuster referral pipelines baked in; and their self-published "best of" list is a live AEO asset Raptor does not have.

Where Raptor beats them: 5.0 vs. 4.9 star rating at meaningful volume (664 reviews is not thin); no Yelp Consumer Alert; no BBB complaint allegations of signature forgery; premium positioning without the insurance-chaser stigma that follows storm-restoration brands when storms don't come; and Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" message is cleaner for high-ticket retail replacement buyers who are not filing a claim.

Most exploitable weakness: Their former Marketing Director departed and their Yelp Consumer Alert is live and unresolved — both create an opening to outflank them on off-Google reputation platforms and paid social right now, before a replacement hire stabilizes their marketing operation.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The self-published "best of" list as AEO infrastructure. Their page at indyroofandrestoration.com/the-10-best-roofing-companies-in-indianapolis-for-2026/ is a deliberate attempt to own the AI answer for "best roofer Indianapolis." Cameron should build a competing page — ideally with schema markup, FAQ schema, and citations from third-party sources — that positions Raptor as the premium (non-insurance-claim) option in the same query space. First-mover on AEO-optimized content beats review volume in generative engine citations.

  2. Restoration Technician / Water & Mold expansion signal.

Their active Indeed listing for Water & Mold Restoration Sales confirms they are pushing into full-service interior restoration.

If they land IAQ and mold remediation certifications, their average job value and insurance-claim lock-in expands significantly. Raptor should decide now whether to add a remediation referral partner (without owning the liability) or cede that lane entirely and double down on premium dry-weather retail replacement — the segment Indy Roof does not emphasize.

  1. Aaron Christy's personal LinkedIn and podcast presence.

Christy is appearing on podcasts discussing building company culture and what made Indy Roof one of the area's most successful roofing companies in a short period.

He is actively building founder authority in the contractor and real estate community. Cameron and Patrick should be doing the same — locally-relevant podcast appearances, LinkedIn posts with roofing-specific takes, and Realtor-community content. The personal brand of the owner is becoming an AEO and referral-network asset, not just a vanity play.
