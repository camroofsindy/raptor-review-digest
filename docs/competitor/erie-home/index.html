Erie Home — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Updated: May 19, 2025

1. Who They Are

Erie Home, founded in 1982 and headquartered in Toledo, Ohio, is a leading direct-to-consumer residential re-roofing platform operating across North America.

Pat Trompeter is the company's founder (now Chairman of the Board), with Jenilee Common serving as CEO and Randy Hamilton as President.

Their differentiator is a fully vertically integrated model —

Erie handles the end-to-end process from lead generation through final installation, positioning themselves on the peace-of-mind pitch rather than local craftsmanship.

Critically,

as of September 8, 2025, Erie Home was acquired by Leaf Home

, folding it into the largest DTC residential home services platform in North America — a post-acquisition integration dynamic that should be front of mind for Raptor.

2. Estimated Scale

Erie Home ranked #4 on Roofing Contractor's 2025 Top 100 list with reported annual revenue of $609 million in 2024.

Confidence: High (cited in trade press with named list ranking.)

The company operates more than 90 locations across 37 states.

For headcount,

Indeed estimates salary data from 10,700+ employees, users, and past/present job advertisements

— suggesting a workforce north of 3,000–5,000 FTEs nationally. Confidence: Medium (no hard FTE figure publicly confirmed post-acquisition). The Indianapolis location is one branch of a national machine; local decision-making authority is limited.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Erie Home's active Indeed posting for Indianapolis lists roofing subcontractors, noting the company has been ranked in the Top 10 on the Qualified Remodeler Top 500 list for consecutive years.

ZipRecruiter shows multiple "Hiring Immediately for Roofing Subcontractors" listings — Erie is actively seeking crews for consistent, year-round work.

Nationally, Erie is recruiting Sales Representatives with commission structures averaging $2,500 per sale, citing $600M+ in annual revenue as a recruitment hook.

No marketing, ops leadership, or branch management postings were identified for Indianapolis specifically in the last 30 days. The subcontractor-heavy hiring posture confirms Erie does not self-perform installs locally — a meaningful accountability gap Raptor can exploit.

4. Off-Google Presence

Erie's Indianapolis Google profile sits at 277 reviews / 4.6 stars with zero reviews in the last 90 days and no owner responses on record — a dead profile for a company this size. This is not organic momentum; it reads as a centrally managed, now-neglected local GBP.

Erie claims an A+ BBB rating nationally

, but the BBB profile is managed from Toledo — there is no Indianapolis-specific BBB entity.

Consumer Affairs reviews nationally include complaints about leaking roofs post-installation and improper ridge vent installation

— the kind of execution failures consistent with a subcontractor-dependent model.

LinkedIn marketing copy touts "outstanding ratings across all major review sites including Google, BBB, GuildQuality, TrustPilot, Birdeye, Indeed and Glassdoor"

— but the Indianapolis Google stagnation tells a different story. No active Facebook Ad Library presence was confirmed for the Indianapolis market specifically at time of this brief.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

eriehome.com is a nationally scaled domain with substantial authority derived from press coverage of the Leaf Home acquisition, Roofing Contractor Top 100 citations, and PR wire syndication. The Indianapolis location page (eriehome.com/locations/roofing/indianapolis-in/) is templated and thin.

It targets Indianapolis, Carmel, Greenwood, and Lawrence in copy

but reads like a scaled location page, not locally authored content.

The site's HTML is 154.96 KB — more than 4x the average — which can negatively impact load times and user experience.

No evidence of a local Indianapolis blog content strategy or backlinks from Indy-specific media (IBJ, IndyStar, Indy Chamber). Their AEO posture nationally is strong due to brand volume, but locally they are invisible in terms of schema-rich, city-specific content that would drive AI citation.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Erie Home, due to sheer national revenue scale ($609M, Top 5 nationally) and the high-profile Leaf Home acquisition press coverage, is likely cited in generic "largest roofing companies" AI answers. However, for hyper-local queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville," Erie's thin, templated location pages provide weak AEO signals. AI engines favor locally specific, structured, review-rich content — Raptor's 673 reviews at 5.0 stars, combined with locally authored content targeting those exact suburb queries, represents a direct opportunity to outrank Erie in generative engine responses for Central Indiana. Erie's post-acquisition integration noise is also likely suppressing any new AEO content investment at the local level right now.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Erie beats Raptor: National brand recognition, a lifetime warranty product, third-party financing at scale, and a $609M revenue machine that can spend on paid media without flinching. Their workaterie.com recruitment engine and Sales MIT program means they can flood local markets with commission-driven reps quickly.

Where Raptor beats Erie: Raptor holds a 5.0 star rating at 673 reviews vs Erie's 4.6 at 277 with zero recent review velocity — a dead signal in Google's local algorithm. Erie's subcontractor installation model is its most exploitable weakness:

Erie is a "one call close" company where reps must convince homeowners to pay 3–4x what they expected for a product they did not ask about

— the exact opposite of Raptor's accountability positioning. The most exploitable weakness: zero owner responses on the Indianapolis GBP in any recorded window — Cameron can weaponize this contrast in sales conversations and review strategy immediately.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Subcontractor disclosure as a sales tool. Erie's own Indianapolis job reviews note the install model's friction. Cameron should build a direct comparison into sales scripts and proposal decks: Raptor uses its own crews, Erie uses subcontractors. Make it a documented close, not just a verbal point.

  2. Lifetime warranty language.

Erie's CEO pitch is "we will be with you for the rest of the life of the roof and the next one."

That is a powerful retention and close narrative. Evaluate whether Raptor's warranty language is as visceral and forward-looking in collateral.

  1. Monitor the Leaf Home integration for local service degradation.

The September 2025 Leaf Home acquisition combined two DTC leaders into one entity

— integrations of this scale historically cause local customer service gaps, slower lead response, and review stagnation (which is already showing in Indy). Set a Google Alerts cadence for "Erie Home Indianapolis" and "Leaf Home Indianapolis" and be ready to surge Raptor's local paid media when Erie's GBP rating starts slipping.
