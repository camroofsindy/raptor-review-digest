1. Who They Are

Indy Rooftops was established in 2022 by Mitchel Dotson and Cheryl Edens, originally founded to create a roofing business for Kevin Dotson, a veteran roofing salesman at another local company.

Kevin Dotson served as the cornerstone of the roofing team until his sudden passing from a heart attack on September 10, 2022.

The company identifies as a woman-owned business rooted in Brownsburg, Indiana, serving the broader Indianapolis metro with residential and commercial roofing, siding, gutters, windows, and attic insulation.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

The indyrooftops.com homepage carries a keyword-optimized title tag ("Indianapolis Roofing Contractor - 5-Star Rated") and covers core service pages.

Their on-page copy leans into Indianapolis climate knowledge and local code compliance

as differentiators — functional but generic. The site was indexed as early as July 2022.

They actively point visitors to check ratings on Google, Yelp, Angie's List, and BBB

They are not visibly investing in AEO (Answer Engine Optimization): no FAQ schema, no "People Also Ask" content, no long-tail suburb-level landing pages (Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville) were found. That is a significant gap at current review velocity.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Indy Rooftops does not appear to be cited by name in AI-generated answers for key Indy roofing queries ("best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville"). The absence of a content library, structured FAQ schema, and suburb-level landing pages means the major generative engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) have no strong signal to pull from. Their review volume theoretically gives them brand authority, but without on-page AEO content or local press mentions to serve as training/retrieval anchors, they remain invisible in AI-sourced answers. This is an industry-wide blind spot — and a window for Raptor to close first.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Indy Rooftops has a well-developed insurance-claim workflow —

reviewers specifically call out that the company spent hours on the phone with insurance to maximize claim payouts

— and that story is embedded consistently across Angi, HomeAdvisor, and Google. It functions as a sales script and a retention tool simultaneously. Their 0.8 owner response rate on Google reviews is also meaningfully higher engagement than most competitors at volume.

Where Raptor beats them: 677 reviews vs. 522, a 155-review lead, and Raptor's 5.0 holds at higher volume — the credibility math is unambiguous. Raptor's premium positioning is also defensible against a company whose Angi reviews reference being "cheaper than other quotes."

Most exploitable weakness: Their SEO and AEO infrastructure is essentially non-existent — no blog, no suburb pages, no schema — meaning all 522 of their reviews are doing zero organic search work beyond the GBP listing.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The insurance-advocacy narrative as a conversion tool.

Multiple reviewers specifically credit Indy Rooftops reps for working with their insurance company to secure a full replacement.

Raptor should audit whether this story is surfacing consistently in sales conversations and review copy — if not, it needs to be embedded in every estimate follow-up touchpoint.

  1. Multi-platform review distribution.

Indy Rooftops actively directs customers to check ratings on Google, Yelp, Angi, and BBB simultaneously.

Raptor's review moat is deep on Google; diversifying even modestly to BBB and Angi creates more surfaces for AI retrieval and reduces single-platform dependency.

  1. Realtor referral channel.

At least two Angi reviewers identify themselves as realtors who plan to refer Indy Rooftops to their clients.

This is an underutilized B2B referral lane in Indy residential roofing — a structured realtor partnership program (co-branded materials, priority scheduling for listing-prep jobs) could be a legitimate volume driver for Raptor at low CAC.
