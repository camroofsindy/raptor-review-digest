Raptor Roofing — Competitive Intelligence Self-Brief

Updated: May 19, 2026 | Prepared for Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Raptor Roofing was incorporated on June 21, 2021, and is structured as an LLC based at 5145 S Meridian St, Indianapolis, IN 46217.

Co-founders Cameron Blakely and Patrick Kinney built the brand out of the Center Grove market

and have since expanded across Central Indiana.

The company leads with roof replacement and commercial roofing, serves the broader Indianapolis metro including Carmel, and explicitly positions community accountability — not price — as the core brand pillar.

2. Estimated Scale

The associated LinkedIn entity (Raptor Restoration, LLC — same address, same principals) lists company size as 2–10 employees.

Confidence: low — this almost certainly undercounts W-2 staff plus field crews.

Indeed salary data reports roles including Sales Consultant (~$99K), Senior Sales Consultant (~$116K), Sales Manager (~$116K), and Estimator (~$25K), suggesting a structured sales org of at least 5–8 revenue-generating roles.

Confidence: medium. Revenue estimate: a company producing 73 reviews in 90 days at an average residential ticket of $12K–$15K implies $3.5M–$5M+ annualized run rate if reviews track jobs at even a 20% capture rate. Confidence: medium.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active Raptor Roofing job postings were found on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days as of this brief date. The broader Indianapolis roofing job market is active —

competitors like Trojan Roofing and Bone Dry are actively recruiting outside sales reps and commercial estimators in the market right now

— which makes the absence of Raptor postings notable. Either the team is fully staffed and referral-hiring, or there is a gap in public recruiting visibility that limits top-of-funnel talent acquisition. If a sales or production manager hire is coming, it is not yet public.

4. Off-Google Presence

Raptor holds a BBB A+ rating and has been BBB Accredited since September 30, 2022.

That is a legitimizing signal that many storm-chaser competitors skip.

On Yelp's "Best Roofing in Indianapolis" ranking (updated May 2026), Raptor Roofing appears in the top-10 list

, which is meaningful given Yelp's historically hostile posture toward contractor profiles.

Note: the Yelp listing surfaces under the "Raptor Restoration" brand name at the same 5145 S Meridian address

, creating a brand fragmentation issue — consumers searching "Raptor Roofing" on Yelp may land on a differently-branded profile, diluting review attribution.

The Facebook page at facebook.com/RaptorRoofingIndiana shows 1,062 likes and 65 people talking about it

— respectable engagement for a sub-5-year-old local contractor. No Facebook Ad Library activity was surfaced in search results, suggesting paid social is either inactive or not recently launched.

GuildQuality is also active as a third-party verified review platform

, which is an underrated trust signal for insurance adjuster and commercial decision-maker audiences.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

The raptorroofing.com domain has built out a substantial location-page architecture —

dedicated service-area pages for Indianapolis cover roof replacement, repair, storm restoration, gutters, siding, windows, and attic insulation, with content tailored to local weather patterns and property conditions.

The site is producing crawlable FAQ-style content, which is foundational AEO infrastructure.

Pricing content is present on the site, citing average roof replacement costs of $5,000–$15,000

— this is a direct schema/AEO play targeting "how much does a roof cost in Indianapolis"-type queries. However, some on-page content reads as keyword-stuffed (e.g., pages titled "Roofers Indianapolis Indiana" and "Residential Roofing Indianapolis Indiana"), which can create thin-content dilution risk under Google's Helpful Content standards. The blog does not appear to host original editorial content with local press backlinks — a clear gap for building domain authority signals that matter in competitive local pack rankings.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Web search did not surface any indexed AI-generated answer pages (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Claude) explicitly citing Raptor Roofing by name in response to queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville." This is both a threat and an opportunity. The generative engine citation layer is still thin for all local Indianapolis roofers — no competitor appears to own it either. The companies most likely to get cited first are those with the strongest combination of review volume, third-party directory mentions, and structured FAQ/schema content. Raptor's 673-review count and A+ BBB status are strong citation triggers, but only if the on-site schema (LocalBusiness, FAQPage, AggregateRating) is properly implemented. This should be confirmed via a schema audit at schema.org/validator. The GuildQuality profile and findtrustedroofers.com listing add citation-worthy third-party data points that generative engines can pull from.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Raptor leads: Review volume (673 at 5.0) is the single most defensible competitive moat in this market — no Indy competitor at this volume matches the rating. A 96% owner response rate signals operational discipline that most $5M contractors do not maintain. BBB A+ accreditation, GuildQuality verification, and a buildout service-area page architecture all stack in Raptor's favor for both organic trust and AEO citation potential.

Where the gap exists: The Yelp/brand-name fragmentation (Raptor Roofing vs. Raptor Restoration on the same address) is a lead-leakage problem that likely costs inbound conversions monthly. The most exploitable weakness right now is the absence of a structured content strategy — original editorial pieces, local press backlinks, and schema-validated FAQ pages that would push Raptor into generative engine answers ahead of every competitor in the market.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GuildQuality as a B2B trust amplifier.

GuildQuality collects verified customer feedback via third-party surveys

and the profile is already live. This is an underused asset — insurance adjusters, commercial property managers, and HOA boards search this platform. Cameron should actively route post-job survey requests through GuildQuality to build a parallel verified-review corpus that Google reviews cannot replicate.

  1. Brand consolidation on Yelp. The split between "Raptor Roofing" and "Raptor Restoration" on Yelp at the same address is a resolvable technical problem. Claim, merge, or redirect to a single Yelp profile under the primary brand. A competitor who notices this can easily outrank Raptor on Yelp for high-intent local queries simply because their profile isn't split.

  2. AEO-first content buildout targeting suburb queries.

The service footprint already spans Greenwood, Franklin, Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Avon, Brownsburg, and surrounding markets.

Each of those suburbs needs a 600–900-word service page with embedded FAQPage schema, AggregateRating markup pulling live review data, and at least one local press mention or community backlink. That combination is the specific recipe generative engines use to surface local contractors by name. No Indy roofer has fully executed it yet — this is a first-mover window.
