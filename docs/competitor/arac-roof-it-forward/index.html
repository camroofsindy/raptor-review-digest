Competitive Intelligence Brief: ARAC Roof It Forward

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Updated April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

In business since 2005, ARAC Roof It Forward has offered roofing services to local residential and commercial customers.

John Phillips, a graduate of the Coles College of Business, founded ARAC on the philosophy of doing what they say they will do, with excellence.

Co-owner

Craig Stevens serves alongside Phillips, with Kim Stevens as Office Manager and Mike Susits as National General Manager

— a management structure that signals multi-market ambitions beyond Indiana. Their primary differentiator is a cause-marketing hook:

they donate a portion of every dollar earned to local charities through the Roof It Forward program

, currently naming Peyton Manning Children's Hospital as the Indianapolis-area beneficiary.

2. Estimated Scale

Confidence: medium.

The BBB Georgia profile lists 96 employees across the ARAC enterprise.

The Indiana branch is a franchise/territory of a larger multi-state operation —

ARAC Roof It Forward offers roofing services to local residential and commercial customers and is headquartered in Woodstock, Georgia

, with Indiana as one of several markets. Indiana headcount is likely 15–30 people based on the sales rep density visible in reviews. Estimated Indiana-branch annual revenue: $3M–$6M (medium confidence), consistent with a mid-tier storm-restoration contractor running 10–20 installs per month.

ZoomInfo flags that ARAC has more open Sales roles than at any point in the past 12 months, and lists Salesforce and Google Reviews among their active technologies.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

ZipRecruiter shows ARAC Roof It Forward jobs ranging $19–$52/hr currently posted.

The dominant open role category is Roofing Sales Representative.

Candidates are expected to have strong knowledge of roofing products and sales processes, with familiarity in CRM systems, digital estimating tools, and basic project management software.

No marketing, ops leadership, or production management roles were surfaced — the hiring posture is pure top-of-funnel sales headcount, which is consistent with a storm-chasing, commission-driven growth model rather than a brand-building push. No evidence of a dedicated marketing hire in the last 30 days.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

ARAC operates two Indiana-facing domains — roofitforward.com and roofitforwardindiana.com — with the latter appearing to be a legacy or satellite site. The primary domain has geo-targeted landing pages for Indianapolis, roof repair, roof replacement, and commercial roofing, which is a competent but standard local SEO playbook.

They explicitly target Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, and surrounding areas in their page copy.

The Owens Corning Platinum Preferred status generates a backlink from the Owens Corning contractor directory, which carries domain authority. Their blog content appears thin and dated — no evidence of active content production targeting question-based or AI-answer-optimized queries. Schema markup was not confirmed. The dual-domain setup is an SEO liability; it splits link equity unless one canonically redirects to the other.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI engine behavior for Indianapolis roofing queries, ARAC Roof It Forward does appear in some generative results — primarily because their domain has aged authority (2005 founding), Owens Corning Platinum status is a credentialing signal that AI models weight, and they have sustained Angi/HomeAdvisor presence that feeds training corpora. However, their brand name lacks the citation clarity that AI engines prefer: two domains, "ARAC" vs. "Roof It Forward" as competing brand identifiers, and no authoritative local press links observed. For queries like "best roofer Noblesville Indiana" or "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," they likely appear in AI results as one of several options rather than a named top recommendation. Their review velocity of zero for 90 days is actively working against AEO positioning — AI engines increasingly weight recency of sentiment signals. Raptor's 664 reviews at 5.0 with active velocity is a stronger AEO profile right now.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where ARAC beats Raptor: Cause-marketing infrastructure is their sharpest edge —

donating to children's healthcare and organizations supporting children with cancer

gives them an emotionally resonant story that is genuinely defensible and hard to replicate quickly. They also have multi-state brand infrastructure and an Owens Corning MVP award (

the 2022 Owens Corning Southeast Region MVP Award

), which carries credibility with homeowners who research certifications. Their Angi footprint is also deeper than most competitors.

Where Raptor beats ARAC: Review count advantage (664 vs. 308), a full star-rating advantage (5.0 vs. 4.7), and critically, active review velocity vs. ARAC's complete stall. Raptor's single-brand identity is also cleaner for AEO purposes.

Most exploitable weakness: ARAC's review engine has flatlined — zero reviews in 90 days means their frontline sales reps have stopped asking, their follow-up sequence is broken, or rep turnover killed the habit. Cameron should hammer this gap in any sales conversation and in GMB posting cadence.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The charity tie-in as a closing tool. ARAC bakes cause-marketing into every sales conversation and every owner response.

They openly state that a portion of every dollar earned goes to fund the beneficiary in your area.

Raptor should formalize and quantify its own community-giving story — specificity (dollar amounts, named beneficiaries, quarterly totals) converts better than vague goodwill.

  1. Video inspection as a lead-capture mechanism.

ARAC's "free video inspection" brings the roof down to the homeowner digitally and walks them through what's happening on their roof.

This reduces friction to the first appointment and generates shareable content. It's also a strong AEO hook — "free video roof inspection Indianapolis" is an answerable query that ARAC has built pages around.

  1. Monitor their Angi/HomeAdvisor investment. ARAC clearly directs customers to Angi reviews, not just Google.

Their own website tells customers to see reviews on Angi, BBB, and Nextdoor

— a deliberate multi-platform distribution strategy. As Google's local review dominance gets pressured by AI-driven discovery, owning Angi profile authority is a hedge worth watching. Raptor's Angi presence should be audited for parity.
