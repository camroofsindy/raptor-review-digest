ARAC Roof It Forward — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing Dashboard | Updated: April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

ARAC Roof It Forward was founded by John Phillips, a Coles College of Business graduate, on the philosophy of doing what they say with excellence.

In business since 2005, ARAC operates out of Noblesville, IN (15499 Stony Creek Way) and serves the broader Indianapolis metro

—

including Noblesville, Carmel, Fishers, Greenwood, Indianapolis, Greenfield, Brownsburg, Plainfield, Anderson, and Zionsville.

Their core differentiator is a charity-anchored brand identity:

they donate a portion of every dollar earned to local charities through the Roof It Forward program,

currently tied to Peyton Manning Children's Hospital in the Indianapolis market. Note: ARAC is a multi-state franchise operation headquartered in Woodstock, GA — the Noblesville location is one of several regional franchises, not an independent local shop.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

ARAC leads with their Owens Corning Platinum Preferred status

as the primary SEO hook — it appears in title tags and H1s across most service pages.

They have dedicated city-level pages targeting Noblesville, Carmel, Fishers, Greenwood, Greenfield, Plainfield, Anderson, Zionsville, and Brownsburg

— a systematic location-page strategy that generates long-tail ranking coverage. Their domain (roofitforward.com) is a national franchise site; the Indiana franchise also runs a secondary domain at roofitforwardindiana.com, which creates potential canonical fragmentation — a structural SEO vulnerability Raptor does not share. Blog content is minimal and generic. Schema markup and local business structured data are deployed but not differentiated. No evidence of local press backlinks (IBJ, Indianapolis Star) or neighborhood-level content strategies targeting Hamilton County suburbs specifically.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on known AI training data and citation patterns as of this brief date: ARAC Roof It Forward has moderate but inconsistent generative engine presence. Their brand name ("Roof It Forward") is distinctive and memorable, which helps in AI recall. However, the dual-domain structure (roofitforward.com vs. roofitforwardindiana.com) creates entity-level confusion for LLMs trying to resolve the Indiana-specific entity. Raptor's opportunity: a single authoritative domain, a singular geographic identity, and a growing review corpus at 5.0 stars puts Cameron in a strong position to own AI-generated responses for hyper-local queries like "best roofer in Noblesville Indiana" or "roof replacement Carmel Indiana" — particularly if Raptor invests in structured AEO content (FAQ schema, dedicated suburb pages, first-person testimonial content that LLMs can parse cleanly). ARAC is not doing this with precision. No evidence of AEO-specific content strategy on either domain.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where ARAC beats Raptor: Charity brand narrative is deeply embedded and emotionally resonant — tying every invoice to Peyton Manning Children's Hospital is a sales-room closer that is hard to dismiss. They also have multi-platform review distribution (Angi, HomeAdvisor, BBB, Nextdoor) that gives them a broader funnel surface. Their city-page coverage across Hamilton and Marion County suburbs is more systematic than Raptor's current posture.

Where Raptor beats ARAC: 664 reviews at a perfect 5.0 vs. 308 at 4.7 is not a small gap — it's a dominant trust signal. Zero reviews in the last 90 days is a serious red flag; a stalled review engine is usually a stalled sales engine. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning hits the exact BBB complaint pattern that ARAC is accumulating — non-English-speaking subcontracted crews with no direct accountability loop.

Most exploitable weakness: ARAC's warranty follow-through complaints and crew communication failures are documented and recurring — Cameron should put that contrast on the table in every sales conversation without naming ARAC directly.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Charity tie-in at the point of close.

ARAC's model of donating a portion of every dollar to local charities

is a proven conversion tool in the sales room. Raptor does not need to replicate the structure, but a named, visible community commitment — announced in proposals and follow-up emails — creates emotional permission for customers to say yes at a premium price.

  1. Systematic suburb landing pages. ARAC's location-page architecture covering every major Hamilton and Marion County suburb is generating long-tail organic traffic Raptor is likely leaving on the table. Cameron should audit whether Raptor has dedicated, schema-marked pages for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, and Westfield — each with unique content, not duplicated boilerplate.

  2. Monitor the ZoomInfo sales hiring signal.

ARAC has more open Sales roles than at any point in the past 12 months, with Salesforce listed as a core tech stack.

A sales hiring surge ahead of storm season in a competitor's territory is an early warning that they are preparing to flood Hamilton County with door-knockers. Raptor should pre-empt with a spring campaign targeting post-storm urgency before ARAC reps hit the same driveways.
