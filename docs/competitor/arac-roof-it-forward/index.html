Competitive Intelligence Brief: ARAC Roof It Forward

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed June 9, 2025

1. Who They Are

In business since 2005, ARAC Roof It Forward has many years of experience offering high-quality roofing services to local residential and commercial customers.

They operate out of Noblesville (15499 Stony Creek Way) and serve the broader Indianapolis metro including Carmel, Fishers, Greenwood, Zionsville, and Anderson.

ARAC frames its mission in explicitly faith-based terms — "ARAC exists to honor God through giving," with a commitment to donating a portion of every dollar to local charities through its Roof It Forward program.

That cause-marketing wrapper is the brand's primary differentiator and the thing that makes it sticky with a specific buyer segment.

2. Estimated Scale

ARAC Roof It Forward has 51 to 200 employees

per Indeed's company profile — confidence: medium, as this is a self-reported band.

The BBB Georgia filing lists 96 employees and identifies John Phillips as owner.

That figure reflects the enterprise across all markets (Indianapolis, Atlanta, Nashville, Tampa, and others), not the Indy franchise alone. The Indiana location is a franchise/branch of the larger Georgia-headquartered entity.

BuildZoom records indicate at least 23 permitted projects over the past three years, with a typical permit size of approximately $15,375.

Extrapolating conservatively, Indiana-only revenue likely falls in the $3M–$6M range — confidence: low.

ZoomInfo notes ARAC has raised less than $5 million in one funding round

, suggesting the parent company has outside capital behind it.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

A Sales Associate posting appeared on LinkedIn under ARAC Roof It Forward, dated April 2026.

ZoomInfo flags that ARAC has more open roles in Sales than at any point in the past 12 months, with technologies mentioned including Salesforce and Google Reviews

— a signal they are building out a structured sales funnel, not just relying on inbound leads.

Indeed employee reviews describe the sales role as starting with cold calling and door-knocking, transitioning over time to appointment-based selling.

No marketing, ops leadership, or field management postings were identified for the Indiana market in the last 30 days. The hiring posture is almost entirely sales-rep-oriented.

4. Off-Google Presence

ARAC's Yelp page for the Noblesville location is active with 62 photos as of February 2026

, but review volume there is thin relative to their 310 Google reviews — a classic review-funneling pattern where Google solicitation is aggressive and Yelp is left to chance.

On Angi, the Indianapolis location is rated 4.9 out of 5

, and they carry an Angi Super Service Award.

The Indiana-specific Facebook page (facebook.com/aracroofitforwardin) has only 650 likes with 1 person currently talking about it

— low engagement for a multi-year operation.

The national ARAC Facebook page has 3,171 likes with 27 talking about it

, suggesting most paid social activity is run at the brand level, not localized. No active Indiana-specific Facebook Ad Library creatives were surfaced. The BBB Indiana profile is accredited,

but includes a notable complaint about damage to decking and ceiling caused during installation, including wiring and ductwork.

The Google vs. off-platform gap is significant: 4.7 on Google (310 reviews), strong on Angi, weak on Yelp and Facebook. That gap is a reputation arbitrage opportunity for Raptor.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

ARAC operates two parallel websites: roofitforward.com/indianapolis (the national franchise site) and roofitforwardindiana.com (an apparent legacy local site). Running two domains for the same business splits domain authority and creates brand confusion — a meaningful SEO vulnerability. The national site has city-specific landing pages for Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, and others, which is a competent local SEO play.

Their Carmel/Fishers/Noblesville service pages funnel to a shared phone number and push free video roof inspection as the lead gen CTA

— consistent but templated.

Their own site points visitors to Angi, BBB, and Nextdoor for social proof

, suggesting they are leaning on third-party authority rather than building owned review assets. Blog content on both domains is thin and generic. No evidence of structured schema markup, local press backlinks, or AEO-optimized FAQ content surfaced.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on known AI training patterns and the competitive landscape: ARAC Roof It Forward has the brand signals (Owens Corning Platinum Preferred status, 1,000+ cross-platform reviews, multi-city presence) that increase the likelihood of appearing in generative AI responses for broad queries like "best roofers Indianapolis." However, their dual-domain structure and thin blog/FAQ content reduce their AEO footprint for long-tail, intent-driven queries like "roof replacement Carmel Indiana" or "best roofing company Fishers Noblesville." Their faith-and-charity angle could earn citation in AI answers that emphasize community values, which is a distinct positioning lane. This is a watch-closely item: if ARAC invests in AEO-optimized content across their city pages, they could dominate AI citations in the northern suburbs (Noblesville, Carmel, Fishers) where they are physically located and operationally concentrated.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Cause-marketing narrative (Peyton Manning Children's Hospital partnership) is emotionally sticky and gives sales reps a built-in close line. Angi Super Service Award adds third-party credibility with an older homeowner demographic. Their insurance claim navigation sales process —

with reps actively helping customers work alongside insurance companies to secure full replacement coverage

— is a well-documented conversion accelerator that shortens the sales cycle on storm-damage jobs.

Where Raptor beats them: 677 reviews at 5.0 vs. 310 at 4.7. Raptor's review velocity and rating gap is material. Their review cadence is stalling — only 3 new Google reviews in 90 days is a flashing red warning that their CRM-to-review pipeline is broken or disengaged. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning directly counters ARAC's most exploitable weakness.

Most exploitable weakness:

BBB complaints from the Indiana location describe installation crew members who caused damage to decking, ceilings, wiring, and ductwork, with workers who did not speak English — a liability that directly undermines the "accountability" and "quality craftsmanship" brand claims.

That is the exact attack surface Raptor's positioning was built to own.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The insurance navigation sales track. ARAC has operationalized the insurance claim co-pilot role — reps are clearly trained to sit with homeowners and advocate through the adjuster process. Raptor should audit whether its own sales team is converting insurance-eligible leads at the same clip or leaving that revenue on the table.

  2. The charity tie-in as a close tool.

ARAC donates a portion of every job to help the work of Peyton Manning Children's Hospital

— and uses that in every response template and follow-up. Cameron should evaluate whether Raptor has a similarly concrete, named charity commitment that reps can verbalize in the close. A vague "we give back" claim loses to a specific hospital name every time.

  1. The free video roof inspection CTA.

During their free video inspection, ARAC brings the roof down to the homeowner, walks them through what's going on, and discusses next steps — whether that means repair, insurance-funded replacement, or no action at all.

This format lowers conversion friction and pre-frames the company as a trusted advisor rather than a salesperson. If Raptor is not running a structured remote/video inspection format as a top-of-funnel lead capture, this is worth building.
