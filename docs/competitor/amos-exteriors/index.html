Competitive Intelligence Brief — Amos Exteriors

Refreshed: Tuesday, August 12, 2026 | For: Cameron Blakely, Raptor Roofing

1. Who They Are

Amos Exteriors is a family-owned and operated company founded in 1971, operating as a GAF Master Elite Roofing Contractor out of Indianapolis, Indiana.

They have locations in Indianapolis and Lafayette, positioning themselves as Central Indiana's most trusted exterior remodeling contractor.

Their certified service footprint spans Zionsville, Carmel, Noblesville, Westfield, Fishers, Geist, Plainfield, Avon, Greenwood, and all of central Indiana

— near-identical overlap with Raptor's core market. Their differentiator is legacy credibility (55+ years) combined with a broad multi-trade platform: roofing, siding, gutters, windows, masonry, and painting under one brand.

2. Estimated Scale

Datanyze pegs Amos Exteriors at approximately 40 employees and $28.6M in revenue.

LeadIQ, as of July 2025, estimates approximately 55 employees across their locations.

The PPP loan record is informative as a floor:

Amos Exteriors received an SBA PPP loan of $584,200, noted as retaining 40 jobs.

Revenue estimate: $20–29M | Confidence: medium (Datanyze/LeadIQ figures; not audited). Employee count: 40–55 | Confidence: medium (cross-referenced PPP + LeadIQ). At that revenue-per-head, they are running a lean operation heavily reliant on commission-based sales reps.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Amos Exteriors has posted a Team Lead, Field Marketing role in Indianapolis on ZipRecruiter.

This is the most strategically significant signal in the last 30 days — they are building out a canvassing/field marketing operation, not just backfilling sales reps.

They are also actively recruiting Home Improvement Sales Consultants, with a model that trains reps to run company-provided leads while also generating and managing their own referrals.

ZoomInfo also flags an open Estimator and Project Manager search.

No CMO, digital marketing manager, or paid media hire visible — their marketing leadership appears to remain with existing staff.

BBB lists Jana Perrott as Marketing Manager and Bruce McClain as Director of Marketing

, suggesting a two-person in-house marketing team, which is thin for a $28M operator.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp:

Their Yelp listing at 7301 E 46th St shows only 33 reviews

against 3,241 on Google — a massive funnel gap. This is a near-textbook review-funneling operation: virtually all positive volume is directed exclusively to Google. Yelp is not being managed.

BBB:

BBB accredited since September 19, 1993

, with an A+ rating. However, active complaints are visible and substantive.

One October 2025 complaint alleges an employee deliberately damaged a homeowner's property to simulate hail damage, filed a fraudulent insurance claim, and the company refused to repair the damage — the employee was reportedly trying to hit an end-of-year bonus quota.

This is not a minor service complaint. It is a liability and reputational exposure.

Facebook:

Their Facebook page sits at 1,412 likes with only 6 people currently talking about it

— essentially a dormant social presence. No evidence of active Facebook Ad Library spend was surfaced.

Glassdoor:

A February 2025 Glassdoor review from a former sales rep notes good products but flags that reps must supply their own ladder and boots

— a tells-you-everything detail about their commission-heavy, low-overhead field sales model.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Amos Exteriors' tech stack includes WordPress, PHP, jQuery 1.11.1, and MySQL

— a dated infrastructure. jQuery 1.11.1 is particularly telling; it was released in 2014 and signals a site that has not been modernized at the core, which creates Core Web Vitals drag. Their domain (amosexteriors.com) has deep location page architecture —

they have built out named suburb pages covering Carmel, Noblesville, Fishers, Zionsville, Greenwood, and others

, which is a strong local SEO playbook. They hold certifications that generate third-party backlinks:

Amos Exteriors is a Google Guaranteed contractor and an Angie's List Super Service Award winner

, both of which yield authoritative inbound links. Their blog and schema status are not confirmed from available data; given the WordPress/jQuery age, schema implementation is likely inconsistent. No evidence of active local press/editorial backlinks surfaced.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Amos Exteriors carries strong AI citation inputs: a 55-year brand history, GAF Master Elite status, 3,241 Google reviews at 4.9, BBB A+ accreditation, and named suburb landing pages that match common AI query patterns ("roofing Carmel Indiana," "roofer Fishers Noblesville"). These are exactly the signals LLMs pull when constructing local contractor recommendations. They are almost certainly cited by ChatGPT and Gemini for broad "best roofers Indianapolis" queries — their review volume and longevity are too significant to be excluded from training data signals. However, the October 2025 BBB fraud complaint is now indexed and crawlable. As AI models refresh and begin pulling more recent web content, that complaint becomes a negative citation risk for them in generative results. Raptor's 5.0 rating with zero BBB complaints is a clean AEO signal that Amos cannot match.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Raw review volume (3,241 vs. 697) dominates map pack trust signals and AI training data weighting. Their multi-trade platform (siding, windows, masonry, gutters) allows them to monetize a single customer relationship across multiple jobs in a way a roofing-focused operation cannot. Their 55-year brand legacy creates near-automatic referrals from the insurance adjuster community, as noted in Yelp reviews.

Where Raptor beats Amos: Raptor holds a 5.0 star rating — a perfect score that Amos cannot claim at 4.9. Raptor has zero equivalent BBB fraud-level complaints. Amos's internal sales culture —

canvassers who "will set for anything" with homeowners who don't know a rep is coming

— creates a churn-and-burn field dynamic that eventually manifests in the kind of October 2025 BBB allegation that defines a brand's ceiling. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is a direct surgical attack on that model.

Most exploitable weakness: The October 2025 BBB complaint alleging an employee staged hail damage to hit a bonus quota is a ticking SEO/AEO time bomb — Cameron should ensure Raptor's sales process transparency is explicitly documented in content and review responses before this story gains wider traction.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Suburb landing page depth.

Amos has built named location pages for every major Indy suburb — Carmel, Noblesville, Fishers, Zionsville, Greenwood, Westfield, and more.

If Raptor does not have equivalently optimized, schema-marked pages for each of these markets, Amos will continue to outrank on hyper-local queries and dominate LLM geographic lookups. Build them now, before the next algorithm cycle.

  1. Field marketing team-lead structure. Amos is actively hiring a Team Lead for Field Marketing — meaning they are scaling canvassing with a supervisory layer for quality control. The inside Glassdoor feedback suggests their current canvassing is sloppy. Raptor should watch whether this hire cleans up lead quality or just adds overhead. If it cleans up, the pipeline gap widens. Cameron should pressure-test Raptor's own lead gen diversity against a competitor that is actively building street-level demand generation.

  2. Insurance adjuster relationship channel.

At least two Yelp reviewers reference Amos being trusted by insurance adjusters as a known, reliable contractor.

This is a referral channel most roofing companies never formalize. A structured adjuster outreach program — even a simple lunch-and-learn series or co-branded storm guide — could give Raptor a share of the claim-driven pipeline that Amos has historically owned by default of longevity.
