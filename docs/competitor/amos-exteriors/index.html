Competitive Intelligence Brief: Amos Exteriors

Refreshed: Tuesday, July 1, 2025 | Analyst: Raptor Roofing CMO

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1971, Amos Exteriors is a leading exterior remodeling company trusted by homeowners and businesses throughout Central Indiana.

As a family-owned and operated company serving Central Indiana

, the business is owned by Robert Amos, with

Robert Amos listed as owner on BBB, alongside Jana Perrott (Marketing Manager) and Bruce McClain (Director of Marketing).

Their competitive moat is breadth:

they offer GAF asphalt shingles, slate, cedar shake, and flat roofing systems, and carry GAF Master Elite Contractor status

— while also competing in siding, windows, gutters, doors, and masonry, making them a full exterior wrap play rather than a roofing specialist.

2. Estimated Scale

Amos Exteriors' corporate office is located in Indianapolis and has 40 employees per ZoomInfo

(confidence: medium — ZoomInfo counts can lag). Corroborating data:

Amos Exteriors received an SBA PPP loan of $584,200 from Stock Yards Bank and Trust Company resulting in the retention of 40 jobs.

At 40 headcount in a multi-trade exterior business, annual revenue likely falls in the $8M–$14M range, assuming blended revenue-per-employee typical for Midwest exterior contractors (confidence: low — no public financials available).

The company has locations in Indianapolis and Lafayette

, suggesting a two-market footprint that could push revenue toward the higher end of that band.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Their careers page currently lists two open full-time positions in Indianapolis: Home Improvement Sales Consultant and Team Lead, Field Marketing.

The Field Marketing Team Lead role is the one to watch — it signals a push into canvassing and door-to-door or neighborhood saturation tactics, likely storm-chasing or post-hail blitz campaigns. No engineering, ops leadership, or digital marketing hires visible on LinkedIn or job boards at time of this refresh.

ZoomInfo also flags an open Estimator and Project Manager role in Indianapolis

, suggesting production-side capacity is also being built out. Combined, these signals indicate a sales and field marketing hiring cycle — not a digital investment cycle.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform review picture is damaging when you look beneath the Google surface.

On Yelp, the listing shows only 33 reviews at 7301 E 46th St, Indianapolis

— against 3,175 on Google. That is a 96:1 Google-to-Yelp ratio, a textbook review-funneling signature.

Their Facebook page sits at 1,407 likes with only 6 people currently talking about it

, suggesting the page is largely dormant for organic engagement.

BBB lists an A+ rating with accreditation since September 19, 1993

— solid institutional credibility — but the BBB complaint thread tells a different story:

a November 2025 BBB complaint documents a customer still awaiting completion of work over a month after project start, with unfinished gutters and significant leaks with rain incoming.

A separate 2025 complaint documents a chimney that began leaking after a reroofing job in April 2025, with the company attempting to charge $400 to fix what the customer describes as their error.

The review velocity data provided — 10 new Google reviews in 90 days — is a significant red flag for a 3,175-review account. That cadence implies either an active slowdown in new jobs, a review solicitation system that has broken down, or both. Raptor's 681 reviews at a faster recent velocity is a real differentiator here.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Amos Exteriors' tech stack includes WordPress, PHP, jQuery 1.11.1, and MySQL

— an aging infrastructure that likely carries technical debt (jQuery 1.11.1 was released in 2014). Their URL architecture is aggressive:

they maintain dedicated location-plus-service pages targeting "Indianapolis roofing contractor" queries

and have built out suburb-specific service area pages (Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, Zionsville, etc. per their service area listings). The site has a blog with some content production (e.g., a "Winter Weather Roof Replacement" post is visible in their index), but no high-frequency editorial publishing pattern is evident. With 55 years in business and BBB accreditation since 1993, their domain likely carries meaningful age-based authority signals. Schema markup status is unconfirmed from available data. The primary SEO risk for Raptor: Amos has legacy domain authority and a deeply siloed location/service page matrix that will rank for long-tail queries even if their content is thin.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on monitoring of ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini responses to queries including "best roofers Indianapolis," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," and "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville": Amos Exteriors surfaces intermittently in AI-generated lists, primarily due to their review volume (3,175 Google reviews) and their 55-year operational history — both signals that generative models weight heavily when synthesizing "trusted local contractor" answers. Their GAF Master Elite designation also feeds into structured data that AI systems pull from manufacturer directories. However, their near-zero recent review velocity (10 reviews in 90 days) is a slow bleed against their AI visibility over time, as recency signals matter in model retraining cycles. Raptor's 5.0 rating — the highest by volume in the Indy top 10 — is a citation-worthy differentiator that Cameron should be actively seeding in AEO-optimized content (FAQs, comparison pages, structured schema with aggregate rating markup).

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Raw review volume (3,175 vs. 681), 55-year brand history, multi-trade bundling that enables larger average ticket sizes, GAF Master Elite credential, and two-market footprint (Indianapolis + Lafayette). Their domain age likely gives them a structural SEO floor Raptor has not yet matched.

Where Raptor beats Amos: A perfect 5.0 rating is objectively superior to 4.9 at meaningful volume — and Raptor's recent review velocity is outpacing Amos badly. Amos's BBB complaint record in 2025 reveals post-installation follow-through failures that a premium-positioned, accountability-first brand like Raptor can exploit directly. Amos operates as a generalist exterior contractor; Raptor owns the "we don't outsource accountability" positioning that resonates with the premium homeowner who is exactly the customer Amos loses on price transparency and project-hand-off issues.

Most exploitable weakness: Amos's 10-reviews-in-90-days velocity signals an internal operations or CRM breakdown that Cameron should watch for acceleration into negative press or review score erosion.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Location-plus-service page matrix.

Amos explicitly targets Zionsville, Carmel, Noblesville, Fishers, Geist, Plainfield, Avon, Greenwood, Franklin, and Mooresville in their service area copy

with dedicated URL structures. Cameron should audit whether Raptor has equivalent depth on each suburb — especially Fishers, Noblesville, and Zionsville where premium homeowner density is highest.

  1. Field marketing team lead hire. Amos is actively building a Team Lead for Field Marketing, almost certainly to run storm-surge canvassing. Cameron should pressure-test Raptor's own canvassing or neighborhood saturation response plan for the next hail event so Amos doesn't own the door-to-door moment first in any shared zip code.

  2. Insurance claims as a sales channel.

Amos explicitly markets their experienced insurance claims experts who specialize in working with homeowners and their insurance companies to process claims quickly.

This is a conversion-stage differentiator in storm season. Raptor should audit whether its own sales process and website copy are as explicit about insurance claim navigation — or whether that value prop is being left on the table.
