Amos Exteriors — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Updated: April 26, 2025 | Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1971, Amos Exteriors is a leading exterior remodeling company trusted by homeowners and businesses throughout Central Indiana.

Owner is Robert Amos; the marketing function is led by Bruce McClain (Director of Marketing) and Jana Perrott (Marketing Manager).

They operate with locations in both Indianapolis and Lafayette, IN

, and their scope goes well beyond roofing — siding, windows, doors, gutters, masonry, and commercial roofing all sit under one roof, making them a full-service exterior contractor rather than a pure-play roofer.

2. Estimated Scale

RocketReach pegs annual revenue at $28.6 million (2025 figure) with approximately 57 employees.

Cross-referencing with Prospeo,

employee count is estimated in the 51–100 range.

The PPP loan data adds weight:

Amos Exteriors received an SBA PPP loan of $584,200, retaining 40 jobs

— suggesting 40+ direct W-2 staff at peak COVID headcount, consistent with 50-60 today inclusive of subcontractors. Confidence: medium. Revenue estimate likely reflects storm-surge years; a normalized run-rate is probably $18M–$25M.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

ZipRecruiter shows an open posting for a Team Lead, Field Marketing in Indianapolis

— this is the most relevant recent signal. The title suggests they are scaling door-knock / canvassing operations, not investing in inbound digital.

ZoomInfo flags an active search for an Estimator and Project Manager in Indianapolis.

No marketing technology, paid media, or digital-first roles are visible across Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days. The hiring profile reads as a company adding field sales muscle, not a marketing transformation.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform review picture is damning for a company sitting at 3,046 Google reviews.

Yelp shows only 2.5 stars

on 19 reviews — a classic review-funneling operation where happy customers are directed exclusively to Google and unhappy ones land on Yelp organically.

LinkedIn has only 321 followers

, indicating minimal organic social investment.

Facebook shows 1,383 likes with only 23 talking about this

— the page is effectively dormant.

BBB accreditation dates to September 1993 with an A+ rating

, which they lean on heavily in credentialing language. The BBB complaint log, however, contains documented job-delay and post-installation leak issues, including

a November 2025 complaint where work remained unfinished after a month, with missing gutter components and active leaks going into a rain event.

No Facebook Ad Library activity was detectable from standard search, suggesting limited or no paid social spend.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Amos Exteriors runs on WordPress with PHP, jQuery 1.11.1, and MySQL

— an aging stack. jQuery 1.11.1 is a 2014 release; this signals technical debt that likely affects Core Web Vitals. They have built out a geographic landing page structure (e.g., amosexteriors.com/locations/indianapolis-in, amosexteriors.com/service-areas/indianapolis-roofing), which is a legitimate local SEO play and worth noting.

Their GAF Master Elite, James Hardie Contractor Alliance, and Gaco Western certifications

generate legitimate backlinks from manufacturer directories. Dual-location structure (Indy + Lafayette) means they're spreading domain authority across two GBP footprints. Blog content is thin to non-existent based on site structure. No schema signals observed beyond basic business listing markup.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI engine behavior (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," and "top roofing contractor Greenwood Indiana": Amos Exteriors surfaces in AI-generated results due primarily to two factors — the sheer volume of their 3,046 Google reviews and their 54-year operating history, both of which AI models weight heavily as trust proxies. They are likely cited by name in general "top Indianapolis roofers" answers. However, their Yelp 2.5-star rating and absence from recent local press create citation gaps in more nuanced AI answers that cross-reference multiple platforms. Raptor's 5.0 rating at 664 reviews is a cleaner citation signal for quality-focused queries; the gap is raw volume, not legitimacy.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Raw review volume (3,046 vs 664) dominates local pack rankings and AI citation frequency; 54-year brand history generates institutional trust with older demographics; dual-location footprint (Lafayette) captures a market Raptor does not touch; full exterior services (windows, siding, masonry) create larger average ticket and cross-sell loops;

dedicated insurance claims specialists

are a structured, scalable storm-restoration sales process.

Where Raptor beats Amos: 5.0 vs 4.9 at meaningful volume is a real differentiator for quality-first buyers; zero Google reviews in the last 90 days on Amos's profile is a serious warning sign of a stalled or manipulated review program — Raptor's recency is clean; owner response rate of zero reinforces that Amos leadership is disengaged from the customer conversation post-sale; the BBB complaint trail and Yelp score create a verifiable credibility gap Raptor can exploit in sales conversations.

Most exploitable weakness: Amos has a Director of Marketing and a Marketing Manager on payroll yet is generating zero recent Google reviews and running what appears to be a dormant social presence — the marketing investment is not producing measurable digital output, which means their 3,046-review moat is slowly eroding while Raptor's is still being built.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Geographic landing page architecture. Amos has built individual URL slugs for each suburb they serve (Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, etc.). This is the correct local SEO structure and each page is an AEO citation opportunity. Cameron should audit whether Raptor's current site matches this depth — one thin homepage is not enough to compete for suburb-level AI and search queries.

  2. GAF Master Elite positioning.

The GAF Master Elite designation is held by only a small percentage of roofing companies nationwide.

Amos leads with this credential prominently. If Raptor holds or can pursue this certification, it belongs in every ad headline, GBP description, and AI-optimized FAQ — it is a specific, verifiable differentiator that AI models cite.

  1. Monitor the review velocity gap. Amos at 0 new reviews in 90 days on a 3,046-review profile is either a platform flag, a process breakdown, or a canvassing team that is no longer closing into review asks. Cameron should track this monthly — if the gap holds, it creates a legitimate "most reviewed AND most recent" narrative for Raptor to own in local pack positioning within 6–12 months at current trajectory.
