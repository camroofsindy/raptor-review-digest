Competitor Brief: Amos Exteriors

7301 E 46th St, Indianapolis, IN 46226 | amosexteriors.com
Refreshed: April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1971, Amos Exteriors is a leading exterior remodeling company trusted by homeowners and businesses throughout Central Indiana.

As a family-owned company with more than 50 years of experience, they provide exterior home remodeling solutions spanning roofing, siding, windows, doors, gutters, and masonry.

Owner Robert Amos runs the company with a marketing team that includes a named Director of Marketing (Bruce McClain) —

they hold BBB A+ accreditation and memberships in the NRCA and the Indianapolis Apartment Association.

Their distinguishing structural advantage is a full multi-trade platform (roofing is just one of six service lines) and a Lafayette, IN second location, giving them geographic and revenue diversification that pure-play roofing shops cannot match.

2. Estimated Scale

RocketReach estimates the company at approximately 57 employees and $28.6 million in annual revenue for 2025.

Confidence: medium — third-party revenue aggregators in this space run 20-30% wide.

A more grounded floor: their SBA PPP loan of $584,200 cited retention of 40 jobs

, which aligns with a 40-60 headcount range for a multi-trade shop.

Prospeo independently pegs headcount at 51-100.

Confidence on headcount: medium. At 3,046 Google reviews and a pull-through rate consistent with volume-based home services shops, $15-25M ARR is the defensible range. The $28.6M top-line is plausible if commercial and multi-family work is included.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Their careers page currently lists a Home Improvement Sales Consultant role for the Indianapolis market, with reps running company-provided leads plus building their own referral pipeline.

ZoomInfo flags an open Estimator and Project Manager role in Indianapolis.

ZipRecruiter shows a Team Lead, Field Marketing posting (listed October 2024, still indexed)

— that field marketing team lead role is the most interesting signal: it indicates a canvassing/door-knock operation at scale, not just inbound leads. No digital marketing, paid media, or ops leadership hires were found in the last 30 days. The hiring profile skews toward field sales and estimating, not brand or growth infrastructure.

4. Off-Google Presence

The platform disparity here is glaring and exploitable. On Google they carry 3,046 reviews at 4.9 stars.

On Yelp, Amos Roofing and Exteriors sits at 2.5 stars

across only 19 reviews — a near-textbook review-funneling setup where satisfied customers are routed to Google and dissatisfied customers self-select to Yelp.

Their Facebook page shows 1,383 likes with only 23 people talking about it

, indicating a dormant or near-unmanaged social presence. No active Facebook Ad Library presence was surfaced in search results.

BBB accreditation dates to September 19, 1993

— long tenure, A+ rating, and no pattern of complaints visible in results. The Yelp gap (4.9 Google vs. 2.5 Yelp) is a vulnerability Cameron can reference in sales conversations with prospects who cross-check platforms.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Amos runs a WordPress-based site (confirmed via ZoomInfo tech stack) with a structured location-page architecture: they have dedicated URLs for Indianapolis, Indianapolis Roofing, commercial roofing, and individual suburb pages.

Their certifications include GAF Master Elite, James Hardie Contractor Alliance, GAF Weather Stopper Certified, and Gaco Western Certified

— all of which generate credible third-party backlinks from manufacturer directories. The GAF.com profile page alone is a domain-authority pass. They surface in the top organic results for broad "Indianapolis roofing" and "Indianapolis exterior remodeling" queries. No evidence of an active blog with recent, topically dense content was found. Their site does not appear to be producing structured FAQ or how-to content at the cadence needed to win AI-cited snippets.

They do run a weather-event lookup tool on their site

, which is a smart AEO/lead-gen play — it personalizes urgency by address and functions as a soft insurance-claim pre-qualifier.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Amos Exteriors is a likely AI citation target due to sheer review volume (3,046 on Google), 54-year operational history, named certifications (GAF Master Elite, BBB A+), and multi-platform indexed presence including GAF.com, BBB.org, Yelp, and Facebook. AI models trained on crawl data from those authoritative domains will surface Amos by name when responding to queries like "best roofers Indianapolis" or "roofing contractor Carmel Indiana." Their manufacturer directory listings on gaf.com are structured-data-rich pages that LLMs parse cleanly. Raptor currently has fewer indexed third-party authority signals, which is the core AEO gap. Cameron should be building schema-tagged review aggregation pages, manufacturer directory profiles, and structured Q&A content targeting the exact query strings LLMs are answering — especially suburb-specific queries where Amos does not have individual landing pages.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Raw review volume (3,046 vs. 664), a 54-year brand history that AI models treat as a credibility signal, a formal marketing department with a named Director of Marketing, multi-trade revenue diversification, and a second Lafayette location. Their weather-event lookup tool is a lead qualification asset Raptor does not have.

Where Raptor beats Amos: Raptor holds a 5.0 star average vs. Amos's 4.9 — and more importantly, Raptor's 5.0 is the highest-by-volume in the Indy top 10, which is a verifiable differentiator in both human and AI-cited comparisons. Raptor's owner-response rate and premium accountability positioning contrast sharply with Amos's near-dormant Facebook presence and 2.5-star Yelp profile. Raptor operates a tighter, more defensible quality narrative.

Most exploitable weakness: Amos's Yelp score of 2.5 stars on 19 reviews is a public, searchable liability — Raptor should be specifically asking prospects mid-funnel, "Have you checked their Yelp?" and letting the gap close itself.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. The weather-event address lookup tool. Amos's free weather report tool at amosexteriors.com/indianapolis is a pre-sale urgency engine that converts homeowner curiosity into a scoped lead without requiring a cold call. Cameron should evaluate building or licensing a comparable hail/weather-history lookup tied to Raptor's estimate request flow.

  2. Manufacturer directory profile depth. Amos has active, structured profiles on GAF.com, BBB.org, and Owens Corning's contractor locator — all high-DA pages that feed AI citation engines. Raptor should audit every manufacturer partner (GAF, Owens Corning, etc.) and ensure each profile is fully built out with correct NAP, service descriptions, and photos. These are free backlinks that directly influence generative engine answers.

  3. Field marketing canvassing operation.

Employee reviews note that leads run by their sales reps include long-distance canvasser-set appointments, sometimes with homeowners who didn't know a rep was coming

— a signal that Amos runs a high-volume, high-churn field canvassing machine. Cameron should understand whether Raptor wants to compete on volume canvassing or hold its premium positioning and let Amos's churn-and-burn reputation in that channel do the differentiation work organically.
