Amos Exteriors — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing Dashboard | Refreshed: July 23, 2025

1. Who They Are

Amos Exteriors, Inc. is a family-owned and operated company founded in 1971 and holds GAF Master Elite Roofing Contractor status.

They operate locations in both Indianapolis and Lafayette, Indiana.

Beyond roofing, they service both commercial and residential clients and carry multiple manufacturer certifications including GAF Master Elite, James Hardie Contractor Alliance, and Gaco Western, covering a broad service footprint across nearly every Central Indiana suburb.

2. Estimated Scale

Revenue is cited at approximately $28.6 million

(confidence: low — sourced from Power100.io, a directory-style site with unknown methodology).

Headcount is approximately 40 employees per the same source.

A 2020 SBA PPP loan filing corroborates the 40-person figure, with a loan of $584,200 retaining 40 jobs

(confidence: medium on headcount — two independent sources align).

LeadIQ as of July 2025 estimates approximately 55 employees

, suggesting measured growth since PPP. Revenue estimate of $28.6M is treated as a ceiling; real figure likely in the $10–20M range for a single-market exterior contractor.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Amos has posted a Team Lead, Field Marketing role on ZipRecruiter in Indianapolis

, which is a meaningful signal — field marketing lead generation (door-knocking, canvassing) is a core volume driver for them.

Their careers page is also actively listing a Home Improvement Sales Consultant position, which involves running company-provided leads while also building personal referral pipelines.

ZoomInfo also flags an open Estimator and Project Manager search.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on known AI training data patterns and the profile of amosexteriors.com: Amos Exteriors is very likely cited in generative AI responses for broad queries like "best roofers Indianapolis" or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville" due to two factors — their review volume (3,210 Google reviews) and domain age. High review count is one of the most reliable predictors of inclusion in LLM-generated local contractor recommendations, as models are trained on aggregated review content and entity mentions across the web. Their dual-city footprint (Indianapolis + Lafayette), multiple manufacturer certification pages, and NRCA/BBB membership also increase entity prominence in structured data crawls. Raptor currently has 692 reviews — strong quality signal at 5.0 stars, but the sheer volume gap means Amos is likely outperforming Raptor in AEO at the entity-mention level. This is the most pressing structural disadvantage in the AI-search layer.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Review volume (3,210 vs. 692) is a wide gap that drives both traditional local pack dominance and generative engine visibility. Dual-location footprint expands their addressable market. Broad multi-trade offering (windows, siding, painting, masonry) means more entry points and larger average ticket potential. In-house marketing staff (Director of Marketing + Marketing Manager) gives them more execution bandwidth than most competitors.

Where Raptor beats Amos: Raptor holds a 5.0 star rating versus Amos's 4.9 — meaningful in premium positioning. Raptor's review velocity is the more important flag: Amos pulled only 8 new Google reviews over the last 90 days. That is a near-complete stall on review acquisition for a company with 40+ employees and claimed $28M in revenue. That volume figure also invites scrutiny. An 0.7 owner response rate at 3,210 reviews suggests a disengaged review strategy — the reviews are there, but nobody is working them. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning hits directly at what the Indeed reviews say internally about Amos: canvassers setting unqualified appointments, long drives for small jobs, management inconsistency.

Most exploitable weakness: Amos's review engine has flatlined (8 reviews in 90 days) — Raptor should be aggressively converting every closed job into a review to close the volume gap before Amos reactivates.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Manufacturer certification stacking.

Amos holds GAF Master Elite, James Hardie Contractor Alliance, GAF Weather Stopper Certified, and Gaco Western Certified designations simultaneously.

Multi-cert stacking creates more indexable credential pages, more manufacturer directory backlinks, and more trust signals in AI training data. Cameron should audit which certifications Raptor holds vs. could obtain and build dedicated landing pages for each.

  1. Location/service-area page architecture. Amos has built out suburb-specific landing pages across the full Central Indiana footprint. The execution has duplication problems, but the underlying strategy is correct for both local SEO and AEO. Raptor should build cleaner, unique-content suburb pages for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, and Zionsville — and do it better than Amos's thin versions.

  2. Field marketing canvassing model — with better lead qualification.

Indeed reviewers flag that Amos's canvassers will set appointments for anything, often leaving homeowners unaware a rep is coming.

Amos is investing in scale; Raptor's counter-move is investing in quality. A targeted neighborhood canvassing program run post-storm — focused on blocks with verified insurance claims — would directly compete on their volume model while protecting Raptor's premium close rate.
