Competitive Intelligence Brief: Amos Exteriors

7301 E 46th St, Indianapolis, IN 46226 | amosexteriors.com
Refreshed: Tuesday, May 20, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1971, Amos Exteriors is a leading exterior remodeling company serving homeowners and businesses throughout Central Indiana.

Family-owned and operated since 1971, they hold GAF Master Elite Contractor status and maintain an A+ rating with the BBB, along with memberships in the NRCA and Indianapolis Apartment Association.

They now operate locations in both Indianapolis and Lafayette, IN,

and their service line extends well beyond roofing — encompassing siding, windows, doors, gutters, masonry, and commercial exteriors — making them a full-envelope competitor, not just a roofing shop.

2. Estimated Scale

RocketReach estimates approximately 57 employees and $28.6 million in annual revenue

(confidence: low — third-party modeled data).

Prospeo pegs headcount at 51–100 employees

with a separate revenue estimate of $12M. The wide spread between data sources ($12M–$28.6M) is typical for a company this size that doesn't publish financials. The most operationally meaningful data point:

Amos received an SBA PPP loan of $584,200, retaining 40 jobs,

which suggests a W-2 headcount floor of ~40 at time of application.

Their sales department alone is reportedly led by a Director of Sales and includes 21 employees,

signaling a heavily sales-rep-forward staffing model. Revenue estimate: $15M–$22M. Confidence: medium.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

ZoomInfo flags an active search for an Estimator and Project Manager

in Indianapolis.

Their careers page is actively recruiting Home Improvement Sales Consultants, with reps expected to run company-provided leads as well as generate their own referrals.

The more strategically notable signal:

ZipRecruiter shows a posting for a Team Lead, Field Marketing

— meaning they are building out a canvassing infrastructure with supervisory structure, not just adding bodies.

BBB lists a Director of Marketing (Bruce McClain) and a Marketing Manager (Jana Perrott)

as named contacts, indicating an in-house marketing function that most competitors their size don't maintain. No digital marketing, SEO, or paid media specialist roles were publicly visible in the last 30 days.

4. Off-Google Presence

The Google/Yelp gap here is stark and exploitable. Amos carries 3,094 Google reviews at 4.9 stars but

holds only a 2.5-star rating on Yelp

across

19 Yelp reviews

— a classic review-funneling setup where satisfied customers are directed exclusively to Google and Yelp is left unmanaged.

BBB accreditation dates to 9/19/1993

with an A+ rating, and their BBB page is well-maintained with named contacts.

Facebook shows 1,383 page likes with 23 talking about the page

— thin engagement for a company this size, suggesting Facebook is used as a credibility placeholder, not an active community or paid media channel. No active Facebook Ad Library campaigns were surfaced in the search results. On Angi (formerly Angie's List), they hold multiple positive reviews and

have won the Angie's List Super Service Award.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their tech stack includes Yoast SEO, WP Rocket, Open Graph, and Nginx

— a WordPress build with basic on-page SEO tooling in place. They have built out a dense location and service-area page architecture:

service area pages explicitly name Zionsville, Carmel, Noblesville, Westfield, Fishers, Geist, Plainfield, Avon, Speedway, Broad Ripple, Greenwood, Franklin, Mooresville, Anderson, and Greenfield.

This is aggressive local SEO page sprawl — they are hunting every suburb Cameron targets. However,

their core tech stack runs on jQuery 1.11.1 and MySQL

— legacy infrastructure that suggests slow page speed and poor Core Web Vitals, a compounding weakness as Google increasingly weights page experience. No evidence of structured data / schema markup beyond what Yoast auto-generates. No local press backlinks were surfaced. Their AEO posture is thin — no FAQ schema, no "best of" citations in local media, and no visible content designed to answer long-tail conversational queries.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Amos Exteriors is a credible AI citation candidate due to their review volume and 54-year operating history, but their generative engine visibility is structurally weak. Their website runs on aging infrastructure with minimal structured data, no evident third-party editorial coverage (local press, industry publications), and no content formatted for AI extraction.

Brands are 6.5x more likely to be cited in AI answers through third-party sources than through their own domains,

and

domain authority signals — specifically external referring domains — correlate strongly with AI Overview appearances.

Amos has neither. In manual review of AI-generated responses to prompts like "best roofers Indianapolis" or "top roofing contractor near Carmel Indiana," Amos surfaces due to raw review volume but is not consistently named first or described with specific differentiating language. Their 54-year history and GAF Master Elite status are the two attributes most likely to pull AI citations if they were properly marked up and echoed in third-party sources — they are not doing this deliberately.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Raw review volume (3,094 vs. 673), brand age and legacy trust signals, broader service line (windows, doors, masonry, painting, commercial), dedicated in-house marketing staff, and a multi-location footprint now extending to Lafayette. Where Raptor beats Amos: Perfect 5.0 rating vs. 4.9 (meaningful in AI ranking logic and conversion psychology); zero accountability-outsourcing in the service model vs. Amos's canvasser-driven, high-volume sales rep structure that [per Indeed reviews] sends reps on unqualified 1.5-hour-drive leads; and a cleaner, more premium brand identity vs. Amos's volume-market positioning. Most exploitable weakness:

Inside reviews describe leads that are "usually long drives" with reps traveling 1.5 hours each way for unqualified appointments, and canvassers setting appointments where homeowners "either don't know you're coming or don't understand why you're there"

— a sales ops and customer experience problem that Raptor's high-touch, accountable model directly counters in the market.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Dedicated location/service-area page architecture. Amos has a page for nearly every suburb in central Indiana targeting "[City] + roofing" queries. Raptor should build equivalent depth — specifically for Carmel, Fishers, Zionsville, Noblesville, and Greenwood — with city-specific content, project photos, and schema markup that Amos is not executing properly. Do it cleaner and faster.

  2. Named marketing leadership as a credibility signal.

Amos lists a Director of Marketing and a Marketing Manager by name on their BBB profile.

This is a small but real trust signal in B2C home services. Cameron should ensure Raptor's BBB and Google Business profiles list marketing/leadership contacts and use that visibility to create content authority (op-eds, local press quotes, home improvement columns) that Amos is not generating.

  1. Field marketing team-lead structure. The Team Lead, Field Marketing posting on ZipRecruiter signals Amos is scaling a structured canvassing operation with management hierarchy. Watch whether this translates into heavier presence at Lowes/Home Depot parking lot events, HOA outreach, or neighborhood drop campaigns in Raptor's core zip codes — and prepare a counter-program that emphasizes Raptor's referral-first, non-canvassing reputation as a premium differentiator.
