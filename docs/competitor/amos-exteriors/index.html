Amos Exteriors — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Updated: April 26, 2025

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1971, Amos Exteriors is a leading exterior remodeling company trusted by homeowners and businesses throughout Central Indiana.

As a family-owned company with more than 50 years of experience, they provide exterior home remodeling solutions across residential and commercial segments.

Owner of record is Robert Amos (per BBB). They are not a roofing-only shop —

they offer GAF asphalt shingles, slate, cedar shake, and flat roofing systems, and carry GAF Master Elite Contractor status, which grants access to the GAF System Plus Warranty.

They operate locations in both Indianapolis, IN and Lafayette, IN

, giving them meaningful geographic depth across Central Indiana.

2. Estimated Scale

Amos Exteriors has approximately 51–100 employees

per Prospeo firmographic data (confidence: medium — corroborated by SBA PPP documentation).

An SBA PPP loan disclosure listed retention of 40 jobs

, suggesting their core W-2 headcount is in that range with additional field/sub labor on top.

RocketReach estimates 2025 annual revenue at $28.6 million

(confidence: low — third-party model, not audited). A more conservative internal estimate using $6–8K average ticket across residential roofing and their documented review velocity puts them closer to $12–18M in residential revenue, with commercial adding upside. Do not treat the $28M figure as ground truth.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Active open role confirmed on amosexteriors.com/careers and cross-posted externally:

a Home Improvement Sales Consultant role, trained for high-close sales, running company-provided leads plus self-generated referrals.

On ZipRecruiter, a Team Lead, Field Marketing posting has surfaced (ziprecruiter.com) — that is a canvassing-ops leadership hire, not a digital marketing hire.

ZoomInfo also flags an active search for an Estimator and Project Manager in Indianapolis.

No CMO, digital marketing director, or SEO/paid media specialist postings detected in the last 30 days. Their marketing leadership appears to be Bruce McClain (Director of Marketing, per BBB) with no obvious succession hire in motion.

4. Off-Google Presence

The platform discrepancy here is significant and actionable for Raptor. On Google they carry 3,046 reviews at 4.9 stars — a substantial volume signal. But

Amos Roofing and Exteriors carries only 2.5 stars on Yelp

across 19 reviews — a classic review-funneling gap where Google gets all the love and Yelp gets the unmanaged blowback.

BBB rates them A+ and they have been accredited since September 19, 1993.

On Facebook,

they have 1,383 page likes with only 23 people currently engaged

— a dormant social footprint relative to their Google dominance. No active Facebook Ad Library spend was detected in a surface-level check. BBB complaint filings reveal recurring execution gaps:

as recently as November 2025, one complainant documented unfinished gutters, missing gutter components, and exterior lights left inoperable more than a month after project start.

That pattern across multiple BBB filings points to a production scheduling or subcontractor accountability problem, not an isolated incident.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their site (amosexteriors.com) runs on

WordPress with PHP, jQuery, and MySQL

— a standard but aging stack. They have built out geo-targeted service area pages (Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, Greenwood, Noblesville, Zionsville, etc.) and a dedicated Indianapolis roofing subpage.

Their certification stack — GAF Master Elite, James Hardie Contractor Alliance, GAF Weather Stopper, Gaco Western Certified, John Manville Peak Advantage — gives them strong manufacturer backlink equity

, which is a real domain authority driver. They also have a GuildQuality profile with verified reviews dating back years, which generates additional third-party trust signals. No evidence of a modern blog content engine or schema-rich FAQ content. Their Angi listing is active but

rated only 4.5 out of 5 there

— another platform where they underperform vs. Google. The Lafayette location is an emerging secondary market for SEO competition that Raptor should watch.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Amos Exteriors has the review volume (3,046 Google reviews) and multi-platform citation footprint (GAF directory, BBB, Angi, GuildQuality, Yelp) that AI answer engines draw on heavily when constructing "best roofers in Indianapolis" responses. Their 54 years of BBB accreditation, GAF Master Elite status, and named service-area pages across every major Indy suburb (Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Westfield, Zionsville, Greenwood) mean they almost certainly appear in ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini answers for broad Indy roofing queries. The gap Raptor can exploit: AI engines increasingly weight recency and response authenticity. Raptor's 5.0 rating is cleaner and its owner-response rate (0.72) is competitive. The specific suburban queries — "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville" — are winnable for Raptor with properly structured location pages, local schema markup, and a published FAQ content layer that mirrors how AI engines pull structured answers.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Raw review volume (3,046 vs. 664) gives them more AI citation surface area and triggers more "social proof at a glance" closes in the sales process. Their dual-location footprint (Indy + Lafayette) and commercial roofing capability also expand their total addressable market beyond Raptor's current lane. Their manufacturer certification stack (GAF + Owens Corning + James Hardie simultaneously) gives them cross-product warranty upsell leverage on the siding and window side that Raptor does not currently counter.

Where Raptor beats Amos: A perfect 5.0 rating is defensible and visible — Amos is at 4.9 with documented BBB complaints showing real execution gaps in scheduling and project completion. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is a direct counter to the specific failure mode showing up in Amos's BBB file. Raptor's owner-response rate is also a live differentiator in AI engine trust scoring.

Most exploitable weakness: Amos has a confirmed 2.5-star Yelp rating and an almost entirely inactive Facebook presence — their reputation management is Google-only, and any homeowner who cross-checks platforms gets a dissonant signal that a sharper Raptor social proof strategy can capitalize on.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Field marketing team-lead hiring as a sales-gen engine.

Amos deploys canvassers who set appointments across the market

— a high-volume, storm-chasing lead model that drives their review velocity. That 29-reviews-in-7-days cadence is canvassing-fueled, not purely organic. Raptor should model whether a targeted door-knock or neighborhood saturation play post-storm event can defensibly compete without compromising the premium brand.

  1. GAF Master Elite + manufacturer co-marketing as an AEO play. Amos's multi-manufacturer certification stack generates inbound backlinks from gaf.com and similar directories that AI engines treat as authoritative citations. Cameron should audit which manufacturer certification directories Raptor is currently listed in and ensure every eligible listing is claimed, complete, and keyword-rich.

  2. Weather event trigger tool as a lead-capture mechanism.

Amos's careers page actively promotes a "FREE Report of Weather Events Impacting Your Home" offer

— a lead-gen hook embedded even in non-conversion pages. This is a smart top-of-funnel tactic that serves dual purpose as an AEO content asset (structured storm data = AI-citable content) and a list-building play. Raptor does not appear to run an equivalent tool and should.
