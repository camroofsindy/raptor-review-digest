Competitive Intelligence Brief: Amos Exteriors

Updated: June 11, 2026 | Prepared for: Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1971, Amos Exteriors is a leading exterior remodeling company trusted by homeowners and businesses throughout Central Indiana.

Family-owned and operated, they are a GAF Master Elite Roofing Contractor serving both residential and commercial customers, and hold A+ standing with the BBB, the NRCA, and the Indianapolis Apartment Association.

They operate locations in Indianapolis, IN and Lafayette, IN

, and their service breadth spans roofing, siding, windows, doors, gutters, masonry, painting, and storm restoration — making them one of the widest-footprint exterior contractors in the metro.

2. Estimated Scale

RocketReach lists approximately 47 employees and annual revenue of $28.6 million (2025).

Confidence: medium — third-party revenue estimates for private contractors vary widely;

Prospeo pegs revenue at $12 million with an estimated valuation of $38.3 million

, and LeadIQ puts the range at $10M+. The spread is wide. The PPP data is the most reliable floor:

a PPP loan of $584,200 was taken to retain 40 jobs

, suggesting a true headcount in the 40–55 range. Best working estimate: $15–25M revenue, 45–55 W-2/1099 staff. Confidence: low-medium.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Their open posting is a Home Improvement Sales Consultant role for the Indianapolis market — candidates are trained to run company-provided leads while also creating and managing their own leads and referrals.

ZoomInfo also flags a concurrent Estimator & Project Manager search.

No marketing, ops leadership, or digital roles visible on LinkedIn (321 followers; sparse activity). The dual sales + estimator hiring pattern signals they are trying to push volume on both the front end (lead generation) and back end (project throughput) simultaneously — a classic post-storm-season surge play. No evidence of a dedicated marketing hire in the last 30 days.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform signal is notably weak relative to their Google review mass.

Yelp lists them as "Amos Roofing & Exteriors" at 7301 E 46th St with only 33 reviews

— a massive discrepancy against 3,139 Google reviews. This is a textbook review-funneling pattern: their post-job outreach is clearly routing customers to Google exclusively.

Facebook shows 1,407 page likes with only 6 people currently talking about them

— organically dormant.

BBB shows A+ accreditation since September 1993

, which is legitimately strong institutional credibility.

Nextdoor presence exists with a GAF Master Elite claim and BBB A+ noted

, but the profile copy is outdated (references "48 years" of service, not updated to reflect 55 years).

A second BBB location exists at 9160 Forum Corporate Pkwy, Suite 350, Fort Myers, FL

— worth watching as a potential geographic expansion signal. The Angi/HomeAdvisor profile shows a 4.5 rating — slightly below their Google average.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their tech stack runs on WordPress, PHP, jQuery 1.11.1, and MySQL

— legacy infrastructure that signals the site has not had a meaningful rebuild. jQuery 1.11.1 is over a decade old; Core Web Vitals scores are likely soft.

LeadIQ identifies their tech stack includes Yoast SEO, Nginx, WP Rocket, and Fusion Ads

— they are at least running a caching layer and have basic on-page SEO tooling installed. They maintain dedicated location pages (e.g., /locations/indianapolis-in, /service-areas/indianapolis-roofing) and service-specific subpages, which provides decent topical coverage for local queries. No visible blog content cadence was found — no date-stamped articles or resource hub. Backlink profile appears to rely on directory citations (BBB, GAF, NRCA, Angi) rather than earned editorial links. AEO schema markup is not verifiably deployed beyond what Yoast auto-generates. The absence of a content engine is a real gap at their revenue scale.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current training data patterns in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude, Amos Exteriors surfaces frequently in AI answers for Indianapolis-area roofing queries due to three factors: (1) their 1971 founding date provides strong authority signals AI models associate with longevity; (2) GAF Master Elite status is a named certification that AI models recognize as a quality differentiator; and (3) their review volume (3,139 Google reviews) creates broad citation density across third-party aggregators that LLMs index. They are almost certainly cited by name in responses to "best roofers Indianapolis" and "roof replacement Carmel / Fishers / Noblesville" queries. Their weak content footprint — no blog, no FAQ schema, no topical authority pages — means they win on brand recognition in AI results, not on structured information retrieval. That is an exploitable gap for Raptor.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Raw review volume (3,139 vs. 677) means they dominate frequency-based AI citation and map pack impression share. Their 55-year tenure, GAF Master Elite certification, multi-location footprint, and commercial roofing capability give them a broader addressable market. Their insurance claims support operation — with

experienced insurance claims experts who specialize in working directly with insurers to process claims quickly

— is a systematized upsell engine Raptor does not appear to match at scale.

Where Raptor beats Amos: A 5.0 rating at 677 reviews is a cleaner quality signal than 4.9 at 3,139. BBB complaint threads reveal recurring patterns around

unfinished work, missed deadlines, and incomplete gutter components

— execution gaps that Raptor can own as a differentiator. Raptor's single-trade focus sharpens the "specialist vs. generalist" contrast. Amos's Indeed employer rating sits at

2.8 out of 5

, with employee reviews flagging lead quality problems and favoritism — a cultural liability that eventually bleeds into customer experience.

Most exploitable weakness: Their canvasser-driven lead model produces low-intent appointments —

leads frequently involve long drives for minimal jobs, with homeowners who either don't know a rep is coming or don't understand why

— which is a chronic ops/morale drag that Raptor's accountable sales model can outperform on close rate and customer experience.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GAF Master Elite as a conversion asset.

Amos leans hard on GAF Master Elite status, noting it is held by only a small percentage of contractors nationwide and unlocks better warranties than competitors can offer.

If Raptor holds or can pursue this designation, it should be front-and-center in every paid ad headline, GBP description, and AI-optimized FAQ page — not buried.

  1. Dedicated location + service-area page architecture. Amos has built out granular location URLs for every major suburb (amosexteriors.com/locations/indianapolis-in, etc.) covering

Zionsville, Carmel, Noblesville, Westfield, Fishers, Plainfield, Avon, Greenwood, and all of Central Indiana.

Raptor should have parity on this — each page uniquely written with local schema, not templated — to compete for suburb-level organic and AI queries.

  1. Multi-platform review distribution. Amos's Yelp presence (33 reviews) vs. Google presence (3,139) is one of the widest funnel gaps in the Indianapolis market. Amos has essentially ceded Yelp and Angi entirely. Raptor should actively route a portion of post-job review requests to Yelp and the BBB to own the comparison stack when homeowners vet multiple platforms — and to create a genuine cross-platform rating story that AI citation engines will eventually reward over single-platform dominance.
