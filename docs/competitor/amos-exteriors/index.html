Competitive Intelligence Brief — Amos Exteriors

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Updated: August 13, 2025

1. Who They Are

Amos Exteriors is a family-owned and operated company founded in 1971, holding GAF Master Elite Contractor status and maintaining A+ standing with the BBB, NRCA, and the Indianapolis Apartment Association.

With locations in Indianapolis and Lafayette, Indiana, they have positioned themselves as Central Indiana's most-recognized exterior remodeling contractor.

Owner is Robert Amos; Bruce McClain serves as Director of Marketing and Jana Perrott holds a Marketing Manager title.

Their brand identity leans on longevity, insurance-claim facilitation, and multi-trade scope (roofing, siding, windows, gutters, painting) — making them a volume player rather than a premium-only shop.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on the data gathered: Amos Exteriors has the raw ingredients for AI citation (3,243 reviews, 55-year history, GAF Master Elite status, multiple location pages, BBB A+) but their Yelp thinness, near-zero owner response rate (1.0% per Cameron's data), and dated site tech work against them in LLM training signal quality. AI engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity tend to cite businesses with broad multi-platform review footprints and structured data. With 33 Yelp reviews and minimal Facebook engagement, their off-Google presence is weak enough that Raptor — with aggressive AEO content (FAQ schema, "best roofer Carmel Indiana" answer pages, GBP Q&A stacking) — can outrank them in generative results even while trailing on raw Google review count. Their 1.0% owner response rate is a direct AEO liability; Google's Gemini and AI Overviews weight recency and engagement signals in local pack results.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Raw review volume is overwhelming — 3,243 vs. 699. That volume provides GBP ranking inertia, brand familiarity in search, and social proof at scale for price-sensitive buyers. Their multi-trade scope (siding, windows, painting) also means more entry points for a homeowner relationship.

Where Raptor beats Amos: Perfect 5.0 rating vs. Amos's 4.9 is a visible edge at the top of the market. More critically, Raptor's 1.0% owner response rate is actually Amos's rate (per the data provided) — that is a disqualifying signal for premium buyers who research before calling. Raptor's premium positioning, no-outsourcing narrative, and engaged review profile speak to a buyer segment that Amos's volume/insurance model does not serve well.

Most exploitable weakness: Amos's review engine has nearly stopped — 2 reviews in 90 days on a 3,243-review profile signals either a broken solicitation system, a post-storm work slowdown, or internal operational churn; any one of these is a story Raptor can tell in local media and paid targeting.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GuildQuality as a third-party review layer. Amos uses guildquality.com for verified post-project surveys. GuildQuality reviews surface in AI results and contractor vetting searches independent of Google. Cameron should evaluate adding GuildQuality or a comparable platform (Houzz Pro, Guild) to Raptor's review stack to build AI-citation surface area that Amos has already seeded but is not actively feeding.

  2. Location page infrastructure. Amos has dedicated city-specific landing pages for every major Indy suburb. Raptor should audit whether its own location pages are running schema markup, city-specific testimonials, and localized FAQs — the technical elements that push pages into AI Overviews for queries like "roof replacement Fishers Indiana."

  3. Monitor the field marketing hire closely.

Amos is actively recruiting a Team Lead for Field Marketing in Indianapolis.

If filled, expect an uptick in door-knocking and neighborhood canvassing in storm-affected ZIP codes — the same ZIP codes Raptor targets post-event. Pre-positioning Raptor's brand in those ZIPs via NextDoor sponsored posts and hyper-local Facebook radius ads before Amos's canvassers arrive is a direct counter-play.
