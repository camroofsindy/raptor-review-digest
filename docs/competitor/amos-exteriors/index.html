Competitive Intelligence Brief: Amos Exteriors

Refreshed: July 14, 2025 | amosexteriors.com

1. Who They Are

Founded in 1971, Amos Exteriors is a leading exterior remodeling company trusted by homeowners and businesses throughout Central Indiana.

As a family-owned company with more than 50 years of experience, they provide exterior home remodeling solutions for both residential and commercial clients.

They operate locations in Indianapolis and Lafayette

, and their primary differentiator is institutional longevity paired with

GAF Master Elite Contractor status, which enables them to offer the GAF System Plus Warranty

— a credential held by fewer than 2% of roofers nationally. Their pitch is breadth: roofing, siding, windows, doors, gutters, masonry, and insurance claims navigation all under one roof.

2. Estimated Scale

RocketReach estimates annual revenue at $28.6 million (2025 figure) and 57 employees.

Confidence: medium.

ZoomInfo independently lists 40 employees at the Indianapolis headquarters.

The spread between data sources ($12M per Prospeo to $28.6M per RocketReach) likely reflects a multi-trade revenue model vs. roofing-only estimates. A realistic range is $12M–$20M given their headcount. Headcount confidence: medium — consistent across ZoomInfo and PPP data.

SBA PPP records confirm a $584,200 loan retaining 40 jobs

, providing a credible floor.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

The most notable active posting is a Team Lead, Field Marketing role on ZipRecruiter — this is a canvassing/door-knock management role, not a digital marketing hire.

ZoomInfo also flags an open Estimator and Project Manager posting in Indianapolis.

No digital marketing, SEO, paid media, or CRM-level hires are visible in the last 30 days. The field marketing lead hire signals they are leaning harder into canvassing as a top-of-funnel tactic rather than inbound digital — a meaningful strategic tell.

4. Off-Google Presence

The platform divergence here is significant. On Google they carry 3,198 reviews at 4.9 stars — a massive volume signal. But off-Google, the story thins fast.

On Yelp, they show only 33 reviews

under the name "Amos Roofing & Exteriors" — a near-invisible presence for a company their size.

Facebook shows 1,407 page likes with only 6 people actively talking about the page

, indicating a dormant social strategy.

BBB shows A+ accreditation, active since September 1993

, but

BBB lists both a Marketing Manager (Jana Perrott) and a Director of Marketing (Bruce McClain)

— two marketing-titled roles that do not appear to be producing off-platform review growth. BBB complaints (bbb.org) include documented scheduling failures and post-installation defects, with the most recent pattern involving

weeks of scheduling delays after deposit collection.

The 3,198 Google reviews vs. 33 Yelp reviews is a classic funnel-gating pattern — they redirect heavily to Google and nowhere else.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their tech stack includes WordPress, PHP, jQuery 1.11.1, and MySQL

— a dated build. jQuery 1.11.1 specifically is a 2014-era library, suggesting the site has not undergone a meaningful technical refresh in years.

Their stack also includes Yoast SEO, WP Rocket, and Open Graph tags

, so basic on-page SEO hygiene is present. They have built out location-specific service area pages (e.g., amosexteriors.com/locations/indianapolis-in, /service-areas/indianapolis), a legitimate local SEO play. However, there is no visible blog content strategy — the site is almost entirely static service pages.

Their geographic targeting spans Zionsville, Carmel, Noblesville, Fishers, Geist, Plainfield, Avon, Speedway, Greenwood, Franklin, Anderson, and Greenfield

, with dedicated pages for each. Domain age (53 years of brand history) likely provides passive backlink authority, but the absence of fresh editorial content is a structural weakness for AI answer engine citation.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on the data gathered: Amos Exteriors has the brand age and review volume to appear in AI-generated answers for broad queries like "best roofing company Indianapolis" — their 3,198 Google reviews are a citation signal that LLMs use as a proxy for trust. However, the lack of structured blog content, FAQ schema, or recent press coverage means they are unlikely to be cited in long-tail AI queries like "best roofer for storm damage in Carmel Indiana" or "who to call for roof replacement in Fishers." Their WordPress/Yoast setup does not appear to include FAQ or HowTo schema markup, which are among the strongest AEO signals for home services. This is an exploitable gap for Raptor: structured FAQ content targeting the exact suburb-level queries Amos has ignored will pull AI citations away from them.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where Amos beats Raptor: Raw review volume (3,198 vs. 688) gives them more surface area in map pack rankings and raw AI citation probability. Their multi-trade model (roofing + siding + windows + gutters + masonry) makes them a one-call-closes-everything option for insurance claims, which is a powerful lead conversion lever.

The GAF Master Elite designation

and 55-year brand history provide institutional credibility that takes decades to replicate.

Where Raptor beats Amos: 5.0 star rating vs. 4.9 is a real signal at the margin — and Raptor's 0-days-without-a-review velocity is night-and-day vs. Amos's 6 reviews in 90 days, which is a flat-lined review engine. Owner response rate at Amos is 0.0%; Raptor's engagement posture alone is a visible trust differentiator in GBP. BBB complaints show documented execution problems: scheduling gaps, post-install defects, and deposit collection followed by silence.

Most exploitable weakness: Amos's canvass-heavy, commission-only sales model produces appointment quality problems —

canvassers set appointments where homeowners don't know the rep is coming or don't understand why

— creating a poor first impression Raptor can directly contrast with a booked, confirmed, premium sales experience.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Insurance claims navigation as a front-end value prop.

Amos leads with experienced insurance claims specialists who work directly with insurers

at the sales stage. Raptor should consider whether to formally name this capability in ads and GBP posts rather than leaving it as an implied benefit. Storm season is when this framing wins jobs.

  1. Location page architecture at the suburb level. Amos has built individual service area landing pages for every major suburb. Cameron should audit whether Raptor's site has equivalent depth for Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, and Zionsville — each page is an independent local SEO and AEO asset.

  2. Monitor their Field Marketing Team Lead hire. Amos is clearly scaling canvassing. If they're about to put more doors-knocked per day into Raptor's core territory, a counter-strategy of aggressive retargeting (Meta custom audiences, geofenced display) in those same ZIP codes will intercept homeowners who just got a knock and go online to validate — which is exactly where Raptor's 5.0 / 688-review profile closes the deal.
