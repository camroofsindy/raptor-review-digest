Taylor Home Improvement — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Refreshed: May 21, 2025 | Noblesville, IN

1. Who They Are

Taylor Home Improvement is a family-owned company based in Noblesville, Indiana, owned by father-and-son team Steve and Adam Taylor, serving all of central Indiana with a full range of home improvement services including roofing, siding, gutters, interior and exterior painting, remodeling, and windows and doors.

BBB records show a file opened in March 2008, putting the business at approximately 17 years of operation.

Their primary differentiator is credential stacking:

they claim to be among only 13 roofing contractors in the state of Indiana awarded GAF Master Elite status, placing them in the top 2% nationwide.

2. Estimated Scale

ZoomInfo lists Taylor Home Improvement at approximately 20 employees at their Noblesville headquarters.

Confidence: medium — ZoomInfo notoriously lags on headcount and the website implies a subcontractor crew model rather than a fully W-2 workforce. No revenue figure is publicly disclosed. Based on 20 employees, a multi-trade residential model, and central Indiana market density, a rough estimate of $3M–$6M annual revenue is reasonable, though unverifiable.

BuildZoom confirms the business is owned by Adam Taylor and holds a general contractor license plus 13 additional licenses according to the Indianapolis license board

— a permit footprint consistent with a mid-tier regional operator, not a volume shop.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Active recruiting for sales.

ZipRecruiter shows an Outside Sales Representative opening in Noblesville paying $50,000–$75,000.

Indeed confirms the same role, describing responsibilities around generating leads, building rapport with homeowners, and closing sales.

No marketing, ops leadership, or production management postings were found. The sole open role being outside sales suggests they are trying to grow top-of-funnel revenue, not back-office capacity — a signal that their current sales pipeline may be thinning.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp shows 18 reviews at their Noblesville listing

, with no star rating surfaced in search results — a near-invisible presence on that platform. Contrast that with 274 Google reviews: the gap is stark and classic review-funneling behavior.

Facebook shows 31 reviews at 88% recommendation rate

, with recent activity including before-and-after project posts as recently as two days ago — their most active non-Google channel.

BBB shows accreditation since March 2017 with an A+ rating.

No Facebook Ad Library spend was surfaced in search results, suggesting minimal or no paid social. GuildQuality profile exists but the most recent verified reviews date to 2019, effectively dormant.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

The website (taylorhomeimprovement.net) runs on

WordPress with Cloudflare CDN and reCAPTCHA

— a standard stack with no technical edge. They have geo-targeted service pages (a /locations/noblesville/ page confirmed), which is a sound local SEO practice.

On-page content references a licensed HAAG Certified roofing inspector/adjuster

, which is a credible E-E-A-T signal if properly marked up with schema. However, the site's last meaningful content update referenced 2020 in on-page copy, which is a freshness liability for Google's helpful content signals. No evidence of a functioning blog, link-building program, or local press backlinks was found. Domain authority is likely modest (estimated DA 20–30 range) given the absence of any citation in regional media. Their strongest SEO asset is the GAF contractor directory listing and the BuildZoom profile, not their own domain.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No search results confirmed Taylor Home Improvement being cited by name in ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini responses for queries like "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville," or "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana." Their low domain authority, absence of press mentions, and thin blog content make AI citation unlikely. The GAF Master Elite credential is the one asset that could surface in generative results if a model is pulling from GAF's directory — but that attribution would go to GAF, not directly to Taylor's brand. This is a gap Raptor can exploit aggressively with structured AEO content.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where Taylor beats Raptor: GAF Master Elite status is a tangible warranty differentiator —

GAF entrusts Master Elite contractors to offer their strongest warranties including the Golden Pledge Limited Warranty on qualifying roof systems

— and they lean on it in sales conversations. Their multi-trade model (roofing + siding + remodeling + painting) also creates higher average ticket and stickier customer relationships.

Where Raptor beats Taylor: 675 reviews vs. 274, a full star-rating gap (5.0 vs. 4.6), zero owner response rate on Taylor's Google profile (vs. Raptor's active engagement), and zero reviews in the last 90 days — Taylor's review engine has effectively stalled. That cadence collapse is the most exploitable weakness: a competitor with a flat review profile in a high-trust category is invisible to both Google's local algorithm and AI citation engines.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GAF Golden Pledge as a closing weapon. Taylor actively positions Master Elite status as a warranty-tier differentiator in sales conversations. Cameron should audit whether Raptor's current sales scripts and landing pages are extracting full value from Raptor's own manufacturer certifications in the same way — or if that credential is buried.

  2. FC Tucker Home Services partnership.

Taylor Home Improvement is a listed member of FC Tucker's Home Services vendor network

, which puts them in front of home buyers and sellers at the moment of highest roofing intent. Raptor should pursue a parallel referral relationship with FC Tucker or a competing brokerage (Carpenter, RE/MAX Centerstone) to capture the same pre-move and post-close inspection pipeline.

  1. Before-and-after Facebook content cadence. Taylor is posting project transformation content actively on Facebook while their review engine is dormant — a disconnect, but the content format itself works. Raptor should systematize this as a review-request trigger: post the job completion photo publicly, tag the homeowner (with permission), and use that moment as the review ask. It converts social proof into review velocity rather than treating them as separate workflows.
