Competitive Intelligence Brief — Big Boy's Construction (BBC)

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: June 9, 2026

1. Who They Are

Big Boy's Construction Inc. is a privately held Indiana corporation founded in 2011, headquartered in Indianapolis, specializing in roofing, gutters, siding, interior and exterior remodel, mold removal, and storm damage cleanup.

President Mark Andrew leads the company,

and

their stated service area covers a 75-mile radius around Indianapolis, with extensions into storm-affected areas.

Their primary differentiation hook is GAF Master Elite status and a heavy insurance claim assistance model targeting storm-damage-driven jobs.

2. Estimated Scale

LinkedIn lists company size at 11–50 employees.

Confidence: medium.

Prospeo data pegs it more specifically at 17 employees across 5 departments,

though that likely excludes subcontract crews.

SignalHire estimates annual revenue between $10M and $50M.

Confidence: low — the range is too wide to be actionable, but the volume of 862 Google reviews and a multi-county footprint suggest they're operating closer to the $10–15M range for a shop this size.

Tenure data shows 43% of staff have been with the company 1–2 years,

which signals a salesforce with meaningful churn — a recurring pattern in insurance-claim-heavy roofing shops.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No confirmed active job postings within the last 30 days were surfaced across Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter. The most recent hiring signal found was

a Glassdoor listing from July 21, 2025 — now expired — recruiting for a sales role, framed around joining "the Fastest-Growing Sales Team at Big Boy's Construction."

Their careers page remains live, positioning BBC as investing in employees with training and development above industry standards,

but no specific roles are currently confirmed open. The expired July 2025 sales listing suggests a seasonal push pattern — watch for renewed sales hiring in Q3 2026 as storm season accelerates.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

BBC's site runs on WordPress with reCAPTCHA and PHP infrastructure, hosted on Nexcess.

That stack is serviceable but not cutting-edge.

Their areas-we-serve page explicitly targets 20+ Indianapolis-area municipalities

— Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, Greenwood, and more — which is a well-executed local SEO geo-page strategy.

They also promote drone technology for roof assessments,

a differentiated content angle that likely earns featured snippet opportunities on inspection-related queries. Their blog exists but content appears thin and infrequent based on search result metadata. Domain authority signal was not determinable without Ahrefs/Moz access, but their GAF contractor directory listing (gaf.com) provides a high-authority backlink most competitors don't have. AEO schema markup status is unconfirmed — no structured FAQ or HowTo schema was evident in surfaced content.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

AI citation data for the specific query set (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini for "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," etc.) was not directly observable through standard web search. Based on their SEO posture — strong geo-targeting, a GAF directory presence, GuildQuality verified reviews, and a high Google review volume — BBC likely appears in at least some AI-generated local recommendations, particularly for storm-damage and insurance claim query variants where their content is most focused. However, their thin blog output and lack of confirmed schema markup are structural weaknesses that reduce their LLM citation probability. This is an area where Raptor's 5.0 rating, higher review quality signals, and more authoritative brand positioning could pull ahead with targeted AEO content investment.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where BBC beats Raptor: Raw review volume (862 vs. 677) gives them a quantity-first perception advantage with less-discerning shoppers; their geo-page SEO covering 20+ suburbs is more systematically built out than what's visible from Raptor's current footprint; GuildQuality third-party verification adds a legitimacy layer in the insurance-adjuster channel.

Where Raptor beats BBC: 5.0 rating is a clean, defensible edge — BBC's 4.9 with nearly 200 more reviews signals more variance and likely buried negative experiences; Raptor's premium positioning and "we don't outsource accountability" message targets the higher-value homeowner who self-selects away from insurance-claim volume shops; BBC's 43% staff tenure under 2 years creates a consistency problem Raptor can exploit in messaging around crew continuity.

Most exploitable weakness: BBC's entire brand architecture is built on insurance claim volume — Raptor should explicitly own the "we work for you, not your insurance company" angle to peel away premium cash-pay customers who distrust the claim-chasing model.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GuildQuality enrollment. BBC uses third-party verified surveys via GuildQuality to generate a separate review stream that carries adjuster and commercial credibility. This is a low-cost trust signal Raptor doesn't appear to be using and should evaluate immediately.

  2. Systematic geo-page buildout. BBC's areas-we-serve architecture covering every Hamilton, Hendricks, and Johnson County suburb is a proven local SEO moat. Raptor should audit whether its own suburb pages match or exceed that coverage depth — thin pages on Zionsville, Westfield, and McCordsville are likely leaving organic traffic on the table.

  3. Drone inspection as a content and sales tool.

BBC explicitly promotes drone technology for exterior inspections, keeping crews on the ground while generating detailed assessments faster.

If Raptor isn't already using and marketing drone inspections as a differentiator, BBC is setting the expectation for that feature in the market and Raptor is ceding the narrative.
