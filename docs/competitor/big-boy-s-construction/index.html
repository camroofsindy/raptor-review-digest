Big Boy's Construction — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Updated: August 13, 2025 | Next refresh: August 15

1. Who They Are

Big Boy's Construction was established in 2011.

The company is led by Mark Andrew, President, and is incorporated as an Indiana corporation.

Their stated service area is expansive, covering Indianapolis and surrounding communities including Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, Zionsville, and Brownsburg, spanning Boone, Hamilton, Hancock, Hendricks, Johnson, Marion, Morgan, Putnam, and Shelby counties.

Their market hook is storm-damage insurance claim navigation layered on top of a GAF Master Elite certification — a volume-first play aimed at mid-market homeowners who need hand-holding through the claims process.

2. Estimated Scale

ZoomInfo lists Big Boy's Construction at 11–50 employees.

Confidence: medium.

Confidence: low — that is a wide band and likely reflects a data aggregator's best guess on a private company.

Their LinkedIn company page has only 149 followers

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Big Boy's Construction is actively pushing job applicants through their Facebook page, directing candidates to their Indeed company profile.

Indeed salary data updated July 21, 2025 shows active Sales Representative and Sales Manager roles, with compensation ranging from approximately $57K for a Sales Coordinator to $110K for a Sales Manager.

This is a pure sales-rep hiring loop — commission-based storm chasers are the growth engine here. No evidence of marketing, ops leadership, or digital roles being posted in the last 30 days. That absence is telling: they are scaling headcount through the field, not through marketing infrastructure.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

The company claims more than 30 years of experience

on their website, which conflicts with the 2011 Yelp establishment date — a credibility inconsistency Google's E-E-A-T signals could penalize.

They are leaning into drone technology as a differentiator, using it for roof inspections and assessments

, and they have dedicated service pages for residential, commercial, repair, and specific materials (TPO, EPDM, PVC, metal).

Their stated service radius is 75 miles around Indianapolis, with storm-area exceptions.

The site (bigboysconstructioninc.com) has a workable location/service page structure but no publicly visible blog content cadence — no recent posts found. Schema markup and backlink profile are not confirmed from available data, but the lack of local press citations in search results suggests thin off-page authority. They rank on brand name but are unlikely to dominate non-branded head terms like "roof replacement Indianapolis" against better-optimized competitors.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

No AI-generated results (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) for queries including "best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," or "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville" returned Big Boy's Construction as a cited source in this brief cycle. Their thin backlink profile, inconsistent founding-year claims across web properties, and lack of active editorial content (blog, press coverage, industry citations) are the likely culprits. This is an AEO gap that cuts both ways: they are not winning AI citations, and the space is open for Raptor to claim by producing authoritative, structured, locally-cited content.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume — 878 vs. 699 — gives them a numerical authority signal in Google's local pack algorithm, and their GAF Master Elite certification is a credible trust badge they promote prominently. Their insurance claim assistance positioning is well-developed and serves a broad homeowner segment.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor holds a 5.0 star rating vs. BBC's 4.9 with nearly 200 fewer reviews — meaning Raptor's satisfaction rate on a per-review basis is demonstrably superior. BBC's review velocity has essentially flatlined (1 review in 30 days, 3 in 90 days), which signals a stalled or broken review-generation system. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" premium positioning is a sharper, more defensible brand story than BBC's volume-contractor insurance-claim angle.

Most exploitable weakness: BBC's review engine is clinically dead — 1 review in 30 days for a company with 878 total is a system failure, and any sustained review-generation push by Raptor in the next 60 days can close the volume gap meaningfully while the 5.0 / 4.9 rating differential compounds in Raptor's favor.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GAF Master Elite as a conversion tool.

BBC leans hard on the GAF Master Elite designation in their sales copy, specifically the lifetime limited warranty and 10-year 100% labor-and-materials coverage period.

If Raptor holds the same or equivalent certification, this warranty story needs to be front-and-center in every estimate, on the website, and in paid creative — BBC is winning the warrant conversation and Raptor should not cede it.

  1. Drone inspection as a top-of-funnel hook.

BBC uses drone technology for exterior inspections, keeping crew on the ground while delivering detailed imagery of damage areas.

This is a tangible, visual differentiator that plays well in video ads and estimate presentations. Cameron should evaluate whether Raptor is using drone footage in social content and lead nurture sequences — if not, BBC has a production-value edge in the field.

  1. Multi-county location page architecture.

BBC has built out individual location targeting across more than 20 Indianapolis-area cities and nine counties.

Audit Raptor's location page depth against this structure. If Raptor is thin on suburb-specific pages for Zionsville, McCordsville, or Whitestown, BBC is capturing that long-tail search demand by default.
