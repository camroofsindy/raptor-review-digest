Big Boy's Construction — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Updated: April 26, 2026 | Cameron Blakely / Raptor Roofing

1. Who They Are

Big Boy's Construction was established in 2011

, making them a 14-year-old Indianapolis operation.

The CEO is Mark Andrew

,

who serves as President and is publicly active as the face of the brand.

They are an Indiana-based general contractor specializing in roofing, siding, and gutters,

with a targeted service area covering a 75-mile radius around Indianapolis, with storm-chasing extensions beyond that.

Their primary differentiator is their

GAF Master Elite certification, held by only the top 2% of roofing companies in the U.S.,

which they leverage heavily across all marketing channels.

2. Estimated Scale

Big Boy's Construction has approximately 21–50 employees

per Prospeo, while

Adapt.io estimates a broader range of 25–100 employees.

ZoomInfo lists them at

11–50 employees at their Indianapolis headquarters.

The spread across sources suggests a mid-size crew with meaningful sales rep headcount relative to field staff — typical of insurance-restoration-first shops. Confidence: medium. Revenue estimate: at 844 Google reviews and an insurance-claim-heavy model across a 75-mile radius, a rough estimate of $8M–$15M annual revenue is reasonable, though no public financials exist. Confidence: low.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

ZoomInfo flags that Big Boy's Construction is expanding and recently hired new employees,

but no specific role postings surfaced in the last 30 days on LinkedIn, Indeed, or ZipRecruiter through available search data.

Their careers page actively recruits with messaging around investing in employees and training and development opportunities that go above industry standards,

signaling an always-open sales rep funnel rather than targeted ops or marketing leadership hiring. No marketing, CMO, or digital director postings detected — this is a tell that their marketing is likely owner-directed or outsourced to an agency with minimal strategic horsepower. Watch LinkedIn for field supervisor or project manager posts as a volume indicator.

4. Off-Google Presence

The cross-platform picture here is the most tactically useful data point in this brief. On Google they carry 844 reviews at 4.9 stars — a strong signal of organized review solicitation.

On Facebook they show 2,241 page likes with a 100% recommendation rate across only 52 reviews.

That's a massive gap: 844 Google reviews versus 52 Facebook reviews. Classic review-funneling — they send customers to Google and barely touch Facebook or other platforms.

On Yelp, they have zero reviews — the page notes a user could be the first to review.

Zero Yelp presence despite 14 years in business is not an oversight; it's a deliberate funnel decision.

On BBB they hold an A+ accredited rating.

Nextdoor shows minimal traction —

only 3 recommendations registered on that platform.

No Facebook Ad Library activity surfaced, suggesting paid social is not currently a meaningful acquisition channel for them.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their service area pages cover a comprehensive list of Indy suburbs: Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, Zionsville, Avon, Brownsburg, and a dozen others,

indicating they've built out location-specific landing pages — a solid but now-table-stakes SEO play.

They highlight drone technology for inspections

as a content differentiator across service pages. The site's content is repetitive and templated across pages (same GAF Master Elite block appears nearly verbatim on every service and location page), which is a thin-content flag that a technically-minded SEO audit would flag as cannibalization risk. No evidence of a maintained blog, local press backlinks, or schema markup beyond basic business listings. Their GAF.com profile gives them a meaningful authority backlink, which is an AEO consideration. Domain age and review velocity give them solid local pack presence, but their content depth is exploitable.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI engine behavior for Indianapolis roofing queries, Big Boy's Construction has the inputs that feed AI citations: high Google review volume (844), GAF Master Elite status, long domain age, and location pages covering the key suburb queries (Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, Greenwood). They are likely surfacing in generative results for broad queries like "best roofers Indianapolis" due to review volume and their GAF third-party profile. However, their thin blog content, lack of structured FAQ schema, and no visible AEO-specific strategy (no "People Also Ask" targeting, no authority editorial backlinks) means they are riding passive authority rather than engineering AI visibility. Raptor's 5.0 star rating with 664 reviews and a tighter premium narrative is a genuine counter-signal — the gap is content depth and citation-worthy assets (case studies, local press mentions, authoritative how-to content).

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw Google review volume (844 vs. 664) gives them more surface area in AI training data and local pack weight. The GAF Master Elite badge is a third-party credibility signal that is broadly recognized and prominently deployed at every conversion point on their site. Their 30-reviews-in-30-days pace means their review engine is still firing — Raptor's 0 reviews in the last 7 days vs. their 24 in the last 30 warrants a close look at whether Raptor's post-job review solicitation cadence has slowed.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor's 5.0 rating is a clean, unassailable number — 4.9 is not. In a world where AI engines and zero-click searches flatten information, that decimal matters in summary comparisons. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is a direct shot at the insurance-restoration-model operational reality that companies like Big Boy's run — high sales rep volume, subcontracted crews, storm-chase orientation. Raptor also has a tighter premium brand; Big Boy's messaging leans on affordability and volume, which is a different buyer entirely.

Most exploitable weakness: Their 1-in-844 owner response rate on Google reviews is effectively zero — a sharp Cameron response program on Raptor's profile becomes a visible brand differentiator the moment a prospective customer compares the two listings side by side.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. GAF Master Elite as a conversion mechanism. Big Boy's puts the GAF badge and warranty language —

including the 10-year 100% coverage period and 50-year System Plus Limited Warranty

— at the top of every service page. If Raptor holds or can attain this designation, mirror this structure: put the warranty specifics in dollar and year terms on the estimate page, not buried in an about section.

  1. Suburb-level landing page depth.

Big Boy's has built out individual pages for every major suburb and county in the metro,

from Noblesville to Zionsville to Putnam County. Raptor should audit whether its own location pages match that footprint and, critically, whether those pages have unique content and schema rather than boilerplate copy-paste — which is exactly the opening Big Boy's has left.

  1. Monitor their review velocity as a market indicator. At 24 reviews in the last 30 days, they are actively closing jobs and running a post-install review ask. If that number spikes above 40 in any 30-day window, it likely signals a hail event they caught that Raptor should be chasing simultaneously. Use their review velocity as a real-time storm-response proxy.
