Big Boy's Construction — Competitive Intelligence Brief

Refreshed: May 19, 2026 | Analyst: Raptor Roofing CMO

1. Who They Are

Big Boy's Construction Inc. is a privately held Indianapolis-based contractor founded in 2011, specializing in roofing, gutters, siding, interior and exterior remodeling, mold removal, and storm damage cleanup.

President Mark Andrew leads the company

, and their primary differentiator is a dual-threat positioning as both an insurance claim specialist and a

GAF Master Elite Contractor — a certification held by only the top 2% of roofing companies in the U.S.

Their stated service footprint covers Indianapolis and suburbs including Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, Zionsville, and surrounding counties across central Indiana.

2. Estimated Scale

LinkedIn self-reports a company size of 11–50 employees.

Confidence: medium.

Prospeo.io brackets them at 21–50 employees with an internal count of approximately 17 staff across five departments.

Cross-referencing those data points, a reasonable working estimate is 20–30 W-2/1099 staff plus field crews. Annual revenue is not publicly disclosed; at 854 Google reviews and storm-heavy insurance claim volume across a multi-county footprint, a rough estimate of $5M–$10M ARR is plausible. Confidence: low — no BuildZoom permit data or ZoomInfo revenue figure was accessible without a paid wall.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

The most visible active recruiting posture is in sales.

A Glassdoor posting from mid-2025 was headlined "Join the Fastest-Growing Sales Team" and targeted ambitious, driven candidates, describing BBC as the "#1 highest-rated exterior remodeling company in the Greater Indianapolis area."

The posting specifically leaned on the top-2% GAF credential and emphasized internal training and promotion.

No current open roles in marketing, operations leadership, or project management were surfaced on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days. The sales-heavy recruiting pattern suggests they are prioritizing top-of-funnel lead generation over back-office or ops scaling — typical of a storm-chasing revenue model trying to extend its sales bench heading into hail season.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp shows zero reviews — users haven't posted a single review for Big Boy's Construction there.

That is a textbook review-funneling signal: 854 Google reviews and a zero on Yelp is not organic, it is the result of a deliberate review-request flow that routes exclusively to Google.

Facebook shows 54 reviews at 100% recommendation and 2,245 page likes.

Their Facebook page is active —

they recently posted about participating in a Toyota/Make-A-Wish charity golf outing in 2026 where the team placed first

, which is solid community-engagement content.

BBB lists them as A+ accredited

with

accreditation dating to July 6, 2012.

No Facebook Ad Library activity was surfaced in search results, suggesting limited or no current paid social spend — but that absence should be verified directly in Meta Ad Library at facebook.com/ads/library.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their site runs on WordPress with reCAPTCHA and Nexcess hosting

— a standard mid-market stack with no obvious technical edge.

They do call out drone technology for inspections

and have dedicated service pages per category and an areas-we-serve page with individual suburb targeting — that is competent local SEO structure.

A 2026 "Top 10 Indianapolis Roofers" article from indyroofandrestoration.com named them, crediting their rapid market share gains, GAF Master Elite status, and focus on insurance claims

— that kind of third-party list placement is exactly the backlink currency that feeds both traditional SEO rankings and AI citation engines. Their blog content depth is unclear, but the site's domain age (registered 2018 per site metadata) and review velocity give them domain authority momentum. No evidence of FAQPage or HowTo schema markup was found, which is a gap.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Big Boy's Construction has meaningful AI exposure by proxy.

TrustAnalytica's "Best Roofing Contractors in Indianapolis" list — a type of third-party directory AI engines actively pull from — includes them by name, citing honest assessments, prompt service, and quality craftsmanship.

Popular AI platforms including Google AI Mode and ChatGPT are directly citing "best roofers" list articles when generating answers

, meaning BBC's inclusion on indyroofandrestoration.com's 2026 top-10 list and TrustAnalytica gives them passive AI citation exposure without any deliberate AEO strategy. They do not appear to have structured FAQ or HowTo schema, no dedicated AEO content targeting queries like "best roofer for insurance claims in Indianapolis," and their Yelp absence actually hurts them here —

companies with reviews on Google, Yelp, and Facebook are 2.8x more likely to appear in AI search results.

Their Yelp zero is a concrete AI visibility liability.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume — 854 vs. 673 — and the GAF Master Elite credential give them credibility with insurance adjusters and price-sensitive homeowners. Their insurance claim advocacy positioning ("we find damage others miss") is a specific, conversion-optimized message that resonates post-storm.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor holds a 5.0 rating vs. BBC's 4.9 — at high volume, that gap is statistically significant and defensible in premium positioning conversations. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" narrative is a cleaner brand story than BBC's generalist general-contractor identity, which spans gutters, windows, siding, and mold remediation.

Most exploitable weakness: Their Yelp ghost-town and Facebook review moat of only 54 reviews is the single most exposed flank — a coordinated Raptor push to dominate Yelp, Nextdoor, and AI-cited directories would leapfrog them in every channel where they have chosen not to compete.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

1. Insurance claim advocacy as a sales motion.

BBC is specifically effective for homeowners overwhelmed by insurance processes, and their team focuses on identifying collateral damage adjusters miss to maximize the restoration scope.

Cameron should consider whether Raptor's sales team is trained to the same depth on scope expansion during adjuster meetings — this is a job-size multiplier, not just a closing tactic.

2. Charity/community content for social engagement. Their Make-A-Wish golf outing post generated real organic engagement. Raptor should identify 1–2 cause-marketing hooks per quarter that produce shareable photo content — this is low-cost brand authority that third-party list writers and AI engines both register as trust signals.

3. Exploit the Yelp and AEO vacuum immediately. BBC has zero Yelp reviews and no evident schema markup strategy. Raptor should (a) activate a Yelp review funnel now, (b) add FAQPage and LocalBusiness schema to key service pages, and (c) pitch Patrick on producing 4–6 structured "question-format" content pages targeting suburb-level queries like "roof replacement cost Carmel Indiana" — the exact query type AI engines are currently citing from third-party lists rather than contractor websites.
