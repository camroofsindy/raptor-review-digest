Competitive Intelligence Brief: Big Boy's Construction

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely Dashboard | Refreshed: May 19, 2025

1. Who They Are

Big Boy's Construction Inc. was founded in 2011 and is headquartered in Indianapolis, Indiana, operating as a privately held general contractor.

President Mark Andrew leads the company, which positions itself around delivering roofing solutions with the "highest level of integrity."

The company specializes in insurance-claim support, storm damage repairs, residential and commercial roofing, siding, gutters, windows, and comprehensive home renovation across greater Indianapolis and surrounding counties.

Their primary differentiator is their

GAF Master Elite Contractor status, a certification held by only the top 2% of roofing companies in the U.S.

2. Estimated Scale

LinkedIn lists their company size at 11–50 employees.

Prospeo data puts active headcount at approximately 17 employees across 5 departments,

while

broader estimates from the same source range 21–50.

Confidence: medium — LinkedIn and Prospeo are consistent but don't capture field subcontractors. Based on their review volume (854 Google reviews), sales rep headcount implied in customer feedback (multiple named reps across markets), and GAF Master Elite status, rough annual revenue is estimated at $5M–$9M. Confidence: low — no public financials available;

the company has never raised outside funding.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

Big Boy's Construction has been actively recruiting for sales positions, branding itself as "the #1 highest-rated exterior remodeling company in the Greater Indianapolis area" and advertising that it is "expanding FAST."

The role posted as a hybrid position in Indianapolis with a pay range of $60,000 to $150,000+/year.

That Glassdoor listing expired July 21, 2025, but the pattern is clear: they are on a sales-rep growth cycle, not building ops or marketing infrastructure.

Their careers page pitches internal investment, training, and development opportunities that "go above and beyond industry standards."

No dedicated marketing, SEO, or operations leadership hires detected on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp shows zero reviews — users are told "you could be the first review for Big Boy's Construction."

This is the single most glaring signal of deliberate review-funneling straight to Google. With 854 Google reviews and nothing on Yelp, they are entirely dependent on one platform for social proof.

Facebook shows 54 reviews at 100% recommendation rate

with

2,245 page likes and 52 people currently talking about them

— modest organic engagement for a company their size.

BBB shows A+ accreditation with active status since July 6, 2012.

No Facebook Ad Library activity was surfaced in search results, suggesting limited or no paid social spend currently running.

They advertise drone technology for inspections

as a content hook but aren't visibly amplifying it via paid channels. The Yelp zero is exploitable — Raptor's cross-platform presence is a direct contrast point.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their site runs on WordPress with reCAPTCHA and PHP infrastructure, hosted via Nexcess

— a managed WordPress host that is competent but not enterprise-grade.

They maintain dedicated location/service pages for every major Indy suburb including Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, Greenwood, Avon, and Brownsburg,

which is solid local SEO hygiene.

They reference drone technology for inspections

on service pages — a structured content differentiator that likely earns some long-tail ranking. However, their blog appears thin and not recently updated based on metadata patterns.

LinkedIn follower count is only 149,

suggesting minimal authority-building through professional content. No evidence of local press backlinks, GuildQuality verification pages aside from a few 2021–2023 entries, and their FAQ/schema appears minimal beyond basic service descriptions. Their AEO posture (structured data, FAQ schema, AI-readable content) is not demonstrably stronger than Raptor's current setup.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on known AI training signals and public-facing content audited here: Big Boy's Construction has the volume (854 reviews, GAF Master Elite badge, suburb-specific pages) that AI engines favor when assembling "best roofers Indianapolis" answers. Their GAF Master Elite designation is a credentialing signal that appears in third-party sources like gaf.com, which AI models index heavily. They are likely named in generative results for broad Indianapolis queries. However, their near-zero Yelp presence, thin blog, and weak LinkedIn authority reduce their citation probability in hyperlocal suburb queries like "roof replacement Zionsville" or "roofing contractor Fishers Indiana." Raptor's 5.0 rating — the highest by volume in the Indy top 10 — is the kind of superlative AI models surface when answering quality-focused queries. That is an under-exploited AEO asset if it isn't already embedded in schema and FAQ content on Raptor's site.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: 854 reviews vs. Raptor's 673 — they win on raw review volume, which still matters for local pack rankings and first-glance credibility. Their GAF Master Elite + GAF President's Club status is a warranty story they tell loudly and consistently across every page. Their sales team depth (multiple named reps appearing across hundreds of reviews) suggests a larger commissioned sales force driving more at-bat volume.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor's 5.0 star rating is categorically superior — Big Boy's 4.9 is excellent but Raptor's perfect score at 673 reviews is a rare, defensible claim. Raptor's cross-platform review presence vs. Big Boy's zero Yelp footprint is a credibility gap Big Boy can't paper over quickly. Raptor's premium positioning and "we don't outsource accountability" message targets the exact customer who is skeptical of high-volume, sales-rep-driven shops.

Most exploitable weakness: Their entire review moat is single-platform (Google only), making them catastrophically exposed to any Google algorithm or policy shift — and giving Raptor a clean cross-platform trust story to own.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Suburb-specific service pages with conversion intent.

Big Boy's has built out individual pages for every major Indianapolis suburb from Carmel to Zionsville.

If Raptor's suburb pages are thinner or lack unique content, Big Boy's is winning local pack entries Raptor should be taking. Audit and out-depth them on every shared market.

  1. Named-rep review strategy. Reviews across GuildQuality, Google, and third-party aggregators repeatedly name specific sales reps — Gary, Mike, Robby, Russell — by name.

One reviewer noted their sales rep visited the install site three separate times and was quick to respond to all questions.

This rep-attribution pattern drives review specificity and trust. Cameron should evaluate whether Raptor's post-job review ask explicitly prompts customers to name their rep.

  1. GAF warranty story as a closing tool.

GAF Master Elite status unlocks a lifetime limited warranty with a 10-year 100% coverage period and the option to upgrade to a 50-year System Plus warranty including tear-off.

Big Boy's bakes this into every service page as a decision-stage conversion argument. If Raptor holds equivalent or superior certifications, this warranty narrative should be front-loaded in sales collateral and on landing pages — not buried.
