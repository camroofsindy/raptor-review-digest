🦅 RAPTOR ROOFING — COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE BRIEF

Competitor: Indy Roof & Restoration | Refreshed: Thu, April 25, 2026
Analyst: Cameron's CMO Desk

1. Who They Are

Indy Roof & Restoration was founded in 2018 by Aaron Christy, who serves as President and Founder.

They serve the Indianapolis area including Carmel, Geist, Fishers, Greenwood, Brownsburg, Westfield, and Zionsville

— a nearly identical footprint to Raptor's.

Their stated differentiators include being an Owens Corning Preferred, Malarkey Certified, IKO Certified, MuleHide Certified, and Duro-Last Certified contractor.

Their brand identity leans heavily on insurance advocacy and emergency response — positioning them as a restoration shop first, roofer second.

2. Estimated Scale

Clodura.AI lists Indy Roof & Restoration at 11–50 employees, with named leadership including founder Aaron Christy, Branch Manager Sam Frisby, Sales Manager Daniel Ralston, and Marketing Director Nick Segar.

SignalHire independently estimates approximately 30 employees.

Confidence: medium (multi-source corroboration, but no verified payroll data).

Revenue:

Prospeo estimates annual revenue of approximately $2.4 million, with an estimated valuation of $7.7 million.

Contrarian take: that revenue figure almost certainly undercounts a company pulling 844 Google reviews since 2018 with a dedicated marketing director and 30 staff — real number is likely $4–7M. Confidence: low. Treat as floor, not ceiling.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

LinkedIn shows Indy Roof & Restoration actively recruiting for a Roofing Consultant in Zionsville, IN, posted approximately 3 weeks ago.

Indeed also lists an active posting for a Water & Mold Restoration Sales rep paying $900–$1,300/week.

The Zionsville hire is the tell — that's prime Raptor territory. The water/mold role signals they're pushing deeper into the mitigation/restoration vertical, likely to raise average ticket size. No CMO, ops director, or digital marketing hires spotted in the last 30 days, but they already have a named Marketing Director (Nick Segar per Clodura) in-seat.

4. Off-Google Presence

Google: 844 reviews, 4.9 stars. Owner response rate of 1.0 (100%) is operationally impressive — someone on their team is assigned to this.

Facebook: 4,777 likes, 52 people currently talking about the page.

Active posting — crews deployed same-day post-storm. No confirmed active Facebook Ad Library spend visible, but their storm-response creative is clearly organic/video-first.

Yelp:

28 reviews on Yelp as of March 2026.

Yelp rating not publicly surfaced in results, but

at least one consumer noted: "I saw the reviews on Yelp and they're terrible and I wish I would've seen these before."

Classic review-funneling pattern — Google velocity is extraordinary (844 reviews), Yelp is a ghost town with negative sentiment. This is the discrepancy Cameron should weaponize.

BBB:

They claim an A+ BBB rating since inception in 2018.

However,

at least one formal BBB complaint alleges they misled a customer into gutter upgrades by citing false building code requirements and then failed to properly submit insurance documentation.

A separate complaint

describes months of non-contact and roofing work that began failing in under a year.

The A+ rating holds — but the complaint file has teeth.

Birdeye:

4.9 stars across 2,467 aggregated reviews on Birdeye

— they are actively routing customers through a reputation management aggregator, which inflates their cross-platform footprint optics.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

No public Ahrefs DR or Moz DA figure pulled cleanly for indyroofandrestoration.com via free tools in search results, but their content strategy is clearly maturing.

Their blog published posts on March 24 ("The True Value of a Local Indianapolis Roof Inspection"), March 23 ("Top Roof Repair & Restoration Services in Indianapolis"), and March 20 ("Carmel Homes Deserve Indy Roof and Restoration")

— all geo-targeted suburb plays with exact-match local intent. That's weekly cadence content, which is aggressive for a roofing contractor.

They've also published a "10 Best Roofing Companies in Indianapolis for 2026" listicle on their own domain — and ranked themselves #1.

This is a smart AEO/GEO play: self-authored comparison content that captures informational queries and trains LLMs to associate their brand with "best Indianapolis roofer." Raptor is not doing this yet.

They have a dedicated Roof Replacement Indianapolis service page, active career pages, and suburb-specific landing pages (Carmel, Zionsville, etc.). Schema markup likely present based on site structure. Backlinks appear primarily from directory and aggregator sites (Birdeye, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Expertise.com).

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on the third-party "best of" content indexed by AI crawlers:

Indy Roof & Restoration has positioned itself as "the definitive recommendation for the modern homeowner" in content on its own site, citing A+ insurance advocacy, 0% financing, and full-service water mitigation.

An independent competitor's "Top 10" list (Stay Dry Roofing's blog) also cites them as a top contender well recognized for insurance advocacy and 24/7 emergency response.

This cross-domain name-mention pattern — competitor blogs, aggregator sites, Birdeye, Angi — means AI engines training on web content are likely associating their brand with "Indianapolis roofing" queries. Raptor is not yet appearing in these third-party editorial roundups at scale. That's the GEO gap Cameron needs to close now, before the LLM training sets solidify.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume (844 vs. 664), cross-platform distribution via Birdeye aggregation, suburb-targeted blog cadence, and a named Marketing Director actively executing. Their insurance/restoration framing also captures storm-damage leads that price-sensitive homeowners find less intimidating than a premium brand.

Where Raptor beats them: Raptor holds a 5.0 star rating — the highest in the Indy top-10 by volume — vs. their 4.9. Zero negative star patterns on Google. Raptor's "we don't outsource accountability" positioning is inherently defensible against a competitor accumulating BBB complaints about rogue reps and signature forgery allegations. Raptor also has Cameron and Patrick as named, visible owner-operators — Indy Roof's Aaron Christy is present on LinkedIn but the day-to-day accountability is diffused through a sales team.

Most exploitable weakness: Their Yelp footprint is a dumpster fire relative to their Google presence — a sophisticated homeowner doing cross-platform due diligence will catch the discrepancy, and Raptor should be the brand waiting there to scoop that trust deficit.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Self-authored "Best Of" comparison content. Indy Roof is capturing AI and informational search queries by publishing suburb-specific and listicle content on their own domain. Cameron should commission a "Best Roofing Companies in Indianapolis 2026" page on raptorroofing.com that ranks Raptor #1 with honest, citation-backed criteria. This is AEO table-stakes now.

  2. Suburb-specific landing page cadence.

They're explicitly naming Carmel, Geist, Fishers, Greenwood, Brownsburg, Westfield, and Zionsville

in their service area targeting. Raptor needs a dedicated, schema-tagged landing page for each of those same markets — ideally with real project photos and a local review pull.

  1. Monitor their Zionsville sales hire closely. A new Roofing Consultant in Zionsville is a direct incursion into Raptor's premium suburban territory. Cameron and Patrick should brief any existing Zionsville referral relationships (real estate agents, insurance adjusters) before that rep gets their feet under them.
