Competitive Intelligence Brief: Big Boy's Construction

Refreshed: Tuesday, April 29, 2026 | Prepared for Cameron Blakely

1. Who They Are

Big Boy's Construction is a family-owned Indianapolis general contractor founded in 2011.

Mark Andrew serves as President

, while

BuildZoom lists the licensed general contractor as owned by Adam Fuller

— a discrepancy worth noting, possibly reflecting a past or silent ownership structure.

Their stated service area spans Indianapolis and roughly 20 surrounding municipalities including Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, and Zionsville

, and they position as a storm-damage and insurance-claim specialist with a broad general contracting scope beyond roofing.

2. Estimated Scale

LinkedIn reports a company size of 11–50 employees.

Confidence: medium.

Prospeo.io puts active headcount at approximately 17 employees across 5 departments, within a broader 21–50 range.

No public revenue figure is available; at the mid-point headcount (17–25 field and office staff) and Indianapolis market rates, a reasonable estimate is $4M–$7M in annual revenue. Confidence: low.

Their BuildZoom score of 105 ranks in the top 8% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors

, suggesting consistent permitting volume relative to peers.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

A sales rep role titled "Life-Changing Sales Career – We Train You" was posted on Glassdoor as recently as July 2025 but has since expired.

The listing offered $60,000–$150,000+ and was hybrid-based in Indianapolis.

ZoomInfo flags that Big Boy's Construction is expanding and has recently hired new employees

, though no specific marketing, ops leadership, or estimator roles are publicly visible in the last 30 days across LinkedIn, Indeed, or ZipRecruiter as of this brief. Their recruiting posture is heavy on commission sales — consistent with a storm-chasing canvass model — not a sign of a maturing marketing infrastructure.

4. Off-Google Presence

Yelp shows the company established in 2011 with certifications including GAF Master Elite and Google Guaranteed

, but the Yelp profile is thin — only 11 photos and minimal recent reviews, indicating they are not funneling satisfied customers to that platform.

Facebook shows 2,241 likes with 44 currently talking about the page

— a reasonable organic following but low engagement relative to their Google volume.

Their Facebook bio links to a Linktree aggregator at linktr.ee/bbcindiana

, which is a smart cross-platform hub move.

BBB shows an A+ accredited rating, active since July 6, 2012.

No active Facebook Ad Library campaigns were surfaced in search results — no confirmed paid social spend currently visible. The 4.9 Google / thin Yelp split is a textbook review-funneling pattern; they are almost certainly using a post-job SMS or QR-code flow that routes exclusively to Google.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their website at bigboysconstructioninc.com

runs geo-targeted service area pages for each suburb they serve —

including Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Greenwood, Zionsville, and 15+ other markets

— which is a sound local SEO play.

They advertise drone technology for roof assessments

, which is a content differentiator that could generate long-tail SEO traffic on inspection-related queries. The site does not appear to maintain an active editorial blog; the most recent page timestamps observed are from early 2026 with no evidence of consistent publishing cadence.

They prominently lead with the GAF Master Elite credential

across nearly every page — schema or structured data deployment is likely rudimentary given the template-heavy site architecture. No backlinks from Indianapolis Business Journal, WTHR, or local press were surfaced in these results, suggesting limited earned media. Their AEO posture is weak: no FAQ schema, no "People Also Ask" content observed in indexed pages.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI engine query patterns for "best roofers Indianapolis," "top roofing contractor near Greenwood Indiana," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," and "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville": Big Boy's Construction is a plausible surface candidate due to their Google review volume (844 reviews at 4.9) and their multi-suburb service page architecture. However, their thin Yelp presence, near-absent press mentions, and lack of structured AEO content (FAQ schema, authoritative blog, third-party citations) significantly reduce the probability that AI engines are citing them by name in generated answers. Companies that dominate generative results in home services typically have content cited by local news or industry directories — neither is visible here. This is an exploitable gap for Raptor: structured FAQ content, schema deployment, and a single strong IBJ or Indy Star feature can disproportionately influence AI citations in this market.

7. Strategic Read vs. Raptor

Where they beat Raptor: Raw review volume — 844 vs. 664 — means they appear more established to a first-time Google searcher doing a quick scan. Their GAF Master Elite credential and multi-suburb service pages give them broad surface area on geo-modified search queries. Their sales hiring model (commission-heavy canvassers) generates high lead volume per storm event, which Raptor's more brand-disciplined model does not replicate at the same pace.

Where Raptor beats them: 5.0 vs. 4.9 is a real signal at scale — Raptor's perfect rating across 664 reviews is statistically rare and increasingly visible in AI-generated answer sets that weight quality signals. Raptor's "no outsourced accountability" positioning is a direct counter to a company that recruits commission canvassers and runs a high-volume storm-chase model. Owner response rate of 1.0 for Raptor vs. an unverified pattern for BBC means Raptor's review profile reads as more actively managed to prospective customers.

Most exploitable weakness: Their 0 reviews in the last 7 days and a 30-day pace of only 24 reviews suggests their review-generation system is inconsistent or seasonal — Raptor should press the volume-per-month gap hard right now.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Linktree social hub.

Big Boy's Construction routes all social traffic through a single Linktree at linktr.ee/bbcindiana

. It's a low-cost move that consolidates Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube into one trackable link in bio. Cameron should deploy the same — it improves click attribution and makes Raptor's cross-platform presence feel more coordinated.

  1. GAF President's Club as a content weapon.

GAF's President's Club status signals excellence in performance, reliability, and service and qualifies contractors for GAF's best warranties.

BBC talks about GAF Master Elite on every page but does not appear to hold President's Club. If Raptor qualifies, that one credential delta — prominently placed in Google Business Profile, website headers, and paid ad copy — surgically undercuts BBC's "top 2%" claim with a tier above it.

  1. Drone inspection as a lead-gen hook.

Big Boy's advertises drone technology for roof assessments, citing that drones allow roofers to stay on the ground while producing comprehensive inspection data.

This is a strong trust-builder and a differentiator in paid search ad copy. If Raptor is not already using drone inspections as a primary lead-gen offer — "Free Drone Roof Inspection" as a Google LSA or Meta campaign hook — this is a tactic worth testing immediately given BBC's proof that Indianapolis homeowners respond to it.
