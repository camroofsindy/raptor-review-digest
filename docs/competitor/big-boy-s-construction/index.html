Competitive Intelligence Brief: Big Boy's Construction (BBC)

Raptor Roofing | Cameron Blakely, CMO | Refreshed: June 11, 2026

1. Who They Are

Big Boy's Construction was founded in 2011 and is headquartered in Indianapolis, Indiana.

President Mark Andrew leads the company

, with

Adam Fuller listed as the licensed general contractor of record per the Indianapolis license board, which shows a General Contractor license and 5 other active licenses.

They carry GAF Master Elite status — held by only the top 2% of U.S. roofing companies — and serve a broad footprint including Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, Greenwood, and surrounding counties across central Indiana.

2. Estimated Scale

LinkedIn lists their company size as 11–50 employees.

Prospeo data puts 17 employees across 5 departments, with the 21–50 range corroborated as the broader band.

Confidence: medium. On revenue: a GAF Master Elite shop in this review volume bracket with a multi-county service area and commercial + residential mix likely runs $4M–$8M annually. No public revenue figure available; estimate based on crew size and permit activity. Confidence: low.

Their BuildZoom score of 105 ranks in the top 8% of 22,324 Indiana licensed contractors

, suggesting consistent permit-pulling activity that supports the higher end of that range.

3. Recent Hiring Signals

No active job postings were surfaced on Indeed, LinkedIn, or ZipRecruiter in the last 30 days at the time of this refresh.

Their LinkedIn company page has only 149 followers

, and there is no visible paid recruitment activity. This is either a sign of a stable, low-turnover team or a company that recruits heavily through word-of-mouth and referral — common in insurance-claim-heavy roofing shops. Worth re-checking in 2 weeks; no marketing, sales ops, or estimator leadership hires are visible right now.

4. Off-Google Presence

The review-funnel gap here is real. BBC has 863 Google reviews at 4.9 stars, but

only 54 Facebook reviews

, and

just 8 "Faves" on their Nextdoor business page.

Yelp shows a listing with minimal review volume — no star rating was surfaced in results, which typically indicates fewer than 5 reviews on that platform.

They hold BBB A+ accreditation, active since July 6, 2012.

The pattern is classic review-funneling: near-total concentration on Google, thin presence everywhere else. That's an exploitable gap — homeowners researching on Nextdoor or Yelp get almost no BBC signal.

They do carry a "Google Guaranteed" badge per their Yelp certifications listing

, indicating LSA investment. Facebook page engagement is minimal —

2,245 likes with only 22 currently talking about the page.

No active Facebook Ad Library entries were surfaced.

5. SEO and AEO Posture

Their site runs on WordPress with reCAPTCHA and PHP infrastructure, hosted on Nexcess.

That's a mid-tier stack — capable but not technically aggressive.

They have dedicated service-area pages for every major Indy suburb

(Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, Greenwood, etc.), which is the right move for local SEO.

They mention drone technology prominently in their residential content

, which is a decent long-tail content hook.

Their BuildZoom score of 105 places them above 92% of Indiana contractors

, which correlates with legitimate backlink signals from permit and licensing aggregators. Blog content appears thin based on site structure — no editorial calendar or topical authority cluster is evident. Schema markup and AEO optimization (FAQ schema, HowTo schema) are not visibly promoted. GAF's contractor directory listing at gaf.com provides a legitimate high-DA backlink.

6. Generative Engine Visibility

Based on current AI engine behavior for Indy roofing queries ("best roofers Indianapolis," "roof replacement Carmel Indiana," "roofing company Fishers Noblesville Zionsville"), BBC has the profile attributes that can surface in generative results: high Google review volume, GAF Master Elite certification, named service-area pages, and BBB accreditation. However, their thin blog content, weak off-Google review presence, and low LinkedIn authority reduce their odds of being cited with confidence by LLMs that weight multi-source corroboration. Raptor's 5.0 rating with 677 reviews, combined with a stronger content-authority strategy, is a superior position for AEO citation — LLMs tend to favor the cleaner signal. Neither company has a documented AEO content play (FAQ hubs, structured Q&A pages, entity-authority content) that would make either a dominant AI-cited result. This is wide open for whoever moves first.

7. Strategic Read vs Raptor

Where BBC beats Raptor: Raw review volume — 863 vs. 677 — and the psychological weight that carries in a quick Google scan. Their GAF Master Elite + GAF President's Club signal and longer BBB accreditation history (since 2012) also lend institutional credibility. Their service-area page architecture is more built out.

Where Raptor beats BBC: Raptor's 5.0 rating is a cleaner signal than BBC's 4.9, and the 100% owner response rate vs. BBC's is a visible trust differentiator at the listing level. BBC's off-Google presence is paper-thin, creating authenticity vulnerability. Raptor's premium positioning ("we don't outsource accountability") is a harder narrative to commoditize than BBC's insurance-claim-volume play.

Most exploitable weakness: BBC's entire review moat is concentrated on a single platform — a Nextdoor or Yelp-aware homeowner gets almost zero social proof from them, which is a direct opening for Raptor to dominate alternative discovery surfaces.

8. What Raptor Should Watch or Copy

  1. Service-area page depth.

BBC has individual landing pages for every suburb in their footprint — Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Zionsville, Greenwood, and beyond.

If Raptor's suburb-specific pages are thinner, this is a concrete SEO gap to close before storm season drives search volume.

  1. GAF President's Club positioning.

GAF entrusts a select few Master Elite contractors with President's Club status, showing excellence in performance, reliability, and service.

Monitor whether BBC starts marketing this distinction more aggressively — and if Raptor has or can pursue equivalent manufacturer-tier credentialing, that's a legitimate trust signal to own in paid and organic copy.

  1. Drone inspection as a content and sales hook.

BBC actively promotes drone technology for exterior inspections, allowing roofers to stay on the ground while producing comprehensive damage assessments.

This is showing up in their residential content as a differentiator. If Raptor uses drones operationally, that story needs to be front and center in video, ads, and sales decks — BBC is already training the market to expect it.
